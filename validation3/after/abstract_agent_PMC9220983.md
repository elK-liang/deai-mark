Liquid biopsy is an emerging blood-based detection technology whose value for breast cancer screening is not yet established. We used the MISCAN-Fadia microsimulation model to estimate the benefits, harms, and costs of liquid biopsy-based breast cancer screening compared with digital mammography in women aged 50 to 74. A liquid biopsy with a DCIS sensitivity of 91% and screen detection at a tumor size of 1.39 cm detected 499,859 cases of invasive breast cancer and 211,799 cases of DCIS in routine screening, corresponding to a combined sensitivity for invasive breast cancer and DCIS of 70%; when the liquid biopsy was unable to detect DCIS, the combined sensitivity decreased to 62%. Both benefits and harms increased with higher sensitivity for invasive breast cancer, in terms of mortality reduction and overdiagnosis. For example, a liquid biopsy unable to detect DCIS with a combined sensitivity of 67% resulted in a mortality reduction of 23.1% and 1.9 overdiagnoses per 1000 women, compared with a mortality reduction of 25.1% and 18.1 overdiagnoses per 1000 women for a liquid biopsy able to detect DCIS, both with the same model input for invasive breast cancer. Higher specificity positively influenced quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) and lowered costs at all sensitivity levels, because false-positive screens reduced QALYs gained and increased total screening costs, and thus increased the eventual maximum price of a liquid biopsy. Only a liquid biopsy with a combined sensitivity of 73% without DCIS detection yielded a higher mortality reduction (26.1%) than digital mammography. These model estimates indicate that the sensitivity and specificity profile of a liquid biopsy determines the balance of benefits, harms, and costs in breast cancer screening.
