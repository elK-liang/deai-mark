Abstract: Large-scale genome sequencing of SARS-CoV-2 underpins genomic surveillance of the COVID-19 pandemic, and scalable, low-cost library preparation methods are required to meet its throughput demands. We developed CoronaHiT, a novel library preparation method for SARS-CoV-2 genome sequencing that combines cheap transposase-based introduction of adapters (Illumina Nextera) with symmetric PCR barcoding of up to 96 samples (95 samples plus a negative control) on a MinION flowcell; the barcodes can be switched for Illumina sequencing when higher throughput is needed. CoronaHiT does not require individual sample washes and allows samples to be processed uniformly without quantification or normalization. We tested the method by multiplexing 95 routine COG-UK SARS-CoV-2 samples plus a blank, and 59 rapid response samples, including 18 query outbreak samples plus blanks, the latter sequenced within 24 h of receipt with results available the following day. All samples were also sequenced using the ARTIC LoCost protocol for comparison. For the routine samples, CoronaHiT-ONT yielded 9.6 Gbases of sequence data (average 1145X coverage per sample), ARTIC LoCost produced 8.0 Gbases (1719X) and CoronaHiT-Illumina 15.7 Gbases (4649X). For the rapid response dataset, CoronaHiT-ONT produced 5.7 Gbases (1104X), ARTIC LoCost 4.5 Gbases (1421X) and CoronaHiT-Illumina 7.3 Gbases (3010X). Both CoronaHiT-ONT runs showed less variation in coverage between samples than the ARTIC LoCost runs, with lower standard deviation relative to the mean. CoronaHiT thus enables high-throughput, flexible and cost-effective sequencing of SARS-CoV-2 genomes for pandemic surveillance.
