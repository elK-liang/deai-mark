To characterize the nationwide epidemiological profile of chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) in South Korea, we analyzed data from 2010 to 2020 covering an average annual population of about 50.8 million individuals. Over the 11‑year period, a mean of 22,720 ± 3,284 patients received a new CFS diagnosis each year, with females outnumbering males by a ratio of 1.49. The absolute number of incident cases rose from 16,175 in 2010 to 25,403 in 2020 (a 1.57‑fold increase), with a slightly larger rise in women (1.66‑fold) than in men (1.44‑fold). The mean annual incidence per 100,000 population was 44.71 ± 6.10 (95 % CI 40.57–48.76), climbing from 32.43 in 2010 to 49.47 in 2020 (1.53‑fold). Female incidence (53.49 ± 8.05, 95 % CI 48.00–58.82) exceeded male incidence (35.93 ± 4.30, 95 % CI 33.01–38.79) by 1.49‑fold, a pattern observed across all age groups from 10 to 79 years. The greatest absolute number of new diagnoses occurred in the 50–59‑year cohort for both sexes, while the highest relative frequency was seen in men aged ≥80 years and in women aged 60–69 years. The mean number of recorded CFS patients per year was 29,320 ± 6,456, corresponding to a prevalence of 57.70 ± 12.20 per 100,000. These findings disclose a steadily increasing burden of CFS in South Korea, a consistent female predominance, and distinct age‑related distributions that should guide public‑health strategies and future aetiological research.
