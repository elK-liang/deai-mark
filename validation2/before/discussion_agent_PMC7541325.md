The cellular composition of tissues determines their function in health and their response to disease, but bulk gene expression data, the most widely available measurement modality, measure only the aggregate of the cells present. Computational deconvolution addresses this limitation by inferring cell type abundances from bulk profiles, and in this study we present the murine Microenvironment Cell Population counter, mMCP-counter, a method to estimate the abundance of tissue-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations in murine samples using gene expression. The method follows the marker-based strategy established by the human MCP-counter, relying on transcriptomic markers with high expression in a given cell population, including all its subpopulations, and zero expression in any other population.

The construction of the marker resource is the substantive contribution of this work. We assembled a hierarchical model of murine cell populations by completing the hematopoietic tree provided by ImmGen with a survey of the literature, so that both the categories and their inclusion relationships were represented as comprehensively as possible. Transcriptomic profiles of sorted cell populations from the ImmGen microarray datasets provided the training material, and, importantly, we added non-immune, non-stromal negative controls, including epithelial cell lines, hypothalamic cell lines, melanoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer cell lines, myoblasts, and hepatocytes, to ensure that markers would not cross-react with parenchymal cells. Markers were selected under stringent criteria, requiring a fold change above 2.1, a specific fold change above 2.1, and an area under the ROC curve above 0.97, and only populations fully represented by all their subcategories were considered, yielding signatures for 55 populations. The example of the mast cell marker Tpsb2 illustrates the stringency of this selection: its median expression showed a log2 fold change of 6.48 over all non-mast cell samples and a specific fold change of 5.94 over the expression variance among non-mast cell samples.

The availability of a murine counterpart to MCP-counter addresses a practical gap in translational research. Murine models, including genetically engineered models, syngeneic tumor models, and organ-specific disease models, are the workhorses of preclinical immunology and oncology, yet the abundance estimates for tissue-infiltrating cells in these models have largely depended on flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, or human-derived deconvolution signatures that do not port across species. A species-appropriate deconvolution method allows the wealth of existing and future murine bulk transcriptomic datasets, including public repositories where only the raw expression matrices are available, to be mined for microenvironmental information without new experiments. This is particularly valuable for longitudinal study designs and for rare or hard-to-disseminate tissues, where physical cell isolation is impractical, and for retrospective analyses in which the samples no longer exist.

The marker-based approach embodied in mMCP-counter has specific methodological advantages relative to model-based deconvolution. Because it does not require the full transcriptome of every cell type to be represented in a reference matrix, it is robust to the absence of unmeasured cell types, whose influence on the estimates is limited to the expression dilution that affects all abundance measures. The estimates are relative rather than absolute, and their interpretation as enrichment scores across samples within a study is the appropriate analytical frame. The reliance on ImmGen data, generated under standardized conditions with rigorous sorting strategies, provides a level of marker quality control that ad hoc signatures derived from single studies cannot match.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The method estimates abundance only for the populations for which qualified markers were identified, and cell types absent from the ImmGen hierarchy, or insufficiently distinct at the transcriptomic level, are invisible to it. The signatures were trained on microarray data, and their application to RNA sequencing datasets, while supported by the robustness of well-chosen markers, involves a platform transition that deserves attention for genes with divergent probe behavior. Mouse strain differences, sex, age, and activation state can shift the expression of marker genes, and extreme activation states, such as highly polarized macrophages, may strain the assumption of stable marker expression within a population. The estimates are derived from the sampled tissue and represent the local microenvironment, so comparisons across tissues require care. Finally, as with all deconvolution methods, validation against an orthogonal measurement, ideally flow cytometry or immunostaining on the same samples, remains the appropriate practice when quantitative accuracy is critical. With these caveats, mMCP-counter extends microenvironment deconvolution to murine systems with a rigorously curated marker resource, enabling the extraction of immune and stromal cell abundance information from murine bulk transcriptomic data across research applications.
