Organic solar cells (OSCs) have attracted considerable attention as a next‑generation photovoltaic technology because of their potential for low‑cost, flexible, and semitransparent devices1. The rapid rise in power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) over the past decade has been largely driven by the emergence of non‑fullerene acceptors (NFAs), whose tunable optical and electronic properties enable better light harvesting and reduced voltage losses compared with traditional fullerene derivatives2,3. Despite these advances, the overall energy loss (E_loss) from the bandgap of the active material to the open‑circuit voltage (V_OC) remains a key limiting factor for reaching the thermodynamic efficiency ceiling4,5. In particular, the energetic offset between donor and acceptor frontier orbitals required for efficient charge separation contributes substantially to voltage loss, and the intrinsic reorganization energy (λ) of the acceptor governs the kinetics of charge transfer and recombination processes6,7.

A central strategy to minimize E_loss is to engineer acceptors that possess both a high LUMO level (to boost V_OC) and a low reorganization energy (to reduce the voltage drop associated with exciton dissociation and charge recombination)8,9. The benchmark Y‑type NFA Y6 exemplifies this approach, delivering high efficiencies through a strong intramolecular charge‑transfer (ICT) effect and well‑aligned energy levels with common donors10. Nevertheless, the pronounced ICT in Y6 leads to a relatively large Stokes shift and considerable λ, which can increase non‑radiative voltage losses11,12. Moreover, the electron‑withdrawing benzothiadiazole (BTZ) core in Y6 fixes the energy offsets for hole and electron transfer, often leaving little room to further raise V_OC without compromising charge separation13.

To address these limitations, we replaced the BTZ core of Y6 with a weaker electron‑withdrawing quinoline (Qx) unit and fused Qx derivatives into the heteroaromatic backbone, generating two new Y‑type acceptors designated Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 (Fig. 1a). The weaker electron‑withdrawing character of the Qx core diminishes the ICT effect, thereby widening the optical bandgap while simultaneously elevating both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels. The upward shift of the LUMO provides a larger driving force for obtaining a higher V_OC, while the reduced energy offsets between donor and acceptor HOMOs and LUMOs lower the voltage loss required for efficient exciton dissociation. Notably, the Qx‑based acceptors display markedly smaller Stokes shifts than Y6, indicative of reduced excited‑state relaxation and a smaller reorganization energy. Additionally, both Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 exhibit excellent solubility in common processing solvents (e.g., >13 mg mL⁻¹ in chloroform), facilitating uniform film formation and scalable device fabrication.

In this work we systematically investigate how the Qx‑engineered acceptors translate these molecular features into improved photovoltaic performance. By pairing Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 with a prototypical polymer donor, we demonstrate that the devices achieve substantially reduced voltage losses relative to Y6‑based controls, resulting in enhanced V_OC while maintaining efficient charge generation. These findings underscore the importance of tailoring the acceptor core to simultaneously raise the LUMO level and suppress reorganization energy, providing a viable route toward low‑energy‑loss OSCs that approach the detailed balance limit.
