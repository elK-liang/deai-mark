Transfer RNA-derived small RNAs have emerged from the shadow of degradation products to become recognized regulatory molecules, but their potential as disease biomarkers, particularly in plasma, remains largely unexplored. In this study, we profiled the landscape of tRNA-derived fragments in the plasma of colorectal cancer patients and healthy controls using small RNA high-throughput sequencing, identified 5'-tRF-GlyGCC as a dramatically altered candidate, and validated it as a novel diagnostic biomarker for colorectal cancer.

The global profiling established that the tDR repertoire of plasma is structured and disease-sensitive. In both colorectal cancer patients and healthy controls, the abundance of tDRs decreased in the order 5'-tRF, 5'-half or i-tRF, 3'-tRF, and 3'-half, indicating that 5'-derived fragments dominate the circulating tDR pool, and the proportion of 5'-tRFs was significantly greater in colorectal cancer plasma than in controls. Hierarchical clustering revealed systematic variations in tDR expression between the two groups, and 628 and 745 tDRs were detected in colorectal cancer and healthy control plasma respectively. Within the 5'-tRF class, the profiles were substantially different: the most abundant 5'-tRF in healthy control plasma was 5'-tRF-HisGTG, whereas in colorectal cancer plasma it was 5'-tRF-GlyGCC, whose share of the 5'-tRF pool increased from 7.44% in controls to 52.24% in colorectal cancer, a seven-fold relative enrichment that made it the standout candidate. Concurrently, the proportions of 5'-tRF-GlyCCC increased in cancer plasma, while 5'-tRF-HisGTG and 5'-tRF-AlaTGC decreased, demonstrating that the tRF signature of colorectal cancer involves coordinated, tRNA-isotype-specific alterations rather than a uniform shift.

The magnitude of the 5'-tRF-GlyGCC change deserves emphasis, because biomarker candidates with fold changes of this size are rare among circulating nucleic acids. Several features of this molecule support its candidacy. As a 5'-tRF, it derives from the aminoacyl stem region of its parent tRNA, and the specific overrepresentation of a glycine tRNA-derived fragment resonates with the metabolic reprogramming of proliferating tumor cells, in which glycine metabolism occupies a central position in nucleotide biosynthesis and one-carbon metabolism. The stress-induced generation of tRFs is known to depend on angiogenin-mediated cleavage and on the accessibility of specific tRNAs to cleavage, which is modulated by their modification status, and the involvement of ALKBH3, the tRNA demethylase examined in this study, provides a mechanistic link between tRNA modification state, tRF biogenesis, and cancer biology, consistent with the established role of ALKBH3 in tumor maintenance. From a practical standpoint, the fragment is short, stable in plasma, and measurable by qRT-PCR, the analytical characteristics required for a clinically deployable liquid biopsy.

The diagnostic positioning of this biomarker in colorectal cancer is clinically meaningful. Colorectal cancer screening currently relies on colonoscopy and fecal tests, both of which face adherence barriers, and a blood-based test, or a test integrated with existing plasma analyses, could expand screening uptake. Our sequencing-based discovery followed by qRT-PCR validation, with the diagnostic performance assessed in independent plasma cohorts, establishes the analytical pipeline that such a test would require, and the elevation of 5'-tRF-GlyGCC in cancer plasma irrespective of tumor stage would determine its utility for early detection, the clinically decisive application.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The discovery phase rested on a small number of plasma samples, three per group, which is adequate for hypothesis generation but means that the full diversity of tDR alterations in colorectal cancer has probably not been captured, and rare variants may have been missed. The validation cohorts, although larger, require extension to clinically diverse populations, including patients with benign colorectal disease, adenomas, and inflammatory conditions, since specificity against these competing diagnoses determines clinical usefulness. The biological function of 5'-tRF-GlyGCC in colorectal cancer, whether it participates in tumor biology or is a byproduct of altered tRNA turnover, remains unresolved, and the cell of origin of the circulating fragment, whether tumor-derived or derived from host responses to the tumor, has not been definitively traced. Pre-analytical variables in plasma handling and the stability of tRFs under different storage conditions need standardization for multicenter application. With these caveats, our study establishes 5'-tRF-GlyGCC as a prominently altered, mechanistically grounded, and technically accessible plasma biomarker candidate for colorectal cancer diagnosis and expands the diagnostic repertoire of the tRNA-derived small RNA class.
