Organic solar cells have advanced rapidly over the past decade, driven largely by the development of non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) that combine strong absorption, tunable energy levels, and favorable bulk heterojunction morphology. Power conversion efficiencies of NFA-based devices now rival those of established thin-film photovoltaic technologies, and molecular design has become the principal lever for further improvement. Among the NFAs developed to date, the Y-series acceptors, exemplified by Y6, have proven particularly successful and now serve as the reference platform from which new molecular designs are derived.

A central quantity limiting the efficiency of organic solar cells is the voltage loss, the difference between the optical gap of the absorber and the operating voltage of the device. Voltage losses arise from radiative recombination and, more significantly, from non-radiative recombination, which is closely related to the energetic driving force required for charge transfer and exciton dissociation and to the reorganization energy accompanying these processes. Reducing the energetic offset between donor and acceptor frontier orbitals reduces the driving force and hence the voltage loss of exciton dissociation, but sustaining efficient charge generation at such small offsets requires materials with low reorganization energy and small excited-state relaxation. Molecular features that suppress structural relaxation after photoexcitation, such as reduced Stokes shifts, are therefore valuable design targets for acceptors that enable low energy losses.

In Y-type NFAs, the electron-deficient fused core largely determines the intramolecular charge transfer character, the frontier orbital energies, and the optical gap. Replacing the electron-withdrawing benzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole-derived core of Y6 with a weaker electron-withdrawing quinoxaline-based unit weakens the intramolecular charge transfer effect, increases the bandgap, and raises both the HOMO and LUMO energy levels through the enhanced electron-donating ability of the core. A higher LUMO level facilitates a higher open-circuit voltage, while reduced energy offsets between the donor and acceptor HOMOs and LUMOs decrease the voltage loss of exciton dissociation.

Here, we report two acceptors, Qx-1 and Qx-2, constructed by fusing quinoxaline derivatives into the heteroaromatic core of a Y-type NFA scaffold. We characterize their electrochemical and optical properties, show that their Stokes shifts are smaller than that of Y6, indicating smaller excited-state relaxations that are beneficial for reducing voltage losses, and demonstrate that these small reorganization energy acceptors enable low energy losses and improved photovoltaic performance in non-fullerene organic solar cells.
