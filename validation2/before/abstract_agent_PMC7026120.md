Abstract: BORIS/CTCFL is aberrantly expressed in cancer, and we examined its role in melanoma development and progression. Analysis of expression data for testis, skin and melanoma samples showed significantly higher BORIS expression in melanoma and testis compared to skin samples, with significantly higher expression among metastatic than primary melanoma samples. In a panel of melanoma and non-malignant congenital nevi cell lines, BORIS was observed in melanoma cell lines but not in congenital nevi cells, confirming that BORIS is aberrantly expressed in melanoma cell lines and tumor samples compared to non-malignant cell lines and normal skin. To dissect its function, we established a doxycycline (dox)-inducible model of BORIS expression in the MM057 melanoma cell line, which expresses low BORIS mRNA compared to other melanoma cell lines, with BORIS expression confirmed by immunoblot. BORIS-expressing cells showed a striking reduction in cell number during culture compared to control cells. Expression of BORIS for 3 days did not significantly reduce cell proliferation except at the highest doxycycline concentration, whereas after 5 and 7 days of BORIS expression a significant reduction in proliferation was observed even at low doxycycline concentrations, with doxycycline itself not affecting proliferation of empty-vector cells. Based on these results, BORIS expression was induced with 50-100 ng/ml doxycycline for five days for further functional characterization. These findings show that BORIS/CTCFL promotes a switch from a proliferative towards an invasive phenotype in melanoma cells, identifying BORIS as a candidate driver and marker of melanoma progression.
