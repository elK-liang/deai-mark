Lower-grade gliomas were redefined by the WHO 2021 classification around isocitrate dehydrogenase mutation status and 1p/19q co-deletion, but the relationship between these genetic groups and the transcriptomic and methylation subtypes identified by unsupervised analyses has remained incompletely mapped. In this study, we generated integrated multi-omics maps of lower-grade gliomas that align genetic groups, expression subtypes, and methylation subtypes within a single framework, using self-organizing maps to organize the transcriptome, DNA methylome, and copy number data.

The genetic stratification confirms and extends the WHO 2021 groups. The IDH-wild-type tumors, which by definition include gliomas without IDH1/2 mutation, accumulated the combined chromosome 7 gain and chromosome 10 loss characteristic of glioblastoma in 12 of 19 cases (63%), supporting their classification as molecularly grade IV disease despite histological lower-grade appearance. Among IDH-mutant tumors, the oligodendroglioma group with 1p/19q co-deletion was distinguished by a high amount of TERT promoter methylation, whereas the astrocytoma groups, IDH-A and the IDH-A' variant carrying an additional chromosome 19q deletion, lacked co-deletion, with chromosome 7 gains and chromosome 10 losses occurring mostly uncombined in these groups, as illustrated by the 16 versus 6 tumors with these alterations among the 54 IDH-A cases. These copy number patterns provide molecular support for the separation of IDH-mutant astrocytoma from secondary glioblastoma-like progression and identify the IDH-A' subgroup as a distinct entity warranting clinical follow-up.

The methylation analysis reveals a coherent ordering across the genetic groups. Mean methylation values averaged over the tumors of each group increased in the order IDH-wt, IDH-A/A', IDH-O, and the overall methylation followed the CpG island methylator phenotype signature, confirming that G-CIMP characterizes the hypermethylation of IDH-mutant lower-grade gliomas and that its intensity varies with the genetic subgroup. Prognostically, G-CIMP tumors had better outcomes than IDH-wt tumors in terms of hazard ratio, and the prognosis of oligodendroglioma was slightly better than that of astrocytoma, in line with the established clinical hierarchy. The parallel analysis of G-protein receptor methylation showed group relations similar to the G-CIMP signature but with different slopes between the G-CIMP and GPCR methylation curves of each group, indicating that methylation changes across groups are not uniform genome-wide but proceed at locus-class-specific rates, a finding relevant to understanding how the glioma methylation program is constructed.

The integration of expression and methylation subtypes with the genetic groups constitutes the central contribution of the maps. The previously defined eight expression subtypes and six methylation subtypes accumulated differentially in the genetic groups: E1 and M1 in IDH-wt, E6 and M5 in IDH-O, and E2-E4 with M2-M4 in IDH-A/A', with the neuronal subtypes E7, E8, and M6, reflecting samples of reduced tumor cell content, distributed across all genetic groups and partly displaying molecular characteristics of healthy brain. This correspondence demonstrates that the transcriptomic and epigenomatic heterogeneity of lower-grade gliomas is structured by, but not reducible to, the genetic groups, and that some of the apparent heterogeneity is a sampling artifact of variable tumor purity rather than true biological diversity. Recognizing the neuronal, low-cellularity samples explicitly prevents their misinterpretation as biological subtypes, an important methodological caution for all future classification studies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The mapping relies on the cohorts available for integrative analysis, and while the concordance across data modalities is reassuring, the sample sizes of individual genetic groups, particularly the IDH-wt lower-grade gliomas, are modest, and rare subgroups may be underrepresented. The self-organizing map approach, like all unsupervised methods, depends on the input feature selection and does not by itself assign causality, so the mechanisms by which methylation states and expression programs are connected remain inferential. The clinical annotations, including treatment history, vary across the collected cases, and the prognostic comparisons are adjusted only to the extent the data allow. Finally, the longitudinal stability of the subtype assignments, and their behavior at recurrence after temozolomide treatment, where hypermutation can alter methylome and transcriptome, were not addressed. With these caveats, the integrated multi-omics maps presented here provide a unified view of lower-grade glioma heterogeneity that connects WHO 2021 genetic groups with transcriptomic and methylation subtypes, distinguishes true biological subtypes from tumor purity artifacts, and offers a reference framework for the molecular diagnosis and classification of these tumors.
