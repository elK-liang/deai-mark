tRNA-derived small RNAs (tDRs) are emerging as circulating biomarkers, and we investigated their plasma expression profiles for the diagnosis of colorectal cancer (CRC). Small RNAs ranging from 10 to 50 bp from plasma samples of 3 CRC patients and 3 healthy controls (HCs) were analyzed using small RNA high-throughput sequencing. The abundance of tDRs decreased in the order 5'-tRF > 5'-half/i-tRF > 3'-tRF > 3'-half in both HCs and CRC plasma, and the proportion of 5'-tRFs in CRC plasma was significantly greater than that in HCs, suggesting that 5'-tRFs may be involved in CRC tumorigenesis and progression. Hierarchical clustering showed systematic variations in tDR expression between HCs and CRC plasma, with 628 and 745 tDRs obtained in CRC and HCs, respectively. The 5'-tRF profiles in CRC plasma were largely different from those in HC plasma: the highest-abundance 5'-tRF in HC plasma was 5'-tRF-HisGTG, whereas in CRC plasma it was 5'-tRF-GlyGCC, which accounted for 7.44% of 5'-tRFs in HC plasma but increased to 52.24% of 5'-tRFs in CRC plasma. In addition, the proportions of 5'-tRF-GlyCCC markedly increased in CRC plasma, while the percentages of 5'-tRF-HisGTG and 5'-tRF-AlaTGC obviously decreased. These findings identify the plasma 5'-tRF-GlyGCC as a markedly enriched tRNA-derived small RNA in colorectal cancer and a novel biomarker candidate for CRC diagnosis.
