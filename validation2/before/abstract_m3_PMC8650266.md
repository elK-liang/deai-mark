Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) is a complex and disabling disorder, yet nationwide epidemiological data from many countries, including South Korea, remain limited. This study aimed to characterize the epidemiological features of physician-diagnosed CFS in South Korea over an 11-year period. Methods: We analyzed national data on patients newly diagnosed with CFS between 2010 and 2020. Annual incidence and prevalence rates were calculated per 100,000 individuals using the Korean population as the denominator, and age- and sex-specific patterns were examined. Results: The mean number of patients newly diagnosed with CFS each year was 22,720 ± 3,284, with a consistent female predominance of 1.49-fold (males 9,122 ± 1,152 vs. females 13,598 ± 2,179). The annual number of new CFS diagnoses increased 1.57-fold from 16,175 in 2010 to 25,403 in 2020, with a slightly greater increase in females (1.66-fold) than in males (1.44-fold). The mean annual incidence was 44.71 ± 6.10 cases per 100,000 individuals (95% CI: 40.57–48.76), rising 1.53-fold from 32.43 in 2010 to 49.47 in 2020. Female incidence (53.49 ± 8.05; 95% CI: 48.00–58.82) was 1.49-fold higher than male incidence (35.93 ± 4.30; 95% CI: 33.01–38.79), and this female-predominant pattern was consistently observed across the population aged 10 to 79 years. The absolute number of new diagnoses peaked in the 50–59-year-old age group in both sexes, while the relative incidence frequency peaked in males aged ≥80 years and in females aged 60–69 years (both at 70.7 patients). Conclusions: This nationwide study demonstrates that physician-diagnosed CFS in South Korea increased substantially between 2010 and 2020, with a persistent female predominance across nearly all age groups. These findings provide a foundational epidemiological reference for understanding the disease burden of CFS in South Korea and underscore the need for continued surveillance and sex-specific research.
