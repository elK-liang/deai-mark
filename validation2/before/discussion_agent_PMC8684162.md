Tumor-associated macrophages are abundant constituents of the renal cell carcinoma microenvironment, and their polarization state is increasingly linked to tumor behavior. In this study, we examined the role of M2-polarized macrophages in clear cell renal cell carcinoma and identified the chemokine CXCL13 as a mediator of their pro-tumoral effects. Immunohistochemical assessment of CD206, a marker of M2 macrophages, in 55 ccRCC samples showed that the degree of M2 macrophage infiltration was positively correlated with pathological grade and T stage, being significantly higher in G3-4 than G1-2 tumors and in T3-4 than T1-2 tumors, while no significant association was found with gender or age. These correlations suggest that M2 macrophages accumulate as tumors become more aggressive and advanced.

To move from correlation to mechanism, we established an in vitro co-culture system in which THP-1-derived macrophages were polarized toward the M2 phenotype by ccRCC cells. Co-culture of PMA-converted M0 macrophages with Caki-1 and ACHN cells substantially increased CD206-positive rates, demonstrating that ccRCC cell lines can educate macrophages toward an M2-like phenotype, mirroring the polarization observed in vivo. Conditioned medium from these M2 macrophages, as well as direct co-culture, enhanced the proliferation, migration, and invasion of Caki-1 and ACHN cells. The functional assays were complemented by analysis of epithelial-mesenchymal transition markers, which showed a substantial reduction in E-cadherin expression in ccRCC cells exposed to M2 macrophages, indicating that the pro-migratory and pro-invasive effects are accompanied by induction of EMT, a program centrally involved in the acquisition of invasive capacity.

Our findings identify CXCL13 as a key soluble mediator secreted by M2 macrophages that drives these phenotypic changes in ccRCC cells. This chemokine, classically associated with B-cell recruitment in lymphoid organs, is emerging as a participant in tumor-stroma crosstalk, and our data extend its function to renal cancer, where it promotes the migration, invasion, and EMT of tumor cells. The paracrine nature of this interaction suggests that the M2 macrophage contribution to ccRCC progression is not confined to physical contact within the tumor mass but operates through a diffusable signal that could be therapeutically interrupted. Blocking the CXCL13 axis, at the ligand or receptor level, is an attractive strategy because it targets a stromal-tumor communication node rather than tumor-cell-intrinsic pathways that are prone to resistance.

These results should be integrated with the broader context of renal cancer immunobiology. Clear cell renal cell carcinoma is regarded as an immunogenic tumor, and immune checkpoint blockade has transformed its treatment, yet many patients do not respond or eventually progress. The abundance of M2-polarized macrophages in high-grade and advanced tumors, as documented here, may contribute to this resistance both by direct pro-tumoral signaling through mediators such as CXCL13 and by maintaining an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Strategies that reprogram macrophage polarization or interrupt M2-derived signals could therefore act synergistically with checkpoint inhibitors, a hypothesis that our in vitro system provides a foundation for testing.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The clinical correlation is based on a single-center cohort of 55 patients assessed with one M2 marker, and the prognostic significance of CD206-positive infiltration requires validation in larger cohorts, ideally with multiplexed phenotyping that distinguishes macrophage subsets more precisely. The mechanistic work relies on THP-1-derived macrophages and two ccRCC cell lines, which capture only part of the heterogeneity of macrophage-tumor interactions, and the direct contribution of CXCL13 within the in vivo tumor microenvironment remains to be demonstrated through neutralization or genetic approaches in animal models. In addition, the receptor mediating CXCL13 signaling in ccRCC cells and the downstream pathways linking it to EMT require definition. Within these boundaries, our study establishes a correlation between M2 macrophage infiltration and ccRCC aggressiveness, demonstrates that M2 macrophages promote ccRCC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT, and nominates the CXCL13 axis as a targetable mediator of macrophage-driven progression in renal cancer.
