Early tumor shrinkage (ETS) is increasingly used as an early efficacy marker in metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) but its relationship with patient‑reported quality of life (QoL) remains incompletely understood. This study examined the impact of ETS on QoL in patients receiving first‑line cetuximab plus chemotherapy within the prospective phase II QUACK trial. Methods: Of 140 enrolled patients, 128 with complete data were analyzed. ETS was defined as ≥20% reduction in target lesions at the first response evaluation. QoL was assessed with the EORTC QLQ‑C30 at baseline and subsequent visits; functional scales (global health status/QoL, social, role, physical, cognitive, emotional) and eight symptom items (appetite loss, pain, diarrhea, constipation, dyspnea, fatigue, insomnia, nausea/vomiting) were compared between patients with and without ETS. Results: Median age was 66 years (range 27–89), 68.0% were male, and 83.6% had ECOG performance status 0. At baseline, 39.8% reported clinically relevant symptoms. ETS occurred in 64.1% (82/128) of patients. Baseline characteristics were balanced between groups except for tumor differentiation (p = 0.044). ETS was associated with a higher objective response rate (76.8% vs 21.7%, p < 0.001) and a higher likelihood of surgical resection of metastases (28.6% vs 11.4%, p = 0.031). No statistically significant differences between ETS and non‑ETS groups were observed for any QLQ‑C30 functional domain (all p > 0.05), although a trend toward better physical functioning was noted (p = 0.051). Symptom scores were similar between groups. Conclusion: Although ETS achieved with first‑line cetuximab‑based therapy predicted improved tumor response and increased resectability, it did not translate into statistically significant differences in overall QoL or symptom burden in this cohort; larger studies are needed to clarify the patient‑centered impact of early tumor shrinkage in mCRC.
