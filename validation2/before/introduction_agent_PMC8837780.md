Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) is the most lethal gynecologic malignancy. Because the disease is largely asymptomatic at early stages, the majority of patients are diagnosed with advanced, peritoneally disseminated tumor, and despite cytoreductive surgery combined with platinum-based chemotherapy, most experience recurrence and ultimately chemoresistance. New insights into the molecular drivers of EOC initiation, progression, and metastasis are therefore needed, both to identify prognostic markers and to reveal vulnerabilities that can be exploited therapeutically.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), a heterogeneous class of transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides with little or no protein-coding capacity, have emerged as key regulators of cancer biology. Among their best-characterized modes of action is the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism, in which lncRNAs act as molecular sponges for microRNAs, thereby relieving repression of the microRNAs' target messenger RNAs. Through this and related mechanisms, lncRNAs participate in the control of proliferation, apoptosis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and metastasis. In ovarian cancer, a number of lncRNAs have been implicated in tumor behavior, but the landscape of dysregulated lncRNAs in EOC remains incompletely mapped, and the functional significance of many candidates has not been established.

The microRNA-200 family is of particular interest in this context because it is a master regulator of EMT, governing the expression of E-cadherin through targets such as ZEB1 and ZEB2. Equally, bridging integrator 1 (BIN1) is a well-described tumor suppressor whose loss is associated with progression in multiple malignancies. Whether an lncRNA-mediated ceRNA network connecting these elements operates in EOC has not been reported.

Here, by integrating public lncRNA expression datasets from EOC and matched normal tissues, we identified small nucleolar RNA host gene 10 (SNHG10) as the most markedly downregulated lncRNA among five candidates reproducibly reduced in EOC tissues. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas confirmed its downregulation, and clinical correlation showed that low SNHG10 expression was associated with shorter progression-free and overall survival, with SNHG10 expression and intraperitoneal metastasis emerging as significant predictors of prognosis. We went on to demonstrate that SNHG10 suppresses EOC tumorigenesis and EMT by sponging miR-200a-3p, thereby upregulating BIN1. Our findings establish SNHG10 as a tumor-suppressive lncRNA and a candidate prognostic biomarker in epithelial ovarian cancer.
