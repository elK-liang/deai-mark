In the present study we integrated public transcriptomic datasets (GSE135886 and GSE119054) to interrogate the landscape of dysregulated long non‑coding RNAs in epithelial ovarian cancer. By intersecting the two independent cohorts we identified 26 down‑regulated and 4 up‑regulated lncRNAs. Among the top down‑regulated candidates, five lncRNAs were common to both datasets, and quantitative real‑time PCR in our own patient cohort confirmed reduced expression of all five, with SNHG10 exhibiting the most pronounced decrease. Subsequent analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) ovarian cancer dataset reinforced this pattern, demonstrating significantly lower SNHG10 levels in tumor tissues relative to normal controls (P < 0.001). These observations substantiate SNHG10 as a frequently silenced lncRNA in EOC and provide a rationale for investigating its clinical relevance.  

To assess the prognostic value of SNHG10 down‑regulation, we employed X‑tile software to establish an optimal expression cutoff (0.550) that segregated patients into high‑ and low‑expression groups. Kaplan–Meier survival curves revealed that patients with low SNHG10 experienced shorter progression‑free survival (P = 0.001) and overall survival (P = 0.019). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further demonstrated that low SNHG10 expression independently predicts poorer progression‑free survival when adjusting for intraperitoneal metastasis, and independently predicts poorer overall survival together with lymph‑node metastasis and intraperitoneal metastasis. These findings indicate that SNHG10 correlates with tumor aggressiveness, retains independent prognostic relevance, and may serve as a biomarker for risk stratification in EOC.  

The frequent down‑regulation of SNHG10 and its association with adverse clinical outcomes are consistent with the notion that SNHG10 may act as a tumor‑suppressive lncRNA. In other tumor types, SNHG10 has been implicated in regulating oncogenic pathways, often by serving as a competing endogenous RNA that sequesters specific microRNAs. The title of this work proposes that SNHG10 could exert its effects by sponging miR‑200a‑3p and consequently up‑regulating BIN1; however, the present study did not provide direct experimental validation of this axis. Functional experiments are therefore required to confirm whether a similar microRNA‑sponging mechanism operates in EOC.  

Several strengths of our analysis merit recognition. By using two GEO datasets we minimized the influence of batch effects and identified a robust set of commonly dysregulated lncRNAs. The use of X‑tile for cutoff determination and subsequent Kaplan–Meier and Cox analyses provides a statistically rigorous framework for evaluating prognosis. Nonetheless, our investigation is limited by its reliance on retrospective, publicly available expression data, which may be subject to inter‑study variability in sample processing and clinical annotation. Moreover, the lack of functional experiments (e.g., knockdown/overexpression in cell lines, rescue assays, or in vivo modeling) prevents us from drawing mechanistic conclusions about how SNHG10 influences tumor biology. Additional prospective studies with larger, well‑characterized cohorts and integrated transcriptomic and proteomic data will be essential to validate our findings.  

In summary, our multi‑cohort analysis identifies SNHG10 as a markedly down‑regulated lncRNA in epithelial ovarian cancer and demonstrates that its low expression correlates with accelerated disease progression and reduced patient survival. These results support the notion that SNHG10 may act as a tumor‑suppressor in EOC and highlight its potential as an independent prognostic biomarker. Future work should focus on elucidating the downstream pathways regulated by SNHG10, validating its interaction with miR‑200a‑3p and BIN1, and exploring its therapeutic targeting potential. Such studies will be crucial for translating these observations into clinical applications for ovarian cancer management.
