Melanoma is one of the most aggressive forms of skin cancer, and its progression from localized disease to metastatic spread is driven by complex transcriptional rewiring. BORIS (Brother of the Regulator of Imprinted Sites), also known as CTCFL (CCCTC‑binding factor‑like), is a paralog of the well‑characterized insulator protein CTCF. While CTCF is widely expressed in normal somatic cells and is essential for genome organization, BORIS is normally restricted to germ‑line tissues, notably the testis, where it participates in epigenetic reprogramming. In recent years, aberrant BORIS expression has been documented in a variety of solid tumors, including breast, prostate, and colon cancers, where it has been implicated in the activation of oncogenic pathways and the suppression of differentiation programs. The observation that BORIS can act as a transcriptional activator or repressor depending on cellular context has raised the possibility that it may fulfill distinct functions at different stages of tumor evolution.

A growing body of evidence indicates that BORIS is also expressed in human melanoma, where it appears to be absent from normal skin. Comparative analyses of transcriptomic datasets reveal markedly higher BORIS mRNA levels in melanoma samples than in non‑malignant skin, and further elevation in metastatic lesions relative to primary tumors. These patterns are mirrored in cell‑line models: a panel of melanoma‑derived lines consistently shows BORIS protein and/or mRNA expression, whereas non‑malignant congenital nevi cells remain negative for BORIS. Such differential expression underscores a potential link between BORIS and the acquisition of properties characteristic of advanced melanoma, such as increased invasiveness and survival.

Despite these correlative findings, the functional consequences of BORIS up‑regulation in melanoma remain poorly defined. To begin dissecting its role, we generated a doxycycline‑inducible BORIS expression system in the MM057 melanoma cell line, which expresses low endogenous BORIS compared with other melanoma cells. Induction of BORIS under these conditions produced a striking reduction in cell numbers during routine culture, and this suggests that forced BORIS expression exerts a profound effect on cell viability or proliferation. Systematic titration of doxycycline concentration and exposure time revealed that short (3‑day) induction only diminishes proliferation at the highest inducer dose, whereas prolonged (5‑ and 7‑day) induction suppresses growth even at low doxycycline concentrations. Control cells transduced with empty vector are unaffected by doxycycline, and this confirms that the observed anti‑proliferative effect is attributable to BORIS rather than the inducer itself.

Guided by these initial observations, the present study was designed to elucidate the downstream mechanisms through which BORIS influences melanoma cell behavior. By carefully controlling BORIS expression levels (50–100 ng/ml doxycycline for five days), we aim to characterize its impact on key cellular processes (including proliferation, apoptosis, and invasive capacity) and thus clarify whether BORIS functions as a driver of melanoma progression or as a context‑dependent regulator that can impose a proliferative‑to‑invasive phenotypic switch. The results presented herein provide mechanistic insight into BORIS‑mediated regulation in melanoma and support BORIS as a potential therapeutic target for this deadly malignancy.
