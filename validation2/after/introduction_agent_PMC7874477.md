Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related death. Early-stage CRC is curable, and five-year survival for localized disease is high, but most patients are diagnosed after the tumor has advanced, when survival drops sharply. Although colonoscopy is the gold standard for detection, its invasiveness, cost, and limited acceptance restrict its use as a broad screening tool, and current noninvasive tests based on fecal occult blood or fecal DNA have suboptimal sensitivity, particularly for early lesions. Minimally invasive blood-based biomarkers that enable early, accurate, and repeatable detection of CRC therefore represent a major unmet clinical need.

Transfer RNA-derived small RNAs (tDRs), also termed tRNA fragments and tRNA halves, have recently emerged as a abundant and functionally significant class of small non-coding RNAs. Produced by specific cleavage of mature tRNAs under defined cellular conditions, tDRs are not degradation byproducts but active regulatory molecules that modulate translation, RNA stability, and gene expression. Their abundance is altered in cancer, and circulating tDRs are stably detectable in plasma, where they are protected from degradation by their small size and association with proteins or vesicles. These properties make tDRs attractive candidates for liquid biopsy biomarkers, yet the landscape of circulating tDRs in CRC has not been systematically profiled, and no tDR-based diagnostic marker has been established.

We previously showed that tDR profiles in plasma can be comprehensively quantified by small RNA high-throughput sequencing, and this provides a discovery platform for disease-associated tDRs. Applying this approach to CRC requires not only the identification of differentially abundant tDRs but also rigorous validation in independent clinical cohorts and functional characterization of the selected candidates, including the mechanisms governing their biogenesis.

In this study, we profiled the landscape of tDRs in the plasma of CRC patients and healthy controls by small RNA sequencing and identified 5′-tRF-GlyGCC, a 5′-tRNA-derived fragment, as the dominant tDR in CRC plasma, rising from 7.44 percent of all 5′-tRFs in healthy plasma to 52.24 percent in CRC plasma, while the most abundant 5′-tRF in healthy controls declined. We validated the diagnostic performance of 5′-tRF-GlyGCC by qRT-PCR in independent cohorts of CRC patients and controls, and investigated its biogenesis. This analysis demonstrated a role for the tRNA demethylating enzyme ALKBH3 in the generation of this fragment. Our findings establish 5′-tRF-GlyGCC as a novel plasma biomarker for CRC diagnosis.
