The tumor microenvironment is composed of a heterogeneous mixture of immune and stromal cell populations that together modulate disease progression, therapeutic response, and overall host physiology. In murine models (which serve as a primary experimental platform for cancer biology, immunology, and drug development), characterizing the composition of these infiltrating cells is essential for interpreting experimental outcomes and translating findings to human disease. Traditional methods such as immunohistochemistry or flow cytometry provide detailed cell‑type resolution but are limited by sample size, cost, and the need for specialized reagents. Consequently, computational approaches that infer celltype abundance from genome‑wide gene expression data have become attractive because they can be applied to existing transcriptomic datasets rapidly and at scale.

Several deconvolution methods have been proposed to estimate cell proportions from bulk RNA expression, yet they often rely on pre‑defined gene signatures that may incompletely represent closely related subpopulations or lack rigorous criteria for marker selection. Moreover, without appropriate negative controls (tissues or cell lines that do not contain the target cell types), signature specificity can be compromised, leading to inflated abundance estimates. These limitations call for a transparent, systematic workflow that defines markers based on quantitative performance metrics and incorporates a comprehensive framework of cell‑type relationships.

To meet this need, we developed the murine Microenvironment Cell Population counter (mMCP‑counter). This method builds on a detailed hierarchical model of hematopoietic and stromal lineages, initially derived from the ImmGen consortium’s reference atlas and further expanded through an extensive literature survey. Using a curated collection of transcriptomic profiles from sorted cell populations spanning the major immune and stromal compartments (supplemented with negative‑control samples from epithelial, carcinoma, and other non‑immune cell lines), we applied three stringent criteria to identify robust transcriptomic markers for each cell category: a log2 fold‑change (FC) greater than 2.1, a specific fold‑change (sFC) greater than 2.1, and an area under the ROC curve (AUC) exceeding 0.97. This systematic screen yielded a set of markers for 55 distinct cell populations, and this set ensures high specificity (zero or near‑zero expression in unrelated cell types) and uniform coverage across sublineages.

The resulting mMCP‑counter enables direct estimation of the relative abundances of immune and stromal cell types from bulk RNA‑seq or microarray data of murine tissues. As a demonstration, the probe for Tpsb2 satisfied all three quantitative thresholds and exhibited a log2 fold‑change of 6.48 in mast cells compared with all non‑mast‑cell samples, and this underscores the method’s ability to capture lineage‑specific markers with high confidence. By providing a transparent, reproducible workflow and a curated marker repository, mMCP‑counter offers a valuable resource for the research community to dissect the cellular landscape of the murine tumor microenvironment and to integrate cell‑type resolution into downstream translational analyses.
