The introduction of a weaker electron‑withdrawing quinoxaline (Qx) core in the A–DA′D–A framework provides a systematic way to fine‑tune the electronic and photophysical properties of non‑fullerene acceptors. By replacing the benzothiadiazole (BTZ) unit that underpins the benchmark acceptor Y6, the Qx‑based acceptors Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 exhibit a reduced intramolecular charge‑transfer (ICT) strength, which translates into a modest increase in optical bandgap. This modest bandgap widening is accompanied by an overall upward shift of both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO). The consequent elevation of the LUMO level is particularly relevant for photovoltaic operation, because a higher LUMO reduces the energetic penalty required for electron transfer from the donor and therefore contributes to a larger open‑circuit voltage (VOC) in the resulting devices.

One of the principal sources of voltage loss in organic solar cells is the energetic offset needed to drive exciton dissociation at the donor–acceptor interface. The data show that the HOMO and LUMO offsets between the donor material and the Qx‑based acceptors are substantially smaller than those for Y6. This narrowed offset indicates that the driving force for both hole and electron transfer is reduced, which in turn diminishes the voltage loss associated with exciton dissociation. In parallel, the upward shift of the acceptor LUMO directly raises the theoretical limit for VOC and can thus lower the overall energy losses in the photovoltaic conversion process.

The optical characterization further corroborates the potential of Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 to minimize non‑radiative voltage losses. Although the absorption profiles of Qx‑1 and Qx‑2 in dilute solution closely resemble each other, a clear red‑shift is observed when the molecules are cast into thin films. This indicates the formation of effective π–π stacking and delocalized excited‑state species. The Stokes’ shift decreases progressively from Y6 to Qx‑1 and Qx‑2, and this indicates smaller geometry relaxation upon excitation. Smaller excited‑state relaxations are synonymous with reduced reorganization energy, which is known to suppress non‑radiative recombination pathways and thus contribute to lower voltage deficits.

In addition to the electronic and photophysical advantages, the solubility profiles of the new acceptors are favorable for device fabrication. Qx‑1 exhibits the highest solubility in chloroform among the three acceptors (36.2 mg mL⁻¹), while Qx‑2, though less soluble (13.9 mg mL⁻¹), still surpasses the benchmark Y6 (28.2 mg mL⁻¹) under the same conditions. The ability to dissolve readily in common processing solvents facilitates the formation of uniform blend films, which is essential for achieving optimal bulk‑heterojunction morphology and for scaling up fabrication protocols. The good solubility of Qx‑1, in particular, suggests that high‑loading blends can be prepared without excessive solvent engineering. This simplifies the manufacturing pipeline.

Collectively, the upward repositioning of frontier orbital levels, the reduced driving forces for charge separation, and the diminished Stokes’ shifts point toward a design principle that simultaneously curtails radiative and non‑radiative voltage losses. These attributes are expected to translate into higher VOC and improved power conversion efficiency, although the ultimate device performance will also depend on complementary factors such as donor selection, blend morphology, and charge transport networks. The present study provides a clear mechanistic rationale for why Qx‑based acceptors can achieve low energy losses and points to further optimization through donor engineering, processing conditions, and morphological control.

Looking ahead, the Qx core is amenable to additional structural refinement. Systematic variations of peripheral substituents, side‑chain engineering, and fine‑tuning of the electron‑deficient units can be employed to precisely dial in the energy levels and intermolecular packing while preserving the beneficial reduction in Stokes’ shift. Moreover, combining Qx‑based acceptors with high‑mobility donors that possess complementary absorption profiles may further narrow the overall loss spectrum. Future work should also explore the impact of these acceptors on long‑term stability and scalability, as these aspects are critical for the practical deployment of low‑loss organic solar cells in commercial settings.
