Early tumor shrinkage (ETS) has been associated with improved survival outcomes in metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC); however, its impact on patient-reported quality of life (QoL) during first-line treatment remains less well defined. The prospective QUACK study evaluated first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy in patients with unresectable mCRC, and this provided an opportunity to examine the relationship between ETS and QoL. Methods: Of 140 patients enrolled in QUACK, 128 with measurable disease and available tumor response and QoL data were included in this analysis. ETS was defined as a ≥20% reduction in the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions at the first radiological evaluation (approximately 8 weeks). QoL was assessed using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire at baseline and at the time of response assessment. We compared clinicopathological characteristics, objective response rate (ORR), and rates of metastases resection between patients with and without ETS. Results: ETS was achieved in 82 (64.1%) of 128 patients, consistent with previously reported ETS rates of 61.5–80%. Baseline characteristics were generally well balanced between the ETS and non-ETS groups, except for tumor differentiation, which favored well/moderately differentiated tumors in the ETS group (97.6% vs. 89.1%; P = .044). Patients achieving ETS had a significantly higher ORR than those without ETS (76.8% vs. 21.7%; P < .001) and a significantly higher rate of surgical resection of metastases (28.6% vs. 11.4%; P = .031). At baseline, 51 patients (39.8%) were symptomatic, reporting "very much" or "quite a bit" for at least one of the eight EORTC QLQ-C30 symptom items. Baseline global health status/QoL and functional subscale scores were comparable between the ETS and non-ETS groups. Conclusions: ETS is achievable in a substantial proportion of patients with unresectable mCRC receiving first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy and is associated with higher ORR and increased likelihood of metastasectomy.
