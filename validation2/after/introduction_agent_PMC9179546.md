Lower-grade gliomas (LGG), comprising WHO grade 2 and 3 diffuse gliomas, form a heterogeneous group of brain tumors whose classification has been fundamentally reshaped by molecular diagnostics. The 2021 WHO classification of central nervous system tumors incorporates isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutation status and 1p/19q codeletion as defining parameters: IDH-wildtype tumors are segregated from IDH-mutant astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas, and combined alterations of chromosome 7 and 10 identify adult-type glioblastomas even among histologically lower-grade lesions. This molecular framework captures prognostic differences far better than histology alone, but clinical variability persists within each genetic group, and complementary, genome-wide data layers are needed to understand tumor behavior.

Multi-omics integration addresses this need by jointly examining the genome, transcriptome, and DNA methylome of each tumor. Copy number alterations, gene expression programs, and methylation patterns each carry independent prognostic and mechanistic information, and their coordinated analysis can reveal regulatory modes that single-platform studies miss. In particular, genome-wide DNA methylation has emerged as a robust classifier of CNS tumors and a marker of biological programs such as the CpG island methylator phenotype (G-CIMP), which characterizes the hypermethylation of IDH-mutant gliomas, and of receptor-mediated signaling programs. Unsupervised machine learning approaches, such as self-organizing maps, provide a framework for integrating these diverse data types into coherent, interpretable maps.

We have previously classified LGG into expression subtypes E1-E8 and methylation subtypes M1-M6 by analyzing transcriptomic and methylation domains independently. How these molecular subtypes distribute across the genetically defined WHO groups, and how methylation programs, copy number alterations, and expression states converge to define clinically relevant entities within lower-grade glioma, remain to be systematically mapped.

In this study, we present integrated multi-omics maps of lower-grade gliomas built on the WHO 2021 genetic stratification. We classified tumors into IDH-wildtype, IDH-mutant oligodendroglioma with 1p/19q codeletion, and IDH-mutant astrocytoma groups, characterized the distribution of chromosome 7 gains and chromosome 10 losses across these groups, ranked tumors by G-CIMP signature and linked methylation patterns, including G-protein receptor methylation, to group membership and prognosis, and mapped the previously defined expression (E1-E8) and methylation (M1-M6) subtypes onto the genetic groups. These integrated maps delineate the modes of genomic regulation operative in lower-grade gliomas and provide a framework for their molecular diagnosis and risk stratification.
