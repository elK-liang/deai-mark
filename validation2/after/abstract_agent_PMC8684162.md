Tumor-associated macrophages of the M2 phenotype have been implicated in tumor progression, and we examined their role in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). M2 macrophage infiltration was assessed by immunohistochemical staining of the M2 macrophage marker CD206 in 55 ccRCC samples. The degree of M2 macrophage infiltration was positively correlated with the pathological grade and T stage of ccRCC patients. This indicates a crucial role for M2 macrophages in ccRCC tumorigenesis and development. To investigate their function, THP-1 cells were first converted into M0 macrophages using PMA and then co-cultured with the ccRCC cell lines Caki-1 and ACHN to generate M2 macrophages, as confirmed by substantially increased CD206-positive rates detected by flow cytometry. Both M2 macrophage-conditioned medium and co-culture enhanced the proliferation, migration and invasion of Caki-1 and ACHN cells. Consistent with this pro-metastatic behavior, co-culture with M2 macrophages resulted in a substantial reduction in E-cadherin expression in Caki-1 and ACHN cells, indicative of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). Mechanistically, M2 macrophages secrete CXCL13 to promote ccRCC migration, invasion and EMT. These results demonstrate that M2 macrophage infiltration is associated with aggressive clinicopathological features of ccRCC and that M2 macrophage-derived CXCL13 promotes malignant behavior of ccRCC cells, and this identifies the M2 macrophage-CXCL13 axis as a potential therapeutic target in ccRCC.
