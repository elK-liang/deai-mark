Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) remains the most lethal malignancy among gynecologic tumors, largely owing to late‑stage diagnosis and the limited efficacy of current therapeutic strategies. Despite advances in surgical techniques and platinum‑based chemotherapy, the overall 5‑year survival rate has improved only marginally over the past decades, and novel biomarkers and mechanistic drivers of disease progression urgently need to be identified. In recent years, non‑coding RNAs, especially long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs), have emerged as pivotal regulators of tumor biology, and this offers potential avenues for diagnostic and therapeutic intervention.

Publicly available transcriptomic datasets have revealed widespread dysregulation of lncRNAs in EOC, yet the functional significance of many of these molecules remains unexplored. Re‑analysis of the Gene Expression Omnibus series GSE135886 and GSE119054 identified a set of lncRNAs that are significantly altered in tumor versus normal ovarian tissue. Among the most prominently downregulated candidates, SNHG10 (Small Nucleolar RNA Host Gene 10) was consistently reduced across both datasets, a pattern that was further corroborated by data from The Cancer Genome Atlas. Although SNHG10 has been reported to exert tumor‑suppressive roles in several other malignancies, its specific expression pattern, clinical relevance, and mechanistic role in EOC have not been elucidated.

A growing body of evidence indicates that microRNAs can function as master regulators of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastatic dissemination. The miR‑200 family, including miR‑200a‑3p, is known to modulate EMT by targeting transcriptional repressors such as ZEB1/2, and its dysregulation has been linked to aggressive cancer phenotypes. Moreover, BIN1, a nucleocytoplasmic adaptor protein with well‑documented tumor‑suppressive activity, is frequently downregulated in cancers and can inhibit cell proliferation and invasion. These observations raise the hypothesis that SNHG10 may act as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) for miR‑200a‑3p and thus relieve the repressive pressure on BIN1 and consequently suppress tumorigenesis and EMT in EOC.

In the present study, we first validate the downregulation of SNHG10 in EOC clinical specimens and evaluate its association with patient prognosis. Subsequently, we investigate the mechanistic framework by which SNHG10, through sponging miR‑200a‑3p, upregulates BIN1 expression and thereby restrains malignant transformation and EMT. Together, these efforts aim to establish SNHG10 as a potential independent prognostic biomarker and a functional regulator of the miR‑200a‑3p/BIN1 axis in epithelial ovarian cancer.
