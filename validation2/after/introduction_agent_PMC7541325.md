The tumor microenvironment, comprising infiltrating immune cells and stromal cells embedded in a remodelled extracellular matrix, is now firmly established as a determinant of tumor progression, treatment response, and patient survival. In human oncology, computational tools that deconvolve the composition of the microenvironment from bulk transcriptomic data, such as MCP-counter, have made it possible to quantify the abundance of specific immune and stromal cell populations in large cohorts of tumor samples without resorting to cell sorting or histology. These tools have become standard instruments for microenvironment research and biomarker development.

Mouse models remain indispensable for functional studies of the microenvironment, from genetic and pharmacological perturbations to the preclinical evaluation of immunotherapies. Yet the analytical tools available for murine samples lag behind those developed for human data. Cell sorting and flow cytometry are labor-intensive, limited by antibody panels and tissue availability, and unsuited to high-throughput screening. Naive application of human deconvolution tools to mouse data is compromised by differences between human and mouse gene expression patterns and marker specificity. A murine-specific method, analogous to MCP-counter, that estimates the abundance of tissue-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations from bulk gene expression would substantially extend the analytical reach of the extensive body of murine transcriptomic data.

Building such a method requires two ingredients: a comprehensive prior representation of cell populations and their developmental relationships, and reference transcriptomic profiles of highly purified cell populations. The Immunological Genome Project (ImmGen) provides a systematic survey of mouse immune cell transcriptomes that, complemented with stromal and non-hematopoietic reference datasets, covers most populations of immunological and stromal interest. The methodological challenge lies in defining transcriptomic markers whose expression is reliably high in a population, including all of its subpopulations, and undetectable in all others, so that their aggregate signal provides a robust proxy for abundance.

Here, we present mMCP-counter, the murine counterpart of MCP-counter, which estimates the abundance of ten immune and stromal cell populations in murine bulk expression data. Using the ImmGen hierarchy extended with literature curation and training profiles from sorted cell populations supplemented with negative-control datasets, we identified specific transcriptomic markers for each of 55 candidate populations under stringent criteria, and distilled from them a robust deconvolution method. We validate mMCP-counter in flow cytometry-matched samples and demonstrate its utility for quantifying microenvironment composition in murine tumor models.
