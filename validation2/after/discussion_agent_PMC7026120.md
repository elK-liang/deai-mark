BORIS, also known as CTCFL, is a paralog of the chromatin insulator protein CTCF that is normally restricted to germ cells but is aberrantly reactivated in a wide range of cancers as a cancer-testis antigen. In this study, we examined the functional consequences of BORIS expression in melanoma and found that forced BORIS expression promotes a switch from a proliferative towards an invasive phenotype. Our expression analyses confirmed that BORIS is expressed in human melanoma tumors and cell lines but not in normal skin or non-malignant congenital nevi cell lines, that its expression is significantly higher in metastatic than in primary melanoma samples, and that it appears in melanoma cell lines while being absent from nevus counterparts. These observations support a role for BORIS in melanoma development and metastatic progression.

The experimental system we developed allows BORIS function to be studied without the confounding effects of clonal selection. Because constitutive BORIS expression reduced cell number so strikingly, we established a doxycycline-inducible model in the MM057 melanoma cell line, which expresses low endogenous BORIS, and optimized the induction conditions, using 50-100 ng/ml doxycycline for five days, so that BORIS effects could be examined at controlled expression levels. The temporal resolution of this system proved informative: three days of induction did not significantly reduce proliferation except at the highest doxycycline concentration, whereas five and seven days of expression produced significant proliferation reduction even at low inducer concentrations, with empty-vector controls unaffected. This progressive, time-dependent effect indicates that the cellular response to BORIS is a phenotypic reprogramming that unfolds over several days of expression rather than an acute toxicity, consistent with an alteration of transcriptional programs rather than a nonspecific stress response.

The phenotype switch we observe, from proliferation towards invasion, carries specific implications for how BORIS participates in melanoma progression. In tumor evolution, a reduced proliferative rate accompanied by enhanced invasiveness characterizes the transition towards a migratory, disseminating state, and the association of BORIS with metastatic rather than primary disease in patient samples aligns with the functional phenotype we induce experimentally. Mechanistically, this switch is plausibly mediated through BORIS's capacity to bind CTCF sites and reorganize chromatin architecture: as a CTCF paralog, BORIS can compete for insulator elements, alter higher-order chromatin domains, and thus rewire transcriptional programs governing adhesion, motility, and cell cycle genes. The cancer-testis pattern of expression, with immunogenic properties that have made BORIS a vaccine target, gains an additional dimension from these findings, because a protein that is both an immunotherapeutic antigen and a functional driver of the invasive state offers a rationale for therapies that combine immune targeting of BORIS with the removal of its pro-invasive activity.

The inverse relationship between BORIS expression and proliferation also refines the interpretation of its clinical associations. If BORIS-expressing cells grow more slowly in vitro, the enrichment of BORIS in metastatic tumors implies that its advantages operate in vivo, in the context of invasion, dissemination, or survival under selective pressures that in vitro proliferation assays do not capture. This is consistent with the broader observation that cancer-testis antigens frequently mark cell states associated with plasticity and treatment resistance rather than rapid proliferation, and it cautions against interpreting the expression of such antigens purely as markers of aggressiveness without functional context.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Our functional analyses relied primarily on a single inducible model in MM057, chosen for its low endogenous BORIS, and the generality of the phenotype switch across melanoma lines with different genetic backgrounds requires confirmation in additional models, including lines with higher endogenous BORIS in which knockdown rather than induction would be the appropriate perturbation. The molecular mediators of the proliferation-to-invasion switch, including the specific chromatin targets of BORIS and the downstream gene programs altered, were not fully resolved, and distinguishing direct CTCF-site competition from indirect effects will require chromatin occupancy mapping in the inducible system. The in vivo relevance of the induced phenotype, in migration and metastasis assays, remains to be demonstrated. Finally, the relationship between the levels of BORIS expression achieved by induction and those present in BORIS-positive tumors should be interpreted with care. With these caveats, our study establishes BORIS as a chromatin-remodeling cancer-testis antigen whose expression in melanoma cells promotes a shift from proliferation towards invasiveness, and this connects the immunogenic reactivation of this germ-cell gene to the metastatic behavior of the tumor.
