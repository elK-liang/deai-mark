Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common primary liver malignancies and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide. Early detection is critical because curative therapies are most effective in the localized stage, yet the disease is frequently diagnosed at an advanced phase when treatment options are limited. Surveillance strategies have been well established for patients with cirrhosis or other high‑risk conditions, but a substantial proportion of HCC occurs in individuals without underlying cirrhosis—so‑called low‑risk populations. In these patients, the performance of the current non‑invasive imaging criteria, particularly the Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI‑RADS), is less robust, and the positive predictive value of imaging alone is often insufficient for definitive diagnosis. Consequently, there is a pressing need for complementary non‑invasive tools that can improve diagnostic accuracy in low‑risk settings.

To address this gap, we conducted a multi‑institutional retrospective study involving 681 patients from two academic centers. The study population was divided into a training cohort (n = 324), an internal validation cohort (n = 139), and an external validation cohort (n = 218). Across the cohorts, median age ranged from 53 to 60 years, and males predominated (58–68 %). Histologically confirmed HCC accounted for 35 % of the training cohort, 45 % of the internal validation cohort, and 48 % of the external validation cohort. The remaining cases comprised a spectrum of non‑HCC hepatic lesions, including intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, mixed HCC‑cholangiocarcinoma, and sarcoma among malignancies, and focal nodular hyperplasia, inflammatory pseudotumor, angiomyolipoma, and liver abscess among benign entities. Notably, more than 80 % of all nodules exceeded 3 cm in size. While many demographic and laboratory variables—such as age, family history, smoking, alcohol use, cardiovascular disease, serologic markers (HBcAb, HBeAb), and liver enzymes (AST, ALP, GGT) as well as tumor markers (CA125, CA19‑9)—differed significantly across the three cohorts, the overall distribution of tumor size and AFP positivity remained comparable, underscoring the heterogeneity inherent in low‑risk populations.

Current diagnostic paradigms for HCC rely heavily on characteristic imaging features in the setting of cirrhosis, but these criteria are less specific when applied to patients without underlying liver disease. Moreover, the overlap of imaging appearances among HCC, benign lesions, and other primary liver cancers can lead to misinterpretation and unnecessary invasive procedures. Biomarker‑based approaches have shown promise but often lack the sensitivity or specificity required for solitary nodules in low‑risk individuals. Therefore, an integrated diagnostic strategy that combines readily available clinical data, serum biomarkers, and imaging descriptors may offer a more accurate, non‑invasive means of identifying HCC in this setting.

The primary aim of this study was to develop and validate a comprehensive non‑invasive diagnostic model for HCC in a low‑risk population, leveraging the diverse clinical, laboratory, and imaging variables that were significantly different between cohorts. We hypothesized that a multivariable prediction tool incorporating demographic factors, hepatitis serology, liver function tests, selected tumor markers, and LI‑RADS categories could achieve high diagnostic performance and be generalizable across independent patient populations. By testing the model in both internal and external validation cohorts, we sought to provide a robust framework that could reduce reliance on biopsy and improve early detection of HCC among patients without cirrhosis.
