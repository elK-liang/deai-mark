Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most common malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality. Early detection is critical for improving patient outcomes, yet current screening strategies—colonoscopy, fecal occult blood testing, and stool‑based DNA assays—are limited by invasiveness, compliance issues, or modest sensitivity for early‑stage disease. Consequently, there is a pressing need for minimally invasive, highly specific biomarkers that can facilitate the early diagnosis of CRC.

tRNA‑derived small RNAs (tDRs), including 5′‑tRNA fragments (5′‑tRFs) and 5′‑halves, have emerged as a novel class of short non‑coding RNAs that are abundant in various biological fluids. These molecules are generated through the precise cleavage of precursor or mature tRNAs and are remarkably stable in plasma, partly due to their association with Argonaute proteins or extracellular vesicles. Emerging evidence suggests that tDRs are not merely cellular debris but actively participate in gene regulation and are dysregulated in numerous pathologies, including cancer. Moreover, the biogenesis of certain tDRs has been linked to RNA‑modifying enzymes such as ALKBH3, highlighting a potential regulatory mechanism that could influence their abundance in circulation.

To explore the utility of plasma tDRs as biomarkers for CRC, we performed small‑RNA high‑throughput sequencing on plasma samples from three CRC patients and three healthy controls. Our analysis revealed a distinct repertoire of tDRs in CRC plasma compared with healthy plasma, with 5′‑tRFs comprising the most abundant class in both groups. Notably, hierarchical clustering and quantitative assessment demonstrated a marked shift in the composition of 5′‑tRFs, where the relative proportion of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC increased dramatically from 7.44 % of all 5′‑tRFs in healthy controls to 52.24 % in CRC patients. In contrast, other 5′‑tRFs such as 5′‑tRF‑HisGTG and 5′‑tRF‑AlaTGC were substantially reduced in CRC plasma. Subsequent qRT‑PCR validation of selected tDRs confirmed the sequencing findings, underscoring the reproducibility of the observed changes.

Based on these observations, the present study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic potential of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC in a larger cohort and to assess its performance as a non‑invasive biomarker for colorectal cancer. By integrating comprehensive plasma profiling with targeted quantitative validation, we sought to determine whether 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC can serve as a reliable, early‑stage indicator of CRC and ultimately contribute to improved clinical detection strategies.
