Abstract. 
Necroptosis has been implicated in tumorigenesis. This study aimed to comprehensively investigate the genetic landscape, expression patterns, and prognostic value of necroptosis-related genes (NRGs) in colorectal cancer (CRC).
We analyzed 33 NRGs in CRC using data from 1581 patients integrated across five cohorts (TCGA-COAD/READ, GSE14333, GSE33113, GSE37892, and GSE39582). Somatic mutations, copy number variations (CNVs), and expression differences between tumor and normal samples were evaluated. Univariate Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier analyses were utilized to identify prognostic NRGs, followed by a consensus clustering algorithm to stratify CRC tumor samples.
Gene ontology enrichment confirmed that the 33 NRGs are characterized by necroptosis. In the TCGA cohort, 111 of 502 CRC samples exhibited NRG somatic mutations, with CASP8 showing the highest mutation rate (4%), while FADD, TRADD, TNF, and AURKA presented no mutations. Prevalent CNV alterations were observed, with amplified genes (MYC, FADD, AURKA, TRAF2, PGAM5) significantly enriched and deleted genes (TLR3, CHUK, RIPK1, FAS, NFKB1, AXL) markedly decreased in tumor samples compared to normal samples. Principal component analysis demonstrated that NRGs could effectively distinguish CRC from normal samples. Further network and survival analyses identified seven NRGs (TLR3, TLR4, BIRC2, TRAF2, CASP8, NFKB1, and TNFRSF10B) as prognostic genes, which were subsequently used to stratify CRC samples into distinct molecular subtypes via consensus clustering.
The genetic landscape and expression levels of NRGs differ significantly between CRC and normal samples, indicating that necroptosis plays a crucial role in regulating CRC tumorigenesis. The identification of prognostic NRGs and the necroptosis-based molecular classification provide valuable insights for prognosis prediction and tumor microenvironment characterization in CRC.
