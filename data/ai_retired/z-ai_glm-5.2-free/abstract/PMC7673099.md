Organotypic heart slices serve as a valuable ex vivo model for studying cardiac physiology and gene transfer. This study aimed to establish organotypic heart slices as a model system for studying adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated gene transfer into cardiac cells.

Myocardial slices of left ventricular tissue from transgenic homozygous αMHC-mCherry mice were prepared using a vibrating microtome and cultured in vitro under air-liquid interface conditions. Slice thicknesses ranging from 100–300 µm were evaluated using mCherry signal and MTS metabolic assays. Cell viability was further assessed using the calcein-AM assay. To mimic acute myocardial infarction, heart slices were cultured under hypoxic conditions.

αMHC-driven mCherry expression was confirmed to be co-expressed with the cardiomyocyte marker cTNT. Slices with a thickness of 300 µm demonstrated the strongest mCherry signal and highest metabolic activity, and were therefore chosen for further study due to superior cellular content and handling ease. Under normoxic conditions, resident cells maintained viability for at least 5 days; cardiomyocyte survival decreased by approximately 25% after 1 day but remained stable for the subsequent 4 days, indicating initial sensitivity to the in vivo to in vitro transition followed by adaptation. Under hypoxia, slice viability was significantly reduced, leading to extensive cell death.

Our data indicate that 300-µm-thick organotypic myocardial slices can be maintained in culture for at least 5 days without apparent loss of viability or changes in tissue morphology. This establishes a reliable ex vivo platform for studying AAV-mediated gene transfer and modeling ischemic cardiac injury.
