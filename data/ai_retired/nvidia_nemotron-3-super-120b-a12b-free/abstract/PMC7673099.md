Abstract
To establish organotypic heart slices as a model system for studying adeno‑associated virus (AAV)‑mediated gene transfer in cardiac tissue, myocardial slices were prepared from left‑ventricular tissue of transgenic homozygous αMHC‑mCherry mice using a vibrating microtome and cultured at the air‑liquid interface. Immunostaining for cardiac troponin T (cTNT) confirmed that mCherry expression co‑localized with cardiomyocytes, validating the reporter as a cardiomyocyte‑specific marker. Systematic testing of slice thicknesses (100–300 µm) showed that 300‑µm‑thick slices exhibited the strongest mCherry signal and highest metabolic activity (MTS assay), correlating with greater cardiomyocyte content and easier handling; thicker slices were not examined due to reported oxygenation limitations. Viability assessed by calcein‑AM staining revealed that resident cells remained viable for at least 5 days under normoxic conditions. Cardiomyocyte survival decreased by ~25 % after the first day in culture but remained stable thereafter, indicating an initial adaptation to the ex vivo environment followed by a steady state. Overall, myocardial slices retained viability and morphology for ≥5 days in normoxia. To model acute myocardial infarction, slices were exposed to hypoxic conditions, which markedly reduced viability and led to extensive cell death. These findings demonstrate that 300‑µm‑thick organotypic heart slices provide a viable, reproducible platform for subsequent investigations of AAV‑mediated gene transfer in cardiac cells.
