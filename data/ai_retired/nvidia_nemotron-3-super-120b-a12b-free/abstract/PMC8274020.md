Abstract.
In the immunotherapy era, frail patients with recurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) often receive less intensive chemotherapy. This study compared weekly paclitaxel‑carboplatin (PCC) chemotherapy with the standard platinum‑based regimen plus cetuximab (EXTREME) as first‑line treatment in such patients.  
A retrospective analysis of 124 patients with locally incurable R/M HNSCC treated between [dates not provided] was performed. 103 patients received the EXTREME regimen (platinum‑based chemotherapy + cetuximab) and 21 patients received weekly PCC. Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, smoking status and prior alcohol abuse, were balanced between groups except for performance status (EXTREME: ECOG PS 0‑1; PCC: all PS 2). Progression‑free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), objective response rate and toxicity were assessed.  
Median PFS was 4 months (range 1–20) in the overall cohort; 4 months in the EXTREME group and 5 months in the PCC group (p = 0.275). Median OS was 12 months in both groups (p = 0.400). Objective response rates were 35 % (36/103) in the EXTREME group and 34 % (7/21) in the PCC group; no complete responses were observed. Toxicity in the PCC group consisted mainly of grade 1 anemia (52 %), grade 2 neutropenia (40 %) and grade 1 gastrointestinal toxicity (49 %); no grade 3‑4 events were reported. Univariate analyses showed no clinical feature, including treatment regimen, significantly associated with PFS or OS.  
Weekly PCC chemotherapy provides PFS and OS comparable to the standard EXTREME regimen in frail, PS 2 HNSCC patients, with a favorable toxicity profile. This regimen may serve as a viable first‑line alternative when standard chemo‑immunotherapy is not feasible.
