Dysregulated long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) contribute to esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) pathogenesis, yet their specific roles remain incompletely defined. To identify oncogenic lncRNAs in ESCC, we analyzed two independent lncRNA + mRNA microarray datasets comprising 119 and 60 tumor‑adjacent normal pairs, revealing four consistently overexpressed transcripts; functional screening with siRNA pools showed that knockdown of DLEU1 most strongly suppressed ESCC cell proliferation. DLEU1 expression was markedly elevated in tumors, being higher in 118 (99.2%) and 59 (98.3%) cancers relative to matched normal tissues, and its high level correlated with reduced overall survival, lymph node metastasis, and advanced clinical stage in patient cohorts, findings validated by TCGA data and RNAscope immunohistochemistry. Gene set enrichment analysis of high‑DLEU1 ESCC samples demonstrated enrichment of signatures linked to breast cancer stem cells, metastatic prostate cancer, liver cancer with poor survival, and genes governing mitochondrial membrane permeability and apoptosis, suggesting a role for DLEU1 in modulating cell death pathways. Mechanistically, DLEU1 interferes with RNF114‑mediated ubiquitination, thereby stabilizing the dynein light chain protein DYNLL1 and preventing its proteasomal degradation, which in turn maintains BCL2‑dependent anti‑apoptotic signaling and promotes cell survival. Collectively, these data establish DLEU1 as an oncogenic lncRNA that enhances ESCC cell viability by inhibiting DYNLL1 turnover, implicating the DLEU1‑RNF114‑DYNLL1‑BCL2 axis as a potential therapeutic target in ESCC.
