The present study investigated the molecular mechanisms underlying thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) formation and progression by integrating differential expression analysis with weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA). Using a Log₂ fold‑change cutoff of |0.5|, 171 genes were found upregulated and 85 genes downregulated in TAA tissue compared with normal aortic controls (Table 1, Fig. 2a‑b). Functional enrichment of these differentially expressed genes revealed significant over‑representation of immune‑related Gene Ontology terms, including regulation of immune effector processes, cell–cell adhesion, immune cell proliferation, and immune receptor activity (Fig. 2c). Correspondingly, KEGG pathway enrichment highlighted Th1, Th2, and Th17 cell differentiation, natural killer cell‑mediated cytotoxicity, and B‑cell receptor signaling as potentially perturbed in TAA (Fig. 2d), while also suggesting possible associations with bacterial or viral stimuli such as Staphylococcus aureus, Mycobacterium tuberculosis, influenza A virus, and Epstein‑Barr virus. WGCNA further identified gene modules enriched for chemotaxis and complement components (e.g., CX3CR1, C3, C3AR1, C1QC, HLA‑DPA1/HLA‑DRA, CD74, FCGR3A), whose hub genes were among the top differentially expressed genes listed in Table 1. Collectively, these results implicate heightened immune effector activity, chemotactic signaling, and complement activation—potentially driven by microbial triggers—as key contributors to the pathogenesis of thoracic aortic aneurysms.
