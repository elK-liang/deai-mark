Physical signs such as tendon xanthomas and corneal arcus are clinical markers of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). Methods: Among 466 HeFH patients, 55 (11.8%) exhibited tendon xanthomas or corneal arcus (TX/CA). Propensity‑score matching produced a 1:4 matched cohort of 250 subjects (50 TX/CA, 200 without) and baseline demographics, lipid profiles, genetic characteristics, and atherosclerotic outcomes were compared between groups. Results: The matched groups were similar in age (47.9 ± 13.0 vs 47.7 ± 12.5 years, p = 0.895), sex (66.7% vs 61.1% male, p = 0.492), body‑mass index, hypertension, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, smoking, and family history of coronary artery disease (all p > 0.05). Lipid analysis revealed higher triglyceride levels in non‑TX/CA participants (1.60 vs 1.26 mmol/L, p = 0.001), higher uncontrolled LDL‑C in TX/CA (7.90 ± 2.28 vs 7.70 ± 2.18 mmol/L, p = 0.025), and lower HDL‑C and apo‑A1 in TX/CA (HDL‑C 1.00 ± 0.34 vs 1.14 ± 0.37 mmol/L, p = 0.021; apo‑A1 1.11 ± 0.37 vs 1.30 ± 0.31 g/L, p = 0.001). Genetic profiling showed a markedly higher proportion of definite FH (DLCN > 8) in TX/CA (94.0% vs 48.5%, p < 0.001), mutation positivity (72.0% vs 38.0%, p = 0.001), and LDLR mutations (66.0% vs 32.0%, p < 0.001), including single‑allele carriers (42.0% vs 23.0%, p = 0.007) and double/triple‑allele carriers (24.0% vs 9.0%, p = 0.004). No significant differences were found in prevalent coronary artery disease (78.0% vs 71.1%, p = 0.132) or number of diseased vessels. Conclusions: In patients with HeFH, the presence of tendon xanthomas or corneal arcus identifies a subgroup with a substantially greater genetic burden of LDL‑receptor mutations but a comparable profile of traditional cardiovascular risk factors and atherosclerotic disease burden to those lacking these physical signs.
