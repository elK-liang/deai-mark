Abstract
In this study we explored the role of necroptosis‑related genes (NRGs) in colorectal cancer (CRC) by integrating genomic, transcriptomic and clinical data from multiple cohorts. We first curated a set of 33 NRGs (Additional file 11: Table S2) and confirmed their enrichment in cell‑death processes, notably necroptosis, via Gene Ontality enrichment analysis. Using the TCGA CRC cohort (n = 502), we found that 111 samples harbored genetic alterations in NRGs, with CASP8 exhibiting the highest mutation frequency (4 %), while FADD, TRADD, TNF and AURKA showed no mutations. Copy‑number variation analysis revealed frequent amplifications of MYC, FADD, AURKA, TRAF2 and PGAM5 and deletions of TLR3, CHUK, RIPK1, FAS, NFKB1 and AXL; expression profiling demonstrated that amplified NRGs were significantly up‑regulated in tumor versus normal tissue, whereas deleted NRGs were down‑regulated, and principal component analysis confirmed that NRG expression discriminates tumor from normal samples. To extend these findings, we combined data from five independent CRC cohorts (TCGA‑COAD/READ, GSE14333, GSE33113, GSE37892 and GSE39582), encompassing 1,581 patients, to construct a necroptosis interaction network (Fig. 2A). Univariate Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier survival analyses identified seven NRGs with prognostic significance (TLR3, TLR4, BIRC2, TRAF2, CASP8, NFKB1 and TNFRSF10B) (Additional file 10: Fig. S1; Additional file 12: Table S3). Finally, a consensus clustering algorithm was applied to stratify CRC tumor samples based on NRG expression patterns. Collectively, these results delineate a distinct necroptosis‑associated genetic and expression landscape in CRC, highlight NRGs with prognostic value, and provide a molecular framework for subclassifying tumors that may inform future therapeutic strategies.
