Data linkage between the Taiwan Biobank (TWB) and the National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) yielded a cohort of 19,500 participants, of whom 890 had ischemic stroke, 70 had hemorrhagic stroke, and 16,837 served as controls for comparative analysis. Ischemic stroke patients were significantly older than controls (mean ± SE = 58.47 ± 8.17 years vs. 48.33 ± 10.90 years, p < 0.0001) and had lower educational attainment, whereas hemorrhagic stroke patients showed a non‑significant age difference relative to controls (50.54 ± 10.60 vs. 48.33 ± 10.90 years, p = 0.0897). The prevalence of cerebrovascular risk factors—hypertension, hyperlipidemia, alcohol intake, and diabetes—was significantly higher in both stroke groups compared with controls (p < 0.05 for each). Genotype frequencies in the control population were: ALDH2 rs671 GG = 50.80 %, GA = 40.58 %, AA = 8.62 %; ADH1B rs1229984 TT = 54.40 %, TC = 39.01 %, CC = 6.59 %; sex distribution was similar across groups (women ≈ 51 %, men ≈ 49 %). Smoking, physical inactivity, and body mass index also differed significantly between cases and controls. Analyses stratified by alcohol intake revealed no association between the ADH1B rs1229984 variant and ischemic stroke risk; however, carriers of the TT and TC genotypes exhibited significantly higher prevalences of diabetes, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia. These findings suggest that while ADH1B rs1229984 does not modulate ischemic stroke susceptibility in this sample, it is linked to a metabolic risk profile that may influence cerebrovascular disease pathophysiology.
