Abstract.
We investigated the immunomodulatory effects of combining FK506 (0.3 nM) with Lactobacillus acidophilus (100 µg/mL) on murine T‑cell differentiation and function. Splenocytes from C57BL/6 mice were stimulated with anti‑CD3 antibody for 72 h under vehicle, single‑agent, or combination conditions. Flow cytometry revealed that the combination markedly reduced the proliferation of IFN‑γ‑producing Th1 cells and IL‑17‑producing Th17 cells while significantly increasing the proportion of Foxp3⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) compared with vehicle or either treatment alone. Correspondingly, IFN‑γ and IL‑17 concentrations in supernatants were decreased, whereas IL‑10 was markedly elevated. The combination also suppressed CD8⁺ type 1 cytotoxic (Tc1) and IL‑17‑producing CD8⁺ (Tc17) cell proliferation, with L. acidophilus alone showing greater inhibition of Tc1 cells than FK506 alone. In mixed lymphocyte reactions, the combination treatment markedly inhibited alloreactive T‑cell proliferation relative to allo‑stimulated controls or single agents. Tregs isolated from combination‑treated cultures retained suppressive activity, markedly attenuating proliferation of responder alloreactive T cells upon co‑culture. RNA‑seq analysis of CD4⁺ T cells demonstrated that the combination downregulated Th1‑associated genes (Tbx21, Lgals7) and Th17‑associated genes (Il13, Mapk13, Il21, Slc26a10, Gzma) while upregulating Treg‑signature genes (Nt5e, Foxp3, Ikzf2, Nrp1, Itgb8). Collectively, these findings indicate that FK506 and L. acidophilus synergistically restore the Th17/Treg balance, promoting an immunoregulatory phenotype that attenuates pathogenic T‑cell responses relevant to acute graft‑versus‑host disease.
