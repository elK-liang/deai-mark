Prognostic utility of lipoprotein(a) combined with fibrinogen in patients with stable coronary artery disease: a prospective, large cohort study – Journal of Translational Medicine (2020). In a prospective cohort of 8,417 patients with stable coronary artery disease followed for a median of 37.1 months (interquartile range 22.5–55.4 months), 395 cardiovascular events occurred, comprising 160 deaths, 78 non‑fatal myocardial infarctions, and 157 strokes. Compared with event‑free participants, those who experienced events were older (p < 0.001), had a higher prevalence of hypertension (p = 0.005) and diabetes mellitus (p < 0.001), and a lower body‑mass index (p = 0.016). Baseline lipid profiles (triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL‑C, HDL‑C, apoA1, apoB) did not differ between groups (all p > 0.05), whereas lipoprotein(a) levels were significantly higher in the event group (p = 0.001). Fibrinogen and D‑dimer concentrations were also elevated among patients with events (all p < 0.05). Statin use at admission was lower in the event cohort (p = 0.005) but became balanced at discharge (p > 0.05). Hazard ratios for baseline characteristics predicting future cardiovascular events are detailed in Additional file 1, Table S1. These findings suggest that elevated lipoprotein(a), together with increased fibrinogen, provides prognostic information for cardiovascular outcomes in stable coronary artery disease and may aid risk stratification beyond conventional lipid measures.
