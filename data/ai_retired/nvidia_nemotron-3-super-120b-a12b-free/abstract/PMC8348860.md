Abstract.
We investigated the expression pattern and prognostic relevance of the BCL7 family in glioma by integrating multiple public transcriptomic resources (cBioportal, Oncomine, Gliovis, GEO) with immunohistochemical analysis of 108 glioma specimens. BCL7A mRNA was markedly reduced in glioma tissues compared with normal brain in 7 of 8 GEO datasets and showed a progressive decrease with increasing WHO grade in both CGGA and TCGA cohorts, being lowest in glioblastoma (GBM) versus lower‑grade glioma (LGG). In contrast, BCL7C was significantly elevated in glioma in 6 of 8 datasets and higher in GBM than LGG, while BCL7B displayed no overall difference versus normal brain but was enriched in GBM and increased with tumor grade. Immunohistochemistry confirmed low BCL7A protein and high BCL7C protein in glioma relative to non‑tumor brain, with BCL7B unchanged. These consistent findings across independent datasets and protein‑level validation indicate that diminished BCL7A expression correlates with higher glioma malignancy and therefore represents a novel downregulated prognostic biomarker, whereas the reciprocal upregulation of BCL7B and C parallels tumor grade progression.
