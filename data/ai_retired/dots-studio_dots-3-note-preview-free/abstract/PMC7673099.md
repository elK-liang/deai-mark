Abstract

Organotypic heart slices represent a valuable model system for studying adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated gene transfer into cardiac cells. The aim of this study was to establish and optimize an organotypic heart slice model using left ventricular tissue from transgenic αMHC-mCherry mice to investigate AAV gene transfer.

Myocardial slices of left ventricular tissue were prepared with a vibrating microtome and cultured at an air-liquid interface. Slice thicknesses of 100, 200, and 300 µm were compared regarding mCherry expression, metabolic activity (MTS assay), and handling convenience. Co-expression of mCherry with cardiac troponin T (cTNT) confirmed cardiomyocyte-specificity. Viability of 300-µm-thick slices was assessed over 5 days under normoxic conditions using the calcein-AM assay, and hypoxia was applied to mimic acute myocardial infarction.

Slices with a thickness of 300 µm showed the strongest mCherry signal, the highest metabolic activity, and were easier to handle than thinner slices, and were therefore selected for further experiments. Under normoxic culture conditions, slice viability was maintained for at least 5 days, with an initial ~25% decrease in cardiomyocyte survival after 1 day, followed by a stable phase for the remaining 4 days. Exposure to hypoxic conditions significantly reduced slice viability, leading to widespread cell death.

300-µm-thick organotypic heart slices from αMHC-mCherry mice cultured at an air-liquid interface provide a robust and viable model system for at least 5 days under normoxic conditions, suitable for studying AAV-mediated gene transfer and the cellular response to hypoxic stress mimicking myocardial infarction.
