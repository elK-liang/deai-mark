This prospective, large cohort study evaluated the prognostic utility of combining lipoprotein(a) and fibrinogen in patients with stable coronary artery disease. Among 8417 participants followed for a median of 37.1 months, 395 cardiovascular events (CVEs) occurred, including 160 deaths, 78 non-fatal myocardial infarctions, and 157 strokes. Comparative analysis of baseline characteristics revealed that patients who experienced CVEs were older, had a higher prevalence of hypertension and diabetes, a lower body mass index, and lower statin usage at admission, while standard lipid profiles showed no significant differences. Notably, patients with CVEs exhibited significantly higher baseline levels of lipoprotein(a), fibrinogen, and D-dimer compared to those without events. These findings highlight the potential prognostic value of combining lipoprotein(a) and fibrinogen levels to identify patients with stable coronary artery disease who are at higher risk of future cardiovascular events.
