Abstract
In this study, we examined the role of 33 necroptosis‑related genes (NRGs) in colorectal cancer (CRC). Gene ontology analysis confirmed enrichment of cell death pathways, particularly necroptosis. Somatic mutation and copy‑number variation analyses in the TCGA cohort revealed frequent alterations, with CASP8 showing the highest mutation rate. Principal component analysis demonstrated that NRG expression distinguishes tumor from normal tissues, and genes with copy‑number amplification were up‑regulated in tumors. Integrating five CRC cohorts (n = 1581), we constructed a necroptosis‑associated network and identified seven NRGs (TLR3, TLR4, BIRC2, TRAF2, CASP8, NFKB1, TNFRSF10B) with prognostic significance. Consensus clustering based on these genes stratified CRC patients into distinct subtypes. These findings suggest that NRGs contribute to CRC tumorigenesis and may serve as prognostic markers and classification tools.
