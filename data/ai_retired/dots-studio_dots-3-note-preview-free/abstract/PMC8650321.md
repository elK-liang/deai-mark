This study investigated the relationships between physical signs of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (FH), such as tendon xanthomas (TX) and corneal arcus (CA), and various clinical, genetic, and outcome parameters. Among 466 subjects, 55 (11.8%) exhibited physical signs. After propensity score matching, a cohort of 250 subjects was analyzed, with 50 patients having TX/CA matched 1:4 to 200 patients without these signs. Despite similar baseline cardiovascular risk factors and prevalence of coronary artery disease, patients with TX/CA had significantly higher levels of LDL-C (p=0.051), uncontrolled LDL-C (p=0.025), and lower HDL-C (p=0.021) and Apo A1 (p=0.001). Genetically, the TX/CA group had a higher prevalence of definite FH (p<0.001), mutation-positive status (p=0.001), and specifically LDLR mutations (p<0.001). Notably, despite these adverse baseline lipid and genetic profiles, there was no significant difference in the incidence of coronary artery disease or the number of diseased vessels between the two groups. These findings suggest that while physical signs are strongly associated with more severe genetic and lipid abnormalities, they are not independently associated with worse coronary outcomes in this matched cohort.
