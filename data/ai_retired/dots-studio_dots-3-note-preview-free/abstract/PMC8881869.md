Abstract
The combination of FK506 and Lactobacillus acidophilus was evaluated for its immunomodulatory effects on T‑cell differentiation and function. In vitro, treatment of murine splenocytes with anti‑CD3 plus L. acidophilus and FK506 significantly reduced Th1 and Th17 cell proliferation, decreased IFN‑γ and IL‑17 production, increased IL‑10, and expanded Treg cells. It also suppressed Tc1 and Tc17 cells, with L. acidophilus alone being more effective than FK506 on Tc1 cells. The combination inhibited alloreactive T‑cell proliferation in mixed lymphocyte reactions, and induced Treg cells that further suppressed alloreactivity. RNA‑seq of CD4⁺ T cells showed downregulation of Th1 (Tbx21, Lgals7) and Th17 (Il13, Mapk13, Il21, Slc26a10, Gzma) pathway genes, and upregulation of Treg‑associated genes (Nt5e, Foxp3, Ikzf2, Nrp1, Itgb8). These results indicate that FK506 and L. acidophilus synergistically modulate the Th17/Treg balance, suggesting a potential therapeutic strategy for acute graft‑versus‑host disease.
