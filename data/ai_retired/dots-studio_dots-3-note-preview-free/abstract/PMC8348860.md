Abstract

Glioma is a highly aggressive primary brain tumor with limited prognostic markers. This study aimed to comprehensively analyze the expression and clinical significance of the BCL7 gene family (BCL7A, BCL7B, and BCL7C) in glioma to identify novel prognostic biomarkers.

Gene expression data for the BCL7 family were retrieved and analyzed from multiple public databases, including cBioPortal, Oncomine, Gliovis, GEO, CGGA, and TCGA. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was subsequently performed on a cohort of 108 glioma tissues and non-tumor brain tissues (NBTs) to validate the transcriptomic findings.

Analysis of public datasets revealed that BCL7A expression was significantly lower in glioma tissues compared to normal brain tissues, and it progressively decreased with higher tumor grades (significantly lower in glioblastoma [GBM] compared to lower-grade gliomas [WHO I–III]). Conversely, BCL7C expression was highly elevated in glioma, particularly in GBM. While BCL7B showed no significant difference between glioma and NBTs overall, it was enriched in GBM. IHC validation in 108 clinical samples confirmed a significant decrease in BCL7A and an increase in BCL7C in glioma tissues compared to NBTs, both showing a strong association with tumor grade.

BCL7A expression is significantly downregulated in glioma and inversely correlates with tumor grade and malignancy, establishing BCL7A as a promising novel prognostic biomarker for glioma patients.
