Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a complex vascular condition driven by diverse molecular and cellular mechanisms, where immune infiltration, chemotaxis, and the complement system are hypothesized to play pivotal roles. In this study, we performed a comparative transcriptomic analysis between TAA and normal aortic samples, identifying 85 downregulated and 171 upregulated genes under a Log2 fold change threshold greater than 0.5. To characterize the biological significance of these differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses. The GO analysis revealed significant enrichment in the regulation of immune effector processes, cell–cell adhesion, immune cell proliferation, and immune receptor activity, suggesting that immune-related processes are essential in the formation and progression of TAA. KEGG pathway enrichment further indicated the involvement of Th17, Th1, and Th2 cell differentiation, natural killer (NK) cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and the B cell receptor signaling pathway, alongside potential co-pathways associated with bacterial and viral infections such as Staphylococcus aureus, tuberculosis, influenza A, and Epstein-Barr virus. Among the top-ranked DEGs, key immune and complement-associated genes, including CX3CR1, C3, HLA-DPA1, and CD74, exhibited significant differential expression. Collectively, these findings highlight a strong association between chemotaxis, the complement system, and immune cell infiltration in the pathogenesis of TAA, offering novel insights into the molecular landscape of the disease and pointing to potential targets for therapeutic intervention.
