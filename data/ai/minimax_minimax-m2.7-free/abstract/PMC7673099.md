Abstract  
Adeno‑associated virus (AAV) vectors are promising tools for cardiac gene therapy, yet systematic comparisons of AAV serotypes in a physiologically relevant ex‑vivo cardiac model remain limited. Organotypic heart slices preserve native tissue architecture and multiple cardiac cell types, offering a robust platform to evaluate AAV‑mediated transgene delivery.  

Left‑ventricular myocardial slices were prepared from homozygous αMHC‑mCherry transgenic mice using a vibrating microtome and cultured under air‑liquid interface conditions. Cardiomyocyte identity was confirmed by co‑staining for cardiac troponin T (cTNT). Slice thickness (100, 200, and 300 µm) was optimized by quantifying mCherry fluorescence intensity and metabolic activity (MTS assay). Viability was assessed over 5 days using calcein‑AM (live cells) and lactate dehydrogenase release (cell death) under normoxic culture, and to model acute myocardial infarction, slices were subjected to hypoxic conditions.  

Slices of 300 µm exhibited the strongest mCherry signal and the highest metabolic activity, and were therefore used for all subsequent experiments. Under normoxia, resident cells remained viable for at least 5 days; cardiomyocyte survival decreased ~25 % after the first day but remained stable thereafter. In contrast, hypoxic culture dramatically reduced slice viability, with most cells dying within the first 24 h, reproducing an ischaemia‑like injury.  

The established organotypic heart slice model maintains cardiac architecture and cardiomyocyte viability ex‑vivo for at least five days and provides a reproducible system for directly comparing AAV serotypes for cardiac gene transfer. This platform enables systematic evaluation of vector tropism, transduction efficiency, and therapeutic potential in a setting that closely mirrors in‑vivo myocardial physiology.
