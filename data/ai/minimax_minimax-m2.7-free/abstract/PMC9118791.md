Abstract  
Necroptosis is a form of programmed cell death increasingly recognized for its role in cancer, yet a comprehensive molecular characterization of necroptosis‑related genes (NRGs) and its impact on the tumor microenvironment (TME) in colorectal cancer (CRC) remains lacking. We aimed to delineate the necroptosis landscape in CRC, identify clinically relevant subtypes, and关联 TME infiltration with patient prognosis.  
We analyzed 33 NRGs (Additional file 11: Table S2) across 1581 CRC patients from five cohorts (TCGA‑COAD/READ, GSE14333, GSE33113, GSE37892, GSE39582). Somatic mutations, copy‑number variations (CNVs), and gene expression differences between tumor and normal tissues were evaluated. A necroptosis regulatory network was constructed, and univariate Cox regression combined with Kaplan–Meier analysis identified prognostic NRGs. Consensus clustering was applied to define molecular subtypes, which were subsequently linked to TME infiltration patterns and clinical outcomes.  
Genetic alterations in NRGs were prevalent: 111 of 502 TCGA CRC samples harbored mutations (CASP8 most frequently mutated at 4 %), and widespread CNVs were observed. Principal component analysis demonstrated that NRG expression profiles separate CRC from normal tissue, with expression changes reflecting CNV status (e.g., MYC, FADD, AURKA upregulated; TLR3, CHUK, RIPK1 downregulated). Seven NRGs (TLR3, TLR4, BIRC2, TRAF2, CASP8, NFKB1, TNFRSF10B) were significantly associated with overall survival (Additional file 12: Table S3). Consensus clustering uncovered distinct necroptosis‑based subtypes exhibiting divergent TME infiltration profiles and prognostic outcomes.  
The necroptosis‑based molecular classification provides a robust prognostic tool for CRC, reveals how NRG expression influences the tumor microenvironment, and offers a foundation for personalized therapeutic strategies targeting necroptotic pathways.
