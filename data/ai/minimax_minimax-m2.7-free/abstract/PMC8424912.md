Abstract

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide. While circular RNAs (circRNAs) are increasingly recognized as key regulators of tumor biology, the landscape of dysregulated circRNAs in CRC and their functional relevance remain incompletely characterized.  

We performed an integrative analysis of circRNA expression using the GEO dataset GSE126095 (10 paired CRC and adjacent normal tissues) and supplemented it with custom microarray profiling of 3 independent paired samples. Overlap of the two platforms identified nine up‑regulated circRNAs, with circMYH9 (hsa_circ_0092283) showing the most pronounced increase. The circular nature of circMYH9 was verified by RT‑PCR with divergent primers, resistance to RNase R digestion, and Sanger sequencing of the head‑to‑tail junction. Expression levels were assessed by qRT‑PCR and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) in a cohort of 148 CRC patients, and correlations with clinicopathological variables and five‑year survival were examined. Functional consequences of circMYH9 were investigated in CRC cell lines using loss‑ and gain‑of‑function approaches, including proliferation, colony‑formation, metabolic, and redox assays, with particular attention to p53 status.  

The bioinformatic screen revealed 237 up‑regulated circRNAs in the GEO cohort (FDR < 0.01, |logFC| ≥ 2); the microarray analysis identified 92 up‑regulated circRNAs. Nine circRNAs overlapped, and circMYH9 exhibited the highest fold change (logFC = 3.63 in the GEO set; FC = 4.51 in the microarray set). Divergent primers amplified circMYH9 only from cDNA, and the transcript resisted RNase R degradation, confirming its circular structure. Sanger sequencing validated head‑to‑tail splicing of MYH9 intron 37. Both qRT‑PCR and FISH showed significantly higher circMYH9 levels in tumor versus peritumoral tissue. High circMYH9 expression correlated with larger tumor size, distant metastasis, lymph‑node metastasis, advanced TNM stage, and mutant p53 status (all p < 0.05). Kaplan‑Meier analysis indicated that patients with elevated circMYH9 experienced shorter five‑year overall survival. Functional experiments demonstrated that circMYH9 knock‑down reduced cell proliferation and colony formation, whereas over‑expression enhanced these behaviors; moreover, circMYH9 modulated serine metabolism and redox balance in a manner dependent on p53.  

circMYH9 is a novel up‑regulated circRNA in colorectal cancer that promotes tumor growth, influences serine metabolism and redox homeostasis, and exerts its effects in a p53‑dependent manner. Its expression is associated with aggressive clinicopathological features and poorer survival, highlighting circMYH9 as a potential diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for CRC.
