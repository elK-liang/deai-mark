Skin cancer is a growing public health concern in Iran, and establishing preventive habits early in life may reduce future incidence; this study applied Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) to identify determinants of skin‑cancer preventive behaviors among rural primary‑school children and evaluated a theory‑based educational intervention. A cross‑sectional survey was conducted with 400 fifth‑grade students (135 boys, 265 girls; mean age 11.11 ± 0.79 years) from rural schools in Fasa city; the sample’s parents were mainly self‑employed fathers (72.75 %) and housewife mothers (80.6 %), with 17.75 % of fathers holding an academic degree and 84.75 % of mothers having at least an undergraduate education, and a mean household size of 4.82 ± 1.16. Bivariate analysis showed that skin‑cancer preventive behaviors were significantly positively associated with perceived sensitivity (r = 0.178, p = 0.007), perceived severity (r = 0.124, p = 0.016), protection motivation (r = 0.184, p = 0.004), response efficacy (p = 0.022), self‑efficacy (r = 0.180, p = 0.018), fear (r = 0.144, p = 0.009) and knowledge (r = 0.102, p = 0.037), and negatively associated with rewards (r = –0.126, p = 0.025) and response costs (r = –0.115, p = 0.016). Logistic regression identified protection motivation (β = 0.228, B = 0.165, p = 0.014), self‑efficacy (β = 0.219, B = 0.156, p = 0.025) and perceived sensitivity (β = 0.206, B = 0.142, p = 0.036) as the strongest predictors, together explaining 58.6 % of the variance in preventive behaviors (R² = 0.586). A subsequent quasi‑experimental phase allocated students to an experimental (PMT‑based education) or control group; baseline age (experimental = 11.26 ± 0.72 years, control = 11.18 ± 0.80 years, p = 0.286) and household size (experimental = 4.9 ± 1.0, control = 4.24 ± 1.20, p = 0.222) were comparable. Post‑intervention chi‑square testing revealed no significant difference in the proportion of students reporting adequate skin‑cancer preventive behaviors between the two groups (p > 0.05). The findings confirm that PMT constructs—particularly protection motivation, self‑efficacy, and perceived sensitivity—are key correlates of preventive behaviors, but the brief school‑based intervention did not produce a statistically significant change, suggesting that more intensive or sustained strategies may be needed to promote long‑term sun‑protection habits among rural primary‑school students in Iran.
