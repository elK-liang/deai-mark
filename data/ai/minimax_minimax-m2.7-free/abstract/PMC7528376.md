The independent and combined prognostic value of lipoprotein(a) (Lp(a)) and fibrinogen in stable coronary artery disease (CAD) is not fully defined. Methods: In this prospective, large‑cohort study, 8417 patients with stable CAD were enrolled and followed for a median of 37.1 months (interquartile range 22.5–55.4 months). Baseline clinical data, conventional lipid profile, plasma Lp(a) and fibrinogen concentrations, and medication use were recorded. The primary outcome was a composite of cardiovascular events (CVEs) comprising all‑cause death, non‑fatal myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke. Results: During follow‑up, 395 CVEs occurred (160 deaths, 78 non‑fatal MI, 157 strokes). Patients who experienced CVEs were older (62.2 ± 10.2 vs. 57.2 ± 10.8 years, p < 0.001), more frequently had hypertension (68.8% vs. 61.7%, p = 0.005) and diabetes (37.1% vs. 27.0%, p < 0.001), and had lower BMI (25.5 ± 3.2 vs. 25.9 ± 3.2 kg/m², p = 0.016). Plasma Lp(a) was significantly higher in the event group (median 19.24 mg/dL vs. 15.00 mg/dL, p = 0.001) and fibrinogen levels were also elevated (3.35 ± 0.81 g/L vs. 3.23 ± 0.78 g/L, p = 0.003). Conventional lipid parameters did not differ between groups. Statin use at admission was lower in those who later had events (68.3% vs. 75.8%, p = 0.005) but was comparable at discharge (95.5% vs. 93.9%, p = 0.313). Hazard ratios for Lp(a) and fibrinogen (shown in Additional file 1) indicated association with future CVEs. Conclusion: Elevated baseline Lp(a) and fibrinogen are associated with higher risk of subsequent cardiovascular events in patients with stable CAD, suggesting that their combined assessment may enhance risk prediction.
