We aimed to standardize the histological evaluation of response to preoperative chemotherapy in high‑grade non‑round cell soft tissue sarcomas and to explore its prognostic significance. Between March 2004 and September 2008, 72 patients were enrolled in the JCOG0304 trial, and 64 of them were eligible for this ancillary study; their median age was 47.5 years (range 21–66) and the median major‑axis tumor size was 7.4 cm (range 3.0–26.0 cm). Central pathology review of post‑chemotherapy specimens identified three distinct microscopic compartments: viable tumor tissue, necrotic areas with irreversible nuclear changes, and degenerative zones characterized by fibrosis, hyalinization, or cystic change. The percentage of viable tumor area ranged from 0.1 % to 97.0 % (median 35.7 %), with a similar distribution across histologic subtypes such as undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (n = 20), synovial sarcoma (n = 17), leiomyosarcoma (n = 12), myxofibrosarcoma (n = 7), pleomorphic liposarcoma (n = 3), myxoid liposarcoma (n = 2), and other rare entities. The proposed evaluation method, based on the quantifiable proportion of viable tumor, proved reproducible and enabled clear categorization of treatment response across the cohort. Although the current analysis primarily addresses standardization and the descriptive characteristics of histological response, the data provide a robust foundation for future investigations into the association between the viable tumor fraction and clinical outcomes such as disease‑free or overall survival. In conclusion, a standardized, centrally reviewed histological assessment of preoperative chemotherapy response is feasible in this patient population and may serve as a reliable metric for prognostic evaluation in high‑grade non‑round cell soft tissue sarcomas.
