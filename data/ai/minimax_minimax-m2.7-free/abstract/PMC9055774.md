Gastric cancer (GC) is a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide, and non‑invasive biomarkers for early detection are urgently needed. This study evaluated the salivary concentrations of cystatin B (CSTB) and deleted in malignant brain tumors 1 (DMBT1) in 31 patients with histologically confirmed GC and 31 age‑ and sex‑matched healthy controls. Saliva samples were collected under standardized conditions, and CSTB and DMBT1 levels were quantified by enzyme‑linked immunosorbent assay. Between‑group comparisons revealed significant differences in several demographic and lifestyle factors, including education level, occupational stress, history of gastro‑esophageal reflux disease, gastric ulcers, vegetable consumption, and salty taste preference (p < 0.05). The mean salivary CSTB concentration was significantly lower in GC patients (115.55 ± 7.06 ng/mL) than in controls (128.30 ± 18.06 ng/mL, p = 0.001), whereas the mean DMBT1 concentration was significantly higher in GC patients (171.88 ± 39.67 ng/mL) compared with controls (139.76 ± 39.05 ng/mL, p = 0.002). These findings indicate that salivary CSTB and DMBT1 levels are altered in gastric cancer and may serve as promising non‑invasive biomarkers for GC detection; however, larger prospective studies are required to validate their diagnostic performance and clinical utility.
