Abstract  
Presenilin 1/2 conditional double‑knockout (PS cDKO) mice develop Alzheimer’s disease (AD)–like neurodegeneration, yet early urinary metabolic and gut microbial alterations remain incompletely characterized.  
Using GC–MS–based metabolomics and 16S rRNA gene sequencing of fecal samples, we profiled wild‑type (WT) and PS cDKO mice at 2 and 6 months of age. Multivariate analyses (principal component analysis, PCA; partial least‑squares discriminant analysis, PLS‑DA) were applied to identify differential urinary metabolites and to explore associated microbial community changes.  
Clear separation between WT and PS cDKO mice was observed at both ages (PCA: R²X = 0.821, Q² = 0.508; PLS‑DA: R²X = 0.864, R²Y = 0.982, Q² = 0.959). At 2 months, eight metabolites differed between WT‑2 and cDKO‑2; at 6 months, eleven metabolites differed between WT‑6 and cDKO‑6. Two metabolites, xylitol and glycine, were common to both age groups (cluster II) and were down‑regulated in PS cDKO mice, whereas the remaining altered metabolites formed cluster I with distinct age‑specific patterns.  
PS cDKO mice exhibit age‑dependent urinary metabolomic changes, notably the consistent down‑regulation of xylitol and glycine, suggesting early systemic metabolic signatures of AD‑like pathology. These metabolites may serve as non‑invasive biomarkers for early disease progression in this mouse model.
