In this propensity‑matched analysis of 466 patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, 250 individuals (50 with tendon xanthomas and/or corneal arcus [TX/CA] and 200 age‑, sex‑ and disease‑severity‑matched controls) were examined to determine how these physical signs relate to genetic defects, plasma lipid and inflammatory markers, coronary stenosis/calcification, and clinical outcomes. The study cohort had a mean age of 47.8 ± 12.6 years and was 62 % male; traditional cardiovascular risk factors, body‑mass index and family history of coronary disease were comparable between groups. Patients with TX/CA exhibited a more atherogenic lipid profile—higher LDL‑C (5.94 ± 2.38 vs 5.19 ± 1.80 mmol/L, p = 0.051), higher untreated LDL‑C (8.65 ± 2.53 vs 7.70 ± 2.18 mmol/L, p = 0.025), lower HDL‑C (1.00 ± 0.34 vs 1.14 ± 0.37 mmol/L, p = 0.021) and Apo‑A1 (1.11 ± 0.37 vs 1.30 ± 0.31 g/L, p = 0.001), and lower triglycerides (median 1.26 vs 1.60 mmol/L, p = 0.001). Genetically, TX/CA carriers were more likely to have a definitive FH diagnosis (DLCN score > 8) (94 % vs 48.5 %, p < 0.001), a higher prevalence of LDLR‑positive mutations (66 % vs 32 %, p < 0.001), and a greater proportion with two or more mutant alleles (24 % vs 9 %, p = 0.004). Despite these differences, the prevalence of coronary artery disease (78 % vs 71 %, p = 0.132) and the number of diseased vessels were similar between groups, indicating that, after adjusting for baseline covariates, the presence of TX/CA was not independently associated with a higher burden of angiographically defined atherosclerosis. The findings suggest that tendon xanthomas and corneal arcus serve as clinical flags for a more severe FH genotype and a more adverse lipid phenotype, but they do not independently predict higher coronary stenosis or calcification in this propensity‑matched HeFH population; longer‑term follow‑up is required to clarify their impact on hard clinical outcomes.
