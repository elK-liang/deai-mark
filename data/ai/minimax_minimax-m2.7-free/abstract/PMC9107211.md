In this prospective cohort study conducted at the Ospedale Policlinico San Martino Cancer Center between April 14 and June 23 2020, we enrolled 466 patients with solid malignancies diagnosed before January 2020, of whom 446 underwent systematic SARS‑CoV‑2 serology (IgM/IgG) and were followed until August 23 2020; 20 patients were excluded for prior COVID‑19 symptoms, hematologic disease, previous PCR‑confirmed infection, or chemotherapy regimen changes. The cohort spanned ages 27–90 years and included a wide spectrum of tumor types (breast 146, colorectal 103, prostate 42, pancreatic 28, ovarian 25, renal 18, GIST 17, gastric 14, hepatobiliary 12, lung 11, sarcoma 8, endometrial 5, cervical 4, esophageal 3, urothelial 3, other 7). Serial serology was performed at up to five time points (397 patients had ≥2, 312 had ≥3, and 122 had four determinations). Overall, 14 of the 446 evaluable patients (3.14 %) became positive for at least one SARS‑CoV‑2 immunoglobulin during the observation period, all without fever or other clinical signs of COVID‑19. The seropositive cases exhibited heterogeneous immunomic profiles: some displayed early isolated IgM that later waned, others showed persistent IgM positivity, several seroconverted to IgG after an initial IgM response, and a few manifested concurrent IgM/IgG; antibody dynamics fluctuated over weeks despite consistently negative nasopharyngeal PCR results in most individuals. These findings reveal a low incidence (≈3 %) of apyretic SARS‑CoV‑2 infection among solid‑tumor patients during the early pandemic wave and highlight the diversity of serologic responses, underscoring the need for routine longitudinal serologic surveillance to detect asymptomatic infection and to characterize the immunomic signatures that may inform risk stratification and protective strategies in vulnerable cancer populations.
