Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a debilitating illness whose underlying biology remains incompletely understood, and sex differences are recognized in its presentation and diagnosis. To explore the role of plasma lipids in disease pathogenesis and symptom dimensions, we performed a case‑control lipidomics study in which ME/CFS patients and age‑ and BMI‑matched healthy controls were stratified by sex. Global lipid profiling of phospholipid (PL) and neutral lipid (NL) classes revealed distinct patterns between sexes: healthy females exhibited higher total phosphatidylcholine (PC) than healthy males (p < 0.05). Within males, total hexosylceramide (HexCer) was significantly elevated in ME/CFS patients compared with controls (p < 0.05), whereas female patients showed decreased phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and HexCer relative to female controls (p < 0.05). Analysis of lipid unsaturation did not differ between female groups, but in males, PE‑monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA), phosphatidylinositol‑MUFA, and triglyceride‑saturated fatty acid (TG‑SFA) were increased in ME/CFS (p < 0.05). Levels of arachidonic acid (AA)‑ and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)‑containing PC and cholesteryl esters (CE) were higher, while acylcarnitine‑AA (CAR‑AA) was lower, in healthy females versus healthy males (p < 0.01). Among females, PE‑AA was reduced in ME/CFS compared with controls (p < 0.01), and CE‑AA was higher in female than male ME/CFS patients. These sex‑specific alterations in PL and NL species highlight potentially distinct inflammatory and metabolic pathways that may underlie the heterogeneous symptom triad of pain, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction in ME/CFS, and suggest that lipid signatures could serve as biomarkers or therapeutic targets in a sex‑dependent manner.
