Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a life‑threatening dilation of the thoracic aorta whose molecular pathogenesis remains incompletely understood; to explore the contribution of immune‑related pathways, especially chemotaxis and the complement system, we performed weighted gene co‑expression network analysis (WGCNA) on RNA‑seq data from dilated TAA and normal aortic tissues. Using a stringent |Log2FC| > 0.5 threshold, we identified 256 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), with 171 up‑regulated and 85 down‑regulated in TAAs, and applied Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment to these DEGs. The most significant GO terms included regulation of immune effector processes, cell–cell adhesion, immune cell proliferation and immune receptor activity, while KEGG analyses highlighted Th17/Th1/Th2 differentiation, natural killer cell‑mediated cytotoxicity, B‑cell receptor signaling and pathways associated with bacterial and viral infections (Staphylococcus aureus, tuberculosis, influenza A and Epstein‑Barr virus). Notably, up‑regulated genes such as CX3CR1 (chemokine receptor) and C3AR1 (complement component 3a receptor) point to active chemotaxis and complement activation, and HLA class II molecules (HLA‑DPA1, HLA‑DRA, HLA‑DPB1) together with other immune markers (CD74, MS4A4A, FCGR3A) indicate substantial immune infiltration. WGCNA further revealed co‑expression modules enriched for these immune‑related functions, with hub genes including CX3CR1 and C3AR1, underscoring their potential centrality in the disease network. Collectively, these findings support a model in which chemotactic and complement‑mediated immune responses, possibly triggered or exacerbated by infection, contribute to the formation and progression of TAAs, suggesting that targeting these pathways may offer therapeutic strategies for this devastating vascular disorder.
