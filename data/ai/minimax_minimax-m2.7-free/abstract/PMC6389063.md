Abstract  
Gastric cancer (GC) remains a leading cause of cancer‑related death worldwide, and novel non‑invasive biomarkers for early detection and prognostic assessment are urgently needed. Exosomal long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are emerging as stable, tumor‑derived molecules detectable in bodily fluids. This study evaluated the diagnostic and prognostic potential of exosomal HOTTIP in GC.  

Exosomal HOTTIP was first identified in culture medium of the GC cell line SGC‑7901 by RT‑qPCR, monitoring expression over 24–72 h to confirm tumor‑cell release. For the clinical cohort, serum samples were collected from 126 GC patients and 120 age‑matched healthy controls at Qilu Hospital, Shandong University (January 2011–May 2012) prior to any surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation. Exosomes were isolated, and HOTTIP levels were quantified by RT‑qPCR using GAPDH and UBC as internal controls. Stability was assessed after prolonged room‑temperature exposure or repeated freeze‑thaw cycles. Associations between exosomal HOTTIP expression and clinicopathological parameters were analyzed, and diagnostic performance was compared with conventional serum markers (CEA, CA 19‑9, CA 72‑4) using ROC curve analysis.  

Exosomal HOTTIP was readily detectable in GC cell culture supernatant and increased with incubation time, confirming active release from tumor cells. In the clinical series, exosomal HOTTIP expression was significantly up‑regulated in GC patients compared with healthy controls (P < 0.001) and remained stable under various storage conditions. Higher exosomal HOTTIP levels were positively associated with invasion depth (P = 0.0298) and TNM stage (P < 0.001), but not with age, gender, tumor size, pathological differentiation, or lymph‑node metastasis (all P > 0.05). The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for exosomal HOTTIP was 0.827, markedly higher than those for CEA (0.653), CA 19‑9 (0.685), and CA 72‑4 (0.639) (all P < 0.001). The combined conventional panel yielded an AUC of 0.723, still inferior to exosomal HOTTIP alone.  

Exosomal HOTTIP is a stable, tumor‑derived lncRNA that discriminates GC patients from healthy individuals with superior diagnostic accuracy compared with established serum biomarkers and correlates with aggressive disease features, highlighting its potential as a non‑invasive biomarker for both diagnosis and prognosis of gastric cancer.
