Interleukin‑33 (IL‑33) and its soluble receptor sST2 are promising biomarkers for age‑related macular degeneration (AMD) and asthma, yet accurate measurement demands detection of reduced hIL‑33 at single‑digit pg/mL concentrations in limited ocular and systemic matrices. To identify a suitable assay, we assembled a panel of commercial ELISA, electrochemiluminescence (MSD) and Luminex platforms that have been used to report hIL‑33 levels and evaluated them against four pre‑defined performance criteria essential for human vitreous humor (VH) and serum: (1) a limit of quantitation (LLOQ) in VH < 4 pg/mL, (2) tolerance to ≥ 10 ng/mL hsST2 (mean VH ≈ 2 ng/mL, serum ≈ 20 ng/mL), (3) complete immunodepletion of spiked hIL‑33 (100 % ± 20 %), and (4) a maximum sample volume of 50 µL. After prescreening anti‑hIL‑33 antibodies for the highest buffer sensitivity, we tested the selected pairs on the indicated platforms. The Invitrogen Human IL‑33 ELISA, a rabbit anti‑hIL‑33 (PeproTech)/biotin‑goat anti‑hIL‑33 (R&D) combination, the U‑PLEX Human IL‑33 MSD kit, and the Bio‑Plex Pro Human Th17 cytokine 15‑plex showed the lowest LLOQ in VH (0.25–3.2 pg/mL). However, none of these assays could withstand the physiological hsST2 concentrations present in VH or serum, and none satisfied all four criteria simultaneously. These results demonstrate that current commercial kits are insufficient for ultra‑sensitive hIL‑33 quantification in the matrices relevant to AMD and asthma, underscoring the need for a novel assay that meets the established performance standards. The criteria and comparative data presented here provide a roadmap for developing an ultra‑sensitive, iPCR‑based hIL‑33 biomarker assay to support drug development and mechanistic studies in these diseases.
