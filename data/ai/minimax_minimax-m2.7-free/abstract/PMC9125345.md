Abstract  

Adoptive cell therapy (ACT) using tumor‑infiltrating lymphocytes has shown clinical benefit, but the presence and expandability of tumor‑reactive T cells in tumor‑draining lymph nodes (TDLNs) of colorectal cancer (CRC) patients remain underexplored. We evaluated the ability to detect and expand tumor‑reactive T cells from both non‑metastatic and metastatic TDLNs of CRC patients.  

Autologous tumor‑cell lines were established from tumor tissues of seven CRC patients. Lymphocytes isolated from TDLNs were co‑cultured with autologous tumor cells either immediately after cell suspension (pre‑stimulus) or after a 2‑week enrichment period. Tumor‑reactive T cells were quantified by interferon‑γ (IFNγ) ELISPOT assay, and CD4⁺/CD8⁺ T‑cell subsets were profiled by flow cytometry.  

Tumor‑reactive T cells were identified in lymphocytes from a non‑metastatic lymph node of patient C126 and in metastatic nodes of patients C149 and C152. Among the remaining four patients (C165, C177, C207, C215), pre‑stimulus cultures showed reactivity only in C207, who had Lynch syndrome. After 2 weeks of co‑culture, IFNγ‑spot‑forming cells increased in all four cases, demonstrating expansion of tumor‑reactive T cells. CD8⁺ T cells predominated in three of these patients, whereas CD4⁺ T cells expanded preferentially in patient C215, who possessed two genetically independent primary tumors.  

Co‑culture with autologous tumor cells efficiently expands rare tumor‑reactive T cells from non‑metastatic TDLNs, predominantly CD8⁺ T cells, indicating a viable source for adoptive cell therapy. The enrichment strategy may be especially relevant for patients with high tumor immunogenicity, such as those with Lynch syndrome.
