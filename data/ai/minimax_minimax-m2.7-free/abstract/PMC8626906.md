The tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) of HER2‑positive breast cancers is complex and may change during metastatic progression. Methods: We performed integrated gene expression profiling using the IO360 panel and digital spatial profiling of immune‑related proteins on 15 specimens collected from 8 patients, including 10 primary breast tumors, 2 soft‑tissue metastases, 2 brain metastases and 1 lung metastasis; eight patients were receiving systemic therapy at the time of collection while seven were treatment‑naïve. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of IO360 signatures, TIS (tumor inflammation signature) status, and PAM50 intrinsic subtypes were assessed across primary and metastatic lesions. Results: Metastatic disease and primary tumors formed distinct clusters, with 8 of 10 primary tumors exhibiting high TIS scores. The B‑cell abundance signature was significantly higher in primary breast cancers compared with all metastatic sites (p<0.05). Multiple lymphocyte‑related signatures (TIGIT, CTLA‑4, CD8⁺ T cells, PD‑1, cytotoxic cells, NK cells, TH1, exhausted CD8, PD‑L2, B7‑H3) showed consistent trends toward higher expression in primary tumors but did not reach statistical significance after adjustment. Differential analysis of brain metastases suggested lower expression of several immune‑activation and trafficking genes compared with primary tumors. Conclusions: These findings demonstrate that the immune landscape of HER2‑positive breast cancers is substantially altered upon metastasis, with metastatic lesions displaying a more immunosuppressive phenotype characterized by lower B‑cell and lymphocyte signatures and reduced TIS. The divergence in TIME between primary and metastatic sites may influence responsiveness to HER2‑directed and immunotherapy approaches and warrants further investigation.
