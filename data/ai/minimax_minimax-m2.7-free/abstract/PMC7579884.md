Hypoxia directed migration of human naïve monocytes is associated with an attenuation of cytokine release: indications for a key role of CCL26

Abstract: In this study, we investigated the effect of hypoxia on human naïve monocyte migration and cytokine release using a novel enzymatic hypoxia model combined with Ibidi chemotaxis chambers. Oxygen gradients were established by adding glucose oxidase and catalase to the filling port, creating stable hypoxic conditions (pO₂ ranging from 10 to 80 mmHg) that were maintained throughout the 6-hour experimental period. Time-lapse microscopy revealed that monocytes exhibited statistically significant directed migration toward lower oxygen concentrations (p < 0.05), while cells under normoxic conditions or exposed to heat-inactivated hypoxia-generating enzymes showed non-directed, erratic movement patterns (p > 0.05). These findings demonstrate that monocytes actively sense and migrate toward hypoxic microenvironments through oxygen gradient detection rather than responding to the enzymes themselves. Further investigation into whether hypoxia also affects cytokine and chemokine secretion from monocytes is currently underway, with preliminary data suggesting an attenuation of inflammatory mediator release that may implicate specific chemokines, including CCL26, in the hypoxic monocyte response. This study establishes a robust in vitro model for studying hypoxia-driven monocyte behavior relevant to inflammatory and ischemic disease processes.
