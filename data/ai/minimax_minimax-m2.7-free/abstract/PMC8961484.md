A mass‑spectrometric analysis was performed to characterize the endogenous kinin peptide profile in plasma and nasal lavage fluid (NLF) of healthy adult volunteers and to establish reference ranges for bradykinin (BK) and related peptides (des‑Arg¹⁰‑kallidin, BK1‑8, KD). Twenty‑eight Caucasian subjects (median age 26.5 years, 11 women, 17 men) were enrolled; plasma was successfully obtained from 24 participants and NLF with >30 % recovered volume from 24 participants, and all participants were negative for SARS‑CoV‑2 antigen. Kinin concentrations were measured by a validated LC–MS/MS assay with a defined lower limit of quantification (LLOQ); values below the LLOQ were set to zero and those between the detection limit and LLOQ were recorded semi‑quantitatively. Endogenous kinin levels in plasma were extremely low, with a median BK concentration of 0.0 pM (interquartile range 0.0–1.3 pM) and a maximum of 4.2 pM; des‑Arg¹⁰‑kallidin (max 2.0 pM, n = 10), BK1‑8 (max 4.3 pM, n = 5) and KD (max 0.7 pM, n = 9) were similarly low. Inter‑assay variability was <1.7 pM. No significant gender‑related differences were observed. In a subset of two volunteers sampled every 2 h from 06:00 to 18:00, kinin concentrations remained stable throughout the day, indicating no appreciable circadian rhythm. NLF samples showed kinin levels near or below the LLOQ, precluding precise quantification. An in‑vitro experiment with delayed addition of protease inhibitor (15 min post‑collection) markedly increased BK concentrations (median 322.2 pM), demonstrating the assay’s ability to capture kinin generation when inhibition is not immediate. Together, these findings document that healthy adults have basal plasma kinin peptide concentrations in the sub‑picomolar range with no gender or diurnal variation, and that NLF kinin levels are equally low, providing reference values for future translational studies of the kallikrein‑kinin system.
