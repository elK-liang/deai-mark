Abstract  
Tumor endothelial cells (TECs) are key contributors to tumor angiogenesis, yet their molecular signatures and functional properties in lung adenocarcinoma (ADC) remain incompletely characterized. In this study, we isolated TECs from ADC tissue (TEC‑A), paired paratumor ECs (PEC‑A), and normal tissue ECs (NEC‑A/NEC‑S) and validated their purity (>98 % CD105⁺, >90 % AcLDL uptake) and absence of VSMC or hematopoietic markers. Proteomic analysis identified 1 820 proteins; gene set enrichment analysis of the 81 proteins unique to TEC‑A revealed enrichment in transcription regulation, protein biogenesis, hydrogen peroxide catabolism, and NF‑κB signaling. Unsupervised clustering and principal‑component analysis (PC1 + PC2 = 63.3 % variance) demonstrated distinct proteomic profiles for TEC‑A versus other EC types, with CDH2 (N‑cadherin) and Piezo1 being the most strongly upregulated proteins among the 31 differentially expressed proteins shared between groups. Western blot validation of 15 candidate proteins confirmed these trends in TEC‑A, PEC‑A, and tumor‑cell conditioned medium‑cocultured ECs. Functional assays showed that TEC‑A migrated faster than NECs and TEC‑S in response to tumor‑cell conditioned medium, and induced the greatest VSMC migratory response during EC‑VSMC coculture, underscoring a pro‑angiogenic phenotype. Collectively, these data identify CDH2 and Piezo1 as top‑upregulated TEC‑specific proteins that promote angiogenesis and may serve as prognostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets in lung adenocarcinoma.
