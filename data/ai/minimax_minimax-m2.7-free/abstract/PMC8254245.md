Abstract  

Background  
Alkylating agents such as methyl methanesulfonate (MMS) introduce non‑enzymatic methylated bases in RNA, yet the specific impact on human mRNA has not been directly quantified. Moreover, the mechanisms that recognize and remove MMS‑induced lesions from mRNA remain unclear. This study investigates the effect of a non‑lethal MMS dose on the landscape of methylated nucleosides in human mRNA and identifies a role for the ALKBH3‑interacting protein ASCC3 in processing the damage.

Methods  
HeLa cells were treated with 1 mM MMS for 1 h (≈90 % cell survival) and cell‑cycle progression was monitored. Total RNA and poly(A)‑enriched mRNA were isolated before and 24 h after MMS exposure. To discriminate enzymatic from non‑enzymatic methylation, cells were cultured in a 1:1 mixture of unlabeled and deuterated methionine for >10 generations, converting methionine to S‑adenosyl‑methionine (SAM). Methylated ribonucleosides (m1A, m3C, m7G, m5C, m6A) were quantified by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC‑MS/MS) and the CH₃/CD₃ ratio was used as a read‑out of MMS‑induced (non‑enzymatic) methylation. The involvement of ASCC3 in processing MMS‑generated m1A and m3C was examined by siRNA‑mediated depletion and immunofluorescence analysis of processing body (P‑body) formation.

Results  
MMS treatment elevated the CH₃/CD₃ ratios for m1A, m3C, and m7G in mRNA, indicating increased non‑enzymatic methylation at these sites, whereas m5C and m6A levels remained unchanged, consistent with previous reports that MMS does not induce these lesions. ASCC3 knockdown impaired the recruitment of m1A‑ and m3C‑containing mRNAs to P‑bodies, suggesting that ASCC3 is required for the selective targeting of MMS‑induced 1‑methyladenosine and 3‑methylcytosine for storage or degradation within these cytoplasmic foci.

Conclusions  
This work provides the first direct quantification of MMS‑induced chemical methylation in human mRNA, demonstrating selective elevation of m1A, m3C, and m7G. ASCC3, as a partner of the RNA demethylase ALKBH3, mediates P‑body formation and the selective clearance of these MMS‑generated lesions, highlighting a dedicated pathway for the surveillance of alkylation damage in mRNA.
