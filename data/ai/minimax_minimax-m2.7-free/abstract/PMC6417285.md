Abstract  
Hypoxia is a hallmark of the tumor microenvironment and bone‑marrow‑derived stromal cells (BMSCs) have been shown to promote cancer progression, yet the underlying mechanisms—especially the role of BMSC‑derived exosomal microRNAs—remain incompletely understood.  
Using a syngeneic Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) mouse model, luciferase‑labeled LLC cells were injected subcutaneously either alone or together with BMSCs. Tumor growth and metastatic spread were monitored by bioluminescence imaging, and metastatic nodules in the lungs were quantified by histology (H&E). Plasma exosomes were isolated and their miRNA profiles were compared between the two groups. In vitro, BMSCs were cultured under normoxic or hypoxic conditions for 3 days, and exosome size was measured by nanoparticle tracking analysis.  
Co‑injection of BMSCs with LLC cells markedly increased primary tumor volume and enhanced lung metastasis, as evidenced by higher luciferase activity, a greater number of macroscopic metastatic nodules, and increased histologic metastatic foci. Plasma exosomes from the co‑injection group displayed a distinct miRNA signature: 86 miRNAs were significantly up‑regulated and 354 were down‑regulated relative to the LLC‑only group. Hypoxic BMSCs secreted smaller exosomes (average diameter ≈ 50 nm) compared with normoxic BMSCs (≈ 90 nm).  
BMSCs potentiate LLC tumor growth and lung metastasis in vivo and alter the plasma exosomal miRNA landscape. The shift toward smaller exosomes under hypoxia and the observed miRNA changes suggest that BMSC‑derived exosomal miRNAs may contribute to metastatic progression, potentially through STAT3‑mediated epithelial‑to‑mesenchymal transition.
