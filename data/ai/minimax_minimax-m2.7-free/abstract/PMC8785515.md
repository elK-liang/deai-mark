We used a dual‑staining imaging strategy that combines C‑terminal GFP tagging of CCR5 with extracellular epitope‑specific monoclonal antibodies conjugated to Alexa 647 to visualize and quantify the different subpopulations of CCR5 on the surface of human cell lines. By employing Airyscan‑based super‑resolution microscopy and the ZEN Blue 2.3 analysis platform, we achieved markedly higher resolution of CCR5 localizations and staining puncta compared with standard confocal microscopy, which allowed us to distinguish surface‑bound from internalized CCR5 after selective membrane permeabilization. The approach was applied to two cell lines that express markedly different amounts of CCR5—approximately 2,000 molecules per cell in JC10 cells and roughly 130,000 molecules per cell in JC53 cells—and the intensity of monoclonal antibody staining mirrored these expression differences, demonstrating a quantitative relationship between total cellular CCR5 content and antibody accessibility. Under the experimental conditions tested, both cell lines exhibited a decrease in monoclonal antibody binding to CCR5, indicating that CCR5 conformation and epitope availability can be modulated. These results establish a high‑resolution imaging workflow for enumerating CCR5 coreceptor populations in vitro and underscore the importance of super‑resolution visualization for accurately determining the amounts and spatial distributions of CCR5 that are permissive for HIV‑1 entry.
