Abstract  

Acute lung injury (ALI) is a life‑threatening condition characterized by exaggerated inflammation, increased vascular permeability and neutrophil recruitment. Microsomal prostaglandin E synthase‑1 (mPGES‑1) is the terminal enzyme generating prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) downstream of cyclooxygenase‑2 (COX‑2) and is implicated in perpetuating inflammatory cascades. This study examined whether selective inhibition of mPGES‑1 with BI1029539 can mitigate ALI and how it compares with the COX‑2 inhibitor celecoxib.

Male C57BL/6 mice received intratracheal lipopolysaccharide (LPS) to induce ALI. Animals were treated orally with BI1029539 (30 mg·kg⁻¹), celecoxib (30 mg·kg⁻¹) or vehicle 1 h before and 12 h after LPS. Lung permeability was assessed by bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) protein concentration and Evans blue dye extravasation. Neutrophil infiltration was quantified by myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity and wet/dry weight ratio. Histological injury, expression of mPGES‑1, COX‑2 and ICAM‑1 (immunofluorescence), and BAL cellularity with differentials were evaluated. Cytokine/mediator levels (TNF‑α, IL‑1β, PGE₂, 6‑keto‑PGF₁α) were measured by ELISA. A complementary cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) model was used to confirm the relevance of the pathway in another sepsis‑induced ALI paradigm.

LPS markedly increased BAL protein, Evans blue leakage, MPO activity, edema, and histology scores, together with up‑regulated mPGES‑1, COX‑2 and ICAM‑1. Both BI1029539 and celecoxib significantly attenuated all these permeability and inflammatory markers. In BAL fluid, total cells, neutrophils and macrophages were reduced by 80.7 %, 93.6 % and 67 % with BI1029539, versus 78.6 %, 93.5 % and 74 % with celecoxib. Concentrations of TNF‑α, IL‑1β and PGE₂ in BAL were markedly lower in both treatment groups, whereas PGI₂ (6‑keto‑PGF₁α) was reduced only by celecoxib. In the CLP model, lung damage and BAL cell numbers were similarly increased, underscoring the broader involvement of the mPGES‑1/PGE₂ axis.

Selective inhibition of mPGES‑1 with BI1029539 substantially ameliorates LPS‑induced ALI, achieving reductions in vascular leakage, neutrophil infiltration, inflammatory cytokines and histopathologic injury comparable to COX‑2 blockade. These results identify mPGES‑1 as a promising therapeutic target for acute lung injury, with the added advantage of preserving PGI₂ synthesis.
