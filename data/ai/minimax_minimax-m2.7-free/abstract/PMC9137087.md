AP4 (TFAP4) is a transcription factor whose role in colorectal cancer (CRC) cells and the DNA damage response is not well defined. Using CRISPR/Cas9 we deleted AP4 in DLD‑1 and SW480 CRC cells, obtaining knockout clones that lacked AP4 protein. Loss of AP4 caused a pronounced senescent phenotype, as shown by increased SA‑β‑gal positivity and enlarged, flattened morphology, which was rescued by ectopic AP4 expression. AP4‑deficient cells also exhibited higher rates of apoptosis, reduced colony formation, lower metabolic activity (MTT) and slower proliferation, again reversible upon AP4 restoration. Moreover, untreated AP4‑deficient DLD‑1 cells displayed elevated endogenous DNA damage, evidenced by more γH2AX‑positive foci, increased γH2AX protein levels, and a longer comet assay tail, indicating accumulation of unrepaired DNA. Ectopic AP4 reversed these DNA‑damage markers. Collectively, these results demonstrate that AP4 suppresses senescence and limits DNA damage in CRC cells, and that its depletion leads to increased apoptosis and reduced viability, suggesting a tumor‑suppressive function for AP4 in colorectal cancer.
