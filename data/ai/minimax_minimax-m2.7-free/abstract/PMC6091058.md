Colorectal cancer (CRC) progression is shaped by the tumor microenvironment, where cancer‑associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and normal fibroblasts (NFs) communicate partly through exosomal non‑coding RNAs (ncRNAs). In this study, we isolated exosomes from paired NF and CAF cultures derived from nine CRC patients, pooled the RNA from three independent replicates to generate NF‑EXO, CAF‑EXO, NF‑CELL and CAF‑CELL samples, and performed next‑generation sequencing to profile 1,668 long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and 1,761 small non‑coding RNAs (sncRNAs) spanning 16 biotypes. Unsupervised clustering revealed that the cellular fractions (NF‑CELL and CAF‑CELL) are more similar to each other than to the exosomal fractions, indicating distinct RNA repertoires between compartments. Within the cellular compartment, sense intronic RNAs, lincRNAs and miRNAs were more abundant, whereas YRNAs and piRNAs were enriched in exosomes. Notably, snoRNAs were significantly higher in the cell fraction, while YRNAs showed a reciprocal enrichment in exosomes, consistent with previous reports of YRNA‑containing extracellular vesicles from colon cancer cells. These findings demonstrate selective packaging of specific ncRNA biotypes into fibroblast‑derived exosomes and highlight a potential role for encapsulated YRNAs in CRC intercellular communication, warranting further functional investigation.
