In this study we investigated the expression, clinical relevance and functional impact of the androgen receptor (AR) and prostate‑derived Ets factor (PDEF) in estrogen‑receptor (ER)‑negative breast cancer (BC). Immunohistochemical analysis of 100 ER‑negative BC specimens showed that PDEF exhibited a predominantly nuclear staining pattern and was positive in 60 % of cases, whereas AR was positive in 69 %; co‑expression (AR+PDEF+) was observed in 55 % of tumors. PDEF positivity was significantly associated with higher tumor grade (p = 0.032), advanced pTNM stage (p = 0.011), presence of lymphatic metastasis (p < 0.001) and AR expression (p < 0.001). Patients with the AR+PDEF+ phenotype showed more advanced pTNM stage (p = 0.047) and higher HER2 expression (p = 0.039). Western blotting confirmed that PDEF protein levels were elevated in tumor tissue relative to adjacent normal breast tissue. Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that high PDEF expression predicted poorer overall survival (OS, p = 0.040) and recurrence‑free survival (RFS, p = 0.031), and the AR+PDEF+ status further stratified patients toward worse OS (p = 0.043) and RFS (p = 0.027). Functional experiments demonstrated that the AR–PDEF signaling axis enhances tumor cell proliferation and up‑regulates MYC‑mediated transcription by promoting degradation of the MYC inhibitor MAD1. Collectively, these findings indicate that AR and PDEF co‑expression defines an aggressive subtype of ER‑negative BC and that the AR–PDEF pathway contributes to tumor progression through activation of MYC signaling via MAD1 down‑regulation, suggesting potential therapeutic targets for this lethal disease.
