During February–May 2020 we analyzed 40,730 nasopharyngeal swabs from the Brescia region for SARS‑CoV‑2 RNA; 11,344 (28.8 %) were positive, with the highest positivity rate (62 % of 13,547 samples) in March, reflecting the pre‑lock‑down surge, and a marked decline to 4.8 % (611 of 12,705) by May. Quantification of viral load by real‑time PCR targeting the E, RdRP and N genes showed that median Ct values in 100 randomly selected March samples were significantly lower (E = 23.3, RdRP = 24.6, N = 25.3) than those in 100 May samples (E = 32.1, RdRP = 34.0, N = 34.3), confirming higher viral burdens during the epidemic peak. Notably, one specimen (GZ69) collected on 8 May from an asymptomatic healthcare worker displayed exceptionally low Ct values (E = 11.6, RdRP = 13.9, N = 15.5) that fell within the lower quartile of early‑phase samples, indicating a high viral load despite the absence of symptoms and the late stage of the outbreak. This pattern suggests that GZ69 may represent a persistently replicating SARS‑CoV‑2 variant shed by an asymptomatic individual, underscoring the potential for substantial viral replication in the absence of clinical signs and the importance of systematic screening in controlling transmission.
