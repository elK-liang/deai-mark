Despite the pressing need for effective therapies in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), chemotherapy resistance remains a major obstacle and the role of ferroptosis in this setting is only beginning to be defined. Here we performed genome‑wide CRISPR loss‑of‑function screens in multiple HCC cell lines treated with six targeted inhibitors—including the CDK4/6 inhibitor abemaciclib and the ferroptosis inducers sorafenib and erastin—to systematically identify genes that modulate drug sensitivity. Normalized Z‑scores calculated with DrugZ distinguished resisters (negative Z) from sensitizers (positive Z), and circus and Sankey plots visualized the shared resistance and sensitization networks across cell lines and treatments. The analysis revealed markedly higher connectivity among resistance genes than among sensitizers, and KEGG pathway enrichment highlighted the selenocompound metabolism, folate biosynthesis and peroxisome pathways as key mediators of chemotherapy resistance. Subgroup analyses by inhibitor class showed that the peroxisome pathway was enriched in CDK4/6‑inhibitor‑treated cells, whereas selenocompound metabolism was specifically associated with sorafenib‑ and erastin‑treated cells. Among the top resistance genes linked to selenocompound metabolism, phosphoseryl‑tRNA kinase (PSTK)—the enzyme that converts seryl‑tRNA to O‑phosphoseryl‑tRNA during selenocysteine biosynthesis—was identified. Consistent with a protective role, PSTK emerged as a regulator of chemotherapy‑induced ferroptosis, as its depletion (negative normalized Z‑score) sensitized HCC cells to ferroptotic cell death. These findings position PSTK as a novel node in the selenocompound metabolic network that safeguards HCC cells from ferroptosis and suggest that targeting PSTK may improve the efficacy of ferroptosis‑based chemotherapy in HCC.
