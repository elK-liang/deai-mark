Abstract  
Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disorder driven by multiple risk genes. While protein‑protein interaction (PPI) networks have highlighted several MG susceptibility genes, the contribution of long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) in disease pathogenesis remains unclear. This study aimed to delineate the modular architecture of MG risk genes and to identify lncRNAs that act as regulators within a ceRNA framework.  

A PPI network was constructed using known MG risk genes (250 genes, 4 211 edges). Module detection identified nine sub‑networks (ranging from 3 to 54 genes). Functional enrichment (GO and KEGG) was performed for each module (p < 0.05). To capture lncRNA‑mediated regulation, a lncRNA‑mediated module‑associated ceRNA (LMMAC) network was built by integrating lncRNA‑miRNA and miRNA‑gene interactions. The random‑walk‑with‑restart (RWR) algorithm was applied to prioritize candidate lncRNAs.  

The PPI network revealed modular organization, with the nine modules enriched for immune‑related GO terms (e.g., T‑cell differentiation, T‑cell activation, interleukin‑12 production, positive regulation of cytokine production) and KEGG pathways (Th17 cell differentiation, T‑cell receptor signaling pathway, natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity). The LMMAC network comprised 25 genes, 50 miRNAs, 64 lncRNAs and 228 edges. Among the lncRNAs, OIP5‑AS1 displayed the highest number of lncRNA‑miRNA and miRNA‑gene interaction pairs, indicating a central position. RWR analysis prioritized lncRNA HCG18 as a top candidate regulator, suggesting its potential role in MG.  

Integrated network analysis delineates the modular landscape of MG risk genes and uncovers a ceRNA network centered on immune pathways. The identification of key lncRNAs such as OIP5‑AS1 and HCG18 provides new insights into post‑transcriptional regulation of MG pathogenesis and highlights potential therapeutic targets.
