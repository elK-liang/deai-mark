Abstract  
Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are emerging as important regulators in cancer biology, yet their expression landscape and functional relevance in colon cancer (CC) remain largely unexplored. This study aimed to characterize dysregulated circRNAs in CC and to validate the most up‑regulated candidate, circPPP1R12A, as a potential driver of tumor pathogenesis.  
circRNA microarray profiling was performed on paired tumor and adjacent non‑tumor tissues from 10 patients to identify differentially expressed circRNAs (cut‑off: P < 0.05, fold change > 1.5). The top candidate, hsa_circ_0000423 (circPPP1R12A), was further validated by quantitative real‑time PCR (qRT‑PCR) in an independent cohort of 20 patients and in a panel of nine CC cell lines (HT‑29, HCT‑116, SW480, SW620, LoVo, SW48, DLD‑1, Caco2, HCT‑15) relative to the normal colon epithelial cell line NCM460. The circular nature of circPPP1R12A was confirmed using divergent versus convergent primer sets and by assessing its resistance to RNase R degradation.  
The microarray analysis revealed 126 dysregulated circRNAs, with 110 up‑regulated and 16 down‑regulated in CC tissues. circPPP1R12A, derived from exons 24/25 of the PPP1R12A gene at 12q21.2, exhibited the highest up‑regulation. Validation by qRT‑PCR confirmed a consistent and significant increase of circPPP1R12A in tumor versus matched normal tissues (P < 0.001) and markedly elevated expression in all CC cell lines compared with NCM460 cells, with the highest levels in HCT‑116 and LoVo cells. In contrast, the linear PPP1R12A transcript remained unchanged between tumor and normal tissues. The circular structure of circPPP1R12A was verified by specific amplification patterns and by its resistance to RNase R treatment.  
circPPP1R12A is a novel, highly up‑regulated circRNA in colon cancer that is distinct from its linear counterpart. Its tumor‑associated over‑expression and structural features suggest a potential role in CC pathogenesis, warranting further functional studies to elucidate its mechanistic contributions and downstream pathways.
