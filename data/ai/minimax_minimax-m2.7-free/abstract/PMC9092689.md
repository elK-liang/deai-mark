Abstract  
Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) frequently metastasizes through ascites, yet the molecular drivers of this peritoneal spread remain poorly defined. Epithelial‑mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a key process enabling cancer cells to acquire migratory and invasive properties.  
We performed transcriptome profiling of 22 samples collected from 9 EOC patients: 9 primary tumor lesions (T), 4 ascites (ASC), and 9 matched metastatic lesions (M). Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to visualize global gene‑expression patterns. EMT phenotypes were assessed by single‑sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) with curated epithelial (n = 31) and mesenchymal (n = 54) gene sets. Differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) among the three groups were identified using the DESeq2 package.  
PCA clearly separated ascites from the solid tumor samples (T and M), which largely overlapped. ssGSEA revealed that ASC exhibited significantly lower enrichment of epithelial markers compared with T and M, and higher enrichment of mesenchymal markers relative to M, whereas T and M shared similar EMT gene‑set scores. Heatmaps of EMT‑related genes confirmed a distinct pattern in ASC, characterized by down‑regulation of epithelial genes and up‑regulation of mesenchymal genes. DESeq2 analysis identified 72 DECs in the T‑ASC comparison (5 up‑regulated, 67 down‑regulated) and 71 DECs in the ASC‑M comparison (57 up‑regulated, 14 down‑regulated). Among these, hsa_circ_0000497 and hsa_circ_0000918 displayed consistent deregulation, suggesting a potential link between ascitic dissemination and peritoneal metastasis.  
Ovarian cancer cells undergo EMT during ascites‑mediated metastasis, and specific circRNAs—including hsa_circ_0000497 and hsa_circ_0000918—are differentially expressed in this context. These findings provide insight into the molecular landscape of ascitic spread and highlight candidate circRNAs that may contribute to peritoneal metastasis of EOC.
