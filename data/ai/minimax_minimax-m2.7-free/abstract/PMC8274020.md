Abstract  
Background  
The advent of immunotherapy has reshaped the treatment landscape for recurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (R/M HNSCC). Nevertheless, many patients are frail and cannot tolerate the standard EXTREME regimen (platinum, 5‑fluorouracil, cetuximab). We retrospectively evaluated a weekly palliative chemotherapy (PCC) schedule as first‑line treatment for such patients.  

Methods  
We identified 124 patients with locally incurable R/M HNSCC who received first‑line platinum‑based chemotherapy combined with cetuximab. Two treatment strategies were compared: the standard EXTREME schedule (n = 103) and a weekly PCC regimen (n = 21). All patients in the PCC group had ECOG performance status (PS) 2, whereas EXTREME patients were PS 0–1. Endpoints included progression‑free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), response rate, and toxicity. Univariate analyses were performed to assess the influence of clinical variables on outcomes.  

Results  
Baseline characteristics (age, sex, smoking, alcohol use) were balanced between groups, with only PS differing (all PCC patients PS 2). In the weekly PCC group, toxicities were mild: 52 % had grade 1 anemia, 40 % grade 2 neutropenia, and 49 % grade 1 gastrointestinal events; no grade 3–4 toxicities were observed. Median PFS for the entire cohort was 4 months (EXTREME = 4 months, PCC = 5 months; p = 0.275). Median OS was 12 months in both groups (p = 0.400). Overall response rates were 35 % (EXTREME) and 34 % (PCC); no patient achieved a complete response. Univariate analysis found no clinical feature—including treatment regimen—to be significantly associated with PFS or OS.  

Conclusions  
Weekly PCC showed comparable PFS, OS, and response rates to the standard EXTREME regimen in frail patients with R/M HNSCC, with a favorable toxicity profile. This regimen may represent a feasible palliative option for patients unable to receive the intensive standard treatment.
