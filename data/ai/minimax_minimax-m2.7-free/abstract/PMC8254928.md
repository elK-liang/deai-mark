Abstract  
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression is variable, and accurate risk stratification is essential for clinical decision‑making. We aimed to develop an easy‑to‑operate web‑based calculator that predicts CKD progression based on a parsimonious prognostic model.  

This retrospective cohort study used data from CKD patients who were screened for inclusion. After excluding 93 individuals with missing data, 1,045 participants remained. Patients were randomly divided at a 6:3 ratio into a training set (N = 696) and an internal validation set (N = 349). Univariate Cox regression identified variables associated with CKD progression (defined as >50 % eGFR loss or dialysis initiation). Multivariate Cox regression, followed by Akaike information criterion selection, retained five independent prognostic factors (etiology, hemoglobin, creatinine, proteinuria, and urinary protein‑to‑creatinine ratio). A nomogram incorporating these factors was constructed, and risk scores were calculated for each patient. Participants were stratified into high‑ and low‑risk groups based on the median risk score. Kaplan–Meier analysis assessed survival differences between groups. A free web application (https://ncutool.shinyapps.io/CKDprogression/) was developed to provide individualized progression probabilities.  

In the whole cohort, 69.9 % were male, the mean age was 67.3 ± 13.6 years, and 260 patients (24.9 %) experienced disease progression. The five‑factor model identified etiology, hemoglobin, serum creatinine, proteinuria, and UPCR as independent predictors. Risk score distribution clearly separated patients into high‑ (N = 348) and low‑risk (N = 348) groups, and the Kaplan–Meier curves demonstrated a significant difference in CKD progression (log‑rank p < 0.001). The model showed good predictive performance in the training set, providing a visual nomogram and a user‑friendly web calculator that yields estimated progression probabilities for individual patients.  

A five‑factor nomogram reliably stratifies CKD patients according to their risk of disease progression. The accompanying web‑based calculator enables clinicians to obtain rapid, individualized risk estimates at the bedside, facilitating timely intervention and personalized management. External validation in independent cohorts is warranted to confirm generalizability.
