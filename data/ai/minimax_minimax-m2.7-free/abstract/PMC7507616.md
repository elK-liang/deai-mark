Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) has a poor prognosis, and identification of reliable prognostic biomarkers is urgently needed. In this study we developed and validated a cancer stem cell (CSC)–related gene signature for predicting overall survival (OS) in PDAC. By interrogating public transcriptomic datasets with stringent criteria (adjusted P < 0.01 and |log2FC| > 2), we identified 334 CSC‑associated genes. Univariate Cox regression classified them as protective (hazard ratio < 1) or risky (hazard ratio > 1), and we further removed genes whose expression in CSCs conflicted with their prognostic direction. Cross‑dataset intersection (Venn diagram) identified four genes—DCBLD2, GSDMD, PLOD2, and PMAIP1—that consistently predicted unfavorable outcome in three independent cohorts; all four were highly expressed both in CSCs and in PDAC tumor tissue compared with normal pancreas. A risk score was computed as 0.31113 × DCBLD2 + 0.293903 × GSDMD + 0.514119 × PLOD2 + 0.19192 × PMAIP1. Application of this score to datasets containing both tumor and normal samples revealed significantly higher risk scores in PDAC tissues relative to adjacent normal tissue and other pancreatic cancer subtypes. Validation in six independent PDAC cohorts demonstrated that patients with high risk scores had markedly shorter OS than those with low risk scores, and OS decreased progressively across low, moderate, and high risk categories. Calibration analysis showed that the signature accurately predicted 1‑, 2‑, and 3‑year survival probabilities, indicating good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes. These results demonstrate that the CSC‑related four‑gene signature reliably risk‑stratifies PDAC patients and may serve as a practical prognostic tool for clinical decision‑making.
