Colorectal cancer (CRC) cells often survive chemotherapy under the low‑oxygen microenvironment that characterizes solid tumors, but the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. In this study we established an in vitro model of chronic hypoxia (1 % O₂ for 72 h) using the CRC lines HCT116 and SW480 and treated them with clinically relevant concentrations of 5‑fluorouracil (5‑FU). Hypoxic exposure was validated by a robust increase in carbonic anhydrase 9 (CA9), a canonical HIF‑target protein, confirming activation of the hypoxic response. Cell viability assays (MTT) revealed that 5‑FU dramatically reduced the survival of both cell lines under normoxic conditions, whereas the same drug concentrations had minimal effect on cells maintained in hypoxia, demonstrating a hypoxia‑induced resistance to 5‑FU. Quantitative RT‑PCR analysis demonstrated that miR‑675‑5p expression was significantly up‑regulated after 72 h of hypoxic stress in both HCT116 and SW480 cells relative to normoxic controls. Together, these results indicate that chronic hypoxia protects CRC cells from 5‑FU‑induced cytotoxicity and that the hypoxia‑responsive microRNA miR‑675‑5p is co‑up‑regulated under these conditions. Our findings suggest that miR‑675‑5p may contribute to the acquisition of hypoxia‑driven drug resistance and highlight the need for further investigation into its role and downstream targets in colorectal cancer therapy.
