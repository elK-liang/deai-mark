Abstract

Background  
FBXO43 has been reported to be associated with poor survival and metastasis in breast cancer (BC). This study aimed to further clarify the functional role of FBXO43 in BC cells and to evaluate the effects of its targeted knockdown on tumor growth and related phenotypes.

Methods  
FBXO43 expression in BC tissues and normal adjacent tissues was assessed using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset. Its mRNA levels in human BC cell lines (MDA‑MB‑231, MCF‑7, T‑47D) were determined by RT‑qPCR. MDA‑MB‑231 cells were transduced with lentiviruses encoding FBXO43‑specific shRNA or a scrambled control shRNA. Knockdown efficiency was verified by western blot and RT‑qPCR. Cell proliferation was measured by cell counting and MTT assay, clonogenic potential by colony formation assay, apoptosis by flow cytometry, and migration/invasion capabilities by wound‑healing and Transwell assays.

Results  
FBXO43 was significantly overexpressed in BC tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues and was also elevated in all tested BC cell lines. shRNA‑mediated silencing efficiently reduced FBXO43 mRNA and protein levels. Functional assays revealed that FBXO43 knockdown (i) inhibited cell growth and reduced viability, (ii) decreased colony‑forming ability, (iii) increased the proportion of apoptotic cells, and (iv) impaired both migration and invasion of MDA‑MB‑231 cells.

Conclusions  
Silencing FBXO43 suppresses proliferation, viability, and metastatic potential while promoting apoptosis in breast cancer cells, indicating that FBXO43 may serve as a therapeutic target for BC. Subsequent studies will investigate whether these phenotypic changes are mediated through modulation of FBXO43’s interaction with PCNA.
