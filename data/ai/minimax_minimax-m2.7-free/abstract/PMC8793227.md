Abstract  
Psychiatric comorbidity (PC) is common in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and may share genetic contributors with IBD itself. We examined the polygenic architecture of PC in IBD patients of European ancestry.  
We conducted a genome‑wide association study (GWAS) of PC status adjusted for gender, age, marital status, disease subtype and the top five multidimensional scaling (MDS) components. Risk SNPs were selected at three P‑value thresholds (5 × 10⁻², 5 × 10⁻³, 5 × 10⁻⁴) and subjected to LD pruning. The remaining SNPs were matched to an external IBD GWAS. Polygenic risk scores (PRS) derived from the IBD GWAS were compared between PC and non‑PC groups. Functional enrichment of genes nearest the retained SNPs was assessed using Gene Ontology (GO) analysis.  
Compared with non‑PC patients, those with PC had a lower proportion of males (35 % vs 49 %; p = 0.054) and a higher proportion aged 17–40 years (64 % vs 58 %). No SNP reached the genome‑wide significance threshold (5 × 10⁻⁸); 37 SNPs showed suggestive association (p < 5 × 10⁻⁵) across multiple chromosomes, indicating polygenicity. After pruning, 21,748, 1,104, and 72 SNPs remained at the three thresholds; 9,620, 471, and 33 of these overlapped with the IBD GWAS, respectively. All individuals carrying the 33 overlapping SNPs possessed at least one risk allele. GO enrichment (adjusted p = 0.11) highlighted pyrimidine ribonucleotide metabolic process, toll‑like receptor 2 signaling pathway and protein localization to axon. PRS built from the IBD GWAS were modestly higher in patients with PC than in those without at each threshold.  
PC in IBD exhibits a polygenic signal with modest genetic overlap with IBD risk loci and suggestive enrichment for pathways involved in nucleotide metabolism and innate immunity. Larger cohorts are required to pinpoint specific susceptibility loci.
