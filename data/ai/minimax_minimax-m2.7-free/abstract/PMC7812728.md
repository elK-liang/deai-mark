Abstract  
Non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) displays intratumor heterogeneity for EGFR mutation status, influencing response to EGFR‑targeted therapy. Yet, how wild‑type EGFR (wtEGFR)‑expressing tumor cells affect the sensitivity of mutant EGFR (mutEGFR) cells to the third‑generation TKI osimertinib remains poorly understood.  
NSCLC lines harboring wtEGFR (H460, A549, H1299) or mutEGFR (H1975, PC9) were used. EGFR mutation status was confirmed by Sanger sequencing and baseline osimertinib sensitivity by MTT assay. Co‑culture experiments were performed with H1299‑GFP and H1975‑RFP cells (1:1 ratio) in the presence of osimertinib. Contact‑independent communication was examined using transwell inserts (0.4 µm pores) that separated wtEGFR cells from H1975 cells. Conditioned medium (CM) collected from wtEGFR cell cultures was applied to H1975 cells prior to osimertinib treatment; viability (MTT, colony formation) and apoptosis were assessed.  
Direct co‑culture with H1299 cells significantly enhanced H1975 cell survival under osimertinib. Similarly, transwell co‑culture with H460, A549, or H1299 cells markedly increased the IC₅₀ of osimertinib for H1975 cells. Exposure to CM from wtEGFR cells attenuated osimertinib‑induced inhibition of proliferation and reduced apoptosis in H1975 cells, indicating that a soluble, transferable factor from wtEGFR cells promotes resistance.  
These findings demonstrate that wtEGFR‑expressing NSCLC cells secrete a factor—likely packaged in exosomes—that can be transferred to mutEGFR cells, thereby conferring resistance to osimertinib. This intercellular transfer represents a novel mechanism of heterogeneity‑driven EGFR‑TKI resistance and may have implications for therapeutic strategies in NSCLC.
