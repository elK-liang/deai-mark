Glioblastoma (GBM) is a highly aggressive brain tumor with limited therapeutic options, and overexpression of the kinase IKBKE has been linked to its malignancy. In this study we evaluated the effects of CYT387, a potent IKBKE inhibitor, on U87‑MG and LN229 cells, which both exhibit high IKBKE expression, using a series of in vitro assays. A concentration‑response analysis by CCK‑8 showed that the half‑maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) declined with longer drug exposure, from ~25 µM at 24 h to ~5–6 µM at 72 h for both cell lines. A five‑day viability assay performed at 5 µM revealed a dramatic reduction in cell proliferation compared with solvent controls, and a 12‑day colony‑formation assay at 6 µM demonstrated a marked decrease in both colony number and size (P < 0.001). Wound‑healing experiments conducted at the same concentration showed a significant retardation of cell migration relative to controls. Together, these results indicate that CYT387 effectively suppresses glioblastoma cell proliferation and migration in vitro, an effect consistent with activation of the Hippo tumor‑suppressive pathway, and they support further exploration of IKBKE targeting as a potential therapeutic strategy for glioblastoma.
