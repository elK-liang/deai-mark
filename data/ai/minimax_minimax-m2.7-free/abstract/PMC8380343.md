Abstract  
Angiogenesis is a critical driver of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) progression, yet the specific regulators that influence prognosis and tumor behavior remain incompletely characterized.  
We compiled 154 known angiogenesis‑related genes and examined their mRNA expression in the TCGA‑LUAD cohort. Differential expression analysis (|log₂FC| > 1, adjusted P < 0.05) identified 66 differentially expressed angiogenic genes (28 up‑regulated, 38 down‑regulated). Functional enrichment (KEGG and GO via Metascape) highlighted pathways such as angiogenesis, endothelial development, PI3K‑Akt signaling, and cytokine secretion. Univariate Cox proportional‑hazards regression was used to assess the prognostic value of each gene; multivariate analysis then selected independent predictors of overall survival (OS). The functional roles of the two selected genes (COL5A2 and EPHB2) were explored by siRNA‑mediated knockdown in A549 and H1299 LUAD cells, followed by western blotting, CCK‑8, EdU, and Transwell migration assays.  
The 66‑gene signature was enriched in processes linked to blood vessel formation and immune‑related signaling. Multivariate Cox regression identified COL5A2 and EPHB2 as independent adverse prognostic factors for OS in LUAD patients. Knockdown of either gene markedly reduced COL5A2 and EPHB2 protein levels, and both CCK‑8 and EdU assays demonstrated significant inhibition of cell proliferation. Transwell migration assays confirmed that silencing COL5A2 or EPHB2 substantially impaired the migratory capacity of A549 and H1299 cells.  
COL5A2 and EPHB2 are up‑regulated angiogenic genes that independently predict poorer overall survival in LUAD. Experimental suppression of these genes attenuates proliferation and migration of LUAD cells, indicating their potential contributions to tumor progression and suggesting them as candidate therapeutic targets for LUAD.
