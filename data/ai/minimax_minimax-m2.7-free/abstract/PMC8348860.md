Abstract  
Glioma is a heterogeneous central nervous system neoplasm with limited reliable prognostic biomarkers. The BCL7 gene family (BCL7A, BCL7B, BCL7C) has not been comprehensively characterized in glioma. This study investigates the expression patterns of BCL7 family members and their association with tumor grade.  
BCL7 expression was examined using multiple public repositories (cBioPortal, Oncomine, Gliovis, GEO) and validated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) on 108 glioma tissue samples. Grade‑specific expression was assessed with the CGGA and TCGA datasets.  
BCL7A mRNA was significantly reduced in glioma versus normal brain tissue in 7 of 8 datasets, and its level decreased progressively with higher tumor grade (WHO I–IV). BCL7C was consistently elevated in glioma in 6 of 8 datasets and was higher in glioblastoma (GBM) than in lower‑grade glioma. BCL7B showed no consistent difference between tumor and normal tissue, although it was enriched in GBM in the CGGA and TCGA data. IHC confirmed lower BCL7A and higher BCL7C protein expression in glioma compared with non‑tumor brain, while BCL7B remained unchanged.  
Distinct expression profiles of BCL7A, BCL7B, and BCL7C correlate with glioma malignancy. The inverse relationship between BCL7A expression and tumor grade suggests that BCL7A may serve as a novel prognostic biomarker for glioma patients.
