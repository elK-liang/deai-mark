Abstract  

Background  
Non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is a heterogeneous disease comprising lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC). Although immune infiltration influences prognosis, a robust immune‑based classifier that integrates molecular typing with histopathology remains lacking.  

Methods  
We applied non‑negative matrix factorization (NMF) to the LM22 immune gene signature from transcriptomic data of a training cohort of NSCLC patients. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to visualize sample distribution. Pseudotime inference (Monocle) assessed the developmental trajectory of immune signatures across subgroups. Immune cell composition was quantified with single‑sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) using individual cell‑type scores.  

Results  
All samples were positioned at the negative end of PC1, indicating consistent baseline transcriptional patterns. PC2 effectively separated LUAD from LUSC cases, mirroring the NMF clustering pattern. NMF identified three robust groups: NMF1 (predominantly LUSC, 92.24 % [333/361]), NMF3 (predominantly LUAD, 96.23 % [357/371]), and NMF2 (mixed histology, 45.39 % LUAD and 54.61 % LUSC). To resolve the mixed NMF2 cluster, we integrated pathological type, subdividing NMF2 into NMF2A (LUSC) and NMF2B (LUAD). Pseudotime analysis placed NMF2A and NMF2B at the developing end of the immune spectrum, reflecting highly active immune compartments, whereas NMF1 exhibited the least active immune profile. NMF3 displayed subtle intra‑group heterogeneity that did not disrupt the overall classification. Comparative immune cell profiling revealed distinct compositions: NMF2 had significantly higher fractions of CD8⁺ T cells, activated CD4⁺ memory T cells, and M1 macrophages; NMF1 was enriched for resting NK cells, M0 macrophages, and activated mast cells; NMF3 showed higher proportions of resting memory CD4⁺ T cells, M2 macrophages, and resting dendritic cells.  

Conclusions  
Our multi‑omics approach delineates four biologically distinct NSCLC subgroups with unique immune landscapes. These findings provide a foundation for an immune‑based prognostic predictor that integrates molecular typing and histopathology, potentially guiding personalized therapeutic strategies.
