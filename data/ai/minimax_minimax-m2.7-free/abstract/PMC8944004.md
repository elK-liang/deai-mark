Bladder cancer (BLCA) frequently harbors alterations in DNA damage response (DDR) genes, and we examined the prevalence of mutations in key DDR pathways using TCGA datasets. Mutations in ATM, ERCC2, BRCA2, ATR and TP53 were found in a substantial proportion of BLCA tumors and were associated with higher mortality, confirming that roughly a third of BLCA cases carry DDR defects. These observations provided the rationale to evaluate the activity of five clinically relevant PARP inhibitors (olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib, talazoparib, veliparib) and cisplatin in BLCA cells. Dose‑response experiments in UM‑UC‑3 cells revealed IC₅₀ values of 8.2 µM for olaparib, 8.6 µM for niraparib, 15.5 µM for rucaparib, 1.1 µM for talazoparib, 39.4 µM for veliparib and 3.2 µM for cisplatin. At fixed concentrations (5–20 µM for most PARPi, 0.5–2 µM for talazoparib), talazoparib and niraparib reduced viability by >50 % in both UM‑UC‑3 and T‑24 bladder cancer lines and in the normal urothelial line SV‑HUC‑1, whereas veliparib showed minimal cytotoxic effect even at the highest dose. These findings demonstrate that potent PARP inhibitors such as talazoparib and niraparib exert strong single‑agent activity against BLCA cells and provide a mechanistic foundation for future studies exploring whether PARP inhibition can chemopotentiate cisplatin‑based regimens in bladder cancer.
