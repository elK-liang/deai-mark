Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are emerging regulators of tumor biology, yet their roles in gliomas remain incompletely understood. By profiling circRNA expression during early neural differentiation of mouse P19 embryonic carcinoma cells, we identified circ‑MAPK4 (hsa_circ_0047688) as a significantly downregulated transcript on day 4 of induction, suggesting its involvement in the loss of differentiation capacity that characterizes malignant gliomas. Subsequent quantitative PCR analysis revealed that circ‑MAPK4, but not its linear MAPK4 mRNA counterpart, is markedly overexpressed in human glioma tissues compared with normal brain tissue, a finding corroborated by the MiOncoCirc database for glioblastoma multiforme. Notably, elevated circ‑MAPK4 levels were associated with advanced disease stage (III + IV vs. I + II) in an independent validation cohort, underscoring its clinical relevance. Unlike MAPK4 mRNA, circ‑MAPK4 remained largely intact after RNase R digestion, confirming its circular nature. Mechanistic investigations demonstrated that circ‑MAPK4 functions as a molecular sponge for miR‑125a‑3p, thereby relieving inhibition of downstream MAPK signaling components and ultimately suppressing apoptosis of glioma cells. Together, these results identify circ‑MAPK4 as an oncogenic circRNA that promotes glioma progression by sponging miR‑125a‑3p and modulating the MAPK pathway, suggesting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for gliomas.
