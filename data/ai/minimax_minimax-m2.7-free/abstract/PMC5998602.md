Emerging evidence indicates that long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play pivotal roles in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression, but the specific mechanisms linking the oncogenic lncRNA HULC to known tumor suppressors remain unclear. In this study we investigated the expression patterns of HULC, miR‑15a and PTEN in 30 paired HCC and adjacent non‑tumor liver specimens. In situ hybridization and RT‑PCR showed that HULC transcripts were markedly elevated in tumor tissue (P < 0.01), while real‑time RT‑PCR demonstrated a significant reduction of mature miR‑15a (P < 0.01). Correspondingly, immunohistochemistry and western blot analysis revealed a pronounced decrease of PTEN protein in the same HCC samples (P < 0.01). Statistical analysis uncovered strong negative correlations between HULC levels and both miR‑15a and PTEN. To explore the functional consequences of HULC up‑regulation, we established stable Hep3B cell lines over‑expressing HULC and confirmed a robust increase in HULC transcription. Together, these findings demonstrate that HULC is frequently over‑expressed in HCC and is inversely associated with miR‑15a and PTEN, suggesting a novel regulatory axis that may contribute to liver cancer pathogenesis.
