Abstract  
Colorectal carcinoma (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide. Reprogramming of lipid metabolism is a recognized feature of tumors, yet the link between lipid metabolism‑related genes (LMRG) and the tumor immune microenvironment remains unclear. This study aimed to develop a prognostic biomarker based on LMRG that reflects the tumor immune landscape in CRC.  
LMRG expression profiles were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts. Differential expression analysis (FDR < 0.05) identified dysregulated LMRG between normal and tumor tissues. Univariate Cox regression applied to the intersecting genes selected those with prognostic value. Functional enrichment (Gene Ontology and KEGG) was performed on the overlap of dysregulated and prognostic LMRG. A risk signature was constructed using LASSO Cox regression and evaluated with ROC curves, Kaplan‑Meier survival analysis, and principal component analysis (PCA).  
Of 945 LMRG common to TCGA and GEO, 729 were differentially expressed (365 up‑regulated, 364 down‑regulated). Univariate Cox analysis identified 57 prognostic LMRG, 47 of which were also differentially expressed. Enrichment analysis highlighted lipid metabolic pathways such as glycerolipid/glycerophospholipid metabolism, PPAR signaling, and fatty acid degradation/metabolism. LASSO regression selected four genes (PROCA1, CCKBR, CPT2, FDFT1) to build a risk score:  
risk score = 0.03071 × PROCA1 + 0.58956 × CCKBR + 0.00972 × CPT2 + 0.01381 × FDFT1. In the training set, the signature achieved AUCs of 0.690 (1‑year), 0.678 (3‑year) and 0.595 (5‑year). Patients stratified as high‑risk by the median score had significantly shorter overall survival (log‑rank p < 0.001) and a higher proportion of deaths compared with low‑risk patients. PCA based on the four‑gene signature separated the risk groups, whereas PCA using all genes did not.  
A four‑gene lipid metabolism‑related prognostic signature was established that stratifies CRC patients into distinct risk groups with significantly different survival outcomes. This signature may serve as a biomarker for risk assessment and provide insight into the interplay between lipid metabolism and the tumor immune microenvironment in colorectal carcinoma.
