Abstract

Background  
Patients with advanced and metastatic pancreatic cancer have a poor prognosis. We aimed to develop and validate two nomograms for individualized prediction of overall survival (OS) using baseline clinical variables and early chemotherapy response.

Methods  
We performed a retrospective analysis of 323 patients with advanced or metastatic pancreatic cancer. Two Cox proportional‑hazards models were constructed: a baseline model based on pretreatment variables and a chemotherapy response‑based model that added tumor response after the second treatment cycle. Candidate variables were identified by univariate survival analysis and selected via stepwise AIC backward regression. Model performance was assessed by concordance index (C‑index) and calibration in the training set and an independent validation cohort.

Results  
The median OS for the cohort was 10.6 months (95 % CI 9.7–11.7), with 1‑year and 2‑year survival rates of 42.1 % and 12.2 %, respectively. The most common first‑line regimen was nab‑paclitaxel + S1 (57.5 %); 23.6 % of patients showed progressive disease after the second cycle.  
- In the baseline model, six independent predictors were retained: gender, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS), baseline total bilirubin (TB), baseline neutrophil count (N), baseline white blood cell count (WBC), and baseline CA19‑9.  
- The chemotherapy response‑based model incorporated 16 variables, including progressive disease after the second cycle.  
Patients receiving nab‑paclitaxel + S1 had a longer median OS than those on gemcitabine monotherapy (9.92 vs 6.18 months, p = 0.004), and gemcitabine‑based combination therapy also conferred superior survival compared with gemcitabine alone (11.04 vs 6.18 months, p = 0.019). Both nomograms demonstrated favorable discrimination and calibration in the training cohort and were confirmed in the validation cohort.

Conclusions  
We developed and validated two nomograms that accurately predict OS for patients with advanced and metastatic pancreatic cancer, integrating readily available baseline parameters and early chemotherapy response. These tools may assist clinicians in prognostic stratification and treatment planning.
