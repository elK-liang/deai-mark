Abstract  

Background  
Heteroclitic peptides—engineered analogs of tumor‑associated epitopes—can acquire enhanced binding to major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC‑I) molecules while preserving T‑cell receptor recognition. We investigated whether systematic single‑amino‑acid substitutions at the TCR‑facing position 4 (p4) of the weak H‑2Db‑binding epitopes Trp2 (melanoma) and HPV‑E7 (cervical cancer) could improve their MHC‑binding affinity without altering the anchor residues.

Methods  
For each 9‑mer wild‑type (wt) peptide, 171 single‑substitution variants (9 positions × 19 alternative amino acids) were generated. Binding to H‑2Db was predicted for all variants using NetMHCpan 4.0. Variants that scored higher than the wt were filtered, and those bearing a substitution at p4 were selected for experimental validation. Binding affinities were measured in vitro, and structural consequences of the p4 changes were assessed by homology modeling and molecular docking against the H‑2Db crystal structure (PDB 1F2).

Results  
NetMHCpan predicted 47 Trp2 and 45 HPV‑E7 heteroclitic peptides with improved H‑2Db binding. Experimental validation confirmed that p4 modifications dramatically affect binding despite being outside the MHC‑binding groove. For Trp2 (wt = 6990 nM), the best p4 substitution (Asp→Gln) reduced the IC₅₀ to 735 nM, a 9.5‑fold improvement, although it remained a weak binder. For HPV‑E7, all p4 replacements of the polar Tyr with non‑polar Val or polar Gln increased binding; only the non‑polar Trp substitution did not yield a significant gain. Structural models revealed that the Tyr→Val/Gln changes remodel the p4 side‑chain orientation without disrupting the critical Asn5 anchor shared with the reference peptide, thereby stabilizing the peptide‑MHC complex.

Conclusions  
Targeted substitution at the TCR‑facing p4 position can substantially enhance H‑2Db binding of otherwise weak epitopes. The polar, uncharged Glutamine proved particularly effective when replacing either a negatively charged Asp in Trp2 or the polar Tyr in HPV‑E7. These findings provide a rational strategy for engineering heteroclitic peptide vaccines that combine improved MHC presentation with preserved T‑cell recognition.
