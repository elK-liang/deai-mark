Using linked data from the Taiwan Biobank and the National Health Insurance Research Database for 19,500 participants, we identified 890 ischemic stroke cases, 70 intracerebral hemorrhagic stroke cases, and 16,837 controls after data cleaning. Ischemic stroke patients were older (mean ± SD 58.47 ± 8.17 vs. 48.33 ± 10.90 years, p < 0.0001) and had lower educational attainment than controls, whereas hemorrhagic stroke cases showed age comparable to controls (50.54 ± 10.60 vs. 48.33 ± 10.90 years, p = 0.09). The prevalence of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, alcohol intake, and diabetes differed significantly between the stroke groups and the control group (p < 0.05). The ADH1B rs1229984 genotype distribution (TT, TC, CC) was similar between ischemic stroke cases and controls, and no interaction with alcohol consumption was observed for ischemic risk. In contrast, carriers of the rs1229984 TT and TC genotypes were more frequent among hemorrhagic stroke cases and showed significant associations with diabetes, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia; after adjusting for conventional risk factors, the variant remained independently associated with an increased risk of intracerebral hemorrhagic stroke (p < 0.05). These results indicate that the ADH1B rs1229984 polymorphism contributes to susceptibility to intracerebral hemorrhagic stroke, potentially through its link with metabolic risk factors, rather than via a direct effect of alcohol consumption on ischemic stroke.
