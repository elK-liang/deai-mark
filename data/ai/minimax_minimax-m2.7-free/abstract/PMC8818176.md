In this study we characterized the sputum microbiome of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) across different clinical stages and of healthy controls using 16S rRNA gene sequencing, and explored its relationship with clinical indices. Seventy‑six sputum samples were collected from 10 healthy individuals, 28 patients with acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD), 15 patients during recovery, and 23 patients with stable COPD. Baseline demographic and most clinical parameters were similar among groups, while lung function (FEV₁% predicted and FEV₁/FVC) was significantly lower in all COPD groups compared with healthy controls (p < 0.001) and the AECOPD group had higher dyspnea scores on the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) scale than stable patients (p = 0.023). Routine hematology, inflammatory biomarkers (procalcitonin and C‑reactive protein), and GOLD classification did not differ significantly across groups. Sequencing of the 16S rRNA gene revealed distinct sputum microbial community compositions among the four cohorts, with certain taxa showing differential abundances that were examined for correlations with clinical parameters. These findings provide insight into the composition of the sputum microbiome in COPD exacerbations and highlight potential links between microbial signatures and disease severity.
