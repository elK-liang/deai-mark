Abstract  
Inflammation is increasingly recognized as a modulator of adipose tissue expansion in obesity. To clarify the role of inflammatory pathways in human fat deposition, we compared the transcriptomic profiles of purified adipocytes (ACs) and preadipocytes (preACs) isolated from lean and obese subcutaneous adipose tissue.  

ACs and preACs were obtained from lean (L) and obese (O) donors, and mRNA‑seq libraries were generated for each cell type. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined using thresholds of P < 0.01 and false‑discovery‑rate Q < 0.05. Four DEG classes were constructed: (i) Class I – AC‑DEGs (L‑AC vs O‑AC), (ii) Class II – preAC‑DEGs (L‑preAC vs O‑preAC), (iii) Class III – Lean_Ag‑DEGs (L‑preAC vs L‑AC) reflecting lean adipogenesis, and (iv) Class IV – Obese_Ag‑DEGs (O‑preAC vs O‑AC) reflecting obese adipogenesis. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering with heatmaps was applied to assess sample separation. The DEG lists were cross‑validated against a publicly available microarray dataset (GEO GSE80654).  

At the selected thresholds, 1,198 AC‑DEGs and 314 preAC‑DEGs were identified. Heatmap clustering of AC samples did not segregate lean from obese donors, indicating that the overall transcriptomic distinction at the mature adipocyte level was limited. In contrast, the Lean_Ag‑ and Obese_Ag‑DEG sets captured distinct gene‑expression changes associated with adipogenesis in each metabolic state. Overlap analysis with the GSE80654 dataset showed partial concordance, supporting the biological relevance of the identified DEGs.  

The observed alterations in inflammatory‑gene expression during obesity‑associated adipogenesis provide transcriptomic evidence for a possible positive contribution of inflammation to human fat deposition. The modest separation of lean and obese ACs suggests that early adipocyte populations share substantial transcriptomic similarity, with divergence more evident at the preadipocyte stage or during the adipogenic transition.
