Using a rapid label‑free phosphoproteomic strategy that combines deep fractionation, high‑resolution LC–MS/MS, and a multi‑blind spectral alignment algorithm (MODa/Byonic) with Gaussian regression to screen delta masses, we identified 4,243 phosphorylation events (Δ mass = 79.9665 ± 0.0002, R² = 0.91) spanning 832 unique phosphorylated sites on 382 proteins in paired tumor and adjacent non‑tumor gastric cancer (GC) tissues. Comparative analysis revealed 25 sites significantly up‑regulated and 52 down‑regulated (fold change > 2, p < 0.05) in GC. Functional enrichment of the up‑regulated phosphosites highlighted strong association with nucleotide, DNA and RNA interactions, DNA damage response (DDR) pathways, DNA replication and cell‑cycle regulation, while the down‑regulated sites were enriched for muscle‑contraction processes, reflecting loss of differentiated functions in cancer. Notably, the most prominently up‑regulated phosphoprotein was BCLAF1, with its Ser290 site showing the highest increase in phosphorylation. Gene set enrichment analysis of differentially expressed proteins further confirmed activation of multiple DDR pathways, including positive/negative regulation of DNA repair, de‑alkylation repair, mitochondrial DNA repair, DNA synthesis and ligation. These findings suggest that heightened BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation in gastric tumors contributes to DDR regulation and may influence cellular radiosensitivity, positioning this modification as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for GC management.
