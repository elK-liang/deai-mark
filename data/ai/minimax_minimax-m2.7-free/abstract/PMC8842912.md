Abstract

The POR rs10954732 (G>A) polymorphism was investigated for its impact on hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) susceptibility, patient prognosis, and related molecular mechanisms. Genotyping of four POR SNPs (rs10954732, rs2286822, rs1135612, rs1057868) showed that only rs10954732 was associated with HCC risk; individuals carrying the GA or AA genotype (A allele) had a 68.8 % reduction in HCC risk compared with GG carriers (OR = 0.312, 95 % CI 0.116–0.838, P = 0.021). Survival analysis revealed that A‑allele carriers experienced longer overall survival (median OS = 437 days) than GG patients (median OS = 220 days, P = 0.015). Serum markers of liver injury (AST and ALT) and cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1) activity were elevated in HCC patients but were significantly lower in A‑allele carriers, suggesting that the protective effect of the A allele may be mediated by reduced CYP2E1 activity. Proteomic profiling of liver tissues from 16 GG and 44 GA + AA genotype carriers identified hepsin as a differentially expressed protein that correlated with immune cell infiltration, indicating a potential role in the tumor microenvironment. Collectively, the rs10954732 A allele emerges as a protective factor for HCC, influencing both risk and outcome, and hepsin warrants further evaluation as a prognostic biomarker linked to immune infiltration.
