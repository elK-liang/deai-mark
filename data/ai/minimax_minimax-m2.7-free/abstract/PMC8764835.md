We performed small‑RNA sequencing of salivary exosomes isolated from a pilot cohort of three esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients and three healthy volunteers, confirmed exosome integrity by transmission electron microscopy, immunoblotting for the exosomal markers Alix, TSG101, CD63 and CD9 together with the absence of the intracellular protein Calnexin, and determined an average particle diameter of 95 nm by nanoparticle tracking analysis. Comparative analysis identified 1366 differentially expressed small non‑coding RNAs (sesncRNAs) (p < 0.05), of which 32 displayed >2‑fold up‑regulation in ESCC; the five most significantly increased were chosen for downstream validation. In an independent prospective cohort of 33 ESCC patients and 33 matched controls from Changhua Show Chwan Hospital (CHSUMC), RT‑qPCR showed that two candidates— the tRNA‑derived fragment tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 and a previously uncharacterized small RNA residing between SSX2IP and LOC102724892, which we named sRESE (small RNA identified in Exosomes from Saliva of ESCC patients)—were significantly overexpressed (p < 0.01). These findings were corroborated in a second validation cohort from an external institution (ATH). Both sesncRNAs were highly abundant in exosomes secreted by ESCC cell lines and in conditioned media compared with immortalized esophageal epithelial cells. Functional assays demonstrated that the two sesncRNAs significantly affected cell migration and invasion. Receiver‑operating‑characteristic (ROC) analysis in the CHSUMC cohort indicated that each RNA alone or in combination achieved high discriminative power between ESCC and control samples. Collectively, these results identify tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 and sRESE as cancer‑enriched salivary exosomal small RNAs that hold promise as non‑invasive biomarkers for ESCC detection and risk stratification.
