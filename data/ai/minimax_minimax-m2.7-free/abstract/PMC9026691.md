Abstract

Background  
Adult T‑cell leukemia/lymphoma (ATLL) is divided into acute, chronic and smoldering subtypes with distinct clinical courses. Accurate molecular discrimination of these subtypes is essential for prognosis and personalized therapy. This study aimed to identify mRNA‑based classifiers for each ATLL subtype using machine‑learning approaches.

Methods  
Differential expression analysis was performed comparing each ATLL subtype (acute, chronic, smoldering) to adjacent control (AC) samples. Unique differentially expressed genes (DEGs) specific to each subtype (521 for ATLL_chronic, 594 for ATLL_acute, 187 for ATLL_smoldering) were used as candidate features. Support‑vector‑machine recursive feature elimination with cross‑validation (SVM‑RFECV) was applied to select the most discriminative gene sets and to train classification models. Model performance was assessed on independent test sets using accuracy, confusion matrices and classification reports. Functional enrichment of the selected gene panels was explored to interpret the underlying biological pathways.

Results  
SVM‑RFECV identified 27, 9 and 32 optimal gene classifiers for ATLL_acute, ATLL_chronic and ATLL_smoldering, respectively. Test‑set classification accuracy was 1.00 for ATLL_acute, and 0.95 for both ATLL_chronic and ATLL_smoldering. Pathway enrichment of the ATLL_acute classifiers revealed involvement in glutathione metabolism, urea cycle and amino‑group metabolism, beta‑alanine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, sulfate activation for sulfonation, CXCR4‑mediated signaling, polyamine metabolism, amino‑acid metabolism, metabolic pathways, cancer pathways, hypoxia‑p53 signaling in the cardiovascular system, interferon signaling, planar cell‑polarity and non‑canonical Wnt pathways, and cell‑cycle regulation by cyclins. For ATLL_chronic, enrichment included tRNA modification in the nucleus and cytosol and TGF‑β signaling; ATLL_smoldering classifiers pointed to additional metabolic and signaling routes (data not shown).

Conclusions  
High‑accuracy mRNA classifiers were obtained for distinguishing ATLL subtypes, especially the acute form. These gene panels provide potential biomarkers for subtype classification and highlight subtype‑specific metabolic and signaling pathways that may drive disease progression. Future work will validate the classifiers in independent cohorts and investigate the contribution of miRNAs to ATLL classification.
