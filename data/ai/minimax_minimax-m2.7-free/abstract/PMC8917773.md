Abstract
The lack of stable circulating tumor cell (CTC) expansion models from non‑metastatic non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has limited mechanistic studies of CTCs and the ability to predict recurrence after curative resection. We generated patient‑derived xenograft (ptPDX) models from primary tumors of non‑metastatic NSCLC patients and explored the tumorigenic potential of ptPDX‑derived CTCs.  
Ten ptPDX models were established by subcutaneous implantation of primary tumor fragments in immunodeficient NOD scid gamma (NSG) mice. CTCs were isolated from ptPDX blood, enriched, and immunostained using standard CTC identification criteria. ptPDX‑derived CTCs were subsequently injected subcutaneously into naïve NSG mice to generate CTC‑derived xenograft (CDX) tumors. Global transcriptomic profiling of ptPDX and CDX tumors was performed by single nuclear RNA‑sequencing (snRNA‑seq), and cell types were identified using differentially expressed genes and canonical cell‑specific markers.  
CTCs from ptPDX phenocopied CTCs isolated from corresponding patients. Stable CDX tumors formed in 2 of 10 ptPDX models (MU150, MU197; 20%), and high numbers of CTC clusters correlated with successful CDX development. Pathology‑reported biomarker expression was conserved in matched ptPDX and CDX tumors. snRNA‑seq identified nine cell‑type clusters across most samples, with ten clusters in MU197 ptPDX; CDX tumors contained two AT2‑like populations versus one in ptPDX, and an additional AT2‑like cluster in CDX was validated by SFTPB expression.  
CTC‑derived xenograft models can be established from non‑metastatic NSCLC, providing a liquid‑biopsy platform to dissect single‑cell heterogeneity, preserve patient‑specific biomarkers, and molecularly profile CTCs. These models offer a valuable tool to investigate CTC‑driven recurrence mechanisms and explore therapeutic vulnerabilities and drug responses after curative resection.
