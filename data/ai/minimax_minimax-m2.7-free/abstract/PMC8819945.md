Abstract

Cisplatin‑based chemotherapy is a mainstay for bladder cancer, but resistance remains a major clinical problem. This study aimed to identify genes whose loss can sensitize bladder cancer cells to cisplatin.

Cytotoxicity of cisplatin was evaluated by CCK‑8 assay in six bladder cancer cell lines (T24, RT4, HT1197, SW780, RT112, HT1376). The most resistant line, T24, was transduced with a GeCKO sgRNA library (123 411 sgRNAs targeting 19 050 genes) and subjected to puromycin selection, followed by chronic low‑dose cisplatin treatment (0.25 µM) for 7 or 14 days. Surviving cells were harvested and enriched sgRNAs identified by high‑throughput sequencing. Enriched genes were functionally annotated (GO) and further filtered to the top 21 down‑regulated genes. Six sgRNAs per gene were designed and their effect on proliferation and cisplatin sensitivity was assessed by high‑content screening (HCS) over 5 days. Sensitivity enhancement was quantified as fold‑change in sensitivity enhancement (FSE).

CCK‑8 assays revealed that T24 and RT4 cells were the most resistant to cisplatin (IC50 ≈ 7.6 µM), whereas HT1376 was the most sensitive. CRISPR screening identified 327 genes significantly altered under cisplatin pressure, with 46 genes changed at day 7 and 284 at day 14. GO analysis highlighted enrichment in tissue development, organonitrogen compound metabolism, ion transport and pathways linked to cancer stem cells. Validation by HCS showed that individual knock‑out of HNRNPU, OCLM, PRKCDBP and CCDC146 markedly reduced T24 cell proliferation and increased cisplatin sensitivity (FSE ≈ 1.4). Among these, sgRNA‑mediated knock‑down of HNRNPU produced the strongest sensitization effect.

Genome‑wide CRISPR screening in T24 bladder cancer cells identified HNRNPU (along with OCLM, PRKCDBP and CCDC146) as a candidate whose loss compromises cisplatin resistance. These findings suggest that targeting HNRNPU may be a viable strategy to overcome cisplatin resistance in bladder cancer.
