Abstract  
The m6A methyltransferase KIAA1429 has been implicated in various cancers, but its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains unclear. This study examined the expression patterns of KIAA1429 in HCC and its association with clinicopathological features and patient outcomes.  
KIAA1429 expression was analyzed in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort (50 paired HCC samples) and in a validation cohort from West China Hospital (WCH) comprising 70 paired HCC tissues using quantitative PCR, immunohistochemistry, and western blot. Diagnostic performance was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Correlations between KIAA1429 levels and clinical parameters were assessed, and Kaplan‑Meier analysis was performed to evaluate overall and disease‑free survival. In vitro, KIAA1429 expression was measured in seven hepatoma cell lines, and functional effects of KIAA1429 knockdown on cell growth and metastasis were investigated in SK‑Hep1 and HCCLM3 cells.  
KIAA1429 was significantly upregulated in HCC tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues in both the TCGA and WCH cohorts (P < 0.05). ROC analysis showed AUCs of 0.85 (TCGA) and 0.75 (WCH), indicating high diagnostic value. High KIAA1429 expression correlated with larger tumor size (P = 0.0303), elevated serum AFP (P = 0.0157), presence of microvascular invasion (P = 0.0168), higher TNM stage (P = 0.0220), and advanced BCLC stage (P = 0.0051). Patients with high KIAA1429 had shorter overall survival (P < 0.05) and disease‑free survival (P < 0.05). Among the hepatoma cell lines examined, KIAA1429 was highly expressed in SK‑Hep1 and HCCLM3, and knockdown of KIAA1429 markedly suppressed proliferative and metastatic capacities in these cells.  
KIAA1429 is frequently overexpressed in HCC and is associated with aggressive clinicopathological features and poor prognosis. Its oncogenic effects on cell growth and metastasis suggest that KIAA1429 may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for HCC.
