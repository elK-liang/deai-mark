In this phase I dose‑escalation study, 36 Asian patients with advanced melanoma received camrelizumab at three dose levels after failing at least one prior line of therapy. The enrollment of a predominantly pre‑treated cohort (more than two‑thirds had ≥2 previous lines) and the balanced distribution of primary sites (acral, mucosal, cutaneous and unknown) reflect the heterogeneous nature of melanoma in this region. The absence of dose‑limiting toxicities across all dose levels and the fact that the maximum tolerated dose was not reached indicate that the selected regimen is well tolerated and supports the continuation of dose expansion.

The safety profile observed is consistent with the known class effects of PD‑1 blockade, although the incidence of reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (RCCEP) was notably high (63.9%). RCCEP typically manifested early, often within the first treatment cycle (2–4 weeks), and was predominantly located on the face and trunk. Other treatment‑related adverse events included rash, fever, thyroid dysfunction (both hypo‑ and hyper‑thyroidism), vitiligo and fatigue, most of which were mild to moderate in severity. Grade 3 or higher events were rare and limited to isolated cases of liver injury, elevated γ‑glutamyltransferase and blood triglycerides, all of which resolved with appropriate management. Importantly, no patient discontinued study medication because of adverse events and there were no drug‑related deaths, underscoring a favorable tolerability profile in a heavily pre‑treated population.

Of the 33 patients who underwent at least one efficacy assessment, preliminary evaluation revealed disease control in a subset of individuals, suggesting that camrelizumab can exert anti‑tumour activity in refractory melanoma. Although the response data are not fully reported here, the observation of stable disease or better in some patients is encouraging, especially given the limited therapeutic options for acral and mucosal subtypes. A more detailed analysis of objective response rates, duration of response and survival outcomes will be presented in a subsequent report.

Pharmacokinetic sampling was performed throughout the study; while the full PK characterization is planned for separate publication, early indications suggest that plasma concentrations achieved at the evaluated dose levels are within the range reported in earlier camrelizumab phase I trials. The lack of a defined MTD in this cohort further supports the feasibility of the selected dosing schedule for future efficacy‑expansion phases.

Collectively, these findings highlight camrelizumab as a promising anti‑PD‑1 agent for Asian patients with advanced melanoma, a population historically under‑represented in clinical trials of immune checkpoint inhibitors.
