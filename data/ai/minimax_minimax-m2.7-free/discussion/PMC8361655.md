The construction of a KTR‑specific miRNA‑mediated protein‑protein interaction network (miR‑PPIN) integrating differential expression, miRNA‑mRNA targeting and physical protein interactions provided a systems‑level view of the regulatory architecture that accompanies kidney transplant rejection. The network comprised 949 miRNA‑mRNA pairs linking 31 miRNAs to 642 genes, together with 1 447 PPIs, reflecting a highly interconnected module of molecules that are aberrantly expressed during KTR. The observation that the node‑degree distribution followed a power‑law pattern (slope ≈ ‑1.34) is consistent with the scale‑free topology typical of biological networks, indicating that a few hub genes dominate the interaction landscape while the majority of targets occupy peripheral positions. Functional enrichment of the network genes highlighted a strong bias toward T‑cell‑related biological processes—including NK T‑cell proliferation, antigen processing and presentation, and positive regulation of memory T‑cell activation—thereby anchoring the miRNA‑driven regulatory program to the immune mechanisms that are central to graft rejection.

To distill the most biologically relevant players from the large network, we prioritized target genes by three complementary centrality metrics (degree, closeness, and betweenness) and designated the top‑ranked genes as the CORE set. This strategy identified a compact group of genes that serve as hubs in the PPI network and are therefore likely to exert a critical influence on the rejection phenotype. By quantifying how many CORE genes each miRNA regulates, we derived two novel topological parameters, NTGA and NTGC, which capture the extent and intensity of miRNA targeting of essential nodes. Three miRNAs—miR‑145‑5p, miR‑155‑5p, and miR‑23b‑3p—exhibited significantly higher NTGA and NTGC values than other miRNAs, positioning them as the most potent regulators of the CORE module. Notably, miR‑145‑5p and miR‑23b‑3p were down‑regulated in KTR samples, whereas miR‑155‑5p was over‑expressed, especially in cases of chronic mixed rejection (CMR). These opposing expression patterns suggest that the loss of repressive miRNAs (miR‑145‑5p, miR‑23b‑3p) may unleash CORE genes that promote T‑cell activation, while the up‑regulation of miR‑155‑5p could reflect a compensatory or pathogenic feedback loop that further amplifies immune signaling.

Diagnostic utility of the three candidate miRNAs was assessed by receiver‑operating‑characteristic (ROC) analyses performed on the training cohort and validated in an independent dataset. Both individual miRNAs and their combination achieved high discriminative power for distinguishing KTR from stable grafts, and the multi‑miRNA signature further improved classification of rejection subtypes, notably separating CMR from other forms of rejection. The ability of miR‑155‑5p to be preferentially elevated in CMR aligns with its known role in T‑cell proliferation and cytokine production, reinforcing the notion that this miRNA may serve as a subtype‑specific marker. Because miRNAs can be detected in peripheral blood, these findings open a pathway toward a minimally invasive, network‑guided biomarker panel for early detection and subtyping of KTR.

While the results are promising, several limitations merit consideration. The miR‑PPIN was built primarily from publicly available PPI databases and kidney‑related expression data, which may not capture tissue‑specific or dynamic interactions that occur during the acute to chronic transition of rejection. The CORE gene set was defined using static topological metrics; incorporating time‑resolved expression or single‑cell分辨率 data could refine the identification of stage‑specific hubs. Moreover, the current study relied on bulk transcriptomic data, which may obscure heterogeneity within the graft microenvironment. Experimental validation—such as luciferase reporter assays for the predicted miRNA‑target pairs, functional inhibition/overexpression of the three miRNAs in relevant immune cell models, and correlation of their levels with histopathologic scores—is required to establish causality and to delineate the precise mechanistic contributions of each miRNA to the rejection process.

Future work should focus on (i) expanding the multi‑omics integration to include proteomic and metabolomic layers, which could reveal downstream effects of miRNA‑mediated regulation; (ii) performing prospective clinical studies with larger, multi‑center cohorts to confirm the diagnostic performance and generalizability of the miRNA signature; and (iii) exploring the potential of the identified CORE genes as therapeutic targets, either through miRNA‑based mimics/antagomirs or small‑molecule interventions that restore the balance of immune activation and tolerance. Collectively, the network‑based characterization presented here not only uncovers novel miRNA biomarkers for KTR diagnosis and subtyping but also provides a framework for dissecting the regulatory logic underlying transplant rejection, paving the way toward more precise and personalized management of kidney transplant patients.
