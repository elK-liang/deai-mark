Primary breast double‑hit lymphoma (DHL) represents a rare and particularly aggressive subset of B‑cell non‑Hodgkin lymphoma that arises within mammary tissue. Although isolated case series have highlighted the aggressive clinical course of DHL in general, data specifically focusing on primary breast involvement are scarce. In this multicentre, real‑world cohort of 48 patients, we sought to characterise the clinicopathological features, current therapeutic approaches and early survival outcomes of primary breast DHL, thereby addressing a gap in the literature regarding management in this anatomic site.

The pathological profile of our cohort underscores the hallmark molecular alterations that define DHL. All cases expressed C‑MYC protein, and the vast majority also showed co‑expression of BCL2 and BCL6 (43/48), with an additional five tumours demonstrating isolated BCL2 or BCL6 positivity. Consistent with the germinal‑centre B‑cell (GCB) origin typical of most DHL, 83 % of patients were classified as GCB type according to the Hans algorithm. Chromosomal rearrangements involving C‑MYC and either BCL2 or BCL6 were identified in a subset of patients, and next‑generation sequencing revealed additional somatic mutations, most notably TP53 alterations in four patients. These molecular events are known to cooperate in driving proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, lending a biologic rationale for the aggressive clinical behaviour observed in this series.

Clinically, the patients presented with a median age of 48 years and predominantly with a painless unilateral breast mass (79 %). The left breast was affected more frequently than the right, a lateralisation that has been noted in other primary breast lymphoma cohorts but remains incompletely understood. Tumour size was highly variable (median 2.6 cm, with 17 patients harbouring lesions >5 cm and one >10 cm), and disease stage spanned from localized (Ann Arbor IE/IIE) to advanced (IV) disease. The distribution of age‑adjusted International Prognostic Index (IPI) scores reflected a mixed risk profile, with approximately half of the cohort classified as low or low‑intermediate risk and the remainder as high‑intermediate or high risk. This heterogeneity in baseline risk underscores the need for individualised therapeutic strategies, even within a seemingly localised anatomic site.

Treatment approaches were diverse, reflecting real‑world practice across participating institutions. The majority of patients received intensive chemo‑immunotherapy regimens known to be effective in aggressive B‑cell lymphomas, including dose‑adjusted EPOCH‑R (DA‑EPOCH‑R) in various combinations, R‑HyperCVAD, and DA‑EPOCH‑R/MA. Notably, 18 patients (37.5 %) proceeded to autologous stem‑cell transplantation (ASCT) after induction, a strategy often employed in an attempt to consolidate remission in high‑risk disease. Despite these aggressive interventions, the observed mortality was substantial: 28 of 48 patients died during a median follow‑up of 47 months, indicating that primary breast DHL carries a grave prognosis even with modern therapy. The limited number of patients with detailed molecular data precluded definitive survival analyses according to TP53 or other mutation status, but the presence of TP53 alterations—a known predictor of chemoresistance in diffuse large B‑cell lymphoma—may partly explain the high event rate.

The strengths of this study include the multicentre design, which captures the heterogeneity of real‑world management, and the comprehensive molecular profiling performed in a subset of cases. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The retrospective nature introduces potential selection bias, particularly regarding the choice of therapy and the timing of diagnostic procedures. The cohort size, although largest for primary breast DHL to date, still limits statistical power for subgroup analyses. Pathologic evaluation was performed locally, and although the Hans algorithm was applied consistently, inter‑institutional variability in immunohistochemistry scoring and FISH analysis cannot be excluded. Moreover, the relatively short median follow‑up may underestimate late relapses, and survival outcomes were not adjusted for comorbidity or detailed treatment‑related toxicities. Consequently, the findings should be interpreted as hypothesis‑generating rather than definitive.

In conclusion, primary breast double‑hit lymphoma presents as an aggressive entity with a high frequency of GCB origin, universal C‑MYC expression, and frequent co‑expression of BCL2/BCL6. Despite the use of intensive chemo‑immunotherapy and consolidation with ASCT in a substantial proportion of patients, outcomes remain poor, highlighting an unmet need for optimised treatment strategies. Future prospective studies should focus on standardising molecular diagnostics—including centralised FISH and NGS assessment—and on evaluating novel targeted approaches (e.g., BCL2 inhibitors, PI3K or BTK inhibitors) in this rare but lethal presentation. Risk‑adapted regimens that incorporate both molecular profiling and functional imaging may ultimately improve survival for patients with primary breast DHL.
