The present study provides compelling evidence that the sodium/myo‑inositol co‑transporter SLC5A3 is frequently over‑expressed in non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Analysis of the TCGA cohort revealed a marked increase in SLC5A3 transcripts in tumor samples compared with normal lung tissue, and paired‑sample data confirmed elevated mRNA levels in the same patients. This pattern was reproduced in a local series of 13 resected NSCLC specimens, where both mRNA and protein levels of SLC5A3 were significantly higher in tumor than in adjacent normal lung. Immunohistochemistry and western blotting across multiple patients reinforced the up‑regulation at the protein level, and the same trend was observed in established (A549) and primary human NSCLC cell lines relative to non‑malignant lung epithelial cells. Taken together, these findings unambiguously establish SLC5A3 over‑expression as a consistent feature of NSCLC at the level of gene transcription, protein abundance, and cellular localization.

The functional significance of SLC5A3 up‑regulation likely stems from its role in cellular myo‑inositol homeostasis. Myo‑inositol is a critical precursor for phosphatidylinositol and for inositol‑phosphate signaling pathways that regulate cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic adaptation. In the NSCLC cells examined, intracellular myo‑inositol concentrations were markedly elevated, despite the fact that the key biosynthetic enzyme ISYNA1 (inositol‑3‑phosphate synthase) was expressed at low levels and was even further reduced in tumor cells. This reciprocal pattern suggests that NSCLC may compensate for limited de‑novo synthesis by increasing uptake of extracellular myo‑inositol through SLC5A3, thereby securing a supply of this metabolite to support rapid cell growth and membrane biogenesis. Such a shift in metabolic strategy is consistent with the heightened biosynthetic and signaling demands of malignant cells and warrants further investigation into the downstream signaling cascades that may be modulated by altered myo‑inositol flux.

The consistent over‑expression of SLC5A3 in patient samples and cell models positions this transporter as a potential biomarker for NSCLC. Its elevated levels in tumor tissue compared with matched normal lung could aid in diagnostic discrimination, particularly in situations where histopathological assessment is ambiguous. Moreover, because SLC5A3 mediates an active transport process, it represents a druggable target that could be exploited to interfere with myo‑inositol acquisition in tumor cells. Inhibitors of SLC5A3 or analogues that compete for myo‑inositol uptake may blunt the metabolic advantage conferred by transporter up‑regulation and thereby suppress tumor progression. Preliminary experiments to assess the consequences of SLC5A3 knock‑down or pharmacological inhibition on NSCLC cell growth are underway, and the results of these studies will be essential to validate the therapeutic potential of targeting this transporter.

While the data are robust within the cohorts examined, several limitations should be acknowledged. The local patient cohort, although well‑characterized, comprised a modest number of individuals (n = 13), and the TCGA dataset, although large, reflects a heterogeneous population that may not fully capture the diversity of NSCLC subtypes or treatment histories. In addition, the cell‑line models used, including A549 and primary pCan cells, provide valuable mechanistic insights but may not completely recapitulate the complexity of the tumor microenvironment, including stromal interactions and immune surveillance. Future studies employing in‑vivo tumorigenesis assays, patient‑derived xenografts, and larger retrospective clinical collections will be required to confirm that SLC5A3 over‑expression contributes causally to NSCLC development and to evaluate its prognostic relevance.

In summary, this work demonstrates that SLC5A3 is markedly up‑regulated in NSCLC at both the transcript and protein levels, a phenomenon accompanied by increased intracellular myo‑inositol and reduced ISYNA1 expression. These observations raise the intriguing possibility that SLC5A3‑mediated myo‑inositol uptake supports the metabolic and signaling needs of lung cancer cells. Further functional studies will clarify whether SLC5A3 is a driver of tumorigenicity and whether it can be effectively targeted for therapeutic intervention. If validated, SLC5A3 may emerge as both a diagnostic marker and a novel therapeutic target in the management of non‑small cell lung cancer.
