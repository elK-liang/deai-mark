The present study demonstrates that the glycolytic inhibitor 2‑deoxy‑d‑glucose (2‑DG) can markedly enhance the cytotoxic activity of imatinib (IM) in both IM‑sensitive KBM5 cells and IM‑resistant KBM5‑T315I cells. While the T315I mutation confers a ~30‑fold increase in the IM IC50 (0.09 μM vs. 2.84 μM), the IC50 values for 2‑DG are comparable between the two lines (1.62 mM vs. 1.28 mM), indicating that resistance to IM does not extend to glycolytic inhibition. Importantly, co‑treatment with 2‑DG and IM produces a dose‑dependent reduction in cell viability and proliferation, and the calculated combination indices are predominantly below 1.0, signifying a strong synergistic interaction. These data suggest that metabolic targeting can restore, or at least augment, the sensitivity of T315I‑mutated chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) cells to BCR‑ABL inhibition.

Accumulating evidence indicates that oncogenic BCR‑ABL drives a pronounced glycolytic phenotype, which supports rapid energy production and biosynthetic demands (refs. 5, 9). The T315I mutation, which disrupts the binding of imatinib to the kinase domain, has been reported to be accompanied by further metabolic rewiring that may confer a survival advantage under therapeutic stress. Our observation that KBM5‑T315I cells retain a similar sensitivity to 2‑DG as their wild‑type counterparts implies that, despite acquiring drug resistance, these cells remain dependent on glycolysis for maintenance of cellular homeostasis. This metabolic vulnerability provides a rational basis for combining a glycolytic blocker with IM to circumvent the resistant phenotype.

The synergy between 2‑DG and IM likely arises from the convergence of two distinct anti‑leukemic mechanisms. 2‑DG reduces glucose utilization, depletes intracellular ATP, and hampers the generation of glycolytic intermediates required for nucleotide and lipid biosynthesis. The resulting energy crisis can impair the function of survival pathways downstream of BCR‑ABL, such as PI3K/AKT and MAPK signaling, and may promote the activation of AMP‑activated protein kinase (AMPK). In parallel, IM blocks the enzymatic activity of BCR‑ABL, diminishing proliferative and anti‑apoptotic signals. When administered together, the metabolic stress inflicted by 2‑DG may sensitize cells to the pro‑apoptotic effects of BCR‑ABL inhibition, leading to an enhanced cytotoxic response. Moreover, the induction of autophagy, a common adaptive response to energy deprivation, could be harnessed in this context: while autophagy may initially protect cells from metabolic stress, simultaneous BCR‑ABL blockade may prevent the activation of compensatory survival autophagy, thereby shifting the balance toward cell death.

Quantitative analysis of the drug interaction supports the biological interpretation of synergy. The CI values < 1 observed across a broad range of concentrations indicate that sub‑maximal doses of each compound can be combined to achieve an effect greater than the sum of their individual actions. This is particularly relevant for the IM‑resistant KBM5‑T315I cells, where even high doses of IM alone have limited efficacy. The ability of 2‑DG to lower the effective concentration of IM required to achieve a given level of cytotoxicity suggests a potential strategy to reduce the overall drug burden while overcoming resistance, a consideration of clinical significance for patients harboring the T315I mutation who have limited therapeutic options.

Nevertheless, several caveats merit attention. The experiments were performed with established cell lines cultured under controlled conditions, and the relevance of these findings to primary CML cells and the complex tumor microenvironment remains to be established. Additionally, the present study did not directly quantify autophagic flux or metabolic intermediates; future work should incorporate autophagic markers, Seahorse respirometry, and metabolomic profiling to delineate the precise mechanistic underpinnings of the observed synergy. In vivo validation in xenograft or genetically engineered models of T315I‑positive CML will be essential to assess whether the combination translates into therapeutic benefit without unacceptable toxicity to normal hematopoietic cells.

In summary, our data demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of glycolysis with 2‑DG synergistically enhances imatinib‑mediated cytotoxicity in both IM‑sensitive and IM‑resistant CML cells. This synergistic interaction provides a compelling rationale for exploiting metabolic vulnerabilities as an adjunctive strategy to overcome BCR‑ABL inhibitor resistance. Future investigations that integrate metabolic inhibitors with BCR‑ABL targeted therapy, and that carefully examine autophagic responses and systemic toxicity, may ultimately lead to novel, more effective treatment regimens for patients with T315I‑mutated chronic myeloid leukemia.
