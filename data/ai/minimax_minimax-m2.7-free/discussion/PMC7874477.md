The profiling of plasma‐derived small RNAs revealed a striking shift in the composition of tRNA‑derived fragments (tDRs) between colorectal cancer (CRC) patients and healthy controls (HCs). While the overall abundance of tDRs followed the order 5′‑tRF > 5′‑half/i‑tRF > 3′‑tRF > 3′‑half in both groups, the proportion of 5′‑tRFs was significantly higher in CRC plasma. Hierarchical clustering of the sequencing data demonstrated a clear separation between the two groups, with 628 distinct tDRs detected in CRC samples compared with 745 in HCs, indicating systematic alterations in the tDR landscape associated with tumorigenesis. These findings suggest that the plasma tDR profile may reflect disease‑specific changes in RNA metabolism or release, positioning tDRs as a class of promising circulating non‑coding RNAs in cancer biology.

Among the 5′‑tRF species, the most dramatic change was observed for 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC. In healthy individuals, 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC represented only 7.44 % of the total 5′‑tRF pool, whereas in CRC patients it surged to 52.24 %, making it the dominant 5′‑tRF in the circulation. Concomitantly, the relative abundance of 5′‑tRF‑GlyCCC increased, whereas 5′‑tRF‑HisGTG and 5′‑tRF‑AlaTGC were markedly reduced in cancer plasma. The magnitude of these shifts implies a selective enrichment of specific tRNA fragments rather than a global increase in all 5′‑tRFs, which may be driven by tumor‑associated ribonucleases (e.g., angiogenin) or by differential stability of particular tDRs in the bloodstream. The selective elevation of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC could thus serve as a molecular signature of CRC.

Functionally, tRNA fragments have been shown to act as regulators of gene expression, influencing translation fidelity, cellular stress responses, and oncogenic pathways. The predominance of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC in CRC plasma raises the hypothesis that this fragment may be secreted by tumor cells and participate in inter‑cellular communication that favors tumor progression. Although the present study does not establish a causal relationship, the enrichment pattern mirrors observations in other cancers where specific tRFs are implicated in proliferation, invasion, or immune evasion. Further mechanistic investigations are needed to determine whether 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC retains biological activity in the extracellular milieu and whether it can modulate pathways relevant to colorectal tumorigenesis.

From a clinical perspective, the differential abundance of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC was reproducibly validated by qRT‑PCR across independent plasma samples, confirming its potential as a non‑invasive biomarker. The robustness of the signal in a limited set of CRC and HC plasmas suggests that circulating 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC could complement existing stool‑based or protein biomarkers for CRC detection. Nonetheless, the current cohort is modest, and the observed fold‑change and statistical significance (adjusted p < 0.05) will require confirmation in larger, prospectively collected cohorts to assess sensitivity, specificity, and stage‑dependent variation. Additionally, exploring the stability of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC in different blood compartments (e.g., exosomes versus cell‑free plasma) could refine sample processing protocols for diagnostic assays.

The present work also highlights several avenues for future research. First, an expanded validation study across diverse geographic and ethnic populations is essential to establish generalizability. Second, mechanistic studies should investigate the upstream events that lead to the selective generation or clearance of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC, including the possible involvement of tRNA‑modifying enzymes such as ALKBH3, which has been implicated in tRNA stability and could thus influence tDR biogenesis. Third, integrating plasma tDR profiling with other omics layers (e.g., transcriptomics, proteomics) may uncover cross‑talk between tRNA fragmentation and oncogenic signaling pathways. Finally, longitudinal sampling could clarify whether 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC levels change with tumor progression or treatment response, thereby extending its utility from diagnosis to disease monitoring.

In summary, our comprehensive small‑RNA sequencing analysis uncovers a pronounced alteration in plasma tDR composition during colorectal cancer, characterized by a dramatic enrichment of 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC. This fragment not only distinguishes CRC from healthy plasma with high fold‑change but also offers a readily measurable candidate for liquid‑biopsy‐based diagnostics. While further validation and mechanistic exploration are required, the findings position 5′‑tRF‑GlyGCC as a novel, tumor‑associated small RNA that could improve early detection strategies for colorectal cancer.
