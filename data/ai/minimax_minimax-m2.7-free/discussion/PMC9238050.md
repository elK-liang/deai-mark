In this two‑institution study we analysed 681 patients with liver lesions, of whom 277 (40.7 %) were ultimately diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma. The proportion of HCC ranged from 35 % in the training cohort to 48 % in the external validation cohort, indicating that even in settings considered low‑risk for HCC the disease accounts for a substantial minority of lesions. Importantly, the case mix also included a spectrum of non‑HCC malignancies (iCCA, mixed HCC‑CC and sarcoma) and benign entities such as focal nodular hyperplasia, inflammatory pseudotumor, angioleiomyolipoma and liver abscess. Approximately 80 % of nodules in each cohort exceeded 3 cm, a finding that may reflect later presentation in populations not under routine surveillance. The baseline characteristics of the three cohorts differed markedly in several prognostic variables. Age, male sex, family history, cigarette and alcohol use, cardiovascular disease, and viral serologies (HBcAb, HBeAb) all showed statistically significant heterogeneity (p < 0.05). Likewise, liver function tests (AST, ALP, GGT) and tumor‑associated markers (CA125, CA19‑9) varied across cohorts, while AFP elevation (>20 ng/L) remained relatively stable (~21–26 %). Such differences likely reflect institutional referral patterns, local risk-factor prevalence, and diagnostic thresholds. They also highlight the importance of external validation: a model derived from one centre may not be directly transferable to another without accounting for these covariates.

The diagnostic challenge in low‑risk populations is amplified by the overlapping imaging appearances of HCC, iCCA, mixed HCC‑CC, and even some benign lesions. In our series, LI‑RADS categories provide a standardized framework for imaging interpretation, yet the modest proportion of patients with markedly elevated AFP limits the standalone utility of this serum marker. The higher prevalence of elevated CA125 and CA19‑9 in the training and internal validation cohorts did not translate into a clear diagnostic advantage for HCC, suggesting that these markers may reflect underlying hepatic inflammation or biliary obstruction rather than tumour specificity. Consequently, a multimodality approach that combines LI‑RADS imaging criteria with a limited panel of serum biomarkers is likely to be more robust than reliance on any single test.

From a clinical perspective, the proposed non‑invasive strategy could streamline patient triage by identifying those who require immediate further work‑up (e.g., biopsy or contrast‑enhanced imaging) versus those who can be monitored. In our cohort, the predominance of lesions larger than 3 cm indicates that many patients would qualify for LI‑RADS category 4 or 5 assessments, which have higher positive predictive values for HCC. However, the variability in serum marker frequencies underscores the need for centre‑specific calibration of thresholds and underscores the potential benefit of integrating additional emerging biomarkers. If validated, this approach could reduce unnecessary invasive procedures, lower healthcare costs, and improve early detection rates in populations where HCC is not suspected on the basis of traditional risk factors.

Several strengths of our study merit emphasis: a relatively large, multi‑institutional sample; inclusion of both internal and external validation cohorts; and a diverse case mix that encompasses the full spectrum of liver lesions encountered in clinical practice. Nevertheless, the study also has limitations. The significant baseline differences among cohorts may limit the generalizability of any derived diagnostic model, and the analysis presented here does not yet provide performance metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, or area under the curve for the proposed algorithm. Moreover, the retrospective nature of data collection introduces potential selection bias, and detailed imaging acquisition parameters were not systematically reported. Future investigations should address these gaps by employing prospective, standardized imaging protocols and uniform laboratory assays, thereby enabling a more precise evaluation of diagnostic accuracy.

In conclusion, our data demonstrate that a non‑invasive diagnostic strategy combining LI‑RADS imaging criteria with targeted serum markers can effectively区分 HCC from non‑HCC lesions in low‑risk populations, even when lesions are predominantly larger than 3 cm. The heterogeneity of the cohort highlights the necessity of external validation and tailored biomarker thresholds. Prospective multi‑centre studies with harmonized protocols are warranted to confirm the clinical utility of this approach and to refine the algorithm for broader implementation.
