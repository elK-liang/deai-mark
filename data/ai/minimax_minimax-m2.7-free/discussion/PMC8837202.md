The results presented here establish a robust in vitro system to examine the interaction between the Mdn1 MIDAS domain and its physiological substrate Rsa4. By fusing an N‑terminal SNAP tag to the minimal MIDAS fragment (aa 4381–4717) we were able to express and purify both the wild‑type domain and the Y4666R point mutant in Escherichia coli, while the full‑length Rsa4‑GFP construct was produced in insect cells and isolated to homogeneity. The integrity of the purified Rsa4‑GFP was confirmed by SDS‑PAGE, native mass spectrometry and mass‑photometry, all of which indicated a monomeric species of the expected mass. Size‑exclusion chromatography revealed that, whereas Rsa4‑GFP alone eluted as a single peak at ~14.5 mL, co‑injection with the wild‑type MIDAS domain produced a shift to earlier elution volumes, consistent with the formation of a higher‑molecular‑weight complex. In contrast, the Y4666R mutant failed to alter the elution profile of Rsa4‑GFP, and no co‑elution was observed. Quantitative analysis by microscale thermophoresis yielded a dissociation constant (KD) of 6.9 ± 2.0 µM for the wild‑type MIDAS domain, whereas the Y4666R variant produced only marginal fluorescence changes that precluded reliable fitting. Together, these data demonstrate that the MIDAS domain of Mdn1 binds Rsa4 with moderate affinity and that the tyrosine at position 4666 is essential for this interaction.

The KD of ≈7 µM falls within the range reported for many transient protein–protein interactions that underlie ribosome biogenesis, where rapid association and dissociation are required to accommodate the sequential and collaborative nature of the assembly process. The relatively modest affinity suggests that the interaction is likely to be regulated by additional cellular cues—such as the ATPase cycle of the AAA mechanoenzyme, post‑translational modifications, or the presence of neighboring ribosomal components—that can modulate the effective KD in the context of the pre‑ribosome. The observed specific binding also supports the notion that the MIDAS domain serves as a dedicated docking site for UBL‑domain‑containing factors such as Rsa4, thereby contributing to the substrate recognition step of Mdn1‑driven remodeling.

The dramatic loss of binding upon the Y4666R substitution underscores the functional importance of this conserved residue. In previous work, the analogous tyrosine in related MIDAS domains was shown to contact the UBL fold directly, and its mutation to arginine abolishes UBL recognition in vitro (Ahmed et al., 2019). Our biochemical data corroborate these findings and extend them to the Mdn1–Rsa4 pair, indicating that Y4666 participates in a key hydrogen‑bonding or electrostatic interaction that stabilizes the binding interface. MIDAS domains are best known for mediating mechanical coupling in adhesion complexes, where they often exhibit catch‑bond behavior—i.e., the lifetime of the bond increases under tensile force. The present results provide a foundation for exploring whether the Mdn1 MIDAS domain operates similarly during ribosome maturation. The specific, moderate‑affinity interaction observed here is consistent with a scenario in which mechanical forces generated by the AAA ATPase could reinforce the MIDAS–UBL interface, prolonging the bound state precisely when directional translocation is required. However, the data presented do not directly address force‑dependent kinetics, and definitive evidence for catch‑bond formation will require single‑molecule force spectroscopy or optical‑tweezer assays that monitor bond lifetime under load.

Several limitations of the current study should be acknowledged. First, we employed a minimal MIDAS construct, whereas the full‑length Mdn1 protein may contain additional regulatory elements that influence substrate affinity. Second, all binding experiments were performed in the absence of nucleotides, whereas the AAA domain is expected to undergo nucleotide‑dependent conformational changes that could modulate MIDAS activity in vivo. Third, our analysis is limited to Rsa4; the manuscript title implies that the MIDAS domain interacts with two distinct substrates, and future work will need to test whether the binding properties observed for Rsa4 extend to other UBL‑domain proteins. Addressing these points will be essential for fully elucidating the mechanistic role of the MIDAS domain in Mdn1 function and for demonstrating the catch‑bond nature of its interactions. Nonetheless, the quantitative binding data and the identification of Y4666 as a critical residue lay a solid groundwork for subsequent mechanistic and cellular studies of this essential ribosome‑maturation factor.
