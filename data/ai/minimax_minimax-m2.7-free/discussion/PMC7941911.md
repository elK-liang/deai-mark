In the present study we performed a comprehensive lncRNA‑mRNA microarray analysis to map the transcriptomic changes that accompany the progression from normal liver to non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and further to non‑alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). Pathological evaluation confirmed that db/db mice fed a normal diet develop the hallmark lipid accumulation of NAFLD, whereas the methionine‑choline‑deficient (MCD) diet induces the inflammatory phenotype characteristic of NASH, as evidenced by pronounced F4/80‑positive macrophage infiltration and markedly elevated plasma ALT and AST levels. These clinical chemistry and immunohistochemical readouts not only validated the experimental models but also provided a contextual backdrop for interpreting the observed transcriptomic shifts. The microarray platform identified >3 000 differentially expressed lncRNAs between NAFLD and control livers, with a comparable number of changes when NASH was compared with either normal or NAFLD tissue. A Venn analysis revealed 63 lncRNAs that were consistently altered across all three comparisons, highlighting a core set of transcripts that may be fundamental to the fatty‑liver‑to‑steatohepatitis continuum. Importantly, a series‑cluster analysis uncovered 381 lncRNAs that exhibited a stepwise increase in expression from normal to NAFLD and to NASH, suggesting a potential link between their abundance and disease severity. Among these progressively up‑regulated transcripts we focused on the novel long non‑coding RNA Gm9795, which exhibited the strongest progressive elevation and thus represented a promising candidate for further functional exploration.

The observation that Gm9795 expression rises in parallel with the histological and biochemical indices of liver injury raises the hypothesis that this lncRNA may participate in the inflammatory cascade that drives NASH. The NF‑κB and JNK signaling pathways are well‑recognized mediators of cytokine‑driven inflammation in the liver, and both are known to be activated by endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS), a condition that accompanies lipid overload and hepatocyte injury. In the context of NASH, excessive free fatty acids and cholesterol can trigger ERS, leading to activation of the unfolded‑protein response (UPR) that converges on NF‑κB and JNK, thereby amplifying the production of pro‑inflammatory cytokines such as TNF‑α and IL‑1β. Our current expression data do not provide direct evidence of pathway activation, but the progressive up‑regulation of Gm9795 is temporally aligned with the emergence of inflammatory changes, suggesting that Gm9795 could be part of a regulatory network that couples metabolic stress to inflammatory signaling. For instance, several lncRNAs have been shown to act as molecular scaffolds or decoys that modulate the activity of transcription factors, kinases, or components of the ERS‑UPR machinery. If Gm9795 follows a similar mode of action, it might influence the phosphorylation status of IκB or JNK, or interact with key UPR sensors such as IRE1α, PERK, or ATF6, thereby contributing to the observed inflammation.

Although the mechanistic details remain to be elucidated, the present findings open several avenues for future investigation. Loss‑of‑function approaches, such as siRNA‑mediated silencing or CRISPR‑based knockout of Gm9795 in hepatocytes or macrophage cell lines, would help determine whether the lncRNA is required for the activation of NF‑κB/JNK and the induction of ERS‑related genes. Conversely, gain‑of‑function studies, including forced over‑expression of Gm9795 in lean mice or in vitro cultures, could test whether elevated Gm9795 alone is sufficient to provoke inflammatory signaling. Moreover, the identification of Gm9795‑bound protein partners or target microRNAs through RNA‑pulldown or RIP‑seq experiments would provide molecular insight into its mode of action. From a translational perspective, the stepwise increase in Gm9795 abundance suggests that it could serve as a non‑invasive biomarker of disease progression within the NAFLD spectrum, especially if its levels in plasma or extracellular vesicles correlate with histological inflammation or fibrosis stages. Preliminary exploration in human cohorts would be a logical next step to assess the diagnostic potential of this lncRNA.

Several limitations of the current work should be acknowledged. First, the microarray platform, while comprehensive, measures relative abundance and may miss low‑abundance transcripts or alternative splice variants; targeted qPCR validation of Gm9795 and other key candidates is therefore essential. Second, our observations are confined to db/db mice, a genetic model of obesity and insulin resistance, and to the MCD diet, which, although useful for inducing NASH‑like inflammation, does not fully recapitulate the human disease spectrum, where diet, genetics, and gut microbiota contribute variably. Third, the present study is descriptive; the functional relevance of Gm9795 was not tested experimentally, and the mechanistic links to NF‑κB/JNK signaling and ERS remain speculative. Future studies employing hepatocyte‑specific and macrophage‑specific knock‑down models, as well as integrative multi‑omics (e.g., chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing, proteomics), will be required to establish causality and to map the regulatory circuitry in which Gm9795 participates. Additionally, human tissue validation and longitudinal clinical sampling would strengthen the translational impact of the findings.

In summary, our microarray profiling has identified a distinct set of lncRNAs that are dynamically regulated during the transition from NAFLD to NASH, with a particular focus on the novel transcript Gm9795, which is markedly up‑regulated in parallel with histological inflammation and serum transaminase elevation. The alignment of Gm9795 expression with disease progression, together with the established role of NF‑κB/JNK signaling and endoplasmic reticulum stress in NASH pathogenesis, provides a plausible framework for future mechanistic studies. Elucidating how Gm9795 interfaces with these pathways may not only deepen our understanding of the molecular underpinnings of fatty‑liver disease but also unveil new therapeutic targets or biomarkers for clinical management.
