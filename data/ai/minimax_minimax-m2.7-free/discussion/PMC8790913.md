The integrative analysis of four independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets revealed a robust set of 124 up‑regulated and 98 down‑regulated genes that are consistently altered in osteosarcoma (OS) relative to normal osteoblastic controls. By requiring genes to be differentially expressed in at least three of the four collections, we minimized the impact of dataset‑specific variability arising from differences in control tissue sources (primary osteoblasts, differentiated mesenchymal stem cells, and an osteoblast cell line) and in OS specimen characteristics (high‑grade pre‑chemotherapy biopsies, pediatric samples, and metastatic lesions). Construction of a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network from these consensus dysregulated genes enabled the identification of the ten most highly connected hub nodes, among which TYROBP occupied a central position. This hub structure, together with the enrichment of the network for known regulatory axes such as SPI1‑TYROBP‑FCER1G, suggests that a coordinated transcriptional and signaling program underpins the oncogenic phenotype of OS.

The up‑regulated gene set was markedly enriched for components of the collagen‑containing extracellular matrix, processes related to smooth‑muscle cell proliferation, and cell‑substrate adhesion, as well as the p53 signaling pathway. These findings align with the well‑established role of extracellular‑matrix remodeling and altered adhesion in sarcoma progression, where robust deposition of fibrillar collagen facilitates migration, invasion, and metastasis. The concurrent activation of the p53 pathway, although possibly reflecting a cellular response to genotoxic stress, may also indicate a context‑dependent rewiring that favors survival under microenvironmental pressure. Moreover, the up‑regulation of genes linked to smooth‑muscle cell proliferation hints at a contribution of vascular or stromal smooth‑muscle‑like cells to the tumor niche, a phenomenon that has been reported in other bone malignancies and could support neovascularization and nutrient supply to the growing tumor.

In stark contrast, the down‑regulated genes were dominated by immune‑related functional annotations, most notably the MHC class II protein complex, lymphocyte/leukocyte proliferation, and related binding activities. The consistent loss of MHC II expression and diminished lymphocyte proliferative capacity suggests that OS specimens, regardless of their source, exhibit a broadly suppressed antigen‑presentation and adaptive immune response. This immune‑evasive signature is consistent with the notion that solid tumors often create an immunosuppressive microenvironment to escape cytotoxic T‑cell surveillance, and it provides a molecular basis for the limited efficacy of immunotherapeutic approaches observed in OS. The down‑regulation of these genes across heterogeneous datasets reinforces the notion that immune suppression is a core, rather than incidental, feature of osteosarcoma biology.

Among the ten hub genes identified by degree centrality, several have known functions that intersect both structural and immune pathways. FN1 (fibronectin) and VWF (von Willebrand factor) are extracellular‑matrix constituents that mediate cell adhesion and mechanotransduction, while EGFR and CSF1R are receptor tyrosine kinases that drive proliferation and macrophage recruitment, respectively. TYROBP, together with its associated C1Q components (C1QA, C1QB) and co‑stimulatory molecule CD86, occupies a strategic position linking innate immune signaling (via the TYROBP‑FCER1G complex) with complement activation and antigen presentation. The enrichment of the hub module for osteoclast differentiation, integrin binding, and glial‑cell activation further implies cross‑talk between tumor cells and the bone microenvironment, potentially fostering osteolysis and disease progression. The prominence of TYROBP within this network, coupled with its known involvement in activating immune receptors, positions it as a plausible mediator of the observed immune‑suppressive landscape.

The SPI1‑TYROBP‑FCER1G axis, although not among the top ten hub nodes, emerges as a plausible upstream regulatory module that could orchestrate the coordinated dysregulation observed in our gene set. SPI1 encodes the PU.1 transcription factor, which is a master regulator of hematopoietic and myeloid cell differentiation and has been implicated in the transcriptional control of TYROBP and FCER1G in immune cells. Given that TYROBP functions as an adaptor bridging FCER1G (the Fc receptor for IgE) with downstream signaling cascades, a SPI1‑driven regulatory loop could modulate both innate immune responsiveness and osteoclastogenesis within the tumor stroma. Although direct transcription‑factor binding evidence is not provided in this study, the network topology and functional enrichments are consistent with the hypothesis that SPI1‑mediated transcriptional programming contributes to the oncogenic and immunosuppressive signature of OS.

While the present work provides a comprehensive, multi‑dataset perspective on the molecular alterations underpinning osteosarcoma, several limitations should be acknowledged. The control groups varied from primary osteoblasts to differentiated mesenchymal stem cells and an immortalized cell line, introducing biological heterogeneity that may influence the detected DEGs. Similarly, the OS samples encompassed pre‑treatment biopsies, pediatric tumors, and metastatic specimens, which could confound the identification of universal driver events versus context‑specific changes. The analysis is primarily correlative; functional experiments (e.g., knock‑down of SPI1, TYROBP, or FCER1G in OS cell lines) and in vivo models are required to establish causality and to explore therapeutic targeting of this network. Moreover, prognostic associations were not directly assessed in the current results, and future studies should integrate clinical outcome data to determine whether the identified hub genes or the SPI1‑TYROBP‑FCER1G module can serve as biomarkers for risk stratification or as actionable targets for novel therapies aimed at re‑activating anti‑tumor immunity in osteosarcoma.
