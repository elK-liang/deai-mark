The implementation of a large-scale salivary-based severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) surveillance program at the University of Padua represents a significant public health initiative that leveraged non-invasive testing methodology to monitor viral transmission among university employees during the second and third pandemic waves. The program achieved notable participation rates, with 68% of invited subjects initially enrolling during the critical October–November 2020 period when coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) incidence was escalating throughout Europe. The sustained mean monthly participation of 4,790 employees over the eight-month observation period demonstrates the feasibility of maintaining active surveillance in a large institutional setting, with participation rates ranging from 3,965 to 5,447 subjects per month. This level of engagement substantially exceeded typical passive reporting mechanisms and enabled comprehensive monitoring of viral spread within the university community.

The phased enrollment strategy proved essential for maximizing program coverage. The initial surge in participation during October and November 2020 captured a substantial proportion of willing participants early in the surveillance period, with 4,122 new subjects joining during these two months alone. The subsequent decline in new enrollments to merely 25 subjects in December 2020 reflected the exhaustion of readily accessible participants rather than diminished interest, as evidenced by the successful recruitment of an additional 556 subjects following the second invitation launched in January 2021. The final cohort expansion to 6,284 subjects under surveillance by January–May 2021, compared with 5,605 subjects during the earlier period, demonstrates that targeted re-engagement efforts can effectively increase program reach even amid ongoing pandemic restrictions. The relatively modest influx of 123 subjects during the final two months suggests that near-saturation of the willing participant pool had been achieved by spring 2021.

The demographic composition of enrolled participants provides important insights into surveillance program representativeness. Across all enrollment months, the mean age of participants remained relatively stable, generally ranging from 36 to 47 years, which likely reflects the working-age composition of the university employee population. Gender distribution showed month-to-month variation, with female participants comprising the majority in October, November, and March 2021, while male participants predominated in December 2020, January 2021, and April 2021. This variability may reflect differential uptake patterns among professional categories or random fluctuations inherent in rolling enrollment. The overall balance between male and female participants enhances the generalizability of surveillance findings across the university workforce.

The division of university permanent staff into surveillance and non-surveillance cohorts provided a natural comparison framework for evaluating program effectiveness. The progressive increase in the surveillance cohort from 5,605 subjects (representing 73% of tracked permanent staff) during October–December 2020 to 6,284 subjects (representing 82% of tracked permanent staff) during January–May 2021 indicates expanding program penetration over time. Correspondingly, the non-surveillance cohort decreased from 2,047 to 1,368 subjects during these periods, suggesting successful conversion of non-participants to active surveillance status. However, the persistence of a non-surveillance cohort of 1,368 subjects by the study's conclusion highlights the ongoing challenge of achieving complete population coverage in voluntary surveillance programs, even within a defined institutional setting with mandatory occupational health structures.

The cumulative evidence from this surveillance initiative suggests that salivary-based testing programs can achieve sufficient participation rates to provide meaningful epidemiological insights during pandemic conditions. The non-invasive nature of salivary collection likely facilitated participation by reducing barriers associated with nasopharyngeal sampling, while the institutional support from the University of Padua's Preventive Medicine department ensured systematic tracking and follow-up. The successful maintenance of approximately 4,790 monthly participants despite fluctuating pandemic conditions and evolving public health measures demonstrates program sustainability over an extended period. These findings support the broader applicability of salivary-based surveillance strategies to other large institutions seeking to monitor respiratory pathogen transmission among employee populations during epidemic periods.
