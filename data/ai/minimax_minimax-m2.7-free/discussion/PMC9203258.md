The findings presented in this study demonstrate that SARS-CoV-2 infection induces extensive remodeling of the host cellular lipidome in both HEK293T-ACE2 and A549-ACE2 cell models. The observation that approximately 80% of the detected lipids in HEK293T-ACE2 cells and over 50% in A549-ACE2 cells were significantly altered following infection highlights the profound impact of viral replication on host lipid metabolism. These changes, ranging from modest 1.1-fold alterations to dramatic 64-fold shifts in HEK293T-ACE2 cells, underscore the extensive viral dependence on host lipid pathways for successful replication. The fact that such a large proportion of the lipidome is remodeled suggests that lipid metabolism represents a critical vulnerability that SARS-CoV-2 exploits to support its life cycle, consistent with reports from other virus-host systems where lipid biosynthesis and turnover are manipulated to facilitate viral entry, replication, and assembly.

The use of two distinct cell lines in this study provides important insights into the conservation of lipid alterations across different cellular contexts. HEK293T-ACE2 cells, while useful for their high transfection efficiency and robust ACE2 expression, represent a more artificial system, whereas A549-ACE2 cells, derived from human alveolar epithelial tissue, offer a more physiologically relevant model of natural infection. The observation that a substantial subset of lipid changes is conserved between these two cell types suggests that certain lipid metabolic pathways represent core host dependency factors that SARS-CoV-2 manipulates regardless of the cellular background. This conservation across cell lines strengthens the argument that these lipid alterations reflect fundamental viral requirements rather than cell type-specific artifacts, though the differences in the magnitude of changes between the two systems may reflect variations in viral replication kinetics, basal lipid metabolism, or the relative permissiveness of each cell type to infection.

The quantitative approach employed in this study, utilizing internal standards for normalization and rigorous statistical testing with Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons, represents a methodological strength that enhances the reliability of the findings. The demonstration by principal component analysis that infection status is the primary driver of lipid variation, with clear separation between infected and mock-infected samples in both cell lines, provides confidence that the observed changes are attributable to viral infection rather than technical variability or confounding factors. The biological quintuplicates used in this study provide sufficient statistical power to detect meaningful changes while also allowing assessment of reproducibility. Furthermore, the comprehensive profiling of lipids spanning glycerolipids, phospholipids, sphingolipids, and acylcarnitines enables a systems-level understanding of how SARS-CoV-2 reorganizes multiple lipid metabolic pathways simultaneously.

While the current study provides a valuable snapshot of the lipid alterations induced by SARS-CoV-2 at 24 hours post-infection, it is important to consider the dynamic nature of viral replication and the lipid remodeling that likely occurs at different stages of the infection cycle. Future time-course experiments could reveal whether certain lipid classes are altered early in infection to facilitate viral entry and replication, while others are modulated later to support virion assembly and release. Additionally, while the study identifies conserved changes across variants, the specific lipid species affected and their potential roles as host dependency factors warrant further investigation through targeted functional studies, including lipid supplementation experiments, lipid pathway inhibition, and assessment of viral replication under these conditions.

In summary, this lipidomic profiling study establishes that SARS-CoV-2 infection triggers extensive and reproducible remodeling of the host cellular lipidome across multiple cell types. The conservation of key lipid alterations between HEK293T-ACE2 and A549-ACE2 cells suggests the presence of host dependency factors that are consistently exploited by the virus regardless of cellular context. These findings provide a foundation for future studies aimed at understanding the mechanistic basis for viral lipid manipulation and may ultimately inform the development of host-directed therapeutic strategies to combat COVID-19.
