In the present study we performed an integrative analysis of transcriptomic and methylomic data to delineate genomic and epigenomic signatures that may be associated with the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatoblastoma. By interrogating two independent expression datasets (GSE75271 and GSE81928) we identified 134 up‑regulated and 191 down‑regulated genes that were consistently altered in tumor samples relative to normal liver tissue. Parallel examination of the methylation landscape (GSE78732) revealed a predominance of hypomethylated sites (2,337) over hypermethylated sites (1,125), suggesting a broad epigenetic reprogramming that could influence transcriptional fidelity in malignant cells. The intersection of differentially expressed and differentially methylated loci yielded 83 genes that exhibit concerted changes at both the RNA and DNA‑methylation level, providing a refined set of candidates that may directly link epigenetic regulation to gene expression in hepatoblastoma.

The concordant up‑regulation of genes such as BMP4, AXIN2, NKD1 and ROBO1 points to a pronounced activation of the Wnt/β‑catenin signaling axis, a pathway frequently implicated in pediatric liver malignancies and known to modulate cellular proliferation and differentiation. Conversely, the down‑regulation of genes involved in metabolic functions (e.g., CYP1A2, CYP2C8, SLC10A1, XDH, HAO2) reflects the shift from normal hepatic metabolism to a pro‑tumorigenic phenotype, a phenomenon observed in many cancers that may also affect the composition of the tumor microenvironment by altering metabolite availability for infiltrating immune cells. Moreover, the reduced expression of detoxification enzymes such as GSTP1 and GLS2 could diminish cellular protection against oxidative stress, thereby fostering an environment conducive to genomic instability and immune evasion.

The methylation analysis uncovered a pattern of widespread hypomethylation interspersed with focal hypermethylation, reminiscent of the “methylation paradox” reported in several adult cancers. Global hypomethylation may contribute to oncogene activation and retrotransposon re‑activation, while site‑specific hypermethylation, as seen at loci such as SP5, NOTUM and TBX3, could silence tumor‑suppressive or developmental regulators. Of particular note, several of the hyper‑methylated genes (e.g., TNFRSF19, MSX1, HOXA3) encode transcription factors or ligands that are known to participate in immune cell recruitment and cytokine signaling, suggesting that epigenetic silencing of these loci might blunt anti‑tumor immune responses.

Among the 83 genes that displayed coordinated differential expression and methylation, many are mechanistically linked to immune modulation. For instance, TNFRSF19 (a member of the tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily) can influence the activity of natural killer cells and T lymphocytes, while GSTP1 is implicated in the regulation of inflammatory signaling pathways. The concomitant down‑regulation and hypermethylation of these genes may therefore represent a strategy by which hepatoblastoma cells create an immunosuppressive niche. Similarly, the altered methylation status of genes involved in cell‑cell communication (e.g., ROBO1, PTK7) could affect the crosstalk between tumor cells and infiltrating immune populations, further shaping the immune landscape of the lesion.

While the current work provides a comprehensive inventory of candidate biomarkers, several limitations merit consideration. The analyses were performed on publicly available datasets with relatively modest sample sizes, and the findings rely on correlation rather than functional experimentation. Moreover, the precise impact of each identified gene on the tumor immune microenvironment remains to be elucidated through in vitro and in vivo models. Future investigations should focus on (i) validation of the expression and methylation signatures in independent pediatric cohorts, (ii) functional assays (e.g., CRISPR‑based epigenome editing) to confirm causality, and (iii) integration with immune‑cell profiling data to correlate the genomic/epigenomic alterations with specific immune cell subsets. Demonstrating that these signatures predict immune checkpoint responsiveness or patient outcomes would pave the way for their translation into clinically actionable biomarkers for hepatoblastoma management.
