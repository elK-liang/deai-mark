In the present cohort, baseline patient characteristics were comparable between the two treatment groups, with the exception of aneurysm morphology. The WEB‑treated aneurysms had a significantly wider neck (4.7 mm vs. 3.9 mm, p = 0.028) and a correspondingly lower dome‑to‑neck ratio (1.4 vs. 1.7, p = 0.035). These morphological differences suggest that the WEB device was preferentially selected for lesions that are more challenging for conventional coiling, particularly those with a broader neck that could compromise coil containment.

Procedural data revealed a marked contrast in the need for adjunctive devices. While only one patient (2.5 %) in the WEB group required an additional stent, 25 of the 35 coiling procedures (71 %) were performed with stent assistance, and a small number also needed balloon assistance. This disparity reflects the technical difficulty of achieving stable coil occlusion in wide‑neck basilar tip aneurysms without supplementary hardware, and it likely contributed to the longer average treatment time observed in the coiling arm.

The overall procedural efficiency favored the WEB technique. Treatment duration was significantly shorter in the WEB group (111 ± 57 min) compared with the coiling group (152 ± 81 min, p = 0.048). In addition, trends toward reduced patient radiation exposure (DAP) and contrast volume were noted in the WEB arm, although these differences did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.088 and p = 0.071, respectively). The reduction in procedural time and the tendency toward lower radiation and contrast usage may translate into improved patient comfort and a lower cumulative radiation burden, especially for elderly patients.

From a safety perspective, the WEB group experienced two procedure‑related thromboembolic events (5.0 %). In both cases the neurologic deficit was transient, and no patient suffered permanent morbidity (mRS remained unchanged). The absence of treatment failures in either group underscores the technical feasibility of both approaches for unruptured basilar tip aneurysms. Nevertheless, the small sample size limits the power to detect rare adverse events, and longer‑term follow‑up is required to evaluate durability of aneurysm occlusion and late complication rates.

The findings of this study should be interpreted in the context of several limitations. The relatively modest sample size (40 WEB and 35 coiling) reduces the precision of the estimated complication rates and may have contributed to the lack of significance for secondary procedural metrics. Baseline differences in aneurysm neck width and dome‑to‑neck ratio introduce potential confounding, as they may have influenced the choice of device and the likelihood of adjunctive stent use. In addition, the single‑center design and the absence of long‑term angiographic follow‑up limit the generalizability of the results. Future multicenter investigations with larger cohorts and standardized follow‑up protocols are needed to clarify the relative benefits of WEB versus coiling in this specific anatomical location.
