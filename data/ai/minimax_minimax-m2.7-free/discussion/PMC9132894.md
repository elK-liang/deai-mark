The design of FR17 merges a well‑characterized self‑assembling peptide backbone with an MMP2‑responsive linker and the immunomodulatory pentapeptide thymopentin (TP5). The core sequence FFKY, derived from the β‑amyloid fragment Aβ36, confers the ability to form ordered nanostructures, while the GPLGLAGG linker is selectively cleaved by MMP2, an enzyme that is prominently expressed in the pre‑metastatic niche (PMN). Upon cleavage, the monomeric fragment FG8 (FFK(GPLG)Y) is released and spontaneously folds into a lamellar, membrane‑like architecture. Notably, this assembly proceeds efficiently both at neutral pH and under the mildly acidic conditions that characterize the inflammatory PMN microenvironment, ensuring that nano‑blanket formation occurs precisely where and when it is needed. The incorporation of TP5 adds a hydrophilic, immune‑modulating component that can locally influence cytokine profiles, whereas the scrambled control sFD17 provides a structurally identical assembly that lacks TP5 activity, enabling a direct comparison of the contribution of each functional element.  

Morphological characterization by transmission electron microscopy and cryo‑TEM revealed that enzymatically generated assemblies adopt a sheet‑like, lamellar morphology with an average diameter of roughly 500 nm. The consistent thickness and extended surface area of these structures are reminiscent of native membrane bilayers, suggesting that the nano‑blanket could act as a physical barrier at the cell–matrix interface. Dynamic light scattering and AIE‑based fluorescence confirmed that FG8 rapidly aggregates in aqueous solution, forming fluorescent nanoparticles that can be tracked in tissue sections. In vivo imaging of PMN lungs showed fluorescent puncta corresponding to FG8 assemblies, with sizes ranging from hundreds to thousands of nanometers, reflecting the variable dimensions of the intercellular spaces in which they deposit. The lamellar geometry thus appears to accommodate the irregular three‑dimensional architecture of the PMN, providing a conformal coating that can envelop cellular surfaces.  

The inhibitory effect of the nano‑blanket on fibroblast activation likely arises from a combination of steric and biochemical mechanisms. The dense lamellar sheets may physically impede the diffusion of soluble pro‑fibrotic factors such as TGF‑β, thereby reducing their availability to fibroblasts. Concurrently, the TP5 segment released upon MMP2 cleavage retains its known immunomodulatory properties, potentially skewing the local cytokine milieu toward an anti‑inflammatory phenotype that further discourages fibroblast-to‑myofibroblast transition. The fact that the scrambled peptide sFD17, which generates the same FG8 assemblies but lacks TP5, serves as a negative control suggests that both the physical barrier and the immune‑modulating payload contribute to the observed inhibition of fibroblast activation. Because assembly occurs specifically within the MMP2‑rich environment of the PMN, the therapeutic effect is localized, minimizing systemic exposure and off‑target actions.  

Compared with conventional approaches—such as broad‑spectrum MMP inhibitors or direct anti‑fibrotic drugs—the FR17 platform offers distinct advantages. Its activation is driven by endogenous enzymatic activity, conferring spatial specificity that reduces unintended inhibition of MMPs elsewhere in the body. The self‑assembling nature of FG8 allows rapid, autonomous formation of a stable nanostructure without external triggers or cross‑linking agents. By embedding TP5 within the same construct, the system simultaneously addresses the extracellular matrix barrier and the immune signaling that underpin PMN formation. The pH resilience of the assembly ensures functionality across the range of microenvironments encountered during metastatic priming, including hypoxic and acidic niches. These attributes position the nano‑blanket as a multifaceted, locally acting intervention capable of preventing the initial steps of metastatic dissemination.  

While the present work provides a compelling proof‑of‑concept, several translational considerations remain. The in vivo longevity of the lamellar coating under physiological fluid shear and ongoing enzymatic activity has yet to be defined, and systematic pharmacokinetic studies are required to determine dosing regimens that maintain effective concentrations at PMN sites. Potential immunogenicity of the peptide components, although mitigated by the use of endogenous sequences, warrants careful evaluation. Future investigations could explore the synergistic potential of combining FR17 with standard therapies such as surgery or systemic chemotherapy, as well as the incorporation of additional targeting motifs to enhance accumulation within early PMNs. Nonetheless, the data demonstrate that a cleavable, self‑assembling peptide can generate a protective nano‑blanket that impedes fibroblast activation and curtails the formation of a pre‑metastatic niche, offering a novel therapeutic avenue to inhibit cancer metastasis at its earliest stage.
