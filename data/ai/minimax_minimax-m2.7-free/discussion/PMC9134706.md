The findings of the present study establish that the long non‑coding RNA DLEU1 is markedly overexpressed in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) relative to adjacent non‑tumor tissues. Analysis of two independent patient cohorts revealed that DLEU1 transcripts were elevated in more than 98 % of paired tumor samples, and high expression was significantly associated with reduced overall survival, lymph node metastasis, and advanced clinical stage (Fig. 1C–G; Additional file 1: Table S2). These clinical correlations were reinforced by RNA‑scope validation, which demonstrated localized up‑regulation of DLEU1 within tumor foci. Collectively, these observations position DLEU1 as a previously unrecognized oncogenic lncRNA whose abundance portends a more aggressive disease phenotype in ESCC.

Transient silencing of DLEU1 with a pooled siRNA strategy led to a pronounced reduction in cell growth (Fig. 1B), indicating that tumor cells are dependent on DLEU1 for their survival. Gene‑set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of transcriptomic profiles from the same tumors further revealed that DLEU1‑high tumors are enriched for gene signatures characteristic of cancer stem cells, metastatic progression, and, notably, genes controlling mitochondrial membrane permeability and the intrinsic apoptotic pathway (Fig. 1I). This pattern suggests that DLEU1 may exert its pro‑tumor effects, at least in part, by modulating the cellular decision between survival and apoptosis.

Mechanistic investigations uncovered a novel axis through which DLEU1 promotes cell survival. Co‑immunoprecipitation and ubiquitinylation assays demonstrated that DLEU1 binds to the dynein light‑chain protein DYNLL1, shielding it from poly‑ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation (Fig. 2). The E3 ubiquitin ligase RNF114 was identified as the principal enzyme responsible for DYNLL1 ubiquitination, and this activity was attenuated in the presence of DLEU1. Stabilized DYNLL1, in turn, bound to and inhibited the pro‑apoptotic function of BCL2‑associated agonist of cell death (BAD), thereby dampening the mitochondrial apoptotic cascade. These data delineate a linear pathway—DLEU1 → DYNLL1 stabilization → inhibition of BCL2‑mediated apoptosis—that accounts for the observed dependence of ESCC cells on DLEU1 for survival.

The clinical and biological relevance of the DLEU1‑DYNLL1‑RNF114‑BCL2 axis offers multiple translational opportunities. First, quantifying DLEU1 levels in diagnostic biopsies could serve as a prognostic biomarker to identify patients at risk for nodal involvement and unfavorable survival. Second, strategies that disrupt the physical interaction between DLEU1 and DYNLL1, such as antisense oligonucleotides or small‑molecule inhibitors, merit exploration as targeted therapies aimed at re‑activating the apoptotic program in DLEU1‑addicted tumors. Moreover, the demonstration that RNF114‑mediated ubiquitination governs DYNLL1 stability suggests that pharmacological modulation of this E3 ligase activity could provide an alternative means to unleash cell death in ESCC.

While the present work provides compelling evidence for the oncogenic role of DLEU1, several limitations should be acknowledged. The majority of functional data were generated in established ESCC cell lines; future studies should extend these observations to patient‑derived xenografts and genetically engineered mouse models to confirm the in vivo relevance of the DLEU1‑DYNLL1 pathway. Additionally, the upstream mechanisms that drive DLEU1 over‑expression in ESCC remain to be elucidated, and whether epigenetic or transcriptional regulators contribute to its tumor‑specific up‑regulation warrants investigation. Nevertheless, the convergence of clinical association, functional dependency, and a defined molecular mechanism positions DLEU1 as a promising target for prognostic assessment and therapeutic intervention in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.
