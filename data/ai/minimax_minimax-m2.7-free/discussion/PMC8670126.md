The present study provides quantitative evidence that African American (AA) patients with triple‑negative breast cancer (TNBC) harbor a distinct tumor immune microenvironment compared with non‑African American (NAA) patients. Using multiplex immunofluorescence, we observed a statistically significant increase in overall leukocyte infiltration (CD45⁺ cells) in AA TNBC samples, with a parallel rise in both lymphoid (CD45⁺CD14⁻) and, to a lesser extent, myeloid (CD45⁺CD14⁺) compartments. This pattern aligns with prior reports that AA TNBC is associated with heightened systemic and intratumoral inflammation, suggesting that the racial disparity in TNBC may, in part, be mediated by a more pronounced immune response within the tumor bed.

The enrichment of CD45⁺ cells in AA tumors could reflect several underlying mechanisms. Increased neoantigen load, epigenetic alterations, or differences in the tumor mutational landscape may render AA TNBCs more immunogenic, thereby recruiting larger numbers of immune cells. Alternatively, systemic factors such as chronic inflammation related to comorbidities, socioeconomic stressors, or differences in microbiome composition could prime the tumor microenvironment for enhanced immune cell trafficking. Irrespective of the driver, the present data indicate that the heightened immune infiltration is not limited to a single lineage but spans both myeloid and lymphoid compartments, implying a broad activation of the host immune response.

A particularly noteworthy observation was the higher proportion of CD3⁺ T cells that exhibited an activated phenotype (as defined by Ki67 positivity) in AA TNBC. This finding, combined with the significantly elevated Ki67 expression across the AA cohort, suggests that T cells in these tumors are more proliferative and potentially more engaged in tumor surveillance. In contrast, the level of granzyme B (GzB) was only marginally higher and did not reach statistical significance, indicating that while T‑cell proliferation may be increased, the cytotoxic effector function may not be proportionally up‑regulated. The absence of a significant difference in regulatory T cells (FOXP3⁺CD4⁺) further suggests that the balance between immune activation and suppression is not overtly shifted toward immunosuppression in AA TNBC, at least at the phenotypic level measured here.

When individual immune subsets were interrogated, no statistically significant differences emerged for CD68⁺ macrophages, CD206⁺ alternative macrophages, CD3⁺, CD4⁺, CD8⁺ T cells, or CD20⁺ B cells. Nonetheless, the numerical trends toward higher CD3⁺, CD8⁺, and CD20⁺ counts in AA samples hint that the overall increase in immune cellularity may be distributed across multiple lineages without marked skewing toward any single population. This pattern could reflect a more generalized inflammatory milieu rather than a specific immune subtype dominance, and it may also be influenced by the modest sample size, which limits statistical power to detect subtle differences in rarer cell types.

From a clinical perspective, the observed enhancement of immune infiltration and T‑cell activation in AA TNBC raises the possibility that this patient group could derive particular benefit from immune‑checkpoint inhibitors or other immunomodulatory therapies. However, the lack of survival or treatment‑response data in this cross‑sectional study precludes direct inference about therapeutic efficacy. Moreover, the higher baseline immune activity might also imply a greater risk of immune‑related adverse events, underscoring the need for careful patient selection and monitoring. Several limitations temper the interpretation of our findings: the study was conducted at a single institution with a relatively limited cohort, which may affect generalizability; clinical covariates such as tumor stage, body‑mass index, and socioeconomic status were not fully controlled; and the quantitative immunofluorescence assay, while robust, captures only phenotypic markers and does not provide functional readouts. Future investigations should expand to multi‑center cohorts, integrate transcriptomic profiling to capture immune activation signatures, and correlate the immunophenotypic patterns with clinical outcomes, thereby clarifying whether the heightened immune landscape translates into improved survival or differential therapeutic responsiveness in AA patients with TNBC.
