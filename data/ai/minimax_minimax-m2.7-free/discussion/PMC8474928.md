The comprehensive characterization of 30 m6A regulators in small‑cell lung cancer (SCLC) reveals that genetic and transcriptional alterations of these enzymes are pervasive and likely to shape the malignant phenotype. By curating 11 writers, two erasers and 17 readers, we provided a complete map of the principal components that install, remove and interpret the m6A mark. Our analysis of somatic mutations in 110 SCLC specimens uncovered a 25.5 % mutation burden, with readers accounting for the majority of altered genes and FMR1 standing out as the most frequently mutated member. Notably, no erasers carried mutations, implying that loss of demethylation activity is not a selected event in SCLC, whereas enhanced reading or writing functions may be preferentially favoured. The co‑occurrence of METTL3–YTHDC2 and IGF2BP2–YTHDC2 mutations further hints at functional synergy among specific writer–reader pairs, although the mechanistic consequences of these concurrent alterations remain to be elucidated.

Copy‑number variation (CNV) profiling across 53 SCLC cell lines demonstrated that virtually all m6A regulators undergo genomic amplification or deletion, underscoring their general susceptibility to structural genomic changes. Strikingly, the majority of readers (11/17) exhibited widespread CNV amplification, whereas both erasers displayed a predominance of loss events. This dichotomy suggests a model in which amplified readers augment m6A‑mediated post‑transcriptional regulation, while eraser loss diminishes the capacity to remove the mark, collectively tipping the balance toward a hyper‑m6A state. The broad chromosomal distribution of these CNVs indicates that the alterations are not confined to a single locus but represent a genome‑wide tendency of SCLC cells to reshape the m6A epitranscriptome.

Expression analyses confirmed that the genetic perturbations translate into marked transcriptomic differences between normal lung tissue and SCLC. Principal component analysis separated normal and tumor samples, and detailed comparisons showed that most writers and readers are significantly up‑regulated in tumors, whereas erasers tend to be down‑regulated. The concordance between CNV amplification and increased expression for many of these genes supports the notion that copy‑number gains contribute directly to the observed over‑expression. The overall pattern—elevated writers and readers together with reduced erasers—is consistent with a net increase in m6A deposition, a feature that has been linked to aggressive phenotypes in several other cancer types. Notably, IGF2BP3, a known onco‑fetal reader, was identified at markedly higher levels in SCLC, extending its previously reported elevation in solid tumours to this neuroendocrine malignancy for the first time.

When the cohort was stratified by pathological origin, the majority of writers and readers displayed higher expression in metastasis‑derived cell lines relative to primary‑derived lines. This observation implies that the enhanced m6A regulatory network may not only accompany tumorigenesis but also correlate with the acquisition of invasive properties. Although causality cannot be inferred from expression data alone, the trend suggests that dysregulated m6A machinery could facilitate metastatic spread by modulating the stability or translation of transcripts involved in cell motility, extracellular‑matrix remodeling, or epithelial‑to‑mesenchymal transition. IGF2BP3’s pronounced up‑regulation further aligns with its documented role in promoting invasion and colonization in other cancers, warranting focused functional investigation in SCLC.

Collectively, these findings position m6A regulators as frequent targets of genetic and transcriptional dysregulation in SCLC, with potential ramifications for disease initiation, progression, and therapeutic vulnerability. The prevalence of reader amplifications and writer up‑regulation, coupled with eraser loss, points to a synthetic‑lethal window that could be exploited by inhibitors targeting m6A‑dependent RNA interactions or by strategies that restore demethylation activity. Moreover, the distinct mutation and expression signatures may serve as biomarkers for risk stratification or for monitoring response to epitranscriptomic‑directed therapies. Nevertheless, the current analysis is limited to computational inferences from public databases, and prospective studies are required to validate the functional impact of specific mutations, CNVs, and expression changes in experimental models and clinical cohorts. Future work should dissect the downstream RNA targets of the altered regulators, assess the therapeutic index of m6A‑modulating agents in SCLC cell lines and patient‑derived xenografts, and explore combination regimens that integrate epitranscriptomic inhibition with existing standards of care.
