The identification of a novel missense mutation (c.G344T, p.R115L) in the homeodomain of PITX2 in the egl1 mutant mouse provides the first direct evidence that a Pitx2 allele can drive early‑onset glaucoma. By combining whole‑exome sequencing with functional validation in a CRISPR‑generated knock‑in line, we demonstrated that the R115L substitution is both necessary and sufficient to produce the cardinal features of the disease: elevated intraocular pressure, optic‑nerve cupping, retinal‑nerve‑fiber‑layer thinning and progressive loss of inner retinal architecture. The fact that the phenotype is reproduced in the Pitx2R115L KI mice, which retain normal PITX2 protein levels and subcellular localization, indicates that the glaucoma‑inducing effect of the mutation stems from an alteration of PITX2’s functional activity rather than a gross loss of protein expression.  

The mutated residue resides within the DNA‑binding homeodomain, a region that is highly conserved from rodents to humans and that mediates the canonical interactions of PITX2 with its transcriptional targets. In vitro studies of other homeodomain missense mutations have shown that even subtle changes in this domain can impair DNA‑binding affinity or alter the recognition of specific gene promoters, leading to dysregulated downstream signaling. The absence of detectable changes in PITX2 abundance or overall nuclear distribution in the mutant retina suggests that the R115L substitution selectively disrupts the recruitment of co‑regulators or the transcriptional complexes required for normal ocular homeostasis. Consequently, pathways that rely on PITX2‑mediated transcription may be subtly rewired, setting the stage for the observed ocular phenotype.  

Immunohistochemical analysis revealed robust PITX2 expression in the retinal ganglion cell (RGC) layer, where it extensively overlapped with the RGC marker Brn3a. This anatomical pattern positions PITX2 as a plausible intrinsic regulator of RGC survival or function. Because RGCs are the primary cell type lost in glaucomatous neurodegeneration, a defect in a transcription factor that is enriched in these cells could explain the selective inner‑retinal thinning and optic‑nerve deterioration observed in the mutants. The maintenance of normal PITX2 protein levels in the mutant retina further suggests that the disease mechanism does not involve simple haploinsufficiency but rather a qualitative loss of transcriptional output specific to RGC‑related genes.  

The title of this work invokes the NRF2‑YAP1 axis as a downstream effector of the Pitx2 mutation. Both NRF2 and YAP1 are well‑characterized nodes that couple cellular redox balance and mechanical stress responses to survival pathways. In many ocular tissues, activation of NRF2‑mediated antioxidant signaling protects against oxidative damage that accompanies elevated intraocular pressure, while YAP1, a key mediator of the Hippo pathway, modulates cell proliferation, apoptosis and mechanotransduction. A plausible scenario emerging from our data is that the R115L mutation in PITX2 disrupts its ability to transcriptionally regulate NRF2 and YAP1, leading to diminished antioxidant capacity and altered mechanical‑stress sensing within RGCs. The resulting vulnerability would be exacerbated by the chronic elevation of intraocular pressure, accelerating RGC loss and optic‑nerve cupping. This mechanistic framework aligns with the observed early onset and progressive nature of the disease and offers testable hypotheses for future biochemical studies.  

The knock‑in model faithfully recapitulates the egl1 phenotype, validating the causal relationship between the Pitx2 missense mutation and glaucoma. Notably, the penetrance of the overt ocular phenotype (≈59 % with bulging eyes) and the high rate of bilateral involvement (≈80 %) closely mirror the variability reported in human Axenfeld‑Rieger syndrome, where not all mutation carriers develop clinically significant glaucoma. While the mouse model displays a relatively mild corneal opacity, the primary pathology—elevated IOP coupled with structural evidence of optic‑nerve damage—mirrors the core features of human disease. A limitation of the current study is that the mechanistic link between PITX2 and the NRF2‑YAP1 axis remains correlative; detailed chromatin immunoprecipitation and transcriptomic analyses in mutant retinas will be required to delineate the precise gene‑regulatory networks that are rewired by the R115L substitution. Moreover, the relevance of these findings to human patients with PITX2 mutations will need to be explored through comparative genomic and functional studies.  

In summary, the discovery that a homeodomain missense mutation in Pitx2 precipitates early‑onset glaucoma via a mechanism that likely involves dysregulation of the NRF2‑YAP1 axis expands our understanding of the genetic architecture of glaucoma. The PITX2‑R115L knock‑in mouse provides a valuable preclinical platform for evaluating antioxidant or Hippo‑pathway–targeted interventions aimed at preserving RGC function under conditions of elevated intraocular pressure. Future investigations that dissect the direct transcriptional targets of PITX2 in the retina and validate analogous mutations in human cohorts will be essential to translate these insights into therapeutic strategies for patients with PITX2‑associated glaucoma.
