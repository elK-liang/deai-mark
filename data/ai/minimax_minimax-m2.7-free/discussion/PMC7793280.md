The findings presented in this study establish a novel genetic regulator of glial cell size through the identification and characterization of the yad70 mutant. The observation that AMsh cells in yad70 animals progressively enlarge from larval stages to adulthood, ultimately reaching approximately four times the size of control cells, indicates a fundamental disruption in the mechanisms controlling glial morphology. The complete penetrance of this phenotype by day 2 of adulthood, combined with the specific enlargement of glial cells without a corresponding increase in animal size, demonstrates that yad70 affects cell-intrinsic pathways governing glial volume rather than general growth processes. Furthermore, the resistance of this phenotype to nutritional manipulation through starvation suggests that the size increase is not a secondary consequence of altered metabolic status but rather reflects a primary defect in cellular architecture. The parallel enlargement observed in phasmid sheath glia indicates that the affected gene may regulate size across multiple glial subtypes, pointing to a potentially conserved mechanism for controlling glial cell volume.

The behavioral analyses provide compelling evidence that the morphological changes in AMsh cells have significant functional consequences for neural circuit function. The deficits in chemotaxis toward benzaldehyde and pyrazine, which are mediated by AWC and AWA neurons respectively, along with the impaired avoidance response to 1-octanol mediated by ADL neurons, closely mirror the behavioral phenotypes previously reported following AMsh cell ablation. This correspondence suggests that the enlarged AMsh cells in yad70 animals are functionally compromised, similar to the consequences of complete cell removal. The observation that amphid neurons appear morphologically normal, with functional cilia as indicated by DiI uptake, argues against a primary neurodegenerative process affecting neurons directly. Instead, the data support a model in which the structural integrity or signaling capacity of AMsh glia is specifically impaired, leading to secondary effects on neuronal function and behavior.

The progressive nature of the glial enlargement phenotype raises important questions about the underlying cellular mechanisms. Given that the size increase occurs over developmental time and is not suppressed by starvation, the mechanism likely involves chronic dysregulation of processes controlling cell volume homeostasis rather than acute osmotic stress. The similarity between the yad70 phenotype and reports of vacuolation or organelle enlargement in various neurodegenerative models raises the possibility that the enlarged AMsh cells may contain accumulated organelles or vesicles. The increase in cell size without a corresponding increase in neuronal size or number suggests that the mutant gene may specifically affect membrane trafficking or organelle dynamics in glia. Further investigation of specific organelles, including late endosomes and lysosomes as indicated by LAMP-2 staining, will be essential to determine whether organelle accumulation contributes to the observed size changes.

The specificity of the yad70 phenotype to glial cells, while sparing neurons, has intriguing implications for understanding cell-type-specific regulation of cell size. This selectivity suggests that the affected gene may function in pathways that are particularly important for glial cell physiology, potentially related to their extensive interactions with neurons or their role in maintaining extracellular homeostasis. The absence of a general increase in animal size despite the dramatic enlargement of specific glial populations indicates that overall body size regulation operates independently of cell-type-specific size control mechanisms. Understanding the molecular basis of this cell-type specificity will be important for comprehending how different cell types maintain their characteristic sizes and how these processes can become dysregulated in disease states.

In summary, the identification of yad70 as a regulator of glial cell size provides a foundation for understanding the genetic pathways controlling glial morphology. The functional consequences of glial enlargement on behavior, combined with the developmental progression of the phenotype and its specificity to glial cells, highlight the importance of glial cell size regulation for nervous system function. Future studies identifying the affected gene and elucidating its molecular function will be crucial for determining whether the mechanism involves altered membrane synthesis, organelle dynamics, or cytoskeletal regulation. The robustness and complete penetrance of the yad70 phenotype make it an excellent model for dissecting the cellular and molecular mechanisms by which glia maintain their characteristic size and how these processes impact neural circuit function and animal behavior.
