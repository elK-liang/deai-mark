The findings presented in this study underscore the critical need for ultra-sensitive biomarker detection methods when quantifying low-abundance cytokines such as human interleukin-33 (hIL-33) in complex biological matrices. Our systematic evaluation of commercially available assay platforms and reagents revealed that while several kits demonstrated impressive limits of quantitation in both buffer and vitreous humor (VH), none satisfied all four essential criteria we established for robust biomarker quantification in human samples. This observation is particularly significant given that hIL-33 functions as a nuclear cytokine with restricted expression patterns, and its detection often requires sensitivities in the single-digit picogram per milliliter range to capture biologically relevant concentrations under non-pathological or moderate disease conditions. The failure of commercially available assays to meet our comprehensive criteria highlights a broader challenge in the field of cytokine biomarker development, where analytical sensitivity must be balanced against matrix interference and assay specificity.

The issue of soluble ST2 (hsST2) tolerance emerged as a particularly critical factor in our assessment. Our preliminary data demonstrated that mean hsST2 concentrations in VH and serum samples were 2.05 ng/mL and 19.8 ng/mL, respectively, values that substantially exceed the tolerance limits of most commercial assays we evaluated. The U-PLEX Human IL-33 MSD kit, which exhibited one of the lowest limits of quantitation in VH at 2.4 pg/mL, could only tolerate 0.5 ng/mL of hsST2—far below physiological concentrations found in human matrices. Similarly, the Bio-Plex Pro Human Th17 Cytokine 15-Plex Kit showed acceptable sensitivity but demonstrated minimal tolerance to hsST2 at only 0.016 ng/mL. This interference is particularly relevant because hsST2 functions as a decoy receptor for IL-33, and the presence of excess hsST2 in biological samples can sequester the cytokine, potentially leading to underestimation of true IL-33 concentrations. Our requirement for tolerance to at least 10 ng/mL hsST2 was therefore not arbitrary but rather based on the physiological concentrations encountered in the target patient populations for age-related macular degeneration and asthma studies.

The specificity assessment via immunodepletion experiments provided another dimension of assay evaluation that many commercial platforms failed to address adequately. While several assays demonstrated acceptable limits of quantitation and hsST2 tolerance, the inability to achieve complete immunodepletion of hIL-33 from spiked human VH samples indicates potential issues with antibody recognition of the target antigen in its native conformation or the presence of interfering substances that block antibody binding. The requirement for 100% ± 20% immunodepletion ensures that the assay can accurately measure total hIL-33 concentrations regardless of whether the cytokine exists in free, bound, or matrix-associated forms. This criterion is particularly important for clinical biomarker applications where sample handling and storage conditions may influence the binding characteristics of IL-33 with its various binding partners, including hsST2 and extracellular matrix components.

The volume constraint of 50 µL maximum sample input presented an additional challenge that some commercial assays could not accommodate. This limitation reflects the practical realities of clinical sample collection, particularly for ocular matrices such as vitreous humor, where sampling is invasive and yields limited volumes. Assays requiring larger sample volumes would necessitate excessive dilution, which compromises the achievable sensitivity for low-abundance targets like IL-33. Interestingly, the Quanterix platform, which was evaluated using the Mu xhIL-33 (Enzo)/Biotin-mu xhIL-33 (Novus) antibody pair, required 150 µL of sample volume—three times our established maximum—making it unsuitable for our intended applications despite potentially acceptable sensitivity in other parameters.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the development of a fit-for-purpose ultra-sensitive hIL-33 assay requires careful consideration of multiple performance characteristics beyond simple sensitivity metrics. The commercial assay landscape, while offering increasingly sophisticated platforms, does not yet provide a ready-made solution for simultaneous quantification of hIL-33 in the presence of physiological concentrations of hsST2 using minimal sample volumes. This gap in available tools necessitated our efforts to develop an optimized assay that balances all four critical parameters. Future studies should focus on systematic optimization of antibody pairs and assay formats to achieve the required performance characteristics, potentially leveraging emerging technologies such as digital immunoassays or novel nanoparticle-based detection systems that may offer improved tolerance to matrix effects while maintaining the necessary sensitivity for clinical biomarker applications in ophthalmology and pulmonary medicine.
