The electrochemically driven C–H phosphorylation of benzoferrocenes described herein represents a mild and regioselective method for constructing C–P bonds on group 8 metallocenes without the need for stoichiometric oxidants. Under the optimized conditions (RVC anode, Pt cathode, nBu₄NOAc electrolyte in methanol, 2 mA constant current, Et₃N as base), the model substrate 1a reacted with diphenyl phosphine oxide 2a to give the monophosphorylated product 3a in 73 % isolated yield with excellent α‑selectivity on the phenyl ring. Notably, the reaction proceeds at room temperature and tolerates a range of functional groups, underscoring its practicality for late‑stage functionalization of ferrocene derivatives.

Optimization studies revealed that the choice of electrolyte is critical: tetrabutylammonium acetate (nBu₄NOAc) furnished the highest yield and selectivity, whereas other salts such as nBu₄NBF₄ or LiClO₄·3H₂O gave markedly lower conversions. This suggests that acetate not only serves as the supporting electrolyte but also acts as a mild base that deprotonates the phosphine oxide without interfering with the electrode processes. The reticulated vitreous carbon (RVC) anode proved superior to platinum or graphite, delivering higher current efficiency and better control over the mono‑ to bis‑phosphorylation ratio. Increasing the applied current to 3.0 or 5.0 mA accelerated the reaction but also promoted over‑phosphorylation, generating side‑products 4a–6a and diminishing regioselectivity. The addition of Et₃N further enhanced the yield, whereas stronger bases (NaOAc, K₃PO₄) or alternative solvents (TFE, DCE, MeCN) were less effective, indicating that a balanced combination of base strength and solvent polarity is essential for optimal performance.

The substrate scope demonstrates that the electrochemical protocol is broadly applicable to variously substituted benzoferrocenes. Electron‑withdrawing (Br), electron‑donating (Me), and aryl (Ph) groups at the α‑position of the aryl ring are well tolerated, delivering the corresponding monophosphorylated products 3a–3c in decent yields. Importantly, substrates lacking protecting groups on the cyclopentadienyl (Cp) ring still undergo phosphorylation exclusively at the α‑position of the phenyl moiety (3d–3f). The observed regioselectivity is likely governed by the electronic bias of the benzoferrocene scaffold, where the α‑C–H bond of the phenyl ring is more susceptible to anodic oxidation. Minor amounts of bis‑phosphorylated side‑products were detected, but their formation could be minimized by maintaining the current at 2 mA and using Et₃N, highlighting the tunability of the process.

Mechanistically, the reaction is thought to proceed via anodic oxidation of the benzoferrocene to generate a radical cation, which undergoes deprotonation at the α‑position of the phenyl ring. The resulting aryl radical is then trapped by diphenyl phosphine oxide to form the C–P bond, with acetate or Et₃N facilitating the deprotonation steps. The observed dependence of selectivity on current density suggests that the lifetime of the radical intermediate is a key determinant: higher currents lead to faster electron transfer, increasing the probability of second‑order radical‑radical coupling and thus bis‑phosphorylation. The absence of product formation under open‑circuit conditions confirms that the electrochemical input is essential for initiating the C–H activation event.

Compared with traditional stoichiometric oxidants (AgF, MnO₂, DDQ), the electrochemical approach offers a more sustainable and controllable manifold, avoiding the generation of stoichiometric waste and providing precise modulation of reaction pathways through potential and current. The ability to perform the transformation at ambient temperature further enhances its synthetic utility, enabling integration into existing synthetic sequences without specialized equipment. Future studies will focus on expanding the scope to other group 8 metallocenes, elucidating the precise radical intermediates through spectroscopic techniques, and exploring preparative‑scale electrolysis for the synthesis of phosphine‑functionalized materials.
