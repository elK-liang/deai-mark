The findings presented in this study corroborate and extend previous observations regarding the aberrant expression of BORIS (CTCFL) in melanoma. Our analysis of expression datasets across normal and malignant tissues demonstrates that BORIS is significantly elevated in melanoma samples compared to normal skin, with expression levels comparable to those observed in testis, which is the only normal tissue known to express BORIS. This pattern of ectopic expression is consistent with the concept of cancer-testis antigen expression in malignancies. Furthermore, the observation that metastatic melanoma samples exhibit significantly higher BORIS expression than primary melanoma lesions suggests that BORIS may not only be associated with melanomagenesis but could also contribute to disease progression. The confirmation of BORIS protein expression in melanoma cell lines but not in non-malignant congenital nevi cells further supports the notion that BORIS expression is a hallmark of malignant transformation in melanocytic lesions rather than a general feature of melanocytic proliferation.

To investigate the functional consequences of BORIS expression in melanoma, we established a doxycycline-inducible system in the MM057 cell line, which normally expresses low levels of BORIS compared to other melanoma cell lines. This experimental approach allowed us to temporally control BORIS expression and assess its effects on cellular behavior without the confounding variables associated with constitutive expression. The initial observation that forced BORIS expression led to a striking reduction in cell number during routine culture prompted us to systematically characterize this phenotype. Our dose-response and time-course experiments revealed that the antiproliferative effect of BORIS is both time and concentration dependent, with significant reductions in proliferation observed after 5 and 7 days of induction even at low doxycycline concentrations. Importantly, control cells harboring the empty vector showed no changes in proliferation regardless of doxycycline treatment, confirming that the observed effects are specifically attributable to BORIS expression rather than any artifacts of the inducible system or doxycycline itself.

This temporal requirement could reflect the need for BORIS to regulate downstream target genes involved in cell cycle control or survival pathways. The optimization of induction conditions (50–100 ng/ml doxycycline for 5 days) provides a framework for future mechanistic studies aimed at dissecting the molecular basis of BORIS-mediated growth suppression in melanoma cells.

The apparent contradiction between elevated BORIS expression in aggressive metastatic melanoma and the antiproliferative effects observed upon forced expression in cell culture may be reconciled by considering the context-dependent nature of BORIS function. In advanced tumors, BORIS expression may confer selective advantages through mechanisms other than direct growth promotion, such as enhanced invasion, resistance to cell death, or modulation of the tumor microenvironment. Alternatively, melanoma cells in vivo may develop compensatory mechanisms that mitigate the antiproliferative effects of BORIS while retaining or amplifying its pro-invasive functions. The discrepancy also highlights the importance of examining multiple cellular phenotypes beyond proliferation when assessing the role of oncogenic regulators, as highlighted by the preliminary observation that altered BORIS expression affects apoptosis in melanoma cells. Future studies will need to integrate these different aspects of BORIS function to fully understand its role in melanoma progression.

In summary, this study provides compelling evidence that BORIS is aberrantly expressed in melanoma and that its induction in melanoma cells results in a profound reduction in proliferative capacity that is dependent on both the duration and level of expression. These findings position BORIS as a regulator of melanoma cell fate and suggest that its elevated expression in metastatic lesions may reflect a switch from a proliferative toward a more invasive phenotype. Further investigation of the molecular targets and downstream pathways activated by BORIS will be essential for understanding how this cancer-testis antigen contributes to melanoma aggressiveness and for evaluating its potential as a therapeutic target in metastatic disease.
