The present analysis of the prospective QUACK trial demonstrates that early tumor shrinkage (ETS), defined as a reduction of ≥20 % in target lesions after the first two cycles of first‑line cetuximab‑based chemotherapy, was achieved in 64.1 % of patients with unresectable metastatic colorectal cancer. Patients who experienced ETS had a markedly higher objective response rate (76.8 % vs. 21.7 %; P < .001) and were more likely to undergo surgical resection of metastases (28.6 % vs. 11.4 %; P = .031). These data corroborate the findings of earlier cohort studies that reported ETS frequencies ranging from 61.5 % to 80 % in similar patient populations treated with anti‑EGFR agents, indicating that the QUACK cohort is representative of the broader experience with cetuximab plus chemotherapy in the first‑line setting.

A biologically plausible link between early tumor debulking and improved outcomes has been proposed. Rapid reduction in tumor burden may decrease the release of pro‑inflammatory cytokines, alleviate mass‑effect symptoms, and enhance the efficacy of subsequent surgical intervention. In our cohort, the higher rate of metastasectomy among ETS‑positive patients suggests that achieving early shrinkage could broaden the window for curative‑intent surgery, a notion supported by surgical series that emphasize the importance of tumor downsizing in converting initially unresectable disease to operable metastases.

Although the primary clinical endpoints were encouraging, patient‑reported outcomes (PROs) at baseline did not differ significantly between the two ETS groups. Global health status, social, role, cognitive, and emotional functioning scores were comparable, with only a marginal trend toward better physical functioning in the ETS cohort (P = .051). Baseline symptom burden, captured by eight EORTC QLQ‑C30 items, was present in 39.8 % of patients, yet the distribution was similar across ETS categories. This suggests that, at the time of treatment initiation, the two groups were largely homogeneous in terms of quality of life, reinforcing the need for longitudinal PRO assessments to determine whether the observed early radiological benefit translates into meaningful symptom relief and improved QoL over the course of therapy.

From a clinical perspective, the strong association between ETS and both response rate and resection probability positions early tumor shrinkage as a promising early surrogate marker for treatment efficacy. If validated in larger, independent cohorts, ETS could be used to guide therapeutic decisions—such as continuation, escalation, or de‑escalation of therapy—within the first few weeks of treatment. However, the present data do not yet establish a direct causal link between ETS and patient‑reported quality of life, and caution is warranted when using ETS as a sole metric for treatment modification.

Several limitations of this analysis should be acknowledged. The QUACK trial was a single‑center, Phase II study with a relatively modest sample size (n = 128), which may limit generalizability and statistical power for subgroup analyses. Although baseline clinicopathological characteristics were well balanced, the only significant imbalance was in tumor differentiation, a factor that could influence treatment sensitivity. Moreover, PRO data were assessed only at baseline; the absence of serial QoL measurements precludes evaluation of temporal changes that might correlate with ETS. Future investigations should incorporate repeated EORTC QLQ‑C30 assessments at standardized intervals, as well as longer follow‑up to determine whether early tumor shrinkage ultimately translates into superior progression‑free and overall survival, and whether the observed improvements in response and resection rates are sustained in a broader patient population.
