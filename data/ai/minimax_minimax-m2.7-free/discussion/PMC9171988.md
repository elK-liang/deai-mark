The findings of the present study demonstrate that Junctional adhesion molecule-like protein (JAML) is significantly upregulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) tissues compared to adjacent peritumor tissues, as evidenced by both mRNA and protein analysis in 15 paired samples, as well as immunohistochemical evaluation in an independent cohort of 30 paired tissues. This consistent upregulation across different methodological approaches strongly suggests that JAML expression is altered during LUAD tumorigenesis. Furthermore, statistical analysis revealed that high JAML expression was significantly associated with advanced pT stage and higher pTNM stage, indicating that JAML may contribute to tumor progression and aggressiveness in LUAD. Notably, no significant correlations were observed between JAML expression and patient age, gender, nodal status (pN), or metastatic status (pM), suggesting that JAML may primarily influence local tumor invasion and staging rather than systemic dissemination or patient demographics.

The association between elevated JAML expression and advanced pT and pTNM stages implies that JAML may play a role in promoting tumor invasion and local progression in LUAD. The pT stage reflects tumor size and local invasion, while pTNM stage provides an overall assessment of disease progression. The correlation of JAML expression specifically with these parameters, rather than with nodal or distant metastasis, suggests that JAML might be involved in early-to-intermediate stages of tumor progression, potentially affecting the invasive capacity of tumor cells. This pattern of association warrants further investigation into the specific mechanisms by which JAML influences tumor cell behavior, particularly regarding invasion and migration capabilities.

To investigate the functional role of JAML in LUAD cells, we selected cell line models based on their endogenous JAML expression levels. A549 and H1299 cells, which exhibited relatively high JAML expression, were chosen for knockdown studies to assess the effects of JAML loss-of-function. Conversely, PC9 and H1975 cells with relatively low JAML expression were selected for overexpression experiments to evaluate the consequences of JAML gain-of-function. This strategy allows for a comprehensive analysis of JAML's role in both high-expressing and low-expressing contexts, providing complementary insights into its function. The validation of siRNA efficiency using siJAML1 ensures the reliability of the knockdown approach in subsequent functional assays.

Immunofluorescence analysis confirmed that JAML is localized predominantly in the cytoplasm of LUAD cells, which is consistent with its described function as a cell surface adhesion molecule that can also interact with intracellular signaling pathways. Cytoplasmic localization suggests that JAML may engage in signaling cascades beyond its traditional role in cell-cell adhesion, potentially influencing downstream effectors that regulate cell proliferation, survival, or motility. Given the title of this manuscript indicating involvement of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, future studies should investigate whether JAML modulates β-catenin nuclear translocation, transcriptional activity, or expression of downstream targets in LUAD cells.

The clinical implications of these findings suggest that JAML may serve as a potential biomarker for identifying LUAD patients with more aggressive tumors, particularly those with advanced local invasion. However, the lack of correlation with pN and pM stages indicates that JAML may not be a determinant of metastatic potential in this cohort, which limits its utility as a sole prognostic marker. Further studies with larger patient cohorts and long-term follow-up data are necessary to establish whether JAML expression correlates with patient survival outcomes. Additionally, investigating the upstream regulators of JAML expression in LUAD and the downstream effectors of JAML-mediated signaling will be essential for fully elucidating its role in lung carcinogenesis and determining its potential as a therapeutic target.
