The present study applied a lipidomic approach to characterize plasma lipid profiles in a well‑matched cohort of patients with Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (ME/CFS) and healthy controls, with the deliberate strategy of stratifying all analyses by sex. The results revealed striking sex‑specific alterations both at the level of global lipid classes and within defined fatty‑acid species, indicating that the lipidomic landscape of ME/CFS is profoundly modulated by biological sex. While total phosphatidylcholine (PC) content was elevated in healthy women relative to healthy men, disease‑related changes in males were dominated by increases in hexosylceramides (HexCer) and monounsaturated fatty‑acid (MUFA)‑containing phospholipids, whereas females with ME/CFS exhibited reductions in phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and HexCer together with a selective depletion of arachidonic‑acid (AA)‑rich PE species and a concurrent rise in AA‑esterified cholesteryl esters (CE‑AA). These divergent patterns underscore the necessity of sex‑specific biomarker and mechanistic investigations in ME/CFS.

Sex differences in the baseline lipidome have been documented in several chronic inflammatory conditions, and our observation that healthy women display higher total PC and higher proportions of AA‑ and docosahexaenoic‑acid (DHA)‑containing PC and CE is consistent with known effects of estrogen on hepatic phospholipid synthesis and on the enrichment of long‑chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) in plasma. The lower circulating carnitine‑bound AA (CAR‑AA) in healthy women further suggests sex‑related modulation of fatty‑acid transport and mitochondrial β‑oxidation. These baseline disparities set the stage for distinct trajectories of lipid remodeling when disease processes are superimposed, potentially explaining why male and female ME/CFS patients present with overlapping yet distinct symptom profiles.

Among male patients, the most prominent disease‑related signature was a significant elevation of total HexCer, a class of glycosphingolipids implicated in membrane raft formation, immune receptor signaling, and apoptosis. HexCer accumulation has been linked to pro‑inflammatory cytokine production in other chronic conditions, raising the possibility that sphingolipid dysregulation may contribute to the heightened inflammatory tone reported in male ME/CFS cohorts. Complementary to this, male patients displayed higher levels of PE‑MUFA and phosphatidylinositol‑MUFA (PI‑MUFA), as well as elevated triglycerides enriched in saturated fatty acids (TG‑SFA). An increase in MUFA‑containing phospholipids can alter membrane fluidity and the spatial organization of toll‑like receptors, while a shift toward saturated TG species may influence systemic energy metabolism and oxidative stress. Together, these changes could amplify pain and fatigue perception by modifying cellular signaling pathways that are sensitive to lipid environment.

In contrast, female ME/CFS patients showed a reduction in total PE and HexCer, suggesting a depletion of structural phospholipids and glycosphingolipids that may affect neuronal and immune cell membranes. The selective decrease in PE containing AA (PE‑AA) is particularly noteworthy because PE is a major component of mitochondrial membranes and a reservoir for AA‑derived eicosanoid synthesis. Lower PE‑AA could diminish the availability of substrate for the production of pro‑inflammatory mediators, potentially reflecting a compensatory down‑regulation of inflammation in female patients. Paradoxically, CE‑AA levels were higher in female patients compared with male patients, indicating an accumulation of AA in an esterified, storage form. This pattern may represent an altered lipid trafficking or remodeling process that sequesters AA away from active signaling pools, which could have implications for the resolution of inflammation and for cognitive functions that rely on delicate balance of AA‑derived mediators.

Collectively, these lipid signatures provide a plausible mechanistic link between metabolic dysregulation and the core symptoms of ME/CFS—pain, fatigue, and cognitive impairment—particularly through their influence on membrane composition, mitochondrial efficiency, and the biosynthesis of lipid mediators. The divergent patterns between sexes also suggest that hormonal milieu (e.g., estrogen, testosterone) may modulate lipid metabolism pathways such as phospholipid remodeling, sphingolipid turnover, and fatty‑acid desaturase activity. However, the cross‑sectional design of this study precludes causal inference, and the relatively small non‑Caucasian subgroups limit the generalizability of findings across ethnic backgrounds. Moreover, plasma lipid profiles may not fully reflect tissue‑specific alterations in the central nervous system or immune compartments where many ME/CFS symptoms originate. Future investigations should incorporate longitudinal sampling to determine whether lipid changes fluctuate with disease state or treatment, expand cohort diversity, and integrate functional assays (e.g., ex‑vivo immune cell stimulation, eicosanoid profiling) to validate the biological consequences of the observed lipid shifts. Lipid‑centric biomarkers that capture sex‑specific signatures could eventually aid in patient stratification and guide personalized therapeutic strategies, such as dietary supplementation with phospholipid precursors or targeted modulation of sphingolipid pathways.
