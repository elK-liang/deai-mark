In this study we compiled a set of 67 necroptosis‑related genes (NRGs) that are widely mutated in breast cancer (BRCA). Genetic alterations were detected in 14.75 % of the 983 patients examined, with ATRX being the most frequently altered locus (2 %) and a panel of 16 additional genes each showing a 1 % mutation rate. Copy‑number variation analysis revealed that the majority of these alterations were amplifications rather than deletions, suggesting that increased dosage of NRGs may contribute to tumor progression. The chromosomal distribution of the 67 NRGs and the interaction network constructed from their co‑expression patterns illustrate a complex regulatory architecture that could influence necroptotic signaling in BRCA.  

Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal breast tissue showed that, apart from RIPK1, RIPK3, TNF, MAP3K7 and STAT3, the majority of NRGs were significantly dysregulated. Among the upregulated genes, TERT displayed the highest log‑fold change (log FC = 2.627), while KLF9 exhibited the greatest downregulation (log FC = –1.601). These contrasting expression patterns imply that the necroptosis pathway is broadly re‑wired in breast cancer, potentially shifting the balance between cell death and survival and thereby affecting tumor behavior.  

By intersecting the transcriptomes of 1,096 BRCA samples with the 67 NRGs, we identified 1,520 necroptosis‑related lncRNAs (NRlncRNAs) that showed strong positive correlation (Spearman > 0.4, p < 0.001). Univariate Cox regression retained 46 of these NRlncRNAs as statistically prognostic (p < 0.05). To minimize redundancy and over‑fitting, a LASSO‑penalized Cox model reduced the panel to 22 lncRNAs, which together constitute a multivariate risk score. Validation of individual lncRNA expression confirmed that LMNTD2‑AS1, AC007608.3 and LINC02446 were significantly overexpressed in tumor tissue, whereas LINC00377, AL450322.1, MEF2C‑AS1 and AC079298.3 were markedly downregulated (|log FC| > 1, p < 0.05). The directionality of these changes aligns with the notion that some NRlncRNAs may function as oncogenic regulators while others act as tumor suppressors within the necroptotic axis.  

The 22‑lncRNA signature proved to be an independent prognostic indicator, stratifying patients into high‑ and low‑risk groups with markedly different overall survival. Because the signature captures both the genetic and transcriptomic alterations of the necroptosis pathway, it may reflect underlying tumor biology that transcends conventional clinical variables. The robustness of the model after LASSO regularization further suggests that these 22 lncRNAs constitute a concise yet powerful biomarker set for risk assessment in BRCA.  

Given the intimate link between necroptosis and immune signaling, the identified NRlncRNAs are likely to influence the tumor immune microenvironment, potentially modulating response to immunotherapy. Future studies that integrate immune cell infiltration profiles, checkpoint‑expression data and clinical outcome information will be essential to determine whether the signature can predict benefit from immune‑checkpoint blockade or other immunotherapeutic strategies. In parallel, functional experiments—such as targeted knockdown or overexpression of candidate lncRNAs in breast‑cancer cell models—are needed to elucidate their mechanistic role in regulating necroptosis and immune recognition. Validation of the signature in independent cohorts and its incorporation into multi‑omics analyses will be critical steps toward translating these findings into clinically actionable tools for personalized prognosis and therapeutic selection in breast cancer.
