Our data demonstrate that glucocorticoid receptor (GR) ligation, whether by the classic agonist dexamethasone (Dex) or by selective GR modulators (SGRMs) that act as antagonists at canonical GR‑regulated genes, suppresses estradiol (E2)‑driven proliferation in GR‑positive/ER‑positive breast cancer cells (MCF‑7 and T‑47D). The inhibitory effect requires both GR expression and the presence of estrogen signaling, because Dex alone does not alter baseline growth and GR‑negative T47D‑Y cells are completely refractory to the antiproliferative action of any GR ligand. Notably, the reduced cell numbers observed after several days of treatment are not explained by increased apoptosis, but rather by a delay in cell‑cycle progression, as evidenced by an accumulation of cells in S phase and a concomitant slowdown of the S‑to‑G2/M transition. A radioactive E2 competition assay confirmed that none of the GR ligands interact directly with the ER ligand‑binding domain, indicating that GR modulates ER activity through an indirect, GR‑dependent mechanism.

The finding that both a full GR agonist and GR antagonists can impede E2‑mediated proliferation suggests that the anti‑proliferative outcome is not solely dependent on the transactivation of classical GR target genes such as FKBP5. Instead, GR may exert its effect by altering the chromatin association of ER, thereby influencing the transcriptional programs that drive cell‑cycle progression. The ability of GR to intersect with ER at the chromatin level is consistent with the growing recognition of extensive cross‑talk between nuclear receptor pathways and implies that GR binding could remodel the enhancer landscape or compete for shared co‑activators, leading to dampened ER‑driven transcription. Further molecular studies are needed to map the concurrent GR and ER cistromes and to determine whether GR recruitment remodels ER binding patterns across the genome, including on clinically relevant mutant ER variants.

From a translational perspective, the use of high‑dose systemic glucocorticoids is limited by metabolic adverse effects, whereas SGRMs such as C134 and C335 retain the capacity to block E2‑driven growth while lacking the canonical GR‑agonist activity that underlies many side‑effects. Thus, selective GR modulators may represent a promising class of adjuvant agents for ER‑positive breast cancer, offering a means to inhibit tumor proliferation without the broad metabolic toxicity of conventional glucocorticoids. However, the therapeutic index of these compounds will require careful evaluation in vivo, and their impact on normal mammary tissue and systemic metabolism remains to be defined.

A major strength of this study is the use of two well‑characterized GR+/ER+ cell lines together with a GR‑negative control line, allowing us to attribute the anti‑proliferative phenotype specifically to GR expression and to rule out off‑target effects on ER. The cell‑cycle analysis provides mechanistic insight beyond simple proliferation readouts, and the competition assay definitively excludes direct ER binding by the GR ligands. Nevertheless, the work is confined to in vitro models, and the duration of treatment (several days) may not fully capture the dynamics of chronic exposure. Additionally, the present experiments do not address the influence of tumor‑relevant ER mutations, which are increasingly recognized as drivers of endocrine resistance; future studies should determine whether SGRM‑mediated GR modulation can also suppress the chromatin association and transcriptional activity of mutant ER alleles.

In summary, our results position GR as a functional regulator of ER activity in ER‑positive breast cancer cells, where ligand‑dependent GR engagement—regardless of its canonical transcriptional profile—curtails estrogen‑driven proliferation by retarding cell‑cycle progression. This observation opens avenues for exploiting GR‑targeted modulators as a complementary strategy to current endocrine therapies, particularly for patients who might benefit from GR‑mediated inhibition of ER signaling without the drawbacks of sustained glucocorticoid agonist activity. Future investigations should focus on delineating the precise molecular interfaces between GR and ER at the chromatin level, validating the efficacy of SGRMs in animal models and ultimately in clinical cohorts, and exploring combination regimens that integrate GR modulation with existing hormonal treatments.
