The identification of reliable, non‑invasive biomarkers for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains a pressing clinical need. Liquid‑biopsy approaches that exploit extracellular vesicles (EVs) and their cargo of small non‑coding RNAs (sncRNAs) have gained momentum because such RNAs are protected from degradation in biofluids and can reflect tumor‑derived molecular changes. While microRNAs have been extensively investigated, the landscape of other sncRNA species—particularly tRNA‑derived fragments (tRFs) and other structured small RNAs—has only begun to be mapped in esophageal cancer.

In this study, small‑RNA sequencing of salivary exosomes from a pilot cohort of three ESCC patients and three healthy volunteers revealed 1 366 differentially expressed sncRNAs (p < 0.05) that were not annotated as miRNAs. Thirty‑two of these displayed a log₂ fold change > 2 in tumor samples, and the five most strongly up‑regulated candidates were selected for further validation. Subsequent RT‑qPCR analysis in an independent cohort of 33 ESCC patients and 33 controls from the Changhua Show Chwan University Medical Center (CHSUMC) showed that two of the five candidates—tsRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 (tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5) and a previously uncharacterized small RNA that we termed sRESE (small RNA identified in Exosomes from the Saliva of ESCC patients)—exceeded the pre‑specified significance threshold (p < 0.01). Importantly, the same expression pattern was reproduced in a second geographically distinct validation cohort (ATH), confirming the robustness of these biomarkers across diverse populations. Receiver‑operating characteristic (ROC) analysis in the CHSUMC cohort demonstrated high discriminative power for both tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 and sRESE, highlighting their clinical potential (Fig. 2F). Moreover, detection of these RNAs within exosomes isolated from ESCC cell lines and conditioned media indicated that their enrichment reflects active secretion by tumor cells rather than a systemic response (Fig. S2A, B).

The identification of tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 is particularly noteworthy because it originates from the 5′ end of the TRG‑GCC tRNA gene, a region known to generate tRNA‑derived small RNAs (tsRNAs). Emerging evidence suggests that tsRNAs are produced under cellular stress and can modulate translation, gene‑silencing pathways, and intercellular communication. Their frequent over‑expression in malignant tissues has been linked to enhanced proliferative and invasive capabilities, making them plausible contributors to tumor progression. Our functional assays revealed that modulation of tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 (and sRESE) levels influences the migratory and invasive capacity of ESCC cells (Fig. 2C), hinting that these molecules may play active roles in disease pathogenesis beyond serving as passive markers. The novel sRESE, encoded in a genomic interval between SSX2IP and LOC102724892, adds a previously unknown member to the expanding list of exosomal small RNAs. Its consistent up‑regulation across multiple cohorts and its impact on tumor cell behavior warrant further investigation into its molecular targets and mechanisms of action.

From a translational perspective, saliva as a source of exosomes offers distinct advantages: collection is non‑invasive, rapid, and suitable for repeated sampling—critical for monitoring disease recurrence or therapeutic response. Combining a standardized exosome isolation protocol with a simple RT‑qPCR detection method positions these biomarkers for integration into clinical workflows. Nevertheless, the current study is limited by the relatively small sample size of the discovery phase and by the fact that only two candidates met the stringent statistical filter. Larger, prospective multicenter trials are required to define precise sensitivity and specificity thresholds, to evaluate the influence of confounding variables such as age, smoking status, and comorbidities, and to explore whether baseline levels correlate with pathologic stage or can predict response to adjuvant therapies.

In conclusion, our multicenter prospective analysis provides compelling evidence that salivary exosomal tRNA‑GlyGCC‑5 and the newly described sRESE are significantly enriched in ESCC patients and can discriminate between diseased and healthy individuals with high accuracy. These findings expand the repertoire of known exosomal small RNAs and set the stage for mechanistic studies to unravel their functional contributions to esophageal carcinogenesis. Future work should focus on validating these signatures in larger cohorts, dissecting the biological pathways they modulate, and developing a clinically actionable assay that could ultimately improve early detection and patient management in esophageal carcinoma.
