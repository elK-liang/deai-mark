The global rise of antibiotic resistance poses a significant threat to public health, necessitating a deeper understanding of the factors that determine antibiotic efficacy. While traditional microbiological methods have provided valuable insights into the mechanisms of antibiotic action and resistance, they often rely on population-averaged measurements that can obscure important variations among individual bacterial cells. Phenotypic heterogeneity within bacterial populations has been increasingly recognized as a critical factor influencing treatment outcomes, yet our ability to investigate the underlying dynamics at the single-cell level has remained limited. A more granular understanding of how antibiotics interact with individual bacteria over time could reveal new strategies for combating resistant infections and optimizing therapeutic regimens.

Recent advances in single-cell technologies have begun to address this gap by enabling real-time observation of bacterial physiology and antibiotic responses at unprecedented resolution. Microfluidic platforms, in particular, offer the capability to trap and monitor individual bacterial cells under precisely controlled environmental conditions while simultaneously quantifying molecular interactions. When combined with fluorescence microscopy, these systems allow researchers to track the accumulation and distribution of antibiotics within single cells as they grow and divide. However, the development of appropriate fluorescent probes that can accurately report on antibiotic behavior without altering the biological activity of the parent compound has presented a significant technical challenge.

In this study, we employed a recently developed single-cell microfluidics-microscopy platform in combination with a library of fluorescently labeled antibiotic derivatives spanning most major drug classes. These probes were functionalized with the nitrobenzoxadiazole (NBD) fluorophore at sites chosen to minimize perturbations to biological activity. We first validated these fluorescent derivatives against their parent compounds using minimum inhibitory concentration assays to ensure that the labeling process preserved the essential antimicrobial properties. We then utilized our platform to quantify the real-time dynamics of antibiotic accumulation in individual Escherichia coli cells, monitoring bacterial elongation as a proxy for growth and physiological state. This approach enabled us to examine how bacterial growth rate influences antibiotic uptake and efficacy across multiple drug classes simultaneously.

Here, we present our findings demonstrating that the dynamics of antibiotic accumulation in single bacterial cells are intimately linked to cellular growth state. Using our platform, we observed that rapidly growing bacteria displayed characteristic patterns of antibiotic accumulation that differed across drug classes. These results provide new insights into the relationship between bacterial physiology and antibiotic susceptibility, highlighting the importance of considering single-cell heterogeneity when evaluating antimicrobial efficacy. This work establishes a robust methodology for dissecting the kinetics of antibiotic-bacterial interactions at the individual cell level and offers a framework for future investigations into the mechanisms underlying phenotypic variability in antibiotic response.
