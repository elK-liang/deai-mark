Basilar tip aneurysms represent a distinct subset of intracranial lesions because of their deep anatomical location, proximity to critical brainstem structures, and a natural history that confers a high risk of rupture.1 Endovascular therapy has become the first‑line treatment for many of these lesions, yet the wide neck and unfavorable dome‑to‑neck ratio often complicate the use of conventional techniques.2 Coiling, with or without adjunctive devices such as stents or balloons, can achieve durable occlusion but may be limited by coil compaction, recurrence, and the need for additional hardware.3 These challenges have prompted the development of alternative intrasaccular flow‑disruption devices that are specifically designed for wide‑necked aneurysms.4  

The Woven EndoBridge (WEB) device is a permanently implanted, intrasaccular mesh that disrupts intra‑aneurysmal flow and promotes thrombus formation while preserving the parent artery.5 Early single‑center series have reported high rates of adequate occlusion and lower frequencies of procedural complications when the WEB is used in appropriately selected basilar tip aneurysms.6 Nevertheless, comparative data between WEB embolization and conventional coiling remain sparse, and the relative impact on procedural parameters such as treatment duration, fluoroscopic radiation, and contrast load has not been thoroughly evaluated.7 Furthermore, the influence of aneurysm morphology—particularly neck width and dome‑to‑neck ratio—on the choice of therapy and on clinical outcomes is still incompletely understood.  

Given the growing adoption of the WEB device and the continued reliance on coiling for complex basilar tip aneurysms, a direct comparison of angiographic results and periprocedural safety is needed. The present study was designed as a retrospective, dual‑center cohort analysis to contrast the two treatment strategies. Patients were stratified according to the performed technique (WEB versus coiling), and baseline anatomic characteristics—including neck width and dome‑to‑neck ratio—were documented to assess the impact of morphology on therapeutic approach. Procedural details such as the use of adjunctive stents or balloons, treatment duration, fluoroscopic radiation dose (expressed as dose‑area product), and contrast volume were systematically recorded. In addition, all peri‑procedural complications, with emphasis on thromboembolic events, were captured to enable a balanced appraisal of safety profiles.  

The primary objective of this investigation is to provide a detailed comparison of angiographic outcomes and complication rates between WEB embolization and coiling for unruptured basilar tip aneurysms, while also reporting relevant differences in peri‑procedural metrics that may influence treatment decision‑making. Bycharacterizing the contemporary experience with both modalities, we aim to assist clinicians in selecting the optimal endovascular strategy for this challenging subset of cerebral aneurysms.
