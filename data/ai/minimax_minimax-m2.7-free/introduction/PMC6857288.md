Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive vasculopathy characterized by elevated pulmonary artery pressure, remodeling of the small pulmonary arteries, and eventual right‑ventricular failure. The disease classification distinguishes idiopathic PAH (IPAH), heritable PAH (FPAH), and PAH associated with other conditions (APAH), such as connective‑tissue diseases, congenital heart disease, portopulmonary hypertension, and exposure to drugs or toxins. Clinical series consistently report a marked female predominance (approximately 3–4:1) and a median age at diagnosis in the fifth decade, although early‑onset disease occurs in a subset of patients. Despite advances in hemodynamic assessment and targeted therapies, PAH remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality, underscoring the need to delineate its underlying molecular mechanisms.

Genetic studies have established that rare pathogenic variants in the bone morphogenetic protein type II receptor (BMPR2) and related TGF‑β signaling genes account for a substantial proportion of familial PAH and a smaller fraction of sporadic IPAH. Additional genes implicated in pulmonary vascular homeostasis (e.g., ACVRL1, CAV1, KCNK3) have been identified through candidate‑gene approaches and small cohort sequencing. Nevertheless, the majority of PAH cases—especially those belonging to the heterogeneous APAH subclasses—lack a clear monogenic explanation, indicating that numerous susceptibility loci remain undiscovered. Large‑scale, unbiased sequencing of the protein‑coding genome offers a powerful strategy to uncover rare variants that contribute to disease risk across the full spectrum of PAH phenotypes.

To address this gap, we performed whole‑exome sequencing on a comprehensively phenotyped cohort of 2572 individuals with PAH from the PAH Biobank. The cohort mirrors the recognized distribution of disease subtypes, comprising 43 % IPAH, 48 % APAH, 4 % FPAH, and 5 % other forms, with APAH further stratified into subgroups including autoimmune connective‑tissue disease (n = 722), congenital heart disease (n = 268), portopulmonary hypertension (n = 139), and miscellaneous disorders. The population is ethnically diverse (72 % European, 12 % Hispanic, 11 % African, 2.7 % East Asian, and 1.1 % South Asian) and shows a 3.7:1 female‑to‑male ratio overall, with notable variations across subclasses. While 91.2 % of participants presented with adult‑onset disease (mean age ≈ 48 years), child‑onset cases were enriched in the APAH‑CHD subgroup (37 % of pediatric diagnoses). Case‑control association testing of rare coding variants across this large, ancestrally diverse cohort was designed to identify novel risk genes and to elucidate biological pathways that contribute to PAH susceptibility and progression.

The findings from this exome‑wide investigation are expected to expand the catalog of genes implicated in PAH, reveal population‑specific allele frequencies, and provide mechanistic insights into the dysregulated signaling networks that drive pulmonary vascular remodeling. By integrating genetic data with detailed clinical and hemodynamic profiles, the study aims to improve risk stratification, inform genetic counseling, and ultimately guide the development of more targeted therapeutic interventions for patients with PAH.
