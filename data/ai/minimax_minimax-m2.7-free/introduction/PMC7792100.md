Breast cancer remains one of the most frequently diagnosed malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality, with the majority of deaths attributable to metastatic disease rather than the primary tumor itself. The tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a pivotal role in shaping disease progression, and the composition and spatial organization of immune cells within the TME have emerged as critical determinants of clinical outcome and therapeutic response. In particular, the presence and localization of cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells can dictate whether a tumor is perceived as immunologically “hot” (inflamed) or “cold” (immune‑excluded or desert), concepts that have profound implications for the efficacy of immunotherapeutic interventions.

The classification of tumors into immune phenotypes based on the density and distribution of CD8⁺ T cells provides a framework for predicting prognosis and responsiveness to checkpoint blockade. “Hot” tumors, characterized by robust intratumoral CD8⁺ T‑cell infiltration, are generally associated with improved disease‑free survival (DFS) and enhanced benefit from immune‑checkpoint inhibitors. Conversely, “cold” tumors, which lack appreciable T‑cell infiltration or display a predominantly stromal rather than intratumoral pattern, often exhibit immune evasion mechanisms and derive limited benefit from immunotherapy. Understanding the factors that govern the transition between these phenotypes, especially in the context of metastatic spread, is essential for developing strategies that can convert “cold” lesions into immunologically active sites.

PD‑1 (Programmed cell death protein 1) and LAG‑3 (Lymphocyte‑activation gene 3) are co‑inhibitory receptors frequently upregulated on tumor‑infiltrating T cells and are implicated in T‑cell exhaustion and immune evasion. While PD‑1 has been extensively validated as a therapeutic target in multiple cancer types, LAG‑3 has recently gained attention as a complementary checkpoint that can cooperate with PD‑1 to further dampen T‑cell function. Preclinical and early clinical data suggest that dual blockade of PD‑1 and LAG‑3 can restore T‑cell activity and improve anti‑tumor responses, highlighting the relevance of assessing their concurrent expression. Nonetheless, the patterns of PD‑1 and LAG‑3 expression within the distinct immune compartments of breast cancer primary tumors and their matched metastatic lesions remain incompletely characterized.

To address this gap, we performed a comprehensive immunohistochemical analysis of CD8, PD‑1, and LAG‑3 on whole‑tissue sections from primary breast tumors (PBTs) and their corresponding metastatic sites (METs). By semiquantitatively evaluating CD8⁺ T‑cell frequencies in intratumoral and stromal compartments, we classified each case into the established “hot”/inflamed or “cold”/immune‑excluded phenotypes. We further examined the co‑expression of PD‑1 and LAG‑3 across these compartments and correlated the immune profiles with clinicopathological variables, molecular subtypes, anatomical metastatic sites, and disease‑free survival. The goal of this study is to delineate how immune landscapes evolve from the primary tumor to distant metastases and to determine whether the combined assessment of immune phenotype and checkpoint expression can refine prognostic stratification and guide rational combinatorial immunotherapy strategies for patients with metastatic breast cancer.
