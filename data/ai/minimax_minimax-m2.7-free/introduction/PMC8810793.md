Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most common malignancies and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide. Radiotherapy is a mainstay for treating locally advanced CRC, yet the effectiveness of ionizing radiation (IR) is often compromised by intrinsic or acquired radioresistance, which promotes tumor recurrence and poor clinical outcomes. A deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms that underlie the response of CRC cells to radiation is therefore essential for developing novel radiosensitizing approaches.

Recent investigations have revealed that non‑coding RNAs and key signaling pathways play pivotal roles in shaping the tumor microenvironment and determining therapeutic sensitivity. The long non‑coding RNA MALAT1 and the Hippo‑pathway effector YAP1 are frequently upregulated in CRC and have been linked to tumor growth, invasion, and resistance to DNA‑damaging treatments. Moreover, the ankyrin repeat‑containing protein ANKHD1 has emerged as a regulator of gene expression and cell survival, although its involvement in the response of CRC to radiation has not been established. The AKT kinase, a central mediator of survival signals downstream of multiple oncogenic inputs, is known to facilitate DNA repair and inhibit apoptosis after IR, contributing to radioresistant phenotypes. Elucidating how these molecules interact may uncover a network that reinforces the resistant state of CRC cells.

In the present study, we demonstrate that depletion of ANKHD1 markedly enhances the radiosensitivity of CRC cells in vitro and suppresses tumor growth in vivo following irradiation. Clonogenic survival assays revealed sensitization‑enhancement ratios of 1.378 and 1.225 in HCT116 and HCT8 cells, respectively, indicating a substantial increase in radiation‑induced cell death upon ANKHD1 knock‑down. Moreover, ANKHD1 silencing led to a pronounced elevation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, an increase in DNA double‑strand breaks (DSBs) as evidenced by prolonged comet tail moments, and heightened γH2AX focus formation after IR. In a CRC xenograft model, the growth of ectopic tumors was significantly inhibited after a single 10 Gy dose in the sh‑ANKHD1 group, and histological analysis confirmed reduced tumor volume and enhanced γH2AX staining, underscoring the radiosensitizing effect of ANKHD1 loss in living organisms.

Further mechanistic exploration uncovered a regulatory circuit in which ANKHD1 cooperates with MALAT1 to modulate YAP1 expression and activity, thereby sustaining AKT signaling and establishing a positive feedback loop that reinforces the radioresistant phenotype of CRC. By delineating the ANKHD1/MALAT1/YAP1 axis and its downstream AKT pathway, this work provides new insight into the molecular basis of CRC radioresistance and identifies potential therapeutic targets for future radiosensitization strategies.
