Breast cancer remains the most commonly diagnosed malignancy and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide. Despite advances in early detection and therapeutic strategies, clinical outcomes vary significantly among patients due to tumor heterogeneity and intrinsic or acquired treatment resistance. Accurate prognosis prediction and identification of patients who may benefit from specific therapeutic approaches, particularly immunotherapy, represent critical challenges in breast cancer management. Therefore, there is an urgent need to discover robust molecular biomarkers that can more precisely stratify patients and guide personalized treatment decisions.

Necroptosis is a programmed form of cell death characterized by necrotic morphology and regulated by specific molecular machinery, distinct from apoptosis. This cellular process has emerged as a crucial player in tumor biology, influencing cancer progression, metastasis, and response to therapy. The necroptotic pathway involves a cascade of signaling events mediated by receptor-interacting protein kinases (RIPKs) and mixed lineage kinase domain-like pseudokinase (MLKL), with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) serving as a key upstream regulator. Recent evidence suggests that dysregulation of necroptosis-related genes (NRGs) contributes to breast cancer development and aggressiveness. In the present study, we identified 67 NRGs with known roles in necroptosis, among which genetic alterations were observed in 14.75% of breast cancer patients, with ATRX showing the highest alteration frequency. Copy number variation analysis revealed that most genetic variations in these genes involved amplification events, suggesting potential oncogenic implications. Network analysis demonstrated complex interactions among these NRGs, highlighting their coordinated functions in regulating necroptotic cell death in breast cancer.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are non-protein-coding transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides that play diverse roles in gene regulation, including chromatin remodeling, transcriptional control, and post-transcriptional processing. Accumulating evidence indicates that lncRNAs are frequently dysregulated in breast cancer and contribute to tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance. Notably, lncRNAs can interact with necroptosis-related machinery, modulating the sensitivity of tumor cells to necroptotic cell death. In this investigation, we performed Spearman correlation analysis between the 67 NRGs and genome-wide lncRNA expression profiles from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, identifying 1,520 necroptosis-related lncRNAs (NRlncRNAs) with significant correlations. Subsequent univariate Cox regression analysis revealed 46 prognostic NRlncRNAs significantly associated with patient survival. To develop a robust prognostic signature, we applied LASSO-penalized Cox regression analysis, which selected 22 NRlncRNAs for constructing a multi-gene signature. Among these, LMNTD2-AS1, AC007608.3, and LINC02446 were significantly upregulated in breast cancer tissues compared to normal breast tissue, while LINC00377, AL450322.1, MEF2C-AS1, and AC079298.3 showed decreased expression, suggesting their potential roles as diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers.

In this study, we aimed to develop and validate a necroptosis-related lncRNA signature for predicting prognosis and revealing the immune microenvironment characteristics in breast cancer patients. By analyzing transcriptomic data from 1,096 breast cancer specimens and 112 normal specimens from the TCGA cohort, we constructed a prognostic model based on the expression patterns of 22 NRlncRNAs. This signature may provide valuable information for risk stratification and prediction of immunotherapy response, as the tumor immune microenvironment plays a pivotal role in determining clinical outcomes and therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, we performed comprehensive analysis to identify distinct molecular subtypes associated with the necroptosis-related lncRNA signature, which may help elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms linking necroptosis to immune infiltration patterns in breast cancer. Our findings contribute to a better understanding of the complex interplay between necroptosis, lncRNAs, and the immune landscape, offering potential novel targets for therapeutic intervention and prognostic assessment in breast cancer management.
