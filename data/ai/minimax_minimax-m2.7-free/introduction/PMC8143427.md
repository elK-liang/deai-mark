White adipose tissue (WAT) is traditionally viewed as a static energy reservoir, yet it is now recognized as a dynamic endocrine organ that responds to a wide range of physiological cues, including temperature. In mammals, the body’s core temperature is tightly maintained, but peripheral tissues experience gradients of cooler temperatures that can influence cellular metabolism and gene expression. Bone marrow adipose tissue (BMAT) constitutes a substantial fraction of the marrow cavity and has been shown to differ from classic WAT in lipid composition and transcriptional programs. Although the functional consequences of these differences are still being elucidated, BMAT may serve both as a local energy source and a regulator of the marrow microenvironment, particularly under conditions that alter tissue temperature.

Recent work demonstrated that isolated bone marrow adipocytes (BMAds) from the distal tibia (dTib) exhibit markedly elevated expression of stearoyl‑CoA desaturase 1 (Scd1) and accumulate higher proportions of monounsaturated lipids compared with adipocytes from the proximal tibia or from subcutaneous and visceral WAT depots. This depot‑specific enrichment of Scd1 suggested that the distal skeleton experiences a cooler thermal milieu, prompting the hypothesis that temperature per se drives the observed lipid remodeling. Supporting this idea, telemetry studies in rodents housed at 22 °C have documented a temperature decline from ~38 °C at the first cervical vertebrae to ~32 °C by the ninth caudal vertebra, indicating that distal skeletal sites are subjected to physiologically relevant cool temperatures despite overall core thermoregulation.

To test whether exposure to reduced temperature directly alters the lipid landscape of BMAT, we raised rats from birth to 11 weeks under either standard room temperature (22 °C) or thermoneutral conditions (29 °C). Lipidomic analysis of triacylglycerols isolated from distal BMAT revealed a consistent pattern: rats housed at 22 °C displayed lower relative abundances of saturated fatty acids (myristic, palmitic, and stearic acids) and a concomitant increase in unsaturated species, most notably oleic acid, compared with animals maintained at 29 °C. Notably, phospholipid composition remained unchanged, suggesting a selective remodeling of the storage lipid pool rather than a global membrane alteration. Consistent with the observed shift toward unsaturation, western blot analysis showed a pronounced increase in SCD1 protein levels within the distal vertebrae (CV) and dTib BMAT of the cooler‑housed rats, indicating that SCD1 expression is dynamically regulated by ambient temperature.

To determine whether similar temperature‑dependent regulation extends to classic white adipocytes in vivo, we performed parallel experiments in mice, where posterior hair was removed to reduce insulation and to accentuate skin cooling. Subcutaneous temperature recordings confirmed that posterior subcutaneous depots were approximately 3 °C cooler (≈33–34 °C) in mice housed at 22 °C compared with those at 29 °C. Under these conditions, SCD1 protein was markedly up‑regulated in the posterior subcutaneous WAT depots (dorsolumbar, inguinal, and gluteal), whereas no significant temperature‑induced change was detected in anterior subcutaneous or visceral WAT, which remained near core temperature (~37 °C). Collectively, these findings support a model in which cool physiological temperatures that are experienced by distal skeletal and posterior subcutaneous adipose depots drive a molecular program characterized by enhanced SCD1 expression and a lipid composition enriched in monounsaturated fatty acids, thereby providing a mechanistic link between thermal cues and metabolic remodeling in white adipocytes.
