RNA interference (RNAi) is a conserved nucleotide‑sequence‑specific mechanism that underlies antiviral immunity in plants. Upon infection, viral double‑stranded RNA intermediates are processed into virus‑derived small interfering RNAs (vsiRNAs), which are loaded into Argonaute‑containing RNA‑induced silencing complexes to guide the sequence‑specific degradation of viral RNA. To overcome this defense, many plant viruses encode RNA‑silencing suppressors (VSRs) that disrupt distinct steps of the RNAi pathway. The 2b protein of Cucumber mosaic virus (CMV) is a classic VSR that binds Argonaute proteins and interferes with the assembly or activity of the silencing complex. Consequently, CMV encodes a potent suppressor that can attenuate antiviral RNAi, making it an excellent model for studying virus–host interactions. A mutant of the subgroup I CMV strain Fny, termed CMV‑Δ2b, carries nucleotide substitutions that abolish expression of the 2b protein, rendering the virus hypersensitive to RNAi and allowing the identification of host factors that modulate antiviral silencing.

Arabidopsis thaliana exhibits extensive natural genetic variation that can be harnessed to uncover the molecular basis of pathogen resistance. Genome‑wide association studies (GWAS) in this model plant have successfully identified loci controlling resistance to bacterial, fungal, and oomycete pathogens, often revealing novel components of immune signaling networks. Despite the central role of RNAi in antiviral defense, relatively few GWAS have focused on quantitative variation in susceptibility to viruses, especially those that are sensitized to RNAi by the loss of a VSR. In this study, we employed a collection of 496 fully re‑sequenced A. thaliana accessions to perform GWAS for natural variation in the accumulation of CMV‑Δ2b, using enzyme‑linked immunosorbent assay of the viral coat protein as a read‑out of virus load.

Initial association mapping revealed a single, highly significant SNP that mapped to chromosome 4 within the coding region of At4g11040. This gene corresponds to Reduced Dormancy 5 (RDO5), also known as Delay of Germination 18 (DOG18), which has been characterized primarily for its role in seed dormancy and germination timing. The top SNP distinguishes two haplotypes, designated T and G, and accessions carrying the T haplotype consistently displayed lower CMV‑Δ2b titers than those with the G haplotype, suggesting that natural variation at the RDO5 locus influences the outcome of CMV infection.

These observations raise the hypothesis that RDO5 functions as a regulator of antiviral RNAi, potentially acting as a positive host factor that limits virus accumulation. To test this hypothesis, we generated loss‑of‑function rdo5 mutants in the resistant accession Columbia‑0 (Col‑0) and complemented them with the Col‑0 RDO5 allele. In parallel, we introduced the RDO5 transgene into the susceptible accession Antwerpen‑1 (An‑1), which carries a frameshifting deletion in the endogenous RDO5 gene. By comparing viral coat‑protein accumulation in these genetic backgrounds, we aimed to determine whether RDO5 is required for the reduced CMV‑Δ2b levels observed in the T‑haplotype accessions. The results of these functional analyses are expected to clarify the role of RDO5 in antiviral defense and to reveal whether this gene acts as a positive or negative modulator of the RNAi pathway in Arabidopsis.
