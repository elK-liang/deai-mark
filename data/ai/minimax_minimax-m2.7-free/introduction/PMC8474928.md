Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) represents one of the most aggressive subtypes of lung cancer, characterized by rapid growth, early metastasis, and poor prognosis. Despite advances in understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying SCLC development and progression, therapeutic options remain limited, and the overall five-year survival rate remains dismally low. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify novel molecular targets and prognostic markers that can improve our understanding of SCLC biology and guide the development of more effective treatment strategies. In recent years, epitranscriptomics—the study of chemical modifications on RNA molecules—has emerged as a critical regulator of gene expression and cellular homeostasis, with N6-methyladenosine (m6A) being the most prevalent internal modification in eukaryotic messenger RNA.

The m6A modification is dynamically regulated by a complex network of proteins, including writers (methyltransferases that install m6A marks), erasers (demethylases that remove m6A modifications), and readers (proteins that recognize and interpret m6A marks). These regulators play essential roles in various biological processes, including RNA splicing, stability, translation, and subcellular localization. mounting evidence demonstrates that dysregulation of m6A regulators contributes to tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis across multiple cancer types. However, the landscape of m6A regulators in SCLC remains largely unexplored, creating a significant knowledge gap in our understanding of epitranscriptomic alterations in this devastating disease.

In this study, we undertook a comprehensive investigation of the molecular characteristics, immuno-oncology features, and clinical relevance of m6A regulators in SCLC. We curated a catalog of 30 m6A regulators, comprising 11 writers, 2 erasers, and 17 readers, and systematically examined their genetic alterations, including somatic mutations and copy number variations, across SCLC patient samples and cell lines. Furthermore, we analyzed the expression patterns of these regulators in normal lung tissue versus SCLC samples to determine whether genetic alterations translate into differential expression. Finally, we explored the potential association between m6A regulator expression and SCLC metastatic behavior using cell line models with distinct pathological origins. Our findings provide the first comprehensive characterization of the m6A regulatory landscape in SCLC and may offer new insights into the molecular mechanisms driving this aggressive malignancy.
