Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide, with metastatic disease accounting for the majority of deaths. Understanding the biological factors that contribute to tumor dissemination is essential for developing effective treatments. The MMTV-PyMT mouse model has been extensively used to study mammary carcinogenesis, as it faithfully recapitulates the progression from hyperplasia to invasive carcinoma and spontaneous metastasis to the lungs.

Pregnancy induces dramatic changes in the mammary gland, including ductal elongation, branching, and the formation of lobuloalveoli necessary for lactation. However, the impact of parity on mammary tumor development remains incompletely characterized. In the present study, we examined mammary gland morphology and tumor progression in MMTV-PyMT mice during pregnancy and the early postpartum period. Our analysis revealed that while ductal elongation and branching proceeded normally, pregnant MMTV-PyMT females exhibited failed lobuloalveoli development (FLAD), resulting in a glandular architecture indistinguishable from that of age-matched virgin controls. This developmental failure was associated with the inability to lactate, as these dams failed to feed their offspring.

Histopathological examination of the mammary ductal tree demonstrated the characteristic progression of neoplastic lesions previously described in this model, including hyperplasia, adenoma/MIN, and early carcinoma, with late carcinoma predominating at advanced stages. These lesions exhibited a spatial gradient corresponding to epithelial age, with PyMT antigen levels lowest in hyperplastic regions and highest in adenoma/MIN. Notably, parous mice displayed two novel pathological features associated with milk accumulation: engorged adenoma/MIN characterized by highly distended, milk-filled ducts staining strongly for milk-associated protein, and engorged carcinoma, defined by misshapen, fluid-filled lumina with thickened basement membranes containing fibroblasts and immune cells.

Consistent with the aggressive phenotype of MMTV-PyMT tumors, we observed lung metastases in postpartum females, which displayed a uniform pseudo-alveolar pattern. These findings indicate that pregnancy induces distinct morphological changes in mammary tumors and may influence metastatic potential, highlighting the importance of studying breast cancer in the context of reproductive history.
