Gliomas represent the most common primary malignant neoplasms of the central nervous system, accounting for roughly 80 % of all malignant brain tumors. They comprise a spectrum of histologic subtypes—from low‑grade (WHO grade I–III) astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas to the highly aggressive glioblastoma (GBM, WHO grade IV)—that differ markedly in clinical behavior, treatment response, and patient survival. Despite advances in surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy, the median overall survival for GBM patients remains below 15 months, underscoring the urgent need for reliable prognostic biomarkers that can refine risk stratification and guide personalized therapeutic strategies. While molecular markers such as IDH1/2 mutation status, MGMT promoter methylation, and 1p/19q codeletion have improved patient stratification, additional prognostic indicators that capture the underlying biological heterogeneity of gliomas are still required.

The B‑cell lymphoma 7 (BCL7) family comprises three conserved paralogs—BCL7A, BCL7B, and BCL7C—that have been implicated in various cellular processes, including transcriptional regulation and chromatin remodeling. Although the family has been studied in several hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, its role in neuro‑oncology remains largely unexplored. Emerging evidence suggests that alterations in BCL7 expression may influence tumor progression in other cancer types, yet systematic analyses of BCL7 family members in human glioma cohorts have not been reported. Consequently, the potential contribution of BCL7A, BCL7B, or BCL7C to glioma pathogenesis and their utility as prognostic biomarkers have not been established.

To begin addressing this gap, we performed a multi‑center evaluation of BCL7 gene expression using large public transcriptomic datasets (cBioPortal, Oncomine, GlioVis, and GEO), corroborated by immunohistochemical analysis of a dedicated tissue microarray comprising 108 glioma specimens. These analyses revealed a consistent pattern of reduced BCL7A mRNA and protein levels in tumor tissue relative to normal brain, with the decrease becoming more pronounced as tumor grade advanced from lower‑grade glioma to GBM. In contrast, BCL7C exhibited markedly elevated expression in gliomas compared with normal brain and was highest in GBM, whereas BCL7B displayed variable or no significant differences across datasets. Moreover, within the glioma cohort, BCL7A expression progressively declined with increasing WHO grade, while BCL7B and BCL7C levels rose correspondingly, suggesting a coordinated dysregulation of BCL7 family members during malignant transformation and progression.

Based on these observations, we hypothesized that the distinct expression profiles of BCL7A, and to a lesser extent BCL7B and BCL7C, may correlate with patient outcomes and could serve as novel prognostic biomarkers for glioma. The present study was therefore designed to (i) validate the differential expression of BCL7A across independent cohorts, (ii) assess the association between BCL7A levels and clinicopathological parameters, including overall survival, and (iii) evaluate the potential of BCL7A as a independent predictor of patient prognosis in gliomas. By integrating comprehensive transcriptomic, proteomic, and clinical data, we aim to establish BCL7A as a actionable molecular marker that could improve risk stratification and inform therapeutic decision‑making for glioma patients.
