Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus‑2 (SARS‑CoV‑2) relies extensively on the host cellular machinery to complete its replication cycle. Lipid metabolism is increasingly recognized as a central facet of viral infection: viruses modulate the synthesis, storage and trafficking of lipids to generate membranous replication compartments, to acquire envelope components and to buffer the metabolic demands of the infected cell. For several positive‑strand RNA viruses, profiling of the host lipidome has revealed dramatic remodeling of glycerolipids, phospholipids, sphingolipids and neutral lipids, and these changes have been linked to the formation of double‑membrane vesicles, the expansion of the endoplasmic reticulum and the activation of signaling pathways that favor viral replication. Although early studies highlighted altered cholesterol and fatty‑acid metabolism during coronavirus infection, a comprehensive, quantitative map of the global lipid landscape during SARS‑CoV‑2 infection, especially one that captures features conserved across viral variants, remains incomplete.

A systematic understanding of the lipidomic reprogramming induced by SARS‑CoV‑2 could illuminate host‑derived dependency factors that the virus exploits regardless of genetic variation. Such conserved factors represent attractive targets for broad‑spectrum antiviral strategies. Previous transcriptomic and proteomic surveys have identified metabolic pathways that are commonly rewired across multiple variants, but the lipid‑level consequences of these transcriptional changes have not been systematically investigated in a unified quantitative framework. Moreover, the use of permissive cell lines that express the viral entry receptor ACE2 enables robust infection kinetics, while more differentiated models are needed to reflect the physiology of the respiratory epithelium. Bridging these models with global lipid profiling would therefore provide both mechanistic insight and a resource of candidate lipid dependencies that are likely to be relevant in the natural host.

To address this gap, we performed untargeted lipidomics in two complementary cell systems: HEK293T cells engineered to overexpress ACE2 (HEK293T‑ACE2) and the alveolar epithelial line A549‑ACE2, which more closely mimics the cell type targeted in a natural infection. Cells were infected with SARS‑CoV‑2 or subjected to a mock procedure, and total cellular lipids were extracted using a Bligh–Dyer protocol and analyzed by liquid chromatography–electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (LC‑ESI‑MS/MS). Quantitative comparisons were carried out on five biological replicates per condition, and statistically significant changes were assessed with analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Benjamini–Hochberg correction for multiple testing. Principal component analysis (PCA) was employed to visualize the overall variance structure of the lipidomic data sets.

Our analysis revealed a pervasive reprogramming of the host lipidome. In HEK293T‑ACE2 cells, 514 unique lipid species spanning glycerolipids, phospholipids, sphingolipids and acylcarnitines were identified, of which 409 (≈80 %) displayed statistically significant alterations upon infection, with fold‑changes ranging from 2‑ to 64‑fold. In A549‑ACE2 cells, 443 lipids were quantified, and 227 of them were significantly changed, exhibiting more modest fold‑changes (1.1‑ to 19.7‑fold). PCA demonstrated clear separation between infected and mock‑treated samples, indicating that viral infection dominates the lipidomic variance in both cell models. Collectively, these results provide a detailed, quantitative map of SARS‑CoV‑2–induced lipid remodeling and highlight a core set of lipid alterations that are conserved across the cell types examined, offering a foundation for future studies aimed at dissecting host dependency factors and exploring lipid‑targeting therapeutic interventions.
