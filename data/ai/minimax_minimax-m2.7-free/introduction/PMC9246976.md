The electrochemical conversion of abundant biomass‑derived substrates into high‑value chemicals offers a promising route toward a sustainable chemical economy. Glycerol, a major by‑product of biodiesel production, represents an attractive platform molecule whose oxidation can replace energy‑intensive, fossil‑fuel‑based synthetic routes. However, the development of efficient electrocatalysts that can drive the glycerol oxidation reaction (GOR) at low overpotentials while maintaining high selectivity remains a central challenge. Understanding and manipulating the elementary steps of proton and oxygen anion transfer at the catalyst surface is considered a key lever for improving both activity and product distribution.

Nickel‑based hydroxides have emerged as earth‑abundant, cost‑effective catalysts for oxidation reactions in alkaline media. Their activity is intimately linked to the formation of NiOOH species, which serve as the active phase for oxygen‑atom transfer. Nevertheless, pristine Ni hydroxide typically suffers from sluggish kinetics and requires relatively high potentials to achieve practical current densities. Doping with transition metals such as cobalt can modify the electronic structure of the hydroxide lattice, thereby modulating the deintercalation of protons and oxygen anions during the electrochemical cycle. Despite this conceptual appeal, a systematic exploration of how Co incorporation influences the GOR activity of Ni hydroxide has not been thoroughly addressed.

To tackle this issue, we electrodeposited Ni hydroxide onto carbon cloth and subsequently introduced Co into the lattice by a cation‑exchange reaction, yielding a NiCo hydroxide with a Ni:Co atomic ratio of 1.26. The resulting materials were comprehensively characterized, and their GOR performance was evaluated in a double‑compartment H‑cell using a three‑electrode configuration. By combining cyclic voltammetry, Tafel analysis, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, electrochemical surface area normalization, turnover‑frequency calculations, and product analysis, we interrogated both the intrinsic activity and the kinetic barriers of the catalysts.

The NiCo hydroxide exhibited markedly enhanced GOR activity compared with the Ni and Co hydroxide counterparts, requiring only 1.35 V versus the reversible hydrogen electrode to reach a current density of 100 mA cm⁻². Electrochemical impedance spectra revealed the smallest charge‑transfer resistance for NiCo hydroxide, indicating the fastest reaction kinetics. After normalization by the electrochemically active surface area, NiCo hydroxide still delivered the highest activity, a finding corroborated by superior turnover frequencies and mass activity. Product analysis by high‑performance liquid chromatography identified glycerate as the major product, confirming that the enhanced activity does not compromise selectivity. Collectively, these results demonstrate that modulating proton and oxygen anion deintercalation through strategic Co doping is a viable design principle for developing high‑performance electrocatalysts for biomass oxidation.
