Junctophilin‑2 (JP2) is a critical structural component of the cardiomyocyte junctional membrane complex, where it bridges the plasma membrane T‑tubules and the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) to maintain the tightly organized dyad architecture required for efficient calcium‑induced calcium release (CICR) and excitation–contraction coupling. JP2 comprises a conserved N‑terminal membrane‑association domain followed by a divergent “joining” region and a C‑terminal divergent region that interact with both ryanodine receptors and the cytoskeleton. Because the integrity of the dyad is essential for proper calcium handling, alterations in JP2 expression or processing have been implicated in the pathogenesis of heart failure, arrhythmias, and hypertrophic remodeling.

Calpains are calcium‑dependent neutral cysteine proteases that modulate a variety of cellular substrates, including cytoskeletal proteins, signaling molecules, and membrane receptors, thereby influencing remodeling, survival, and stress responses in many tissues, including the heart. Previous work has shown that JP2 can be proteolyzed by calpains, generating truncated fragments that may disrupt dyad stability and calcium signaling. Early attempts to characterize JP2 cleavage relied mainly on immunoblotting with antibodies directed against specific epitopes or on fusion‑tag approaches. However, these strategies are limited by the presence of competing proteolytic pathways, species‑restricted antibody availability, and the anomalous migration behavior of JP2 in SDS‑PAGE, leading to an incomplete and sometimes contradictory picture of the calpain‑generated JP2 fragment landscape.

Recent computational predictions using the DeepCalpain algorithm have identified multiple plausible calpain cleavage sites within the joining and divergent regions of JP2, suggesting the existence of additional cleavage products that have not yet been experimentally documented. To resolve this uncertainty, we adopted a reductionist, substrate‑controlled in‑vitro system. Full‑length mouse JP2 (FL JP2) was heterologously expressed in Escherichia coli, purified, and incubated with purified calpain‑1 or calpain‑2 across a four‑order‑of‑magnitude concentration range (0.01–10 U ml⁻¹). By using Coomassie Blue R250 staining, which provides unbiased detection of all protein species, we captured the full spectrum of cleavage products generated under standardized reaction conditions without relying on antibody recognition.

Our in‑vitro analysis reveals a hierarchical fragmentation of JP2. At the lowest calpain‑2 concentration (0.01 U ml⁻¹), a single cleavage event produces two major fragments: an N‑terminal product (NT1) of ≈75 kDa and a C‑terminal product (CT1) of ≈25 kDa. Increasing protease activity (0.1 U ml⁻¹) introduces three additional cleavage reactions, generating the N‑terminal fragments NT2 (~20 kDa), NT1‑NT2 (~55 kDa), NT3 (~30 kDa), and NT1‑NT3 (~45 kDa), as well as a smaller C‑terminal fragment CT2 (~10 kDa). Notably, several fragments predicted by the DeepCalpain model (e.g., CT1‑CT2 and NT3‑NT2) were not observed, indicating that not all predicted sites are accessible or preferentially cleaved under these conditions. Importantly, the NT1 fragment harbors a DNA‑rich domain, a feature that raises the possibility that its calpain‑mediated release could impart novel nuclear or genomic regulatory functions in cardiomyocytes. Together, these findings provide a comprehensive map of calpain‑dependent JP2 cleavage and establish a foundation for future investigations into the functional consequences of JP2 proteolysis in cardiac physiology and disease.
