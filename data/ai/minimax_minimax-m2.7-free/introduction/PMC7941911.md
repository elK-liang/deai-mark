Non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) has become the most common chronic liver disorder worldwide, and its progressive form, non‑alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), is characterized by steatosis, hepatocellular injury, and pronounced inflammation. Chronic hepatic inflammation is a key driver of fibrosis and ultimately cirrhosis, yet the molecular mechanisms that translate simple lipid accumulation into a necro‑inflammatory phenotype remain incompletely defined. Experimental and clinical studies have shown that endoplasmic‑reticulum (ER) stress, reactive oxygen species, and innate immune activation cooperate to sustain inflammatory signaling in the liver, creating a permissive environment for disease progression.

A central node in these inflammatory networks is the NF‑κB pathway, which is activated by ER stress and multiple damage‑associated molecular patterns, leading to transcription of pro‑inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. Concurrently, c‑Jun N‑terminal kinase (JNK) signaling amplifies the inflammatory response and contributes to hepatocyte apoptosis and insulin resistance. Both pathways are tightly linked to ER stress‑mediated activation of the unfolded protein response, suggesting a tripartite interaction between metabolic stress, cellular stress signaling, and inflammation in NASH. Despite this conceptual framework, the upstream regulators that coordinate the crosstalk among ER stress, NF‑κB, and JNK in the context of NAFLD‑to‑NASH progression are still being elucidated.

Long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as versatile regulators of gene expression, influencing diverse biological processes including inflammation, metabolism, and cellular stress responses. Recent transcriptome analyses have revealed distinct lncRNA signatures in liver diseases, indicating that these molecules may participate in the pathogenic cascade that drives NAFLD toward NASH. Nevertheless, the repertoire of liver‑enriched lncRNAs that are dysregulated during disease progression and their functional contributions to inflammatory signaling remain largely unexplored. High‑throughput platforms such as mouse lncRNA microarrays provide an unbiased approach to capture global changes in the non‑coding transcriptome and to identify candidate regulators that merit further mechanistic investigation.

In the present study, we employed the Mouse LncRNA Microarray V2.0 to profile hepatic lncRNA and mRNA expression in lean control mice, db/db mice on normal diet (a model of NAFLD), and db/db mice fed a methionine‑choline‑deficient (MCD) diet (a model of NASH). The analyses revealed extensive differential expression, with 3,111 lncRNAs altered between NAFLD and control livers, 2,677 between NASH and control, and 3,337 between NASH and NAFLD. Venn mapping identified 63 lncRNAs that were consistently changed across all three comparisons, while series‑cluster analysis uncovered 381 lncRNAs that exhibited a progressive increase from normal to NAFLD to NASH, suggesting a potential link to disease severity. Among these progressive candidates, we focused on a previously uncharacterized lncRNA, Gm9795, whose expression rose markedly with advancing pathology. Based on the known involvement of ER stress, NF‑κB, and JNK signaling in NASH, we hypothesized that Gm9795 may contribute to the inflammatory milieu by modulating these pathways. The objective of this work was therefore to characterize the expression pattern of Gm9795, assess its correlation with inflammatory markers, and explore its potential mechanisms of action in the context of ER‑stress‑mediated activation of NF‑κB and JNK signaling in NASH.
