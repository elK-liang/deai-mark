C–H functionalization has become a cornerstone for building molecular complexity, particularly in the construction of carbon–heteroatom bonds that impart new electronic, steric, and coordination properties. The introduction of phosphorus functionalities into organic frameworks endows them with unique attributes that are valuable across catalysis, materials science, and medicinal chemistry. In the realm of organometallic chemistry, ferrocene (Fe(C₅H₅)₂) and related group 8 metallocenes are prized for their thermal stability, reversible redox behavior, and broad applicability. Access to phosphinated ferrocene derivatives can greatly expand their utility, yet regioselective C–H phosphorylation of these metallocenes remains a largely unmet challenge.  

Traditional routes to phosphonate‑substituted ferrocenes often rely on pre‑functionalization of cyclopentadienyl or aryl rings, multi‑step synthetic sequences, or stoichiometric oxidants (e.g., AgF, MnO₂, DDQ) that give low yields and limited regioselectivity. Moreover, such protocols frequently generate over‑phosphorylated side products and demand harsh reaction conditions, which restrict their practicality and scalability. Consequently, a mild, selective, and operationally simple method for installing P–C bonds directly onto metallocene scaffolds is highly desirable.  

Electrochemical oxidation provides a green alternative by using electrons as traceless redox reagents, enabling C–H activation under mild potentials while avoiding exogenous oxidants. Recent advances have demonstrated that paired electrolysis in undivided cells can achieve high levels of regioselectivity for C–H heteroatom functionalization when electrode materials, supporting electrolytes, and base additives are carefully matched. Despite these successes, the direct electrochemical phosphorylation of metallocenes has remained largely unexplored, leaving a gap between the potential of electro‑organic methods and the synthesis of phosphinated organometallic compounds.  

Here we disclose a practical, electrochemically driven regioselective C–H phosphorylation of benzoferrocene derivatives using diphenyl phosphine oxide as the phosphorus source. By employing a reticulated vitreous carbon anode paired with a platinum cathode in an undivided cell, with tetra‑n‑butylammonium acetate as the electrolyte and triethylamine as a base in methanol, the monophosphorylated product is obtained in 73 % isolated yield with excellent α‑selectivity on the phenyl ring. The transformation tolerates a broad range of substituents—including bromine, methyl, and phenyl groups—on the benzoferrocene framework and proceeds efficiently at room temperature. Control experiments confirm that current is essential for product formation, and only trace amounts of over‑phosphorylated species are observed, underscoring the high selectivity of the process. This electrochemical platform offers a straightforward, environmentally benign route to phosphinated group 8 metallocenes, thereby expanding the repertoire of C–H functionalization methodologies for organometallic synthesis.
