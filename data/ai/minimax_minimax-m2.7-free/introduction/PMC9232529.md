The sudden emergence of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID‑19) created an urgent need for clinical prediction models that could forecast patient outcomes such as need for mechanical ventilation, intensive‑care unit (ICU) admission, or death. Early in the pandemic, the limited availability of labeled COVID‑19 data hindered the development of robust machine‑learning solutions, prompting researchers to seek ways of leveraging existing electronic health record (EHR) datasets that capture similar clinical information. Electronic health records routinely contain structured codes for diagnoses, medications, procedures, laboratory tests, and demographic attributes, all of which form a common vocabulary that often transcends individual diseases. The possibility of reusing this vocabulary to train models that can later be adapted to a new pathogen offers a promising route toward rapid pandemic preparedness.

Transfer learning has become a cornerstone of modern natural language processing, where models pretrained on large corpora are fine‑tuned for downstream tasks with limited labeled data. In the medical domain, pretraining on large, diverse patient cohorts can impart the model with a broad understanding of clinical concepts before task‑specific adaptation. In this study, we took advantage of a previously collected Severe Respiratory Disease (SRD) cohort that shares a remarkably high proportion of clinical codes with the COVID‑19 cohort: 88.8 % of diagnosis codes, 94.04 % of medication codes, 63.2 % of procedure codes, and 90.57 % of laboratory test codes overlap between the two datasets. Moreover, the SRD cohort provides substantial coverage of severe outcomes—14.6 % of its patients required ventilation compared with 11.4 % in the COVID‑19 cohort, and ICU‑day and mortality proportions are also represented, albeit at different frequencies. These overlaps suggested that a model trained on SRD data could acquire a generalizable representation of the clinical vocabulary and outcome patterns that would be relevant to COVID‑19 prediction.

To exploit this shared vocabulary, we developed a hierarchical multi‑modal BERT architecture that simultaneously processes diagnosis codes, medication orders, procedure codes, laboratory results, and demographic information. The model respects the temporal structure of a patient’s hospital stay by segmenting each stay into fixed‑length aggregation windows and aggregating the multimodal data within each window. This design allows the model to capture both short‑term fluctuations and longer‑term trends in a patient’s condition. After pretraining on the SRD cohort, the model is fine‑tuned on the COVID‑19 cohort for binary outcome prediction tasks, such as the need for mechanical ventilation and in‑hospital mortality. Although the two cohorts differ significantly in age distribution, sex composition, race, and ethnicity (all supported by statistical testing), the fine‑tuning step enables the model to adapt to these demographic differences while retaining the benefits of the pretrained representation.

The primary aim of this work is to demonstrate that pretraining on an existing disease cohort with a highly overlapping clinical vocabulary can facilitate accurate COVID‑19 outcome prediction, even when demographic distributions diverge. By documenting the extent of vocabulary and outcome overlap and describing the hierarchical multi‑modal BERT framework, we provide a blueprint for rapidly building predictive models for future emerging pathogens using currently available EHR data. This approach not only accelerates model development during a crisis but also establishes a reusable paradigm for pandemic preparedness that leverages the rich, structured information embedded in routine clinical records.
