Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is a prevalent complication of diabetes mellitus that leads to progressive nerve damage, resulting in sensory and motor deficits. The underlying mechanisms of DPN are multifactorial, involving chronic hyperglycemia and dysregulation of cellular processes such as autophagy and apoptosis. Schwann cells, which are essential for peripheral nerve maintenance, have been implicated in the pathogenesis of diabetic neuropathy, yet the molecular drivers of their dysfunction remain incompletely understood.

PIAS1 (Protein inhibitor of activated STAT1) is an E3 small ubiquitin‑like modifier (SUMO) ligase that regulates diverse cellular pathways, including transcription and signaling cascades. While PIAS1 has been studied in metabolic and immune contexts, its expression and functional role in the peripheral nervous system under diabetic conditions are unknown. We hypothesized that downregulation of PIAS1 may contribute to the progression of DPN by altering autophagic and apoptotic responses in Schwann cells.

To test this hypothesis, we first analyzed the gene expression dataset GSE15653 from patients with diabetes and identified PIAS1 among the differentially expressed genes. We then validated these findings in vivo using db/db and ob/ob diabetic mice, measuring PIAS1 protein levels in sciatic nerve tissue, and in vitro using high glucose‑treated Schwann cells (SC4). Gain‑ and loss‑of‑function experiments were performed to modulate PIAS1 expression, and the effects on autophagy and apoptosis were assessed by immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and western blot analysis.

Our results demonstrate that PIAS1 expression is markedly reduced in the sciatic nerves of diabetic mice and in Schwann cells exposed to high glucose. Overexpression of PIAS1 increased the levels of autophagy markers Beclin‑1 and LC3II/I and elevated the Bcl‑2/Bax ratio, indicating enhanced autophagic activity and decreased apoptosis. Conversely, knockdown of PIAS1 attenuated autophagy and promoted apoptosis. These findings suggest that PIAS1 plays a protective role in diabetic peripheral neuropathy by promoting autophagy and inhibiting apoptosis in Schwann cells. Future studies will investigate the downstream mechanisms of PIAS1 action, including the potential involvement of SUMOylation of PPAR‑γ and miR‑124‑mediated downregulation of EZH2/STAT3.
