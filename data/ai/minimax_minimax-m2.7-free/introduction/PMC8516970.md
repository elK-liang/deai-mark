Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is one of the most common malignancies of the head and neck region, and its high mortality is largely attributable to locoregional invasion and distant metastasis. The process of metastasis involves a series of coordinated events, including loss of cell–cell adhesion, extracellular‑matrix degradation, intravasation, survival in the circulation, and colonization of distant organs. The tumor microenvironment (TME) has emerged as a critical regulator of these steps, providing bidirectional cues that can either suppress or promote tumor progression. Among the cellular components of the TME, mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) are abundant and can influence cancer cell behavior through direct contact, paracrine signaling, and remodeling of the extracellular matrix.

We previously established MSC cultures from oral leukoplakia (OLK‑MSCs) and from OSCC tissues (OSCC‑MSCs). Both cell populations displayed the canonical MSC surface phenotype (CD29⁺, CD73⁺, CD90⁺, CD105⁺, STRO‑1⁺) and were negative for hematopoietic markers (CD34⁻, CD45⁻). Multilineage differentiation assays confirmed their capability to undergo osteogenic, adipogenic, and chondrogenic differentiation. Functional studies using a transwell co‑culture system revealed that OSCC‑MSCs, but not OLK‑MSCs, markedly enhanced the migration and invasion of OSCC cell lines (CAL27 and WSU‑HN6). Importantly, conditioned medium (CM) collected from OSCC‑MSCs reproduced this effect, indicating that soluble factors secreted by tumor‑associated MSCs are sufficient to promote OSCC cell motility. In vivo, tail‑vein injection of WSU‑HN6 cells together with OSCC‑MSCs produced a significantly greater number of lung metastatic nodules compared with OLK‑MSCs, demonstrating a pro‑metastatic role for OSCC‑MSCs in a living organism.

The NF‑κB signaling pathway is a central coordinator of metastasis, regulating genes that control epithelial‑mesenchymal transition (EMT), inflammatory cytokine production, and cell survival. EMT, characterized by loss of epithelial markers such as E‑cadherin and α‑catenin and gain of mesenchymal markers including N‑cadherin and Vimentin, is a key step enabling carcinoma cells to detach and disseminate. Copine‑7 (CPNE7) is a calcium‑dependent membrane‑binding protein implicated in various cellular signaling cascades, and recent reports suggest it may intersect with NF‑κB activity. In preliminary analyses we observed that CPNE7 expression is elevated in OSCC‑MSCs relative to OLK‑MSCs, raising the hypothesis that CPNE7 upregulation in tumor‑associated MSCs drives OSCC metastasis through activation of the NF‑κB pathway and consequent induction of EMT.

To test this idea, we performed a series of in vitro migration and invasion assays with OSCC cells treated with OSCC‑MSC‑derived CM, evaluated lung colonization after co‑injection of OSCC‑MSCs and cancer cells in nude mice, and examined EMT marker expression and NF‑κB activity. Our results demonstrated that OSCC‑MSCs and their CM substantially increased OSCC cell migration and invasion, an effect accompanied by reduced levels of E‑cadherin and α‑catenin and elevated N‑cadherin and Vimentin, consistent with EMT. The enhanced metastatic burden in vivo was similarly linked to these changes. Moreover, the pro‑metastatic phenotype was associated with activation of NF‑κB downstream of CPNE7, suggesting a mechanistic bridge between MSC‑derived CPNE7 and tumor cell dissemination. Together, these findings identify CPNE7 in OSCC‑MSCs as a novel promoter of OSCC metastasis via the NF‑κB‑mediated EMT program, highlighting the CPNE7/NF‑κB axis as a potential therapeutic target for preventing OSCC spread.
