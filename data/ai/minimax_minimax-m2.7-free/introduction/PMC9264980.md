Gastric cancer (GC) remains one of the most common malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with particularly high incidence rates in East Asian countries including Korea. Despite advances in diagnostic techniques and therapeutic strategies, the prognosis for patients with gastric cancer remains poor, largely due to late-stage presentation and limited understanding of the molecular mechanisms driving tumor progression and metastasis. Identification of reliable prognostic biomarkers that can accurately predict patient outcomes and guide personalized treatment decisions is therefore of critical importance in improving clinical management of this disease.

The prion protein (PRNP) is a well-known glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored cell surface protein that is primarily expressed in the central nervous system, where it plays a role in neuroprotection and synaptic function. However, increasing evidence suggests that PRNP is also expressed in various types of cancer and may contribute to tumor progression, therapy resistance, and poor patient outcomes. Previous studies have reported that PRNP is involved in regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis in several malignancies, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma. The role of PRNP in gastric cancer, however, remains largely unexplored and warrants further investigation.

Emerging evidence indicates that PRNP may influence cancer progression through modulation of key oncogenic pathways, including the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), which is a critical process in tumor invasion and metastasis. Additionally, PRNP has been implicated in the Hedgehog signaling pathway and angiogenesis, both of which are important contributors to tumor growth and dissemination. Understanding the specific mechanisms by which PRNP exerts its effects in gastric cancer could provide valuable insights into disease pathogenesis and identify potential therapeutic targets.

In this study, we aimed to investigate the prognostic significance of PRNP expression in Korean patients with gastric cancer using multiple independent cohorts, including the ACRG, KSKG, YUSH, and TCGA-STAD datasets. We sought to determine whether high PRNP expression is associated with poor patient survival and to explore the potential biological pathways underlying this association through gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). By integrating clinical data with comprehensive molecular profiling, we hope to establish PRNP as a novel prognostic biomarker and provide a foundation for future studies targeting PRNP in gastric cancer therapeutics.
