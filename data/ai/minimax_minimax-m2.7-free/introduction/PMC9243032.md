Introduction

Colloidal quantum dots (CQDs) have emerged as promising materials for solution-processable light-emitting diodes and optically pumped lasers due to their tunable emission wavelengths, narrow linewidths, and high photoluminescence quantum yields. However, practical applications of CQD-based devices at high current densities remain challenging due to thermal management constraints. When operating at elevated current densities, Joule heating within the active device volume can lead to significant temperature increases, degrading device performance and limiting the maximum achievable brightness. Understanding and mitigating these thermal effects is therefore critical for realizing high-performance CQD optoelectronic devices.

Heat dissipation in operating devices depends critically on the active device area and the balance between heat generation and removal. The temperature rise during operation can be described by the relationship ΔT = AjVK⁻¹, where A represents the injection area, j is the current density, V is the device bias, and K is the heat exchange constant. This formulation suggests that reducing the injection area through current-focusing approaches can simultaneously decrease total heat generation and enhance heat exchange with the environment. Additionally, pulsed excitation schemes offer an alternative strategy for thermal management, as they can reduce average overheating by a factor proportional to the ratio of pulse duration to the characteristic heat dissipation time.

In this study, we investigate the combined effects of current focusing and pulsed excitation on device overheating and the maximum accessible current densities in CQD light-emitting diodes. We examine colloidal quantum dots with a CdSe/CdZnSe/ZnSeS core-gradient shell architecture that exhibit a high photoluminescence quantum yield of approximately 80% and a short single-exciton lifetime of 13.4 nanoseconds. These core-gradient quantum dots possess a thick radially graded shell that yields a biexciton lifetime of 1.2 nanoseconds and an electrical cross-section of approximately 5 × 10⁻¹² cm². These characteristics—particularly the reduced exciton lifetime and modified band-edge excitonic structure—make them well-suited for optical gain applications and should enable reduced lasing thresholds compared to conventional quantum dots.

We incorporate one to four layers of these core-gradient quantum dots into a p-i-n light-emitting diode featuring an inverted architecture, wherein the quantum dot emitters are positioned between an inorganic electron transport layer and an organic hole transport layer. By systematically investigating current focusing, pulsed excitation, and their synergistic combination, we demonstrate ultrabright electroluminescence and efficient optical gain at current densities reaching 1000 A cm⁻². These findings provide insights into thermal management strategies for high-power CQD optoelectronic devices and establish design principles for achieving two-band optical gain in colloidal quantum dot systems.
