Introduction

Osteosarcoma represents the most common primary malignant bone tumor in children and adolescents, characterized by aggressive local growth and a high propensity for metastatic spread, particularly to the lungs. Despite advances in multimodal treatment strategies combining surgery and chemotherapy, the overall survival rate for patients with metastatic or recurrent osteosarcoma remains poor, highlighting the urgent need for improved understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying oncogenesis and the identification of novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. The heterogeneity of osteosarcoma and its complex tumor microenvironment pose significant challenges for effective treatment, necessitating comprehensive investigations into the genetic and immunological alterations driving tumor progression.

In recent years, increasing evidence has demonstrated that the tumor immune microenvironment plays a critical role in osteosarcoma pathogenesis, recurrence, and response to therapy. The SPI1-TYROBP-FCER1G gene network has emerged as a potentially important regulator of immune cell function and inflammation within the tumor microenvironment. SPI1 (also known as PU.1) is a transcription factor involved in hematopoietic cell differentiation, while TYROBP and FCER1G encode proteins critical for immune receptor signaling in myeloid cells. Dysregulation of this network may contribute to the immunosuppressive phenotype observed in osteosarcoma, potentially facilitating tumor immune evasion and progression. However, the specific role of this network in osteosarcoma oncogenesis and its association with clinical outcomes remain largely unexplored.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a comprehensive multi-dataset analysis using gene expression data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository. By integrating four independent GEO datasets containing both normal control and osteosarcoma tissue samples, we performed differential expression analysis to identify genes with consistent alterations across datasets. The complementary nature of these datasets, which varied in their sources of normal controls (including primary osteoblasts, differentiated mesenchymal stem cells, and established cell lines) and osteosarcoma samples (including pre-chemotherapy biopsies, pediatric samples, and samples with metastatic involvement), provided a robust foundation for identifying credible differentially expressed genes. Following the identification of overlapping differentially expressed genes present in at least three datasets, we constructed a protein-protein interaction network to identify hub genes with the highest connectivity, followed by functional enrichment analysis to characterize the biological processes and pathways associated with these genes.

The objective of this study was to define the hub genes and functional gene clusters within the SPI1-TYROBP-FCER1G network and assess their association with immune infiltration and patient prognosis in osteosarcoma. Our analysis revealed a significant enrichment of immune-related functions among downregulated genes, particularly those associated with major histocompatibility complex class II proteins and lymphocyte/leukocyte proliferation, suggesting a substantial loss of immune activation capacity in osteosarcoma tissues. Among the identified hub genes with the highest connectivity in the network, including FN1, TYROBP, EGFR, CSF1R, C1QB, PLEK, LCP2, C1QA, CD86, and VWF, functional enrichment pointed to involvement in osteoclast differentiation, integrin binding, and extracellular matrix organization. These findings provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms linking the SPI1-TYROBP-FCER1G network to osteosarcoma development and immune evasion, and may inform the development of new therapeutic approaches targeting both oncogenic pathways and the tumor immune microenvironment.
