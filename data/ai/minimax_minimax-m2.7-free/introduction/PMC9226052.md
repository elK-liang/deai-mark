Active extensional provinces commonly host normal‑slip fault systems that accommodate crustal stretching.  In many of these settings, particularly where faults cut across topographic ridges, the fault geometry may evolve from simple dip‑slip to more complex trans‑tensional regimes, thereby influencing the size and distribution of earthquakes.  Understanding the three‑dimensional architecture and kinematics of such trans‑ridge normal faults is essential for reliable seismic‑hazard评估, yet the interplay between fault segmentation, slip partitioning, and earthquake generation often remains poorly resolved because of limited exposure and sparse kinematic data.

The study area lies in the southern Apennines, a region of ongoing extensional tectonics that includes the Vallo di Diano and Val d’Agri basins separated by the Monti della Maddalena ridge.  Previous investigations have identified several major normal faults in these basins, but the physical continuity, geometric relationship, and kinematic character of the faults that cross the ridge have not been fully documented.  In particular, the potential for a regional‑scale structure—here referred to as the Central Maddalena Fault (CMF)—to link the SSW‑dipping border fault of the Auletta basin at the northern end of the Vallo di Diano basin with the eastern Val d’Agri fault system has remained speculative.  This gap impedes a comprehensive assessment of the sources capable of generating large earthquakes in the area.

To address this issue we compiled and integrated ~370 fault‑ and slip‑attitude measurements collected in the field with previously published fault traces.  The data were organized into fourteen composite sites that represent continuous, homogeneous segments of the CMF.  For each site we derived average fault‑plane orientations, slip‑vector directions, rake values in the Aki‑Richard convention, and the trend and plunge of the associated tensile (T) axes.  The slickenside data were plotted on lower‑hemisphere stereonets using FaultKin8.1, and pseudo‑focal mechanisms were constructed to visualize the average kinematics.  The resulting T‑axis orientations are consistent with independent geodetic strain fields, confirming the reliability of the kinematic analysis.  The integrated dataset reveals a right en‑échelon geometric arrangement of fault strands, a progressive increase in dextral trans‑tensional component toward the southeast, and a marked kinematic partitioning that is strongly controlled by the segmentation of the fault system.

The primary goal of this study is to delineate the essentially continuous, albeit highly segmented, tectonic structure that connects the Auletta basin border fault with the eastern Val d’Agri fault system across the Monti della Maddalena ridge.  By documenting the detailed geometry, slip characteristics, and segmentation of the CMF, we provide a robust framework for interpreting its role in generating large earthquakes and for improving seismic‑hazard models in the southern Apennines.  The results demonstrate that the complex trans‑ridge geometry and associated kinematic partitioning are critical factors governing the earthquake potential of this major normal‑fault system.
