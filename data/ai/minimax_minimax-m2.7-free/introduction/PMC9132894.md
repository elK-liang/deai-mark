The formation of a supportive pre‑metastatic niche (PMN) in distant organs is increasingly recognized as a pivotal step that enables circulating tumor cells to colonize and progress toward metastasis.  A hallmark of the PMN is the coordinated remodeling of the extracellular matrix (ECM) by activated fibroblasts, which deposit collagen and fibronectin, secrete inflammatory cytokines, and create a locale conducive to tumor cell survival.  Tumor‑derived soluble factors and bone‑marrow‑derived hematopoietic cells are known to precondition these sites, yet the precise molecular drivers that sustain fibroblast activation and ECM deposition within the PMN remain active targets for therapeutic intervention.  Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), particularly MMP‑2, are prominently upregulated in premetastatic lungs, where they remodel the ECM and release bioactive fragments that amplify stromal signaling.  Consequently, strategies that can be selectively activated by the local MMP‑2 activity while simultaneously disrupting the pro‑fibrotic microenvironment hold promise for preventing the establishment of a metastatic foothold.

Recent advances in stimuli‑responsive nanomaterials have opened new avenues for programmable modulation of the tumor microenvironment.  Among these, self‑assembling peptides that undergo controlled polymerization upon exposure to disease‑associated enzymes or acidic pH provide a unique opportunity to form physical barriers precisely where and when they are needed.  In particular, peptide architectures that incorporate a cleavable linker susceptible to MMP‑2 can release a hydrophobic core that spontaneously folds into ordered, membrane‑like structures, thereby shielding surrounding cells from aberrant signaling.  Such “nano‑blankets” could theoretically block the interaction between activated fibroblasts and the ECM, interrupting the feed‑forward loop that sustains the PMN.

With this principle in mind, we designed a branched peptide, FR17, that integrates a self‑assembling backbone derived from the Aβ36‑derived Phe‑Phe‑Lys‑Tyr (FFKY) motif with a tumor‑immune‑modulating pentapeptide, thymopentin (TP5, RKDVY), appended via an MMP‑2‑cleavable linker (Gly‑Pro‑Leu‑Gly‑Leu‑Ala‑Gly‑Gly, GPLGLAGG).  The conjugation of the hydrophilic TP5 to the side chain of the central lysine residue enhances the overall solubility of the construct, while the cleavable linker ensures that enzymatic processing by MMP‑2 liberates the core monomer FG8 (FFK(GPLG)Y).  In both neutral and mildly acidic conditions that mimic the inflammatory PMN milieu, FG8 spontaneously self‑assembles into lamellar structures with an average diameter of ≈500 nm, as visualized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and cryo‑TEM.  Importantly, a scrambled version of the peptide (sFD17, FFK(GPLGLAGG‑RKYVD)Y) was used as a control, confirming that the specific sequence of TP5 is not required for assembly after MMP‑2 cleavage.  Using an aggregation‑induced emission (AIE) probe and fluorescence imaging of organ sections, we observed responsive assembly of the nano‑blanket within the intercellular space of PMN lungs, where the structures ranged from hundreds to thousands of nanometers depending on the local architecture.  These data demonstrate that FR17 can be selectively activated by the MMP‑2‑rich environment of the premetastatic lung, leading to in situ formation of a well‑defined, sheet‑like nanomatrix.

Functional characterization revealed that the nano‑blanket formed upon MMP‑2‑mediated activation of FR17 markedly impedes fibroblast activation and, consequently, the development of a pre‑metastatic niche in the lung.  By providing a physical barrier at the sites of tumor‑derived signaling, the assembled peptide matrix prevents the transition of fibroblasts to an activated, ECM‑producing phenotype, thereby disrupting the supportive microenvironment required for metastatic seeding.  This study introduces a new class of enzyme‑responsive, self‑assembling peptides that function as a therapeutic “nano‑blanket,” offering a targeted strategy to block early steps of metastasis by neutralizing the pro‑fibrotic, premetastatic landscape.
