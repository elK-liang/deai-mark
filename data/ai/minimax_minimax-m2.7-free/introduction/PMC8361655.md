Kidney transplant rejection (KTR) remains a major cause of graft loss after solid‑organ transplantation, and timely detection is essential for optimizing immunosuppressive therapy. Current clinical assessment relies heavily on serum creatinine measurements and histopathological examination of biopsy specimens, both of which have limited sensitivity for early or subclinical rejection and are invasive or subject to sampling error. These constraints have spurred intense interest in discovering non‑invasive, molecular biomarkers that can reliably distinguish between rejection phenotypes such as acute cellular rejection (ACR) and chronic humoral rejection (CMR).

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non‑coding RNAs that fine‑tune immune signaling pathways and have emerged as promising biomarkers for a variety of graft injuries. Circulating miRNAs are remarkably stable in body fluids and can reflect ongoing immune activation, making them attractive candidates for monitoring transplant status. Previous studies have documented dysregulated miRNA expression in kidney transplant recipients, yet the functional relevance of these changes within the broader context of immune regulatory networks remains incompletely understood.

To address this gap, we integrated differential expression data of miRNAs and mRNAs, miRNA‑target interactions, and protein‑protein interaction (PPI) information to construct a KTR‑specific miRNA‑PPI network (miR‑PPIN). The resulting network comprised 31 miRNAs and 642 genes connected by 949 miRNA‑mRNA pairs and 1,447 PPIs. STRING‑based enrichment analysis identified a strong over‑representation of T‑cell‑related biological processes (e.g., NK T‑cell proliferation, antigen processing and presentation, and memory T‑cell activation) [17], underscoring the immunological relevance of the identified modules. The network exhibited a scale‑free topology, with node degree distributions following a power‑law trend (slope ≈ –1.34), indicating that a small number of hub genes mediate most interactions. By prioritizing genes based on degree, closeness, and betweenness centrality, we defined a set of “CORE” targets that are likely to be critical for miRNA‑mediated regulation in KTR. Subsequent quantification of CORE target coverage by each miRNA revealed three miRNAs—miR‑145‑5p, miR‑155‑5p, and miR‑23b‑3p—that possessed the highest network‑based scores (NTGA and NTGC) and were selected as candidate biomarkers. Expression profiling showed that miR‑145‑5p and miR‑23b‑3p were down‑regulated in KTR, whereas miR‑155‑5p was up‑regulated, especially in CMR. Receiver‑operating‑characteristic (ROC) analyses performed on both the training cohort and an independent validation set demonstrated the diagnostic potential of these miRNAs, either individually or in combination, for detecting KTR and distinguishing its subtypes.

The present study therefore provides a systems‑level framework for interrogating miRNA‑driven mechanisms in kidney transplant rejection and establishes a panel of miRNA biomarkers that merit further validation as non‑invasive tools for early diagnosis and personalized management of KTR.
