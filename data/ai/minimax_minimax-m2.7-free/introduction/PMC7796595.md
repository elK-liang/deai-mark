Long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have been increasingly recognized as pivotal regulators of gene expression, chromatin remodeling, and signal transduction in both physiological and pathological contexts. In cancer biology, dysregulated lncRNAs can influence tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis by modulating diverse signaling networks, including the Wnt/β‑catenin pathway, which is frequently hyperactivated in many malignancies. Systematic analyses of lncRNA‑disease associations have revealed that specific lncRNAs are not merely bystanders but act as functional drivers of tumor phenotypes, underscoring the need to identify and characterize clinically relevant lncRNAs in human cancers.

Non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains the leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide, with lung adenocarcinoma (LAD) representing its most prevalent histologic subtype. Despite advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the molecular mechanisms that underlie LAD development and aggressiveness are incompletely understood. The integration of transcriptomic data with curated lncRNA‑disease repositories such as LncRNADisease v2.0 provides an opportunity to discover candidate lncRNAs that may serve as diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets for LAD. Recent investigations have highlighted several lncRNAs that are differentially expressed in NSCLC and correlate with patient outcomes, yet many others remain uncharacterized.

Through mining the LncRNADisease v2.0 database, which aggregates experimentally supported and predicted lncRNA‑disease associations from the literature and public resources, we identified CBR3‑AS1 as a lncRNA that is markedly overexpressed in NSCLC tissues. Subsequent validation in independent patient cohorts confirmed that CBR3‑AS1 levels are significantly elevated in LAD specimens compared with matched normal lung tissues, whereas no substantial changes were observed in large‑cell carcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma. Moreover, high CBR3‑AS1 expression in LAD patients was associated with shorter overall survival, suggesting a potential prognostic relevance. Quantitative PCR analyses in a panel of LAD cell lines further demonstrated that CBR3‑AS1 is consistently upregulated relative to normal bronchial epithelial cells, indicating that its dysregulation is a common event in the malignant transformation of lung epithelium.

To elucidate the mechanistic basis of CBR3‑AS1 action, we performed RNA pull‑down assays using in‑vitro‑transcribed CBR3‑AS1 incubated with A549 cell lysates, followed by mass spectrometry. This approach identified β‑catenin as a prominent CBR3‑AS1‑interacting protein, implicating the Wnt/β‑catenin signaling axis as a plausible downstream effector. Given the central role of β‑catenin in regulating cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, we hypothesized that CBR3‑AS1 may potentiate Wnt/β‑catenin signaling, thereby contributing to the aggressive phenotype of LAD. The present study was designed to investigate the functional consequences of CBR3‑AS1 expression on LAD cell behavior and to delineate its interplay with the β‑catenin pathway.
