Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is among the most lethal malignancies worldwide, ranking sixth in incidence and fifth in cancer‑related mortality. Despite advances in multimodal therapy, the prognosis for patients with ESCC remains poor, largely because the disease is frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage and exhibits a high propensity for metastasis. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify molecular drivers that underlie ESCC progression and to uncover actionable targets for therapeutic intervention. In recent years, long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as critical regulators of gene expression, cellular signaling, and tumor biology, highlighting their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic candidates in multiple cancers, including ESCC.

DLEU1 (Deleted in Lymphocytic Leukemia 1) is a conserved lncRNA that has been reported to be overexpressed in several tumor types, where it can function as an oncogenic driver by influencing cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastatic capacity. Although the molecular mechanisms through which DLEU1 exerts its effects are not fully defined, evidence from other malignancies suggests that DLEU1 may interact with components of the ubiquitination–proteasome system and modulate apoptotic pathways. In particular, the cytoplasmic protein DYNLL1 (Dynein Light Chain 1) has been shown to participate in the regulation of apoptosis and to be targeted for proteasomal degradation by E3 ubiquitin ligases such as RNF114, thereby affecting the balance of pro‑ and anti‑apoptotic proteins including BCL2. How DLEU1 might intersect with these regulatory circuits in ESCC has not been previously explored.

To investigate the clinical relevance and functional role of DLEU1 in ESCC, we performed a comprehensive analysis of lncRNA expression profiles across two independent cohorts of ESCC patients, which revealed that DLEU1 is among the most frequently upregulated lncRNAs in tumor tissues compared with matched normal epithelia. High DLEU1 expression was significantly associated with reduced overall survival, advanced clinical stage, and lymph node metastasis, indicating that DLEU1 may contribute to aggressive tumor behavior. Based on these findings, we hypothesized that DLEU1 promotes ESCC cell survival, at least in part, by preventing the ubiquitin‑mediated degradation of DYNLL1, thereby stabilizing downstream anti‑apoptotic effectors such as BCL2. In the present study, we aimed to (i) confirm the oncogenic function of DLEU1 in ESCC, (ii) delineate its impact on the DYNLL1–RNF114–BCL2 axis, and (iii) evaluate the therapeutic potential of targeting DLEU1 in preclinical models. Addressing these objectives will not only deepen our understanding of lncRNA‑mediated regulation of apoptosis in ESCC but may also reveal novel strategies for treating this devastating disease.
