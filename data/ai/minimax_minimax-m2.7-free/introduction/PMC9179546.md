Lower‑grade gliomas (LGGs), encompassing World Health Organization (WHO) grade II and III neoplasms, exhibit marked clinical and biological heterogeneity that complicates prognostication and therapeutic decision‑making. The 2021 WHO classification of central nervous system tumours introduced molecular markers—most notably IDH mutation status and 1p/19q co‑deletion—as decisive diagnostic criteria, stratifying LGGs into IDH‑wild‑type (IDH‑wt), IDH‑mutant astrocytoma (IDH‑A/IDH‑A′) and IDH‑mutant oligodendroglioma (IDH‑O) groups [36]. Despite this refinement, the underlying transcriptomic and epigenomic landscapes that drive the divergent behaviours of these entities remain incompletely resolved, prompting a growing interest in comprehensive, multi‑omics characterizations that move beyond single‑marker classifications.

Advances in high‑throughput technologies now enable simultaneous profiling of the transcriptome, DNA methylome and copy‑number variation (CNV) within the same tumour sample. Integrating these data layers can uncover regulatory modes that are obscured when each domain is examined in isolation, and machine‑learning approaches such as self‑organizing maps (SOM) have proven valuable for visualising high‑dimensional omics landscapes in a biologically interpretable manner. Nevertheless, previous efforts to subtype LGGs using either expression or methylation data independently identified eight expression subtypes (E1–E8) and six methylation subtypes (M1–M6) [25,35], yet the extent to which these subtypes align with the newly defined genetic groups has not been systematically explored.

In the present study we constructed integrated multi‑omics maps of LGGs by applying SOM‑based clustering to matched transcriptome, DNA methylome and CNV data. The resulting genetic classification reproduced the four groups mandated by the WHO 2021 scheme (IDH‑wt, IDH‑O, IDH‑A and IDH‑A′) and revealed distinct patterns of chromosomal alterations, TERT promoter methylation and CpG‑island methylator phenotype (GCIMP) signatures across the groups. Notably, combined gains of chromosome 7 (Chr7⁺) and losses of chromosome 10 (Chr10⁻)—hallmarks of glioblastoma—were enriched in IDH‑wt LGGs, whereas in IDH‑mutant tumours Chr7⁺ and Chr10⁻ occurred largely independently, with Chr7⁺ predominating in IDH‑A. IDH‑O tumours displayed a pronounced TERT promoter methylation profile, whereas mean methylation of the GCIMP signature and of G‑protein‑coupled receptors (GPCR) increased progressively from IDH‑wt through IDH‑A/A′ to IDH‑O, reflecting the hyper‑methylated state of IDH‑mutant gliomas. The GCIMP‑high LGGs showed a more favourable hazard ratio than IDH‑wt lesions, and IDH‑O tumours exhibited a marginally better prognosis than IDH‑A/A′. Mapping the previously reported expression (E1–E8) and methylation (M1–M6) subtypes onto the genetic groups demonstrated that E1 and M1 dominate the IDH‑wt cohort, E6 and M5 are enriched in IDH‑O, and E2–E4 and M2–M4 concentrate in IDH‑A/A′, while a subset of samples classified as neuronal (NL) type (E7/E8/M6) were dispersed across all genetic groups and displayed reduced tumour cell content. Together, these findings provide an integrated view of the molecular architecture of LGGs, linking genetic stratification with distinct transcriptional and epigenetic programmes and offering a resource for refined patient stratification and mechanistic exploration of glioma biology.
