Non‑small‑cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the predominant form of lung malignancy and a leading cause of cancer‑related death worldwide. The aggressive phenotype of NSCLC is characterized by rapid progression, early metastasis and high invasive potential, which together contribute to poor patient outcomes. Although advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy have improved clinical management for a subset of patients, the underlying molecular mechanisms that drive lung cancer progression remain incompletely understood. Identifying novel regulators of tumor invasion and metastasis is therefore essential for the development of more effective therapeutic strategies.

Ubiquitin‑specific peptidase 15 (USP15) is a deubiquitinating enzyme that has been implicated in the regulation of diverse cellular processes, including signal transduction, DNA repair and immune responses. Recent pan‑cancer analyses based on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) have revealed a widespread down‑regulation of USP15 in several tumor types, most notably in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC). In particular, USP15 transcripts are markedly reduced in lung tumor specimens compared with normal lung tissue, and low USP15 expression correlates with decreased overall survival in patients with LUAD and LUSC. These observations suggest that loss of USP15 may contribute to lung cancer pathogenesis, yet the functional consequences of USP15 deficiency in lung adenocarcinoma cells have not been thoroughly investigated.

Based on these clinical associations, we hypothesized that USP15 acts as a tumor‑suppressive regulator in lung adenocarcinoma by modulating key signaling pathways that control cancer cell survival, invasion and autophagy. The TRAF6‑BECN1 axis is a critical intracellular cascade that links ubiquitination events to the induction of autophagy, a process that can both restrain and promote tumor progression depending on context. In the present study, we aimed to determine whether USP15 negatively regulates lung cancer progression through the TRAF6‑BECN1 signaling axis and subsequent autophagy induction.

To address this, we generated USP15‑knockout (USP15KO) clones in the human lung adenocarcinoma cell lines A549 and H1299 using CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing. Using these loss‑of‑function models, we performed transwell invasion assays to evaluate the impact of USP15 deficiency on lung cancer cell invasiveness. Preliminary experiments indicated that loss of USP15 enhances the invasive capacity of lung cancer cells, prompting a detailed mechanistic exploration of the downstream signaling network involving TRAF6, BECN1 and autophagy. The findings from this work are expected to clarify how USP15 contributes to lung cancer progression and to identify potential therapeutic targets for intervention.
