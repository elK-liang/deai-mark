The emergence of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) in late 2019 led to a global pandemic that has profoundly impacted higher education institutions worldwide. Universities, with their dense populations, shared facilities, and interactive learning environments, represent settings where respiratory viruses can spread rapidly. Traditional surveillance methods for respiratory pathogens often rely on symptomatic testing, which may fail to detect presymptomatic and asymptomatic individuals who contribute significantly to transmission dynamics. This limitation has prompted institutions to explore alternative approaches that enable early detection and containment of outbreaks before widespread transmission occurs.

Saliva-based testing has emerged as a promising alternative to nasopharyngeal sampling for SARS-CoV-2 detection. This method offers several advantages including non-invasive sample collection, reduced need for trained healthcare personnel, and increased acceptability among participants. These characteristics make salivary testing particularly suitable for large-scale surveillance programs in institutional settings where repeated testing of asymptomatic individuals is required. The feasibility of implementing such programs depends on achieving sufficient participation rates and establishing sustainable testing protocols that can operate over extended periods.

In September 2020, the University of Padua launched a comprehensive salivary-based SARS-CoV-2 surveillance program targeting its workforce. A total of 8183 subjects, comprising 6764 permanent employees and 1419 temporary staff members, were invited to participate in the initiative. The program commenced in October 2020 with an initial enrollment of 5580 subjects (68% of those invited) who provided their first salivary sample during the autumn months. To optimize coverage, a second invitation was issued in January 2021, resulting in additional enrollment of 556 new participants. The mean monthly participation throughout the study period from October 2020 to May 2021 was 4790 employees, with participant numbers ranging from 3965 in May 2021 to 5447 in November 2020.

The present study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of this institution-wide salivary surveillance program in minimizing viral transmission during the second and third waves of the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic. We compared epidemiological outcomes between employees under active surveillance and those not participating in the program whose clinical status was tracked through routine occupational health monitoring. Our findings demonstrate that the University of Padua surveillance initiative achieved substantial participation rates and provided valuable insights into the role of systematic screening in mitigating SARS-CoV-2 transmission within a large academic community.
