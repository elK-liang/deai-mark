Metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) remains a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality worldwide, and the primary goal of first‑line treatment is to achieve rapid disease control while preserving quality of life (QoL). Over the past two decades, the addition of anti‑epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) monoclonal antibodies, such as cetuximab, to standard chemotherapy regimens has markedly improved objective response rates and, in many patients, leads to early tumor shrinkage (ETS). ETS, typically defined as a ≥20 % reduction in the sum of target lesion diameters at the first restaging, has emerged as a pragmatic surrogate for early treatment efficacy, correlating with higher objective response rates, increased rates of secondary resection of metastases, and, in some analyses, longer overall survival.

While the prognostic value of ETS has been well documented, less is known about its impact on patient‑reported outcomes (PROs). In the palliative setting, achieving tumor shrinkage is meaningful only if it translates into tangible symptomatic benefit and improved QoL for patients. Prospective collection of PRO data using validated instruments, such as the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) Quality of Life Questionnaire‑C30 (QLQ‑C30), enables a rigorous evaluation of how early radiographic changes relate to patients’ lived experience. However, few prospective studies have simultaneously assessed ETS and comprehensive QoL outcomes in patients receiving first‑line anti‑EGFR therapy, leaving a gap in the evidence base that is critical for shared decision‑making.

The QUACK trial (Phase II) was designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of first‑line cetuximab combined with chemotherapy in patients with unresectable mCRC. Among the 140 enrolled participants, 128 underwent baseline PRO assessment and were included in the present analysis. The cohort was characterized by a median age of 66 years (range 27–89), a predominance of male patients (68.0 %), and the majority having an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) of 0 (83.6 %). At study entry, 39.8 % of patients reported clinically relevant symptoms (“very much” or “quite a bit”) on at least one of the eight symptom items of the EORTC QLQ‑C30, indicating a substantial symptomatic burden despite a generally good performance status. Early tumor shrinkage (≥20 % reduction) was achieved in 64.1 % of evaluable patients, a rate consistent with previously reported ranges of 61.5–80 % for similar anti‑EGFR‑based regimens. Notably, patients who attained ETS demonstrated significantly higher objective response rates (76.8 % vs. 21.7 %; P < .001) and a higher likelihood of undergoing surgical resection of metastases (28.6 % vs. 11.4 %; P = .031), underscoring the clinical relevance of early shrinkage in this population.

Based on these observations, the primary objective of the present analysis was to evaluate the association between ETS and QoL outcomes, as measured by the EORTC QLQ‑C30, in patients receiving first‑line cetuximab plus chemotherapy. Specifically, we aimed to determine whether achieving ETS correlates with improved global health status/QoL scores and reduced symptom burden, and whether such benefits are mediated by higher response rates or increased resection rates. By linking radiographic early response to patient‑centred endpoints, this work seeks to provide clinicians with evidence that early tumor shrinkage not only signifies biologic activity but also translates into meaningful improvements in the daily lives of patients with mCRC.
