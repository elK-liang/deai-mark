Two‑dimensional semiconductor crystals have become a paradigm for next‑generation optoelectronic devices because of their atomically thin profile, strong quantum confinement, and tunable band‑structure. Understanding how photogenerated carriers move, relax, and recombine at the mesoscopic scale—where feature sizes are comparable to the carrier diffusion length—is essential for designing efficient light‑emitting diodes, photodetectors, and laser cavities. Conventional far‑field optical spectroscopy provides valuable ensemble‑averaged information, but it lacks the spatial resolution needed to resolve the intricate energy landscapes that govern carrier dynamics in these nanomaterials.

Scanning near‑field optical spectroscopy (SNOS) bridges this gap by combining the sub‑diffraction spatial precision of scanning probe techniques with the spectral richness of optical spectroscopy. By rastering a sub‑wavelength aperture or tip across a sample, SNOS can record a spectrum at each pixel, yielding maps of local emission energy and intensity that directly reflect the distribution of excited carriers and the underlying potential topography. When coupled with transport‑based analysis, the technique enables the visualization of carrier diffusion pathways and the identification of regions that act as sinks or sources for recombination.

The results presented in this work reveal a pronounced tendency for carriers generated in a semiconductor quantum well to drift toward low‑energy “bottom” regions, where the emission intensity is markedly higher than in surrounding hill‑like features. Gentle slopes in the energy landscape are found to increase the residence time of carriers, thereby enhancing recombination probability and leading to elevated intensity on widely spaced contours. Moreover, two‑probe measurements demonstrate that carriers can detour around areas of higher potential, suggesting that saddle‑shaped potentials steer diffusion paths and cause branching of the carrier stream toward adjacent stable valleys. These observations underscore that the local excitation point critically determines the subsequent spatial distribution of emission, implying that the integral intensity maps obtained under C‑mode operation differ from those derived from two‑probe transport measurements.

Motivated by these findings, we systematically apply scanning near‑field optical spectroscopy together with carrier‑transport analysis to map the energy and emission intensity distributions in mesoscopic regions of a two‑dimensional semiconductor. The study aims to clarify how the energy topography of the crystal shapes diffusion pathways, to compare the information content of C‑mode and two‑probe measurement schemes, and to provide guidelines for interpreting optical spectra in device operation regimes where carrier collection and recombination are governed by local potential minima and saddle points.
