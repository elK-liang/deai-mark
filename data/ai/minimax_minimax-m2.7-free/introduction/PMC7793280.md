Glial cells are essential for the support, metabolism, and homeostasis of neurons, and their morphology is tightly linked to proper nervous system function. Alterations in glial size have been observed in various neurological disorders and in models of neurodegeneration, suggesting that the mechanisms that set and maintain glial volume are critical for neuronal health. However, the molecular pathways that regulate the physical dimensions of glial cells remain incompletely understood.  

Forward genetic screens in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans have proven powerful for discovering genes that control cell morphology and physiology, particularly in the accessible peripheral glia. The amphid sheath (AMsh) glial cells, which ensheath the sensory endings of amphid neurons, provide a tractable model for investigating glial size regulation in a living organism. Unbiased mutagenesis followed by quantitative phenotypic analysis offers a means to identify novel regulators of glial morphology without prior assumptions about the underlying pathways.  

In a screen for mutants with abnormal glial dimensions, we isolated the yad70 strain, which displays dramatically enlarged AMsh cells. The enlarged phenotype is fully penetrant by day 2 of adulthood, with the cell bodies increasing in volume progressively from larval stages to reach roughly fourfold larger than those of wild‑type animals by day 3. Notably, the overall body length of yad70 animals is slightly reduced, and the glial enlargement is not suppressed by starvation, indicating that the size increase is a cell‑autonomous defect rather than a secondary effect of altered metabolism. The phasmid sheath glia also exhibit enlarged cell size in yad70 mutants, whereas the 12 amphid neurons stained with DiI appear morphologically normal, suggesting that the primary defect resides in the glial compartment. Consistent with the role of AMsh glia in supporting chemosensory neurons, yad70 mutants display reduced chemotaxis toward benzaldehyde and pyrazine and impaired long‑range avoidance of 1‑octanol, behaviors mediated by distinct sensory neurons. These defects mirror those reported after AMsh glial ablation, linking the morphological change to functional consequences.  

The marked increase in glial volume raises the possibility that the phenotype stems from abnormal accumulation of organelles or vacuoles. Given that eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) influences membrane composition and organelle dynamics and that the Golgi apparatus plays a central role in processing and packaging cellular material, we hypothesized that EPA might regulate glial size through a Golgi‑dependent mechanism. To test this, we examined whether manipulations of EPA levels alter the yad70 glial size phenotype and whether the Golgi apparatus contributes to the observed changes. By combining quantitative measurements of glial volume, subcellular organelle markers, and behavioral assays, we aim to delineate the pathway by which EPA and Golgi function intersect to control glial size and, consequently, neuronal function.
