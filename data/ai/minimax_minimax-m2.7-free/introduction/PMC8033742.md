Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) is a haematological malignancy characterized by rapid proliferation and poor prognosis, with drug resistance representing a major obstacle to durable remission. The FMS‑like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) is a frequently mutated driver in AML, and targeted inhibition of FLT3 has become a therapeutic focus. Several FLT3 inhibitors have entered clinical evaluation, but the emergence of resistance often limits their long‑term efficacy. Understanding the mechanisms by which AML cells acquire resistance and developing strategies to circumvent this phenomenon are critical to improving patient outcomes.

Quizartinib and pexidartinib are potent FLT3‑targeted agents that have shown activity in preclinical models of AML. Prior investigations demonstrated that both compounds reduce viable cell numbers in a concentration‑dependent manner, with half‑maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) in the sub‑nanomolar range for quizartinib and in the low‑nanomolar range for pexidartinib in AML cell lines such as MOLM‑14 and MV4‑11 [12, 27, 28]. These findings establish a baseline sensitivity that provides a foundation for probing how prolonged exposure to the drugs influences cellular behaviour.

A proposed approach to delay or prevent resistance is the rotation of drugs, which aims to expose tumour cells to a single agent at any given time, thereby reducing the selective pressure that favours resistant clones. In the present study, we examined whether repeated cycles of drug exposure followed by removal of the compound (by washing) could alter the resistance profile of AML cells. Because metabolism of quizartinib and pexidartinib is limited in the cell lines used, washing was necessary to avoid simultaneous exposure to both inhibitors. We therefore subjected MOLM‑14 and MV4‑11 cells to six successive generations of growth, drug treatment, and washing, monitoring proliferation at days 3 and 6 of each cycle.

Our initial viability assays confirmed the potent activity of both inhibitors after 48 h of treatment, consistent with the previously reported IC50 values. In the multi‑cycle grow‑treat‑wash protocol, cells initially displayed reduced growth rates, indicating effective inhibition, but subsequently regained proliferative capacity, revealing the development of resistance despite the washing steps. Notably, the washing procedure itself did not impair cell growth, as the populations continued to expand across generations without a decline in growth rate relative to the first cycle. Moreover, MV4‑11 cells exposed to quizartinib showed a progressive increase in day 6 cell numbers across later generations, suggesting an adaptive response that accumulates with repeated treatment. These observations provide insight into the dynamics of resistance emergence under intermittent drug exposure and highlight the need for further optimization of rotation strategies to combat FLT3 inhibitor resistance in AML.
