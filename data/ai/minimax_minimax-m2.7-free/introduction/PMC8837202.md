Ribosome biogenesis requires the coordinated action of numerous AAA+ mechanoenzymes that harness ATP hydrolysis to remodel pre‑ribosomal particles. Among these, Mdn1 (also known as Drg1) is an essential AAA+ ATPase that catalyzes the release of maturation factors from the large ribosomal subunit pre‑cursor (reviewed in Ahmed et al., 2019). The C‑terminal region of Mdn1 contains a Metal Ion‑Dependent Adhesion Site (MIDAS) motif that is predicted to engage the Ubiquitin‑Like (UBL) domains of ribosomal factors such as Rsa4, a conserved pre‑ribosomal protein required for early large subunit assembly steps (Mickolajczyk et al., 2020). Structural studies have hinted that the MIDAS motif may adopt a conformation suitable for a mechanical role, yet the biochemical basis of its interaction with UBL domains has remained incompletely understood.

The UBL domain is a compact β‑grasp fold that frequently mediates protein–protein interactions in ribosome assembly and ubiquitin‑like pathways. In several receptor–ligand systems, mechanical force can convert ordinarily weak, transient interactions into long‑lived “catch bonds” that strengthen under tension, a phenomenon that underpins force‑sensing in cytoskeletal and adhesion complexes (e.g., Thomas, 2008). Whether the MIDAS–UBL interaction in Mdn1 operates as a catch bond has not been tested, although the presence of a dedicated mechanical element in a AAA+ enzyme suggests that force regulation could be physiologically relevant during ribosomal remodeling.

To address this gap, we purified the isolated MIDAS domain of Mdn1 (aa 4381–4717) as a SNAP‑tagged construct, generating both wild‑type (MIDAS‑WT) and a point mutant (MIDAS‑Y4666R) known to impair UBL binding (Ahmed et al., 2019). Full‑length Rsa4 bearing a C‑terminal GFP tag (Rsa4‑GFP) was expressed in insect cells and shown by native mass spectrometry and mass photometry to be a monomer of the expected mass. Binding between the MIDAS domain and Rsa4‑GFP was first examined by size‑exclusion chromatography co‑elution assays, which revealed a clear shift in the elution profile when MIDAS‑WT was mixed with Rsa4‑GFP, indicative of complex formation. In contrast, the Y4666R mutant failed to produce any co‑elution, confirming that the observed interaction is specific and dependent on the conserved tyrosine. Quantitative assessment by microscale thermophoresis (MST) yielded a dissociation constant of KD ≈ 6.9 ± 2.0 µM for MIDAS‑WT, whereas the mutant produced insufficient signal to be fitted, underscoring the essential role of Y4666 in substrate recognition.

These biochemical data demonstrate that the Mdn1 MIDAS domain binds Rsa4 with low‑micromolar affinity and that the Y4666 residue is critical for the interaction. Importantly, force‑loaded binding assays performed in parallel (see accompanying article) reveal that the MIDAS domain forms catch bonds not only with Rsa4 but also with a second UBL‑containing pre‑ribosomal substrate. The force‑dependent reinforcement of the MIDAS–UBL interaction provides a mechanistic link between the ATP‑driven remodeling activity of Mdn1 and the generation of mechanical tension within the nascent ribosome, thereby ensuring proper factor release and fidelity of assembly.
