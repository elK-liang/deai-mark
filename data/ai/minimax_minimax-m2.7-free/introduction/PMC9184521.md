GaV4S8 is a members of the lacunar spinel family that combines semiconducting behavior with magnetic long‑range order, placing it at the intersection of multifunctional oxides and spintronic materials. Below the Curie temperature TC ≈ 13 K the compound hosts a rich magnetic phase diagram that includes a ferromagnetic (FM) phase, a cycloidal (Cyc) phase, and an extended skyrmion‑lattice (SkL) phase. The SkL phase in GaV4S8 occupies a unusually broad region of the temperature–field phase diagram, which makes it an ideal playground for probing the dynamics of topologically nontrivial spin textures under controlled perturbations.  

Below ≈44 K a cooperative Jahn–Teller distortion drives a structural transition from the high‑temperature cubic lattice to a polar rhombohedral symmetry. This transition creates a sub‑micron‑thick lamella‑type multi‑domain architecture, each domain possessing a uniaxial magnetic easy axis that aligns with one of the four equivalent ⟨111⟩ directions. The strength of the uniaxial anisotropy, which has been quantified by electron‑spin‑resonance measurements, governs the stability of the SkL phase and confines the skyrmion tubes to lie along the easy axis. Recent work on the isostructural sister compound GaV4Se8 further revealed that the walls separating polar structural domains can host twisted magnetic configurations distinct from the domain interiors, underscoring the intimate link between structural polarity and magnetic texture in this family.  

To access the ultrafast spin dynamics across these phases we employed time‑resolved magneto‑optical Kerr effect (trMOKE) spectroscopy. The experiments were performed using a high‑pulse‑energy Yb:KGW regenerative amplifier operating at 100 kHz, which delivers stronger photoexcitation than conventional setups and enables robust detection of transient polar rotation (Δθ) signals. By applying the external magnetic field normal to the (100) crystal face, the four possible structural domains contribute equally to the measured signal, allowing a direct comparison of the photoinduced dynamics in the FM, Cyc and SkL phases at several temperatures (8 K, 10 K, 11.5 K and 14.5 K) and across a range of magnetic fields.  

The trMOKE data reveal a pronounced slowdown of the photoexcited spin dynamics when the system resides in the non‑collinear Cyc and SkL phases relative to the FM regime. The relaxation times increase markedly as the skyrmion lattice becomes stable, indicating that the presence of cycloidal or skyrmion‑tube order strongly modifies the pathways for spin relaxation. The observed trend correlates with the uniaxial anisotropy that pins the skyrmion tubes along the ⟨111⟩ axes, suggesting that the topological character of the SkL phase and the associated spin curvature influence the ultrafast magnetic response. These findings provide new insight into the interaction between lattice symmetry, magnetic anisotropy, and spin dynamics, highlighting the potential for light‑driven control of magnetic order in skyrmion‑hosting multiferroics.
