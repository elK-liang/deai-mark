Radiotherapy relies on the precise delivery of ionising radiation to tumour tissue, yet a fraction of the radiation inevitably interacts with surrounding healthy organs and circulating blood. Quantifying the biological impact of this exposure in patients is challenging, and robust molecular markers that can reflect the dose received by circulating leukocytes are needed for treatment monitoring and risk assessment. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) provide an accessible source for such biomarkers, and changes in their transcriptional programs after DNA damage have been explored as indicators of radiation exposure.

The cellular response to DNA double‑strand breaks involves the up‑regulation of a set of damage‑responsive genes, several of which have been investigated as potential radiation biomarkers. Among them, FDXR has attracted attention because it appears to be highly inducible after ionising radiation. Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)‑based measurement of FDXR and related transcripts (CCNG1, PHPT1, CDKN1, SESN1, PUMA, DDB2, MDM2, GADD45) in PBMCs offers a sensitive approach to capture this transcriptional response. Nevertheless, most data on the kinetics of these genes derive from in vitro irradiation models or from animal experiments, and comprehensive in‑patient time‑course studies across different anatomical treatment sites are still limited.

In the work described here, we profiled the expression of nine DNA‑damage‑responsive genes in the circulating leukocytes of six patients who received radiotherapy for four distinct cancer types (laryngeal/oropharyngeal, rectal, lung, and metastatic brain). The patients were treated with fractionated regimens ranging from 10 to 35 fractions, delivering total doses of 30–70 Gy to the tumour while exposing varying volumes of normal tissue (3.5 % of the body for brain, 3.9–4.1 % for neck, 8.3 % for lung, and 16.7–19.1 % for pelvis). The calculated dose per fraction to the blood mirrored these volumes, ranging from 0.038–0.045 Gy for neck irradiations to 0.155–0.169 Gy for pelvic irradiations. Blood samples were collected at multiple time points: 2 h, 6–8 h, 16–18 h, and 24 h after the first fraction, as well as at the middle and at the end of the treatment course. By examining both early and late transcriptional changes, we aimed to identify the gene(s) that show the most consistent and pronounced induction during fractionated radiotherapy.

The analysis revealed that, whereas most genes were not significantly elevated at the early post‑irradiation time points, a subset—CCNG1, SESN1, and DDB2—showed significant up‑regulation at the mid‑treatment and end‑of‑treatment collections when the cumulative dose to the blood was high. Notably, FDXR displayed the highest fold change among the examined transcripts, reaching a mean increase of 2.72‑fold at 8 h after the first fraction. Consequently, FDXR was selected for subsequent detailed evaluation. These findings provide a foundation for using FDXR as a responsive biomarker to monitor radiation‑induced transcriptional activity in the peripheral blood of patients undergoing radiotherapy.
