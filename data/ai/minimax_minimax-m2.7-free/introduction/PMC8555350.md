Hepatoblastoma is the most frequent primary malignant liver tumor in children, accounting for approximately 1 % of all pediatric cancers worldwide. Despite advances in multimodal therapy, including surgical resection and cisplatin‑based chemotherapy, the prognosis for patients with high‑risk or metastatic disease remains poor, and long‑term survivors often suffer from treatment‑related morbidities. The marked heterogeneity of hepatoblastoma at the histological and molecular levels underscores the need for a deeper understanding of its underlying biology and for the identification of robust biomarkers that can guide risk stratification and therapeutic decision‑making. In recent years, the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) has emerged as a critical determinant of tumor behavior in many adult malignancies, but its role in hepatoblastoma pathogenesis and clinical outcome is only beginning to be elucidated.

The interplay between tumor cells and their immune stroma is shaped by both genetic and epigenetic alterations. Differential gene expression can dictate the recruitment and activation of immune cells, whereas DNA methylation—a key epigenetic modification—can silence or activate immune‑related genes, thereby influencing the composition and functional state of the TIME. For example, hyper‑ or hypo‑methylation of promoter regions has been shown to modulate the expression of cytokines, chemokines, and immune checkpoint molecules in several cancers. In hepatoblastoma, a limited number of studies have described dysregulated genes and methylation changes, yet an integrated analysis that links these genomic and epigenomic alterations to the immune landscape is still lacking.

The rapid accumulation of publicly available high‑throughput datasets provides a unique opportunity to explore the molecular underpinnings of hepatoblastoma in large, independent cohorts. Gene expression repositories such as the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) host microarray (e.g., GSE75271) and RNA‑seq (e.g., GSE81928) datasets from both tumor and normal liver tissues, while methylation arrays (e.g., GSE78732) enable genome‑wide profiling of DNA methylation states. By systematically interrogating these datasets, it is possible to delineate a comprehensive set of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and differentially methylated sites/genes (DMSs/DMGs), and to investigate their potential convergence on pathways that regulate the TIME.

The objective of the present study was to develop and validate genomic and epigenomic signatures associated with the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatoblastoma. To this end, we performed a step‑wise analysis of publicly available expression and methylation data, identifying robust DEGs across independent transcriptomic datasets, uncovering genome‑wide methylation alterations, and subsequently intersecting these results to pinpoint genes whose expression and methylation status are coordinately altered. The resulting signatures may not only illuminate the molecular basis of immune evasion in hepatoblastoma but also serve as a foundation for future investigations aimed at prognostic stratification and the exploration of immunotherapeutic targets.
