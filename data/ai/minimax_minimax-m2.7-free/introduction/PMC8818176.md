Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a heterogeneous disorder characterized by persistent airflow limitation and a high burden of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Acute exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD) are pivotal events that accelerate lung function decline, increase healthcare utilization, and worsen long‑term prognosis. The underlying mechanisms driving exacerbation onset and progression remain incompletely understood, but emerging evidence suggests that perturbations in the airway microbiome may contribute to inflammatory bursts and clinical deterioration.

Culture‑independent techniques, particularly 16S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) gene sequencing, have revealed that the lower respiratory tract harbors a diverse bacterial community even in the absence of overt infection. In COPD patients, shifts in the composition and relative abundance of these communities have been documented during stable disease and exacerbations, implying that dysbiosis of the airway microbiome could be involved in disease pathophysiology. However, the majority of studies have focused on selected clinical phenotypes or have been limited by small sample sizes, and the relationship between sputum microbiome characteristics and quantitative clinical indices—such as lung function, dyspnea scores, and inflammatory biomarkers—remains incompletely resolved.

To address these gaps, we performed a cross‑sectional investigation of sputum specimens collected from healthy controls and from COPD patients representing distinct clinical states: acute exacerbation, early recovery, and stable disease. Using 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing, we characterized the bacterial profiles of each group and examined associations between microbiome variables and a panel of clinical parameters, including spirometric measures, modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scores, blood inflammatory markers, and exacerbation frequency. The aim of this study was to delineate patterns of sputum microbiome composition across the COPD spectrum and to evaluate the extent to which these microbial signatures correlate with clinically relevant outcomes.
