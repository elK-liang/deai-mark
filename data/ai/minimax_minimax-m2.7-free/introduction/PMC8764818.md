The accurate interpretation of sequence variants is a cornerstone of clinical genetics, influencing diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic decisions for patients with hereditary disease. Since the publication of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) and the Association for Molecular Pathology (AMP) 2015 guidelines, the field has moved toward a systematic, evidence‑based framework for classifying variants into five categories—pathogenic, likely pathogenic, uncertain significance, likely benign, and benign [1]. Nevertheless, the implementation of these recommendations in everyday practice remains challenging because it requires the aggregation of diverse data sources (clinical phenotypes, population genetics, functional assays, and segregation studies) and the application of disease‑ and gene‑specific criteria. In response, the Clinical Genome Resource (ClinGen) has established Variant Curation Expert Panels (VCEPs) that adapt the ACMG/AMP rules to particular gene‑disease pairs, creating a pathway for high‑quality, expert‑reviewed variant classifications that meet regulatory standards.

To facilitate the standardized, reproducible application of these criteria, we developed the Variant Curation Interface (VCI), a web‑based platform designed for health‑care providers, researchers, and the broader medical genetics community. The VCI aligns with the FDA‑recognized ClinGen variant curation workflow, integrating clinical, genetic, population, and functional evidence within a single environment. Key functionalities include the ability to curate variants individually or in bulk, to attach pertinent evidence to each classification, to assess that evidence according to disease‑ and gene‑specific protocols, to save provisional records, and to support an expert review process before final release. Once a curation is approved, the platform automatically publishes the classification and its underlying criteria assessments to the ClinVar‑linked ERepo repository, ensuring transparent public access.

The current VCI version 2.0 hosts more than 6,300 variant classifications at various stages of curation, demonstrating its immediate utility and adoption. Built on a modernized, modular architecture, the system is engineered to scale to over one million future classifications, accommodating the growing demand for comprehensive variant databases. The underlying database maintains a complete audit trail of all user actions, satisfying the FDA’s requirements for traceability and auditability of the ClinGen curation process. This infrastructure not only supports rigorous quality control but also enables continuous compliance monitoring as new regulatory expectations emerge.

Future enhancements of the VCI will focus on improving workflow efficiency and scalability through task‑management tools, bulk‑curation workflows, and a streaming service that automatically incorporates updated evidence and alerts curators to major data changes. Additional plans include the provision of customized curation experiences tailored to VCEP specifications, permanent archiving of records, and regular knowledge‑base updates driven by systematic literature and database monitoring. Collectively, these developments aim to position the VCI as a durable, FDA‑compliant platform capable of supporting the expanding scope of expert‑driven variant classification for precision medicine.
