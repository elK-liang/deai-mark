Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide, and understanding the molecular mechanisms that drive its progression is critical for developing effective therapeutic strategies. Cellular processes such as DNA damage response, senescence, and apoptosis play complex roles in tumor development and treatment response. While senescence traditionally functions as a tumor-suppressive mechanism to prevent malignant transformation, evidence suggests that DNA damage accumulation and disrupted stress responses can paradoxically promote cancer progression. The transcription factor AP4 (TFAP4) has emerged as a potential regulator of these cellular processes, though its specific functions in colorectal cancer pathogenesis remain incompletely understood.

In this study, we investigated the role of AP4 in colorectal cancer cell lines DLD-1 and SW480 using a CRISPR-Cas9 approach to generate AP4-deficient cells. Our results demonstrate that AP4 deficiency leads to a pronounced senescent phenotype, characterized by enlarged and flattened cell morphology and significantly increased SA-β-gal positivity. This senescence induction was directly attributable to loss of AP4 function, as ectopic expression of AP4 reversed the increased senescence in deficient cells. Beyond senescence, AP4-deficient cells exhibited reduced colony-forming capacity, increased apoptosis, decreased viability, and impaired proliferation compared to AP4-proficient controls.

Notably, the senescence phenotype observed in AP4-deficient cells appeared to result from elevated endogenous DNA damage. AP4-deficient DLD-1 cells displayed significantly increased γH2AX-positive foci and elevated γH2AX protein levels, markers of DNA double-strand breaks. Furthermore, comet assay analysis revealed that AP4-deficient cells accumulated longer tails of unrepaired DNA, indicating impaired DNA damage repair. These defects in DNA damage response were rescued by ectopic AP4 expression, confirming that AP4 actively suppresses DNA damage accumulation and consequent senescence in CRC cells.

Collectively, these findings establish AP4 as a critical suppressor of DNA damage, chromosomal instability, and senescence in colorectal cancer cells. The ability of AP4 to maintain genomic integrity and prevent stress-induced senescence suggests that dysregulation of AP4 may contribute to colorectal cancer progression. Future studies examining the downstream effectors of AP4 in DNA damage repair pathways may reveal novel therapeutic targets for colorectal cancer treatment.
