Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most frequently diagnosed malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality. Despite advances in screening, surgical techniques and systemic therapies, the 5‑year survival rate for patients with metastatic disease remains unsatisfactory, underscoring the need for a deeper understanding of the molecular drivers that underlie tumor initiation, progression and dissemination. Identification of key oncogenic regulators that contribute to the aggressive phenotype of CRC can provide novel biomarkers for prognosis and therapeutic targeting.

DEAD‑box helicases constitute a large family of ATP‑dependent RNA unwindases that participate in virtually every aspect of RNA metabolism, including transcription, splicing, ribosome biogenesis and translation. Dysregulation of several DEAD‑box proteins has been documented in a variety of human cancers, where they often act as oncogenes or tumor suppressors by influencing gene expression programs that control cell proliferation, apoptosis and invasion. DDX10 (DEAD‑box helicase 10) belongs to this family and has been implicated in the maturation of ribosomal subunits and in pre‑mRNA splicing, yet its specific role and clinical relevance in colorectal tumorigenesis have not been thoroughly explored.

Recent integrative analyses of large‑scale transcriptomic datasets have revealed that DDX10 is consistently up‑regulated at the mRNA level in CRC tissues compared with matched normal colonic mucosa, a finding supported by multiple independent cohorts including The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) series GSE74604, and the ONCOMINE and UALCAN databases. Immunohistochemical evaluation of clinical specimens confirmed a corresponding increase in DDX10 protein expression, and Kaplan‑Meier analysis of the GSE17537 cohort demonstrated that higher DDX10 levels are significantly associated with poorer disease‑free survival. In addition, targeted sequencing data indicated that DDX10 harbors somatic mutations in a small proportion (≈3.4 %) of CRC cases, suggesting that genetic alterations may also contribute to its oncogenic potential. Consistent with these clinical observations, DDX10 expression is markedly elevated in several CRC cell lines relative to the non‑transformed intestinal epithelial cell line NCM460, pointing to a cell‑autonomous effect of DDX10 on the malignant phenotype.

Functional investigations have demonstrated that DDX10 can enhance the proliferative and metastatic capacities of CRC cells, indicating that it functions as a novel oncogenic driver in this context. Mechanistic studies have further revealed that DDX10 exerts its effects, at least in part, through the alternative splicing of RPL35, a core component of the 60S ribosomal subunit. Dysregulated splicing of RPL35 may impact ribosomal biogenesis and protein translation, thereby influencing pathways that control cell growth and motility. In the present study, we systematically characterize DDX10 expression in CRC, evaluate its prognostic significance, and delineate the functional consequences of DDX10 knockdown or over‑expression on cellular phenotypes. Moreover, we explore the splice‑regulatory activity of DDX10 with respect to RPL35, aiming to elucidate the molecular circuitry linking DDX10 to the aggressive behavior of colorectal tumors.
