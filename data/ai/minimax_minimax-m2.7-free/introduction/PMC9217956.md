Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is one of the most common malignancies of the head and neck region, accounting for a substantial proportion of cancer‑related deaths worldwide. Despite advances in surgical and adjuvant therapies, the overall 5‑year survival rate for OSCC remains modest, largely because many patients present with advanced disease or develop recurrences that are difficult to treat. This clinical reality underscores the need for reliable prognostic biomarkers that can accurately stratify patients according to their risk of progression and death, thereby guiding individualized therapeutic strategies.

Histone deacetylase 6 (HDAC6), a cytoplasmic member of the HDAC family, has emerged as a key regulator of numerous oncogenic pathways, including those controlling cell proliferation, migration, and immune evasion. Recent evidence indicates that HDAC6 expression is frequently altered in several solid tumors, and its overexpression has been linked to aggressive phenotypes and poor patient outcomes. In the context of OSCC, our preliminary analysis of clinical specimens demonstrated a statistically significant positive correlation between HDAC6 expression intensity and tumor grade, with high HDAC6 levels being associated with a 3.248‑fold increase in mortality risk compared with low expression (P = 0.003). These observations suggest that HDAC6 may serve not only as a marker of biological aggressiveness but also as a potential determinant of patient survival.

The tumor microenvironment is increasingly recognized as a critical driver of cancer progression, and tumor‑associated macrophages (TAMs) constitute a major cellular component that can adopt pro‑tumorigenic (M2‑like) or anti‑tumorigenic (M1‑like) phenotypes. Interleukin‑13 (IL‑13) is a Th2‑type cytokine known to promote the polarization of macrophages toward the M2 phenotype, thereby fostering tumor growth, angiogenesis, and immune suppression. The transcription factor activator protein‑1 (AP‑1) has been implicated in the transcriptional regulation of IL‑13 and other cytokines, linking extracellular signals to the remodeling of the tumor microenvironment. Given HDAC6’s capacity to influence gene expression through both histone and non‑histone substrates, it is plausible that HDAC6 may modulate IL‑13 production and, consequently, macrophage polarization via AP‑1‑dependent mechanisms, thereby contributing to the aggressive behavior of HDAC6‑high OSCC tumors.

In light of the above considerations, the present study was designed to (i) comprehensively evaluate the prognostic value of HDAC6 expression in a well‑characterized cohort of 133 OSCC patients, (ii) determine the relationship between HDAC6 intensity and clinicopathological parameters, including overall survival, and (iii) explore the functional interplay between HDAC6, IL‑13 expression, and AP‑1‑mediated macrophage polarization. By elucidating these connections, we aim to establish HDAC6 as a robust prognostic biomarker and to uncover a mechanistic pathway through which HDAC6 may shape the immune landscape of OSCC, offering potential therapeutic targets for future intervention.
