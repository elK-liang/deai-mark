Primary breast lymphoma (PBL) is an uncommon malignancy, accounting for only a small proportion of breast neoplasms. The majority of PBL cases are diffuse large B‑cell lymphoma (DLBCL), which typically presents as a rapidly enlarging, painless unilateral mass. While the clinical behaviour of PBL generally mirrors that of nodal DLBCL, the breast as a primary site introduces unique considerations regarding local therapy, cosmetic outcome, and the risk of subsequent local relapse.

Among DLBCL, a subset is characterised by concurrent genetic alterations involving the oncogenes MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6. These “double‑hit” lymphomas (DHL) are defined by the simultaneous presence of MYC translocation together with BCL2 or BCL6 rearrangement and are recognised for their aggressive clinical course and unfavourable prognosis. Although DHL has been extensively described in nodal and various extranodal large‑cell lymphomas, data specifically focusing on primary breast DHL remain limited, with most reported series comprising only a handful of cases. Consequently, the optimal management of this rare entity is still ill‑defined.

Current treatment paradigms for DLBCL, including dose‑adjusted etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and rituximab (DA‑EPOCH‑R), rituximab‑hyper‑CVAD, and other rituximab‑based regimens, have been adopted for primary breast DHL. Intensified regimens and consolidation with high‑dose chemotherapy followed by autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) are frequently considered for patients with high‑risk features, yet evidence supporting a uniform approach is lacking. Moreover, the influence of biologic subtypes (e.g., germinal‑centre B‑cell versus non‑germinal‑centre B‑cell origin) and the spectrum of additional mutations detected by next‑generation sequencing (NGS) on therapeutic efficacy have not been systematically explored in this population.

To address these knowledge gaps, we performed a multicentre retrospective review of patients diagnosed with primary breast DHL at four academic institutions. Forty‑eight patients were identified, all of whom showed C‑MYC protein expression; the majority also co‑expressed BCL2 and BCL6 proteins, with a minority harbouring isolated BCL2 or BCL6 expression. Fluorescence in situ hybridisation (FISH) for MYC and BCL2/BCL6 translocations was performed in most cases, and targeted NGS was applied to a subset of tumours. Induction regimens included DA‑EPOCH‑R, DA‑EPOCH‑R/MA, and rituximab‑hyper‑CVAD, with several patients undergoing consolidation with ASCT. We present the clinicopathological features, therapeutic approaches, and survival outcomes of this real‑world cohort, aiming to provide insight into the contemporary management of primary breast double‑hit lymphoma.
