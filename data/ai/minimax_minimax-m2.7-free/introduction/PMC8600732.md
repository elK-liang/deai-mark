Myasthenia gravis (MG) is a chronic autoimmune disorder characterized by fatigable weakness of skeletal muscles, resulting from antibody‑mediated destruction of the neuromuscular junction. The disease pathogenesis is driven by a combination of genetic susceptibility and aberrant immune responses, especially involving T‑cell mediated cytotoxicity, cytokine dysregulation, and autoantibody production. Although numerous MG risk genes have been identified through genome‑wide association studies and candidate‑gene approaches, the underlying regulatory networks that connect these genes to the observable immune phenotype remain incompletely understood. Elucidating these networks is essential for uncovering novel molecular targets and developing more effective therapeutic strategies.

Recent advances in systems biology have enabled the construction of protein‑protein interaction (PPI) networks that integrate MG‑associated genes, providing a comprehensive map of their interconnections. In the present study, we assembled a PPI network comprising 250 genes and 4,211 edges, from which nine functionally coherent modules were extracted. Functional enrichment analysis of these modules revealed a strong over‑representation of Gene Ontology (GO) terms such as T cell differentiation (GO:0030217), T cell activation (GO:0042110), interleukin‑12 production (GO:0032615), and positive regulation of cytokine production (GO:0001819). Pathway analyses further highlighted the involvement of established immune‑related signaling routes, including Th17 cell differentiation (hsa04659), T cell receptor signaling (hsa04660), and natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity (hsa04650). These findings are consistent with the well‑established role of adaptive and innate immune pathways in MG pathology and point to specific modules that may act as regulatory hubs in the disease process.

Beyond protein‑centric networks, long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as key players in post‑transcriptional regulation, often acting as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) that sequester microRNAs (miRNAs) and thereby modulate the expression of target messenger RNAs. To explore the contribution of lncRNAs to MG through a ceRNA mechanism, we constructed a lncRNA‑mediated module‑associated ceRNA (LMMAC) network. This integrated network comprised 25 genes, 50 miRNAs, and 64 lncRNAs connected by 228 edges. Within this framework, the lncRNA OIP5‑AS1 exhibited the highest number of total interaction pairs (lncRNA‑miRNA and miRNA‑gene), suggesting its potential as a central regulator within the MG‑associated ceRNA landscape. To prioritize additional lncRNAs that may influence disease‑relevant pathways, we applied the Random Walk with Restart (RWR) algorithm on the LMMAC network, allowing us to capture the systemic influence of each lncRNA based on the connectivity patterns of the network.

The objective of the present study is to identify and characterize the regulatory role of the lncRNA HCG18 in MG by integrating the above‑described PPI and LMMAC networks with targeted experimental analyses. By combining bioinformatic predictions with validation approaches such as expression profiling, miRNA binding assays, and functional perturbation experiments, we aim to delineate how HCG18 participates in MG pathogenesis through its ceRNA activity. The findings are expected to provide novel insights into the molecular circuitry underpinning MG and may reveal candidate biomarkers or therapeutic targets for future clinical intervention.
