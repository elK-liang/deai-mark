Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) is an autosomal‑dominant tumor‑predisposition disorder that arises from loss‑of‑function mutations in the NF1 gene. NF1 encodes neurofibromin, a Ras‑GTPase‑activating protein that down‑regulates Ras signaling. Consequently, NF1 deficiency leads to hyper‑active Ras‑MAPK and PI3K‑Akt pathways, fostering the development of benign neurofibromas and, in a subset of patients, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors (MPNSTs). MPNSTs are highly aggressive sarcomas with limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis, underscoring the need to identify genetic co‑operating events that drive their malignant progression.

The Ras‑MAPK cascade is a central therapeutic target in Ras‑driven cancers, and MEK inhibitors such as trametinib have shown activity in selected contexts. PTPN11 encodes the non‑receptor phosphatase SHP‑2, which positively modulates Ras activation and can sustain MAPK signaling when mutated. Activating PTPN11 mutations are well‑documented in leukemias and several solid tumors, yet their impact in the setting of neurofibromin deficiency is not fully understood. BRAP (BRCA1‑associated protein) interacts with Ras family members and can function as a feedback regulator of Ras‑MAPK output. How alterations in PTPN11 or BRAP intersect with NF1 loss to influence MEK activity and tumor behavior remains an open question.

We previously reported that the NF1‑deficient human MPNST cell line sNF96.2 (harboring a frameshift mutation c.3683delC) exhibits low tumorigenic potential; subcutaneous or sciatic‑nerve injection of these cells into immunodeficient mice fails to generate tumors [19]. However, when sNF96.2‑derived cells expressing GFP were placed under the kidney capsule, they formed a high‑grade spindle‑cell sarcoma with extensive necrosis, a Ki‑67 labeling index >60 %, and infiltration into adjacent skeletal muscle. The tumor lacked conventional MPNST markers (S‑100, Sox10, GFAP, CD57). From this lesion we established the A‑1 cell line, confirmed by STR profiling to be identical to the parental sNF96.2‑GFP cells. Relative to the parental line, A‑1 cells display markedly increased proliferative capacity in vitro, elevated phosphorylation of both Akt and MEK, heightened sensitivity to the MEK inhibitor trametinib, and substantially greater tumorigenicity upon subcutaneous implantation in nude mice.

These observations raise the hypothesis that an activating PTPN11 mutation may cooperate with NF1 loss to amplify MEK signaling and promote malignant progression. To test this, we introduced an oncogenic PTPN11 allele into NF1‑deficient cells and examined the resulting changes in Ras‑MAPK activity, cell growth, and response to MEK inhibition. In addition, we explored whether a BRAP mutation modulates the magnitude of this PTPN11‑driven effect. Through a combination of in vitro biochemical analyses, cellular proliferation assays, and in vivo tumorigenicity studies, we demonstrate that PTPN11 mutation enhances MEK activation in neurofibromin‑deficient cells, accelerates tumor formation, and renders the cells exquisitely sensitive to trametinib, with BRAP mutation influencing the strength of this oncogenic cooperation. These findings provide new insight into the genetic interplay that drives MPNST progression and identify potential therapeutic vulnerabilities in NF1‑associated malignancies.
