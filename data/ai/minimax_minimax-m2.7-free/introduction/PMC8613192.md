Gastric cancer (GC) remains one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer‑related mortality. While Helicobacter pylori infection is recognized as the major risk factor, the complexity of GC etiology suggests that additional microbial and metabolic factors contribute to disease initiation and progression. Advances in culture‑independent sequencing have revealed that the stomach hosts a diverse microbial community, and growing evidence indicates that dysbiosis of the gastric microbiota is a common feature of GC, potentially influencing tumor biology through immune modulation, genotoxin production, and metabolic crosstalk. However, the precise relationships between compositional shifts in the gastric microbiota and the metabolic landscape of the tumor microenvironment are not fully understood.

In the present study, we performed 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing on paired tumor and non‑tumor gastric tissues from 37 patients with GC (Cohort 1). Our analysis demonstrated a marked increase in microbial diversity within tumor tissues, as reflected by higher observed operational taxonomic units (OTUs) (median 464 vs 231, P < 0.001) and an elevated Shannon index (5.20 vs 2.98, P < 0.001). Weighted UniFrac principal coordinate analysis further revealed distinct clustering of the microbial communities between non‑tumor and tumor compartments (PERMANOVA R² = 0.211, P = 0.001), indicating substantial β‑diversity differences. Linear discriminant analysis effect size (LEfSe) identified 64 discriminative taxa across all taxonomic ranks. At the phylum level, Proteobacteria were more abundant in non‑tumor tissue, whereas Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Actinobacteria, Fusobacteria, and Spirochetes were enriched in tumor tissue. At the genus level, Helicobacter dominated the non‑tumor niche, while Lactobacillus, Streptococcus, Acinetobacter, Prevotella, Sphingomonas, Bacteroides, Fusobacterium, Comamonas, Empedobacter, and Faecalibacterium were significantly over‑represented in the tumor microenvironment. These taxon‑specific alterations underscore a profound restructuring of the gastric microbial ecosystem during GC development.

To investigate whether the observed microbial shifts are accompanied by metabolic changes, we conducted untargeted metabolomics on the same paired tissue samples using ultra‑high‑performance liquid chromatography‑tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC‑MS/MS). A total of 1,198 metabolites were quantified in both positive and negative ionization modes. Partial least‑squares discriminant analysis (PLS‑DA) of the metabolomic profiles clearly separated tumor from non‑tumor tissues, indicating that the metabolic landscape of GC tissues is distinct from that of adjacent normal mucosa. The concurrence of marked compositional changes in the microbiota and a divergent metabolome suggests that microbial dysbiosis may be linked to the metabolic reprogramming characteristic of gastric tumors.

Based on these observations, we hypothesized that the gastric microbiota in GC patients contributes to the altered metabolite signatures of tumor tissues. Accordingly, the primary objective of this study was to characterize the interactions between the gastric microbiota and tissue metabolites by integrating high‑throughput 16S rRNA sequencing with untargeted metabolomics. By correlating taxon abundances with metabolite levels, we aimed to identify key microbial members and metabolic pathways that co‑vary in the tumor microenvironment, providing insight into potential microbiome‑metabolome axes that may influence gastric carcinogenesis. The integrated analysis presented here not only delineates the microbial and metabolic alterations associated with GC but also offers a framework for discovering novel diagnostic or therapeutic targets that exploit the microbiome‑metabolome interface.
