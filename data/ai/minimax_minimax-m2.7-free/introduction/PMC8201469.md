The emergence of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus‑2 (SARS‑CoV‑2) as the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID‑19) has prompted an unprecedented effort to develop vaccines that elicit robust humoral and cellular immunity. While neutralizing antibodies target mainly the spike glycoprotein, CD8⁺ and CD4⁺ T cells recognize viral peptides presented by human leukocyte antigen (HLA) molecules and are essential for controlling infection, clearing infected cells and establishing long‑term immunological memory. Consequently, a detailed map of the HLA‑presented peptide landscape (the “ligandome”) across the SARS‑CoV‑2 proteome can guide the rational selection of vaccine antigens that maximize T‑cell coverage in diverse populations.

To define this landscape computationally, we performed HLA‑I and HLA‑II binding predictions across the entire SARS‑CoV‑2 polyprotein using the most widely adopted algorithms: NetMHCpan 4.0 (EL and BA modes) and MHCflurry for 8–11‑mers, and NetMHCIIpan 3.2 and 4.0 for 15‑mers. Predictions were restricted to HLA alleles with a population frequency greater than 5 % in the United States and in worldwide cohorts, ensuring relevance to the majority of potential vaccine recipients. The accuracy of each predictor was evaluated against empirical binding affinity data from the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) for viral peptides; NetMHCpan 4.0 (BA mode) and NetMHCIIpan 3.2 exhibited the highest correlation with experimental values for HLA‑I and HLA‑II, respectively, and were therefore retained for downstream analyses. To limit false‑positive predictions, we derived stricter binding thresholds (393.4 nM for class I and 220.0 nM for class II) that achieve 90 % specificity when benchmarked against the IEDB validated ligand set.

After applying these filters, we identified 2,486 unique HLA‑I ligands and 3,138 unique HLA‑II ligands distributed across the viral proteome. The predicted MHC ligands were not uniformly present; local peaks and troughs were evident, and the density profiles of HLA‑I and HLA‑II ligands were significantly correlated (Pearson r = 0.703, p < 0.001), suggesting shared constraints in peptide processing or HLA binding. Notably, while previously reported SARS‑CoV‑1 T‑cell epitopes are enriched in the spike (S) and nucleocapsid (N) proteins, our analysis revealed a marked paucity of predicted MHC ligands within the N protein of SARS‑CoV‑2. This disparity may reflect differences in antigen processing, HLA binding preferences, or the limited overlap between the two coronaviruses, and it underscores the need for comprehensive epitope mapping rather than relying solely on homology with related viruses.

Collectively, these results provide a high‑resolution, population‑relevant atlas of potential T‑cell epitopes in SARS‑CoV‑2 and highlight regions of the proteome that are densely sampled by HLA molecules. The identified ligands constitute a resource for prioritizing candidate vaccine targets that can elicit broad CD8⁺ and CD4⁺ T‑cell responses across genetically diverse populations, thereby complementing antibody‑focused vaccine strategies and enhancing the durability of immunity against SARS‑CoV‑2.
