Chemokine (C‑C motif) receptor 5 (CCR5) is a G protein‑coupled receptor that binds the ligands CCL5 (RANTES), CCL3 and CCL4. Originally identified in immune cell trafficking, CCR5 is now recognized to be expressed on several solid‑tumor types, where its activation can drive proliferation, survival, invasion and metastasis. In breast cancer, increasing evidence suggests that high CCR5 expression correlates with aggressive disease phenotypes and that CCR5 signaling contributes to tumor cell migration and extravasation. Nevertheless, the precise dynamics of CCR5 activation and its intracellular trafficking in tumor cells remain incompletely understood.

Advances in confocal microscopy and fluorescently‑tagged chemokines have enabled real‑time visualization of CCR5 expression, ligand‑induced internalization, and co‑localization with endocytic compartments. Signoret et al. and Velasco‑Velazquez et al. pioneered imaging strategies that discriminate low and high surface CCR5 signals and demonstrated that RANTES binding triggers rapid receptor internalization through endosomal pathways (Fig. 2) [6, 7, 16]. Using N‑terminally tagged RANTES, these studies showed that internalized CCR5 can be tracked within LAMP1‑positive endosomes, indicating that the receptor follows a canonical GPCR internalization route. Such quantitative imaging frameworks provide a unique opportunity to assess receptor activation states beyond static surface levels.

Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and other circulating tumor‑derived elements—including large polyploid cells referred to as cancer‑associated macrophage‑like cells (CAMLs) or polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs)—have been detected in the peripheral blood of breast cancer patients and are increasingly appreciated as minimally invasive biomarkers of disease burden and therapeutic response [22, 29, 33]. Phenotypic characterization of these cells has largely focused on epithelial markers and proliferative indices, with limited exploration of functional chemokine‑receptor pathways that may govern their survival or migratory capacity. Because CCR5 is known to facilitate tumor cell invasiveness, determining whether CCR5 is expressed, activated, and internalized in circulating tumor‑derived cells could reveal novel prognostic information.

In the present study we first validated the imaging approaches described by Signoret et al. and Velasco‑Velazquez et al. in the triple‑negative breast cancer cell line MDA‑MB‑231, confirming that RANTES stimulation markedly up‑regulates CCR5 surface expression and promotes its co‑localization with the endosome marker LAMP1 (Fig. 2). We then extended these assays to circulating tumor‑derived cells isolated from the blood of breast cancer patients and assessed the prevalence of low, high, or negative CCR5 expression, the extent of ligand‑induced internalization, and the degree of CCR5/RANTES co‑localization within endosomal compartments. Finally, we correlated these dynamic CCR5 signatures with clinicopathological parameters and patient outcomes, testing the hypothesis that the pattern of CCR5 activation and endocytic trafficking in circulating tumor‑derived cells provides predictive information beyond conventional biomarkers.
