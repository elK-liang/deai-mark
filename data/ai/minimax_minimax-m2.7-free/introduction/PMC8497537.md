Sulforaphane (SFN), an isothiocyanate abundant in cruciferous vegetables, has attracted considerable interest for its chemopreventive and anti‑tumor properties. Although several signaling pathways have been implicated in these effects, the precise molecular events that link SFN exposure to the rewiring of cellular metabolism and cytoskeletal integrity remain incompletely defined. In the present work we employed a proteomic survey together with targeted biochemical analyses to systematically map the changes induced by SFN in non‑small‑cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells and to correlate these changes with clinical pathology.  

Fatty acid synthase (FASN) is the central enzyme catalyzing de novo synthesis of fatty acids and is frequently overexpressed in aggressive tumors, where it supports rapid membrane production and signaling hub functions. In NSCLC, elevated FASN levels have been associated with higher histologic grade. Our tissue‑microarray data reveal a positive correlation between FASN expression and malignancy grade in both lung adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, confirming the link between lipogenic capacity and tumor progression. Notably, quantitative proteomics showed that SFN treatment reduces the abundance of FASN and its transcriptional regulator SREBP1, as well as other lipogenic enzymes (ACACA, ACLY), in a concentration‑dependent manner. Experiments with the proteasome inhibitor PS‑341 further demonstrate that SFN promotes the 26S proteasome‑mediated degradation of these proteins, indicating that SFN suppresses fatty‑acid synthesis at the post‑translational level.  

Microtubules, composed of α‑ and β‑tubulin heterodimers, are essential for intracellular transport, cell shape, and mitosis, and they serve as platforms for autophagic processes such as mitophagy. Disruption of microtubule dynamics is a well‑established strategy for anti‑cancer therapy. In this study, immunoblotting and immunofluorescence reveal that SFN treatment leads to a marked reduction of both α‑ and β‑tubulin, decreases the pool of polymerized (insoluble) tubulin, and diminishes the colocalization of the two tubulin isoforms, indicating a potent depolymerizing effect. The loss of stable microtubules coincides with the appearance of classic apoptotic morphology—chromatin condensation, apoptotic bodies, and extensive cytoplasmic vacuolization—as visualized by transmission electron microscopy, and with an increase in caspase‑3 cleavage. Moreover, the proteomic data show coordinated down‑regulation of autophagy‑related and microtubule‑associated proteins, suggesting that SFN may impair microtubule‑mediated processes such as mitophagy, contributing to the activation of the apoptotic program.  

Collectively, our findings demonstrate that SFN simultaneously targets two critical pillars of cancer cell homeostasis: de novo fatty‑acid synthesis, via proteasomal degradation of FASN and its regulators, and microtubule integrity, leading to defective mitophagy and culminating in apoptosis. The strong positive correlation between FASN expression and tumor grade underscores the clinical relevance of the metabolic axis, while the microtubule‑disrupting activity of SFN provides a mechanistic basis for its cytotoxicity. These results not only deepen our understanding of SFN’s anti‑cancer action but also highlight the potential of SFN as a dual‑targeted therapeutic agent for NSCLC and possibly other malignancies with metabolic and cytoskeletal dependencies.
