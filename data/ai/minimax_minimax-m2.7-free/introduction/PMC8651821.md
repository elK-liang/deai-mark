High‑grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC) is the most prevalent and lethal histotype of epithelial ovarian cancer, accounting for the majority of ovarian cancer‑related deaths worldwide. Despite initial responsiveness to cytoreductive surgery and platinum‑based chemotherapy, the majority of patients develop recurrent disease that is resistant to further treatment. The emergence of chemoresistance, especially to cisplatin, remains a formidable obstacle to improving long‑term survival and underscores the urgent need to identify actionable molecular targets that can be exploited to restore or enhance drug sensitivity.

Systematic analyses of gene‑expression profiles have proven instrumental in uncovering dysregulated pathways that underlie tumor initiation, progression and therapeutic resistance. In the present study, we interrogated two independent GEO datasets of primary serous ovarian carcinoma and normal controls and identified 258 overlapping up‑regulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using stringent criteria (log2 fold change > 1, P < 0.001). Pathway enrichment of these genes revealed that the cell‑cycle pathway was the most significantly enriched, highlighting a central role for mitotic regulators in HGSOC pathogenesis. Among the 22 DEGs annotated to the cell‑cycle pathway, TTK (also known as MPS1) emerged as the only gene for which a clinical‑grade inhibitor is currently under investigation in clinical trials, yet its contribution to HGSOC had not been explored. This observation prompted us to focus on TTK as a candidate driver of ovarian tumor biology and chemoresistance.

Consistent with the bioinformatic findings, analysis of multiple public databases demonstrated markedly higher TTK mRNA levels in ovarian cancer tissues compared with normal ovaries. Validation in our own patient cohort confirmed elevated TTK expression at both the mRNA and protein levels in HGSOC relative to fallopian tube tissue, the purported site of origin. Moreover, TTK expression was significantly up‑regulated in cisplatin‑resistant ovarian cancer cell lines, suggesting a potential link between TTK and the acquisition of platinum resistance. Importantly, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed that high TTK expression correlates with shorter progression‑free and overall survival, implicating TTK as a prognostic biomarker in HGSOC.

Collectively, these data point to TTK as a key cell‑cycle regulator whose over‑expression is associated with ovarian tumorigenesis, chemoresistance and adverse patient outcome. Based on these observations, we hypothesized that pharmacological inhibition of TTK might sensitize HGSOC cells to cisplatin and that this effect could be mediated through modulation of the mTOR/autophagy axis. Therefore, the present study was designed to (i) further characterize TTK expression and its clinical significance in HGSOC, (ii) evaluate the impact of TTK inhibition on cisplatin sensitivity in vitro, and (iii) elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms, with particular emphasis on the mTOR/autophagy pathway.
