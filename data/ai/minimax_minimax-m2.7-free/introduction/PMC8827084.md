Vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) are highly plastic cells that can shift between a quiescent, contractile phenotype and a proliferative, synthetic state in response to environmental cues. The contractile phenotype is characterized by the expression of smooth‑muscle‑specific proteins such as α‑smooth muscle actin (ACTA2) and SM22α, whereas the synthetic phenotype exhibits increased production of osteopontin (OPN) and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA). This phenotypic switch plays a central role in vascular remodeling during development, injury repair, and disease states such as atherosclerosis and restenosis. A key driver of the contractile program is myocardin (MYOCD), a potent transcriptional co‑activator that partners with serum response factor (SRF) to promote the transcription of smooth‑muscle‑specific genes. Loss of MYOCD has previously been shown to down‑regulate contractile markers and favor a synthetic, proliferative VSMC fate, highlighting its essential role in maintaining vascular homeostasis.

Autophagy is a degradative process that recycles cytoplasmic components and organelles, thereby influencing cellular adaptation, survival, and function. In VSMCs, autophagy has been implicated in the regulation of survival under stress, the modulation of extracellular matrix production, and the control of cell proliferation. The autophagy pathway is tightly regulated by a set of evolutionarily conserved proteins, including Beclin1, which orchestrates the initiation of autophagosome formation, and the microtubule‑associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3) family, whose lipidation (LC3‑I to LC3‑II) is a hallmark of autophagosome expansion. Recent evidence indicates that transcriptional programs can intersect with autophagy; however, the mechanisms linking VSMC‑specific transcription factors such as MYOCD to autophagy remain incompletely understood.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) provide an additional layer of post‑transcriptional regulation that can modulate autophagy. The miR‑30 family, particularly miR‑30a‑5p, has been reported to directly target Beclin1 and other autophagy‑related genes in various cell types, thereby restraining autophagic flux. Altered expression of miR‑30a has been documented in cardiovascular disorders, but its specific contribution to VSMC autophagy and phenotypic switching is not well defined. Given that MYOCD influences a broad transcriptional network, we hypothesized that MYOCD might control autophagy in VSMCs through a miR‑30a‑mediated pathway that targets Beclin1, thereby linking the contractile phenotype to autophagic activity.

To test this idea, we performed loss‑of‑function studies in human aortic VSMCs (HA‑VSMCs) by knocking down MYOCD and examined the resulting changes in contractile and proliferative markers as well as key autophagy regulators. Using pharmacological inhibitors of autophagy and live‑cell imaging of GFP‑LC3, we quantified autophagic flux and assessed whether modulating autophagy could reverse the phenotypic effects of MYOCD deficiency. The results reveal that MYOCD depletion drives a synthetic VSMC phenotype while simultaneously enhancing autophagic activity, and that inhibition of autophagy restores contractile protein expression. These findings uncover a novel MYOCD/miR‑30a/Beclin1 signaling axis that governs the phenotypic modulation of VSMCs by regulating autophagy.
