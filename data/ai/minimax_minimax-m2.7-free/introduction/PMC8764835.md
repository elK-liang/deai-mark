Esophageal carcinoma, particularly esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), represents a significant global health burden with high mortality rates, largely due to late-stage diagnosis and limited effective early detection strategies. Despite advances in treatment modalities including surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, the overall five-year survival rate remains poor, emphasizing the urgent need for reliable biomarkers that can facilitate early diagnosis, prognostic assessment, and personalized therapeutic decision-making. Current diagnostic approaches rely heavily on endoscopic examination and tissue biopsy, which are invasive, expensive, and impractical for large-scale screening. Liquid biopsy has emerged as a promising alternative, offering non-invasive detection of tumor-derived molecular signatures in bodily fluids, though most investigations have focused on circulating tumor cells, cell-free DNA, and microRNAs in blood samples.

Saliva has garnered increasing attention as an attractive biofluid for cancer biomarker discovery due to its non-invasive collection, minimal processing requirements, and rich molecular composition reflecting both local and systemic disease states. Salivary extracellular vesicles, particularly exosomes, serve as crucial mediators of intercellular communication and have been shown to contain diverse cargo including small non-coding RNAs (sncRNAs) that may reflect physiological and pathological conditions. While microRNAs have been extensively studied in various cancers, the role of other small non-coding RNA species, particularly transfer RNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs) and other rarely characterized sncRNAs (sesncRNAs), remains largely unexplored in the context of cancer biomarker development. These RNA fragments, generated through precise processing of precursor molecules, have been implicated in gene regulation and cellular homeostasis, suggesting their potential involvement in tumor biology.

Recent advances in high-throughput sequencing technologies have revealed the complexity of the small RNA transcriptome, uncovering numerous RNA species beyond canonical microRNAs. Among these, tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs) and tsRNAs have emerged as abundant and conserved RNA populations with diverse biological functions, including regulation of translation, gene expression, and cell stress responses. Dysregulation of these tRNA-derived small RNAs has been reported in various pathological conditions including cancer, where they may contribute to tumor progression and metastasis. However, the characterization of specific tsRNAs and other sesncRNAs in salivary exosomes and their potential as diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers for esophageal carcinoma remains limited. The identification of cancer-specific signatures within these small RNA populations could provide novel targets for non-invasive cancer detection and monitoring.

In this multicenter prospective study, we sought to identify and validate saliva-derived exosomal small non-coding RNAs (sesncRNAs) as potential biomarkers for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Through comprehensive small RNA sequencing of salivary exosomes from ESCC patients and healthy controls, we discovered 1366 differentially expressed sesncRNAs, from which we identified a panel of highly expressed candidates for further validation. Using quantitative real-time PCR in independent cohorts, we validated two promising sesncRNAs: tRNA-GlyGCC-5, a tsRNA derived from the 5' end of TRG-GCC, and sRESE (small RNA identified in Exosomes from Saliva of ESCC patients), a previously uncharacterized small non-coding RNA. Our findings demonstrate the potential of these salivary exosomal sesncRNAs as non-invasive biomarkers for ESCC detection and highlight the previously underappreciated role of tRNA-derived fragments and other small non-coding RNAs in esophageal carcinoma biology.
