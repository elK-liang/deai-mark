Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS), also referred to as myalgic encephalomyelitis/CFS (ME/CFS), is a debilitating condition characterized by persistent fatigue that is not alleviated by rest and is often accompanied by cognitive dysfunction, sleep disturbances, and musculoskeletal pain. Because of its heterogeneous presentation and the absence of definitive biomarkers, CFS remains a diagnosis of exclusion, making reliable epidemiological tracking challenging. Nevertheless, accurate population‑based data are essential for understanding disease burden, informing healthcare resource allocation, and guiding future research into etiologic mechanisms and therapeutic interventions.

Despite a growing body of literature on CFS in North America and Europe, comprehensive nationwide epidemiologic investigations in Asian populations remain scarce. Existing regional studies have been limited by relatively small sample sizes, reliance on self‑reported diagnoses, or focus on specific sub‑populations, which may not reflect the overall pattern of disease occurrence. In South Korea, where a universal health‑insurance system covers the entire resident population, administrative claim records provide a unique opportunity to capture physician‑diagnosed CFS on a national scale and to monitor temporal trends with high precision.

The present study was designed to characterize the nationwide epidemiology of CFS in South Korea over an 11‑year period (2010–2020). Using the Korean National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) database, which encompasses virtually the entire Korean population (approximately 50 million individuals), we examined annual incidence and prevalence, quantified sex‑specific and age‑specific distributions, and assessed changes in disease occurrence over the study period. By doing so, we aimed to fill the existing gap in Asian epidemiologic data and to provide a robust foundation for public‑health planning and future clinical research.

Our analyses reveal that, on average, 22,720 new cases of CFS were diagnosed each year, corresponding to an overall annual incidence of 44.7 per 100,000 individuals (95 % CI 40.6–48.8). The incidence rose 1.5‑fold from 32.4 per 100,000 in 2010 to 49.5 per 100,000 in 2020, with females experiencing a modestly steeper increase than males (1.66‑fold vs. 1.44‑fold). Females outnumbered males across all age groups after the first decade of life, yielding a female‑to‑male ratio of approximately 1.5. The absolute number of incident cases peaked in the 50‑ to 59‑year cohort for both sexes, although the age‑specific relative frequency of cases was highest among individuals older than 80 years in males and in the 60‑ to 69‑year group in females. These findings provide the first comprehensive, population‑wide description of CFS in South Korea, highlighting a consistent female predominance, an upward trend in diagnosis rates over the past decade, and a distinct age‑distribution pattern that may inform targeted screening and management strategies.
