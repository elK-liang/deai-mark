Abstract

The EXTREME regimen (platinum-based chemotherapy plus cetuximab) is the established first-line treatment for recurrent/metastatic (R/M) head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). However, frail patients with poor performance status are often unable to tolerate this intensive schedule. In the immunotherapy era, weekly platinum-based chemotherapy (PCC) has been proposed as a tolerable alternative for these individuals. The present retrospective study aimed to evaluate the outcomes of weekly PCC compared to the standard EXTREME regimen in frail patients with R/M HNSCC.

We retrospectively analyzed 124 patients with locally incurable R/M HNSCC treated with first-line platinum-based chemotherapy in association with cetuximab. Of these, 103 patients (83%) received the standard EXTREME regimen (ECOG PS 0–1), while 21 patients (17%) received weekly PCC (all ECOG PS 2). Baseline clinical and pathological features, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), response rate, and toxicity were assessed and compared between the two treatment groups.

No significant differences were observed between the EXTREME and PCC groups in terms of age, sex, smoking history, and previous alcohol abuse. In the weekly PCC group, the most common toxicities were G1 anemia (52%), G2 neutropenia (40%), and G1 gastrointestinal toxicity (49%); no G3–4 toxicities were reported. Median PFS was 4 months (range 1–20) in the overall population, 4 months in the EXTREME group, and 5 months in the PCC group, with no statistically significant difference (p = 0.275). Median OS was 12 months in both groups (p = 0.400). The response rate was 35% in the EXTREME group and 34% in the PCC group, with no complete responses achieved in either cohort. On univariate analysis, none of the clinical features, including the type of treatment regimen, were predictive of PFS or OS.

Weekly PCC in association with cetuximab represents a feasible and well-tolerated first-line treatment option for frail R/M HNSCC patients, achieving comparable PFS, OS, and response rates to the standard EXTREME regimen, without G3–4 toxicities. These findings support the use of weekly PCC as a valid therapeutic alternative in this poor-prognosis population, particularly relevant in the immunotherapy era.
