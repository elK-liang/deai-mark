Structured Abstract

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common malignancy among women worldwide and is associated with high rates of morbidity and mortality. Accumulating evidence indicates that F-box proteins play critical roles in tumorigenesis and tumor progression. FBXO43, a member of the F-box protein family, has been reported to be associated with lower survival rates and metastasis in BC patients; however, its functional role and underlying molecular mechanism in BC remain poorly understood.

The expression levels of FBXO43 in BC tissues and cell lines (MDA-MB-231, MCF7, and T-47D) were analyzed using TCGA data and RT-qPCR, respectively. FBXO43 was stably knocked down in MDA-MB-231 cells via lentiviral shRNA transfection, and knockdown efficiency was confirmed by Western blot and RT-qPCR. The effects of FBXO43 knockdown on cell proliferation, viability, colony formation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion were assessed using cell proliferation assays, MTT assay, colony-formation assay, flow cytometry, scratch wound healing assay, and Transwell migration and invasion assays.

FBXO43 was significantly overexpressed in BC tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues and was similarly upregulated in BC cell lines. Lentivirus-mediated shRNA knockdown effectively suppressed FBXO43 expression in MDA-MB-231 cells. Functional assays demonstrated that FBXO43 knockdown significantly inhibited cell proliferation and viability, reduced colony-forming ability, and increased apoptosis. Furthermore, FBXO43 interference markedly impaired the migratory and invasive capacities of MDA-MB-231 cells.

Downregulation of FBXO43 inhibits tumor growth and metastatic potential in human breast cancer cells by suppressing proliferation, promoting apoptosis, and limiting migration and invasion. These findings suggest that FBXO43 may serve as a potential therapeutic target for breast cancer treatment.
