Abstract

Molecular intratumor heterogeneity is a well-recognized feature of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and the proportion of EGFR-mutated cancer cells within a tumor has been reported to correlate with response to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). However, whether NSCLC cells harboring wild-type EGFR (wtEGFR) can influence the therapeutic sensitivity of neighboring EGFR-mutated cancer cells to the third-generation TKI osimertinib remains poorly understood.

NSCLC cell lines with wtEGFR (H460, A549, H1299) and with mutant EGFR (mutEGFR; H1975, PC9) were used. EGFR mutation status was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and osimertinib sensitivity was assessed by MTT assay. Direct co-culture assays using GFP-labeled H1299 and RFP-labeled H1975 cells, transwell co-culture assays separating the cell populations with a 0.4 μm porous membrane, and conditioned medium (CM) transfer experiments were employed to evaluate the effects of wtEGFR-bearing cells on osimertinib sensitivity in mutEGFR H1975 cells. Cell viability and apoptosis following osimertinib treatment were measured by MTT, colony formation, and apoptosis assays.

H1975 and PC9 cells were markedly more sensitive to osimertinib than wtEGFR-bearing NSCLC cells. Direct co-culture of H1975 cells with H1299 cells at a 1:1 ratio dramatically increased H1975 cell survival upon osimertinib treatment. Similarly, in transwell assays, the IC50 of osimertinib in H1975 cells was significantly increased when H460, A549, or H1299 cells were present in the upper chamber. Consistently, treatment of H1975 cells with conditioned medium from wtEGFR-bearing NSCLC cells remarkably reduced the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects of osimertinib.

These findings demonstrate that wtEGFR-bearing NSCLC cells can transfer osimertinib resistance to sensitive mutEGFR H1975 cells through soluble factors released into the medium, indicating that intercellular communication mediated by wtEGFR-expressing cells contributes to osimertinib resistance and may represent a previously unrecognized mechanism of treatment failure in heterogeneous NSCLC tumors.

Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer, osimertinib resistance, EGFR mutation, wild-type EGFR, intercellular communication, conditioned medium
