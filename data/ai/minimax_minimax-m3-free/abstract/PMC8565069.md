Abstract

Patients with metastatic breast cancer (MBC) and visceral metastases have a poor prognosis and limited treatment options. Nab-paclitaxel has demonstrated efficacy in MBC, but data focusing on patients with visceral metastases remain limited.

This single-center Phase II trial enrolled patients with MBC and visceral metastases treated with nab-paclitaxel at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center between March 2016 and September 2020. All patients received at least one dose of study treatment and were evaluable for toxicity and response. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), and safety.

A total of 80 patients were enrolled. The mean age was 52 years (range 26–86). Most patients were estrogen receptor (ER)/progesterone receptor (PR) positive (76.3%), while 22.5% had triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and 2.5% were HER2-positive. Liver (67.5%), lung (61.3%), and brain (6.3%) were the most common visceral metastatic sites, and 62.5% of patients had three or more metastatic sites. The median number of prior metastatic chemotherapy regimens was two, and 15% of patients received concurrent targeted therapy. The median PFS was 5.1 months (95% CI: 4.2–6.0). Best response included partial response in 32.5%, stable disease in 33.8%, and progressive disease in 33.8%, yielding an ORR of 33.8% (95% CI: 21.3–43.8%) and a CBR of 66.2% (95% CI: 56.3–75.0%). Median OS was not reached at the time of analysis. In subset analyses, median PFS was 5.1 months (95% CI: 4.2–6.0) for luminal-type and 4.1 months (95% CI: 1.5–6.7) for TNBC patients (p = 0.8). Patients receiving nab-paclitaxel as first-line therapy had significantly longer PFS than those treated in later lines (12.5 vs. 4.6 months, p = 0.007). Patients with brain metastases had significantly poorer PFS than those without (2.8 vs. 5.1 months, p = 0.004). Postmenopausal women showed a trend toward longer PFS compared with premenopausal women (5.4 vs. 3.6 months, p = 0.051). No significant differences in PFS were observed according to age, number of metastatic sites, or use of concurrent therapy.

Nab-paclitaxel demonstrated promising activity and an acceptable safety profile in patients with metastatic breast cancer and visceral metastases. Treatment benefit was most pronounced in patients receiving nab-paclitaxel as first-line therapy and in those without brain metastases, supporting its consideration in this high-risk population.
