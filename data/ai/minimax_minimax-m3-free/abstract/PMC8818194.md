Abstract

Minimal residual disease (MRD) assessment is increasingly recognized as a powerful prognostic tool in multiple myeloma (MM). While bone marrow (BM) flow cytometry has been the conventional method for MRD evaluation, data on longitudinal flow-MRD monitoring in patients achieving complete remission (CR) within clinical trials remain limited. Here we report results from the Nordic Myeloma Study Group (NMSG) flow-MRD substudy of the EMN02/HO95 MM trial, focusing on MRD dynamics and their relationship to disease progression in young transplant-eligible MM patients.

Transplant-eligible MM patients (age 18–64 years) enrolled in the EMN02/HO95 trial and registered in the NMSG MRD substudy were eligible for analysis if they achieved at least a very good partial response (VGPR) during the treatment course. BM aspirates were analyzed by multiparameter flow cytometry for the detection of malignant plasma cells (mPC) at first response assessment (flow-RA) and at subsequent longitudinal time points (flow-MRD). MRD results were correlated with clinical response status and progression-free survival.

During the inclusion period, 136 patients were registered in the NMSG MRD study, of whom 53 had BM aspirates submitted for flow cytometric evaluation when in CR/stringent CR (sCR). Fifteen patients with confirmed CR or better (12 sCR, 3 CR) and adequate BM samples were evaluable for the 1st flow-RA analysis. Flow cytometry identified mPC-negative status in 80% (12/15) of clinically CR/sCR patients, including 11 patients in sCR. In the remaining three patients (20%), a small residual mPC population was detected (64, 126, and 4319 events; frequency range 8.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 8.8 × 10⁻⁵ mPC/10⁶ cells), despite normal serum free light chain (sFLC) ratios in all three. Time from inclusion to 1st flow-RA varied widely (range 2.7–41.6 months; median 20.1 months), as did sample cellularity (1.3–13.4 × 10⁶ acquired cells; median 5.9 × 10⁶). Because only three patients were flow-RA positive, no formal statistical comparison of progression rates between flow-RA-positive and flow-RA-negative groups could be performed; however, all three flow-RA-positive patients subsequently progressed. Thirty-four patients who achieved CR during the treatment course were included in the longitudinal flow-MRD analysis, of whom 20 had at least three sequential flow-MRD assessments available. The longitudinal dynamics of MRD in this cohort are described in relation to disease progression.

In this substudy of the EMN02/HO95 trial, the majority of young MM patients in clinical CR/sCR were MRD-negative by flow cytometry, but a subset (20%) harbored detectable residual mPC despite normal sFLC ratios. All flow-RA-positive patients subsequently progressed, supporting the prognostic value of flow-based MRD assessment. Longitudinal flow-MRD monitoring is feasible within the framework of a large clinical trial and provides clinically meaningful information on disease dynamics, although the small sample size of this substudy limits definitive statistical conclusions.

Trial registration: EMN02/HO95 MM trial (EMN and HOVON).
