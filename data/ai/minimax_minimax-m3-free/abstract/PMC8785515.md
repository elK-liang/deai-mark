The abundant presence of the CCR5 coreceptor on the cell surface is essential for HIV-1 binding, entry, and productive infection, yet the quantities and distributions of distinct CCR5 subpopulations in relevant cell types remain incompletely defined. To address this gap, we developed a dual-staining imaging strategy using a C-terminus–GFP fusion protein together with extracellular domain–specific monoclonal antibodies (CTC8 against the CCR5 N-terminus and 2D7 against ECL2) and applied Airyscan-based super-resolution microscopy to visualize CCR5 localization with substantially greater clarity than standard confocal imaging, which proved insufficient for resolving individual staining puncta. Using U87.CD4.CCR5-GFP cells and the JC10 and JC53 cell lines, which express approximately 2,000 and 130,000 CCR5 molecules per cell, respectively, we were able to distinguish surface-exposed from intracellular CCR5 populations through comparisons of permeabilized and non-permeabilized samples, and to quantify total surface-bound and internalized CCR5 across different conformational states. Our results establish that the combination of conformationally sensitive antibodies with super-resolution imaging provides a robust approach for enumerating and localizing CCR5 subpopulations, with implications for understanding HIV-1 entry requirements and for interpreting in vivo studies of coreceptor availability and usage.
