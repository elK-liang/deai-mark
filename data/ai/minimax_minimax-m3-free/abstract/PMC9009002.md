Alpha-synuclein (SNCA), traditionally linked to neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's disease, has recently been implicated in cancer biology, yet its role in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains poorly defined. This study aimed to comprehensively investigate SNCA expression, its prognostic significance, and its association with immune infiltration and epigenetic regulation in LUAD. Methods: Pan-cancer expression analysis was performed using the Oncomine and TIMER databases, with LUAD-specific validation through meta-analysis of five Oncomine datasets, UALCAN-based assessment of TCGA mRNA and CPTAC protein expression data, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Correlations between SNCA expression and immune infiltrating cells and immune checkpoint molecules were evaluated using the TIMER and TISIDB platforms, and promoter methylation status was examined via the UALCAN database. Results: SNCA was broadly downregulated across multiple cancer types, including LUAD, at both mRNA and protein levels, and this downregulation was associated with poorer overall survival in LUAD patients. In addition, SNCA expression showed significant correlations with several tumor-infiltrating immune cell populations and immune checkpoint molecules, and SNCA promoter methylation levels were elevated in LUAD tissues compared with normal tissues. Conclusion: SNCA is significantly downregulated in LUAD and serves as a potential prognostic biomarker, with its expression linked to immune infiltration and immune checkpoint signatures, suggesting that promoter methylation may contribute to its silencing and highlighting SNCA as a promising therapeutic and immunomodulatory target in lung adenocarcinoma.
