Abstract

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant internal modification of eukaryotic mRNAs and has been implicated in the progression of various cancers, including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). KIAA1429 is a key component of the m6A methyltransferase complex, but its expression pattern, clinical relevance, and functional role in HCC remain poorly understood.

KIAA1429 expression was examined in HCC tissues from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset (50 paired samples) and an independent West China Hospital (WCH) cohort (70 paired samples) using qPCR, western blot, and immunohistochemistry. The diagnostic and prognostic value of KIAA1429 was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and Kaplan–Meier analyses, and its associations with clinical characteristics were assessed. Functional studies were performed using loss-of-function approaches in HCC cell lines to investigate the oncogenic role of KIAA1429 in cell growth and metastasis.

KIAA1429 expression was significantly upregulated in HCC tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues in both the TCGA and WCH cohorts. The areas under the ROC curves in the two datasets were 85% and 75%, respectively, indicating a relatively high diagnostic value. High KIAA1429 expression was significantly associated with tumor size (P = 0.0303), serum AFP level (P = 0.0157), microvascular invasion (P = 0.0168), TNM stage (P = 0.0220), and BCLC stage (P = 0.0051). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that elevated KIAA1429 expression was significantly associated with shorter overall survival and disease-free survival in HCC patients.

KIAA1429 is upregulated in HCC, correlates with aggressive clinicopathological features and poor patient outcomes, and may serve as a potential prognostic indicator for patients with HCC.
