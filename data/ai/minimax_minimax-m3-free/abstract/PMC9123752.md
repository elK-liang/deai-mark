Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related death worldwide, and the development of resistance to chemotherapeutic agents, particularly 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), represents a major clinical challenge. Hypoxia is a common feature of the tumor microenvironment that promotes aggressive and treatment-resistant phenotypes, although the molecular mechanisms underlying hypoxia-induced drug resistance are not fully understood. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as critical post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression in cancer, and specific miRNAs can modulate cellular responses to stress conditions such as hypoxia. This study investigates the role of miR-675-5p in supporting hypoxia-induced drug resistance in CRC cells. Methods: HCT116 and SW480 CRC cell lines were exposed to chronic hypoxic conditions (1% O₂) for 72 hours in the presence or absence of 5-FU (5 μM and 10 μM). The activation of the hypoxic response was verified by analyzing the expression of carbonic anhydrase 9 (CA9), a primary HIF target, using Western blotting. Cell viability was assessed by MTT assay, and the expression levels of miR-675-5p were measured by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), with normalization to RNU6 and calculation of ΔΔCt relative to normoxic conditions. Results: Chronic hypoxic exposure (72 hours) effectively induced the hypoxic response, as demonstrated by the significant upregulation of CA9 in both HCT116 and SW480 cells compared to normoxic controls. Cell viability assays showed that 5-FU treatment reduced cell survival under normoxic conditions, whereas hypoxia substantially rescued CRC cells from 5-FU-induced cytotoxicity, confirming the suitability of this model for studying hypoxia-induced chemoresistance. Notably, miR-675-5p expression was significantly increased under chronic hypoxic conditions in both cell lines compared to normoxic conditions, suggesting that miR-675-5p induction may be part of the cellular adaptive response to hypoxia that contributes to drug resistance. Conclusions: Chronic hypoxia protects CRC cells from 5-FU-induced cell death and is associated with the upregulation of miR-675-5p. These findings identify miR-675-5p as a potential player in hypoxia-induced chemoresistance and suggest that it may represent a candidate molecular target for improving the efficacy of chemotherapy in CRC.
