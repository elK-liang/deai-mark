Abstract

Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) frequently disseminates via ascites, and circular RNAs (circRNAs) have been increasingly implicated in cancer metastasis. However, the dynamic states of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) during ascitic metastasis and the contributions of specific circRNAs to this process remain poorly understood.

To characterize EMT dynamics, gene expression profiling was performed on 22 samples obtained from 9 EOC patients, comprising 9 primary tumor lesions (T), 4 ascites samples (ASC), and 9 metastatic lesions (M). Principal component analysis (PCA) and single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA), using manually curated epithelial (n = 31) and mesenchymal (n = 54) gene sets, were conducted to evaluate EMT status across the three groups. Differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) between T–ASC and ASC–M groups were identified using the DESeq2 software package.

PCA revealed that ASC samples were clearly distinguishable from T and M, whereas T and M substantially overlapped, suggesting a shared solid-tissue identity. Consistent with this, ssGSEA showed that the enrichment of epithelial gene signatures in ascites was significantly lower than that in T and M, while the enrichment of mesenchymal signatures in ASC was significantly higher than that in M; no significant differences in epithelial or mesenchymal enrichment were detected between T and M. Heatmap analysis of EMT-related genes confirmed a distinct pattern in ASC, characterized by marked down-regulation of epithelial genes and up-regulation of mesenchymal genes relative to T and M, with T and M sharing a similar EMT-related expression profile. These findings indicate that ovarian cancer cells likely undergo EMT–MET transitions while migrating through ascites. circRNA profiling identified 72 differentially expressed circRNAs (5 up-regulated and 67 down-regulated) in the T–ASC comparison and 71 differentially expressed circRNAs (57 up-regulated and 14 down-regulated) in the ASC–M comparison.

Ovarian cancer cells undergo dynamic EMT–MET transitions during ascitic dissemination, and specific circRNAs are differentially expressed between primary tumors, ascites, and metastatic lesions, supporting a role for circRNAs in EOC peritoneal metastasis via ascites.
