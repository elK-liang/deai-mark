Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is among the most lethal malignancies worldwide, and ferroptosis has recently emerged as a promising avenue for therapeutic intervention. To systematically identify genes that regulate sensitivity or resistance to chemotherapy-induced ferroptosis in HCC, we performed genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 loss-of-function screens across a panel of HCC cell lines challenged with a panel of clinically relevant inhibitors. Normalized Z-scores (NormZ) derived from DrugZ analyses revealed that cells carrying sgRNAs targeting resistance genes were negatively selected (NormZ<0), whereas cells carrying sgRNAs targeting sensitizer genes were positively selected (NormZ>0), enabling us to map connections among cell lines and targeted therapies through circus plot visualization. Strikingly, resistance-associated linkages were far more abundant than sensitizer-associated linkages, and KEGG enrichment analyses of top resistance genes (p<0.01) highlighted selenocompound metabolism, folate biosynthesis, and peroxisome pathways as key mediators of chemotherapy resistance in HCC cells, as visualized in Sankey plots depicting relationships among top resisters, treatment groups, and enriched KEGG terms. Subgroup analyses further revealed that the peroxisome pathway was significantly enriched among resistance genes in CDK4/6 inhibitor-treated cell groups, whereas sorafenib and erastin, two canonical ferroptosis inducers, were associated with enrichment of selenocompound metabolism-related genes. Among these candidates, phosphoseryl-tRNA kinase (PSTK), an activated intermediate of selenocysteine biosynthesis that specifically phosphorylates seryl-tRNA[Ser]Sec to O-phosphoseryl-tRNA[Ser]Sec, emerged as a top resister whose loss sensitized HCC cells to ferroptosis-inducing chemotherapy. Together, these CRISPR screening findings establish PSTK as a previously unrecognized regulator of chemotherapy-induced ferroptosis and underscore the selenocompound metabolism pathway as a therapeutic vulnerability in hepatocellular carcinoma.
