Abstract

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder, and presenilin 1/2 conditional double knockout (PS cDKO) mice have been established as a model that recapitulates AD-like neurodegenerative phenotypes, including cognitive deficits, synaptic dysfunction, cortical shrinkage, tau hyperphosphorylation, and neuroinflammation, despite reduced amyloid-β content. Because early intervention is considered most effective for AD, characterization of the early-stage (2-month) and intermediate-stage (6-month) changes in PS cDKO mice is of particular relevance.

Urinary metabolomic profiling using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) was performed on PS cDKO and wild-type (WT) mice at 2 and 6 months of age. Multivariate statistical analyses, including principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), were applied to evaluate metabolic alterations, and differential metabolites were identified between genotype groups at each time point.

Clear separations were observed between WT and PS cDKO mice at both ages in PCA (R²X = 0.821, Q² = 0.508) and PLS-DA (R²X = 0.864, R²Y = 0.982, Q² = 0.959) score plots, indicating significant alterations in the urinary metabolome of PS cDKO mice. Eight differential metabolites were identified between WT and PS cDKO mice at 2 months of age, whereas this number increased to 11 at 6 months. Notably, xylitol and glycine (designated as cluster II) were consistently downregulated in PS cDKO mice at both time points. The remaining differential metabolites in the 2-month comparison constituted cluster I.

PS cDKO mice exhibit age-dependent alterations in urinary metabolites, with increasing numbers of differential metabolites as the disease-like phenotype progresses from 2 to 6 months of age. The consistent downregulation of xylitol and glycine may represent early metabolic signatures associated with PS cDKO pathology.
