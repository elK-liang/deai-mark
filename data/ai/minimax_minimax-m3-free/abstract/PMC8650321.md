Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) is characterized by elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels and increased risk of premature coronary artery disease (CAD). Physical signs such as tendon xanthomas (TX) and corneal arcus (CA) are clinical hallmarks of HeFH, but their relationships with genotype, lipid and inflammatory profiles, coronary atherosclerosis burden, and cardiovascular outcomes remain incompletely defined. Methods: We analyzed 466 subjects with HeFH, of whom 55 (11.8%) exhibited physical signs. Propensity score matching at a 1:4 ratio yielded a cohort of 250 patients (50 with TX/CA and 200 without), balanced for demographic and clinical covariates. Baseline characteristics, lipid profiles, genetic spectrum (including LDLR, APOB, and PCSK9 mutations), coronary atherosclerosis indices, and clinical outcomes were compared between groups. Results: The mean age of the matched cohort was 47.8 ± 12.6 years, and 62.2% were male. Patients with TX/CA showed significantly higher total cholesterol (7.66 ± 2.64 vs 7.06 ± 2.11 mmol/L, p = 0.167), LDL-C (5.94 ± 2.38 vs 5.19 ± 1.80 mmol/L, p = 0.051), uncontrolled LDL-C (8.65 ± 2.53 vs 7.70 ± 2.18 mmol/L, p = 0.025), non-HDL-C (6.66 ± 2.69 vs 5.87 ± 1.99 mmol/L, p = 0.072), and Apo B levels, together with lower HDL-C (1.00 ± 0.34 vs 1.14 ± 0.37 mmol/L, p = 0.021), Apo A1 (1.11 ± 0.37 vs 1.30 ± 0.31 g/L, p = 0.001), and triglycerides (1.26 vs 1.60 mmol/L, p = 0.001) compared with those without TX/CA. Genetic analysis demonstrated that TX/CA was strongly associated with definite FH (DLCN score > 8; 94.0% vs 48.5%, p < 0.001) and with the presence of LDLR mutations (66.0% vs 32.0%, p < 0.001), particularly in patients carrying two or more mutant alleles (24.0% vs 9.0%, p = 0.004). No significant differences were observed between groups in the prevalence of APOB or PCSK9 mutations. Although the prevalence of CAD and the number of diseased vessels tended to be higher in patients with TX/CA (78.0% vs 71.1% and 62.8% vs …), these differences did not reach statistical significance. Conclusions: In patients with HeFH, the presence of tendon xanthomas and corneal arcus is closely linked to a more severe genetic and lipid phenotype, as evidenced by higher LDL-C and non-HDL-C levels, lower HDL-C and Apo A1, and a markedly higher frequency of LDLR mutations, especially in those carrying multiple mutant alleles. However, after propensity score matching, these physical signs were not independently associated with a significantly greater burden of coronary atherosclerosis, suggesting that the prognostic implications of TX/CA may be mediated primarily through their correlation with underlying genetic and lipid abnormalities rather than serving as independent markers of coronary disease severity.
