Bradykinin (BK) and related kinin peptides are key mediators within the kallikrein–kinin system, yet endogenous concentrations in healthy individuals are poorly characterized, particularly in compartmentalized fluids such as nasal epithelial lining fluid (NLF). Sensitive and specific reference data are essential to interpret pathological elevations in inflammatory, infectious, and hereditary angioedema conditions. Methods: Plasma and NLF were collected from 28 healthy adult volunteers (17 male, 11 female; median age 26.5 [25–28] years, all Caucasian). Peptide extraction was performed using stable isotope-labeled internal standards followed by liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) quantification of BK, kallidin (KD), des-Arg⁹-BK, des-Arg¹⁰-KD, BK1-8, and BK2-9. Results: Endogenous kinin concentrations in plasma (n = 24) were in the low picomolar range, with median BK of 0.0 [0.0–1.3] pM (maximum 4.2 pM) and inter-tube variations below 1.7 pM. Other kinin species (des-Arg¹⁰-KD, BK1-8, and KD) were largely below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ), yielding only semi-quantitative estimates in subsets of subjects. Kinin levels remained stable across the circadian cycle (6 a.m. to 6 p.m.) in one male and one female subject, and no gender-related differences were observed. When protease inhibitor addition was deliberately delayed by 15 minutes, plasma BK levels rose sharply (median 322.2 pM), underscoring the importance of pre-analytical handling. NLF sampling yielded adequate recovered volumes from 24 subjects. Conclusion: This study establishes stringent LC–MS/MS-based reference values for kinin peptides in plasma of healthy adults, demonstrates that endogenous circulating BK and related kinins exist at very low picomolar concentrations in the absence of pathology, and highlights the critical influence of sample collection and processing on measured levels. These data provide a methodological and analytical foundation for future investigations of kinin dysregulation in disease.
