Long noncoding RNA HULC has been implicated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression, but its precise molecular mechanism remains unclear. This study aimed to elucidate the relationship between HULC, miR15a, and PTEN in human liver cancer and to investigate how this axis contributes to tumor progression through autophagy regulation. We analyzed the expression of HULC, miR15a, and PTEN in 30 paired human hepatocellular carcinoma and adjacent noncancerous tissue samples using in situ hybridization, RT-PCR, immunohistochemistry staining, and Western blotting. HULC was significantly overexpressed in liver cancer tissues compared with paired adjacent noncancerous tissues, whereas both miR15a and PTEN were significantly downregulated, with a strong negative correlation between HULC and miR15a or PTEN expression. To explore the functional consequences of HULC upregulation, we established stable Hep3B cell lines overexpressing HULC and demonstrated that excessive HULC expression promoted malignant growth of liver cancer cells. Our findings identify a HULC/miR15a/PTEN regulatory network in which HULC overexpression is associated with reduced miR15a and PTEN levels, providing mechanistic insight into how HULC accelerates liver cancer through autophagy-mediated inhibition of PTEN.
