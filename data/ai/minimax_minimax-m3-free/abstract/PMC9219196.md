Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, highlighting the urgent need to identify novel molecular drivers and therapeutic targets. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of cancer progression, yet their functional roles and mechanistic actions in CRC remain largely undefined. In this study, we identified circEXOC6B (hsa_circ_0009043), a circular RNA generated from exons 3–6 of the EXOC6B gene with a mature length of 390 bp, as the second most significantly downregulated circRNA in colon cancer through high-throughput sequencing and validated its reduced expression in an independent GEO dataset (GSE77661). We confirmed the circular nature of circEXOC6B by divergent primer amplification, Sanger sequencing of back-splice junctions, resistance to RNase R digestion, and actinomycin D stability assays, demonstrating that circEXOC6B possesses a closed loop structure without free 5′ or 3′ ends. Clinically, circEXOC6B was downregulated in 78 paired CRC tissues compared with adjacent noncancerous tissues, and its low expression was negatively correlated with tumor size, lymphatic metastasis, and TNM stage, supporting its potential as a prognostic biomarker. Through a series of functional and mechanistic experiments, we demonstrated that circEXOC6B directly interacts with RRAGB, a known activator of mTORC1, and that this interaction antagonizes the HIF1A–RRAGB–mTORC1 positive feedback loop, thereby inhibiting CRC progression. Collectively, our findings uncover circEXOC6B as a tumor-suppressive circRNA that functions through disrupting the HIF1A–RRAGB–mTORC1 signaling axis, providing new insights into circRNA-mediated regulation of CRC pathogenesis and highlighting a potential therapeutic vulnerability in colorectal cancer.
