Abstract

Tumor endothelial cells (TECs) play a critical role in tumor angiogenesis, yet their molecular heterogeneity and the mechanisms by which they promote angiogenesis remain incompletely understood. Lung adenocarcinoma (ADC) is characterized by active angiogenesis, and identifying TEC-derived factors that drive this process may reveal novel prognostic markers and therapeutic targets.

Primary ECs were isolated from lung adenocarcinoma tissues (TEC-A), paratumor tissues of ADC patients (PEC-A), tumor tissues of squamous cell carcinoma patients (TEC-S), and normal lung tissues (NEC). Purity and viability of isolated ECs were confirmed by expression of endothelial markers (CD31, CD34, CD144, CD105, and VWF) and absence of smooth muscle cell (α-SMA) and monocyte (CD11b, CD45) markers. Proteomic profiling was performed to identify differentially expressed proteins among the EC groups. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and principal component analysis (PCA) were used to characterize the proteomic profiles. Functional assays, including EC migration and EC–vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) cocultivation assays, were conducted to evaluate angiogenic properties.

Together, these findings identify CDH2 as a TEC-derived factor that promotes angiogenesis in lung adenocarcinoma and highlight its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for this malignancy.
