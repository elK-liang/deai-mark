Exosomes derived from tumor-associated cells have emerged as important mediators of intercellular communication in the tumor microenvironment, and their non-coding RNA (ncRNA) cargo holds promise as novel biomarkers for liquid biopsy. However, while the exosomal RNA content of cancer cells has been widely studied, little is known about the ncRNA repertoire packaged into exosomes released by stromal cells such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and their normal counterparts (NFs) in colorectal cancer. Results: We propagated primary NFs and CAFs from 9 colorectal cancer patients, prepared pooled cellular and exosomal RNA samples (3 pools per condition, each combining 3 independent samples), and profiled the expression of 1668 lncRNA and 1761 sncRNA species representing 16 different biotypes by next generation sequencing. Unsupervised clustering revealed that cellular samples (NF-CELL and CAF-CELL) were more similar to each other than to their matched exosomal samples (NF-EXO and CAF-EXO), indicating that the ncRNA composition of exosomes is distinct from that of the producer cells. Specifically, sense intronic RNAs, lincRNAs and miRNAs were more abundant in the cellular fraction, whereas YRNAs and piRNAs were markedly enriched in the exosomal fraction. Differential distribution analyses confirmed that snoRNAs were significantly enriched in cells, while YRNAs were significantly enriched in exosomes from both NFs and CAFs, consistent with recent reports describing YRNA accumulation in extracellular vesicles from colon cancer cells. Conclusions: Our findings demonstrate that normal and cancer-associated fibroblasts in colorectal cancer selectively package specific ncRNA biotypes, particularly YRNAs, into exosomes, suggesting a conserved sorting mechanism and pointing to encapsulated YRNAs as candidate biomarkers in the stromal compartment of the tumor microenvironment.
