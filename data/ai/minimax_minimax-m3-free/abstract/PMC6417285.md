Abstract

The tumor microenvironment is characterized by hypoxia, and bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) within this microenvironment are known to contribute to cancer progression. However, the mechanisms by which hypoxic BMSCs promote tumor metastasis remain incompletely understood. Exosomes derived from BMSCs have emerged as important mediators of intercellular communication through the transfer of microRNAs (miRNAs). This study investigates the role of hypoxic BMSC-derived exosomal miRNAs in promoting metastasis of lung cancer cells.

A syngeneic Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) mouse model was employed, in which luci-LLC cells were subcutaneously injected alone or co-injected with BMSCs into C57BL/6 mice. Primary tumor growth and lung metastasis were assessed by bioluminescence imaging, quantification of metastatic nodules, and histological examination. Plasma exosomal miRNA profiles were analyzed to identify differentially expressed miRNAs between the co-injection and LLC-alone groups. BMSCs were cultured under hypoxic conditions, and exosomes isolated from the conditioned medium were characterized.

Co-injection of BMSCs with LLC cells resulted in larger primary allograft tumors compared to LLC cells alone. Notably, mice receiving the mixture of LLC cells and BMSCs exhibited substantially higher luciferase activity in the lungs, indicating increased micrometastatic burden, as well as a greater number of lung metastatic nodules confirmed by hematoxylin and eosin staining. miRNA profiling of plasma exosomes revealed that 86 exosomal miRNAs were significantly increased and 354 exosomal miRNAs were significantly decreased in the co-injection group relative to the LLC-alone group. Characterization of BMSC-derived exosomes showed that hypoxic conditions altered exosome properties, with hypoxic BMSC-derived exosomes exhibiting a smaller average diameter (50 nm) compared to those from normoxic BMSCs (90 nm).

These findings demonstrate that BMSCs promote LLC tumor growth and lung metastasis in vivo and that hypoxia modifies the size and miRNA cargo of BMSC-derived exosomes. The altered exosomal miRNA profile associated with BMSC involvement suggests that hypoxic BMSC-derived exosomal miRNAs may serve as important mediators of tumor metastasis, providing new insights into the mechanisms by which the hypoxic tumor microenvironment drives lung cancer progression.
