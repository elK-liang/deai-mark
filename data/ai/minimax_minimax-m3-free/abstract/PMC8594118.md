Abstract

Pancreatic cancer, especially advanced and metastatic disease, is associated with poor prognosis. Accurate prognostic models are needed to guide clinical decision-making and patient counseling. This study aimed to develop and validate a nomogram to predict overall survival in patients with advanced or metastatic pancreatic cancer.

A total of 323 patients with advanced or metastatic pancreatic cancer were enrolled. Two prediction models were constructed: a baseline model incorporating demographic, clinicopathological, and tumor marker variables available prior to treatment, and a chemotherapy response-based model incorporating additional treatment-related variables. Candidate predictors were screened using univariate survival analysis, and stepwise Akaike information criterion (AIC) backward regression was applied in multivariable analysis to identify independent prognostic factors. Model performance was evaluated, and a nomogram was constructed to visualize the prediction of survival.

The median overall survival of the entire cohort was 10.6 months (95% CI: 9.7–11.7), with 1-year and 2-year survival rates of 42.1% and 12.2%, respectively. In the baseline model, six independent prognostic factors were identified: gender, Karnofsky performance status (KPS), baseline total bilirubin (TB), baseline neutrophil count (N), baseline white blood cell count (WBC), and baseline carbohydrate antigen 19–9 (CA19–9). In the chemotherapy response-based model, 16 variables showing statistical significance in univariate analysis were included for further evaluation. Among the chemotherapy regimens, the median overall survival of patients treated with nab-paclitaxel plus S1 was greater than that of gemcitabine monotherapy (9.92 vs 6.18 months, p = 0.004), and gemcitabine-based combination therapy was superior to gemcitabine monotherapy (11.04 vs 6.18 months, p = 0.019).

Two prognostic nomograms were developed for patients with advanced and metastatic pancreatic cancer. The baseline model allows survival prediction using readily available pretreatment variables, while the chemotherapy response-based model incorporates treatment-related information to refine prognostic estimation. These nomograms may assist clinicians in individualized risk stratification and treatment planning.
