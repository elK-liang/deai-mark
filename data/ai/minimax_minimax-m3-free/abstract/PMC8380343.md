Abstract

Angiogenesis plays a critical role in tumor progression and immune modulation, yet the contributions of specific angiogenesis regulators to lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remain incompletely defined. This study aimed to systematically identify angiogenesis-related genes associated with prognosis, immune infiltration, and tumor progression in LUAD.

A total of 154 angiogenesis regulators were retrieved from the literature and mapped to TCGA-LUAD transcriptomic data. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between tumor and adjacent normal tissues were identified using the Limma package (|log2 fold change| > 1, adjusted P < 0.05). Functional enrichment was performed via Metascape, and prognostic genes were determined by univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses. The functional roles of COL5A2 and EPHB2 in LUAD cells (A549 and H1299) were validated by siRNA-mediated knockdown followed by CCK-8, EdU, and Transwell assays. Western blotting was used to confirm knockdown efficiency.

Sixty-six angiogenesis-related genes were differentially expressed in LUAD (28 upregulated and 38 downregulated), and were enriched in angiogenesis, endothelial development, PI3K-Akt signaling, and cytokine secretion pathways. Multivariate Cox regression identified COL5A2 and EPHB2 as independent prognostic factors associated with overall survival. Knockdown of either COL5A2 or EPHB2 significantly inhibited proliferation and migration of A549 and H1299 cells.

COL5A2 and EPHB2 are angiogenesis-related genes associated with poor prognosis in LUAD and promote tumor cell proliferation and migration, suggesting their potential as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.
