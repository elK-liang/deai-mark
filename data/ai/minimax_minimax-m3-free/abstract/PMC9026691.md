Abstract

Adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma (ATLL) is an aggressive hematological malignancy associated with human T-cell lymphotropic virus type 1 (HTLV-1) infection, encompassing distinct clinical subtypes—acute, chronic, and smoldering—that differ in prognosis and therapeutic response. Accurate molecular classification of these subtypes remains challenging, and the transcriptomic features distinguishing them from asymptomatic carriers (ACs) are not fully defined.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between ACs and each ATLL subtype using transcriptomic profiling. Unique DEGs specific to each subtype were selected and used as input features for support vector machine (SVM) classification coupled with recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV) to identify optimal gene classifiers. Model performance was evaluated on independent test sets, and pathway enrichment analysis was performed on the identified classifiers to explore their biological relevance.

A total of 5327, 5525, and 5185 DEGs were identified between ACs and ATLL acute, chronic, and smoldering subtypes, respectively, with 594, 521, and 187 subtype-unique DEGs selected for downstream analysis. SVM-RFECV identified 27, 9, and 32 gene classifiers for ATLL acute, chronic, and smoldering subtypes, respectively. These classifiers achieved high test-set accuracies of 1.00, 0.95, and 0.95 for ATLL acute, chronic, and smoldering subtypes, respectively. Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that ATLL acute classifiers were involved in glutathione metabolism, CXCR4-mediated signaling, polyamine metabolism, interferon signaling, and noncanonical Wnt signaling, while ATLL chronic classifiers were associated with tRNA modification and TGF-beta–related pathways.

This study identified distinct mRNA gene signatures capable of accurately discriminating each ATLL subtype from ACs and revealed subtype-specific pathway enrichments, providing candidate molecular classifiers and mechanistic insights into ATLL heterogeneity.
