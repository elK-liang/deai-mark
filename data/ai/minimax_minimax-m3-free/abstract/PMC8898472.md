Abstract.

The Implantable Venous Access Port (IVAP) is widely used for chemotherapy delivery in breast cancer patients. While ultrasound-guided internal jugular vein (IJV) puncture is the conventional approach, axillary/subclavian vein (AxV/SCV) puncture has been proposed as an alternative. This randomized controlled study compared patient comfort, procedure duration, and early complications between ultrasound-guided IJV and AxV/SCV approaches for IVAP placement in breast cancer patients.

A total of 248 breast cancer patients scheduled for IVAP placement were randomly assigned to either the IJV group (n = 124) or the AxV/SCV group (n = 124). All punctures were performed under ultrasound guidance. Patient comfort was assessed using a grading scale on postoperative days 1, 2, and 7. Procedure duration and early complications (including catheter misplacement, subcutaneous hematoma, and artery puncture) were recorded and compared between the two groups. Three patients in the IJV group and one patient in the AxV/SCV group were lost to follow-up on day 2 and day 7.

Baseline characteristics including age, BMI, marital status, history of diabetes, tumor side, chemotherapy, and breast/lymph node surgery were comparable between the two groups. Patient comfort in the AxV/SCV group was significantly better than that in the IJV group on day 1 (P < 0.001), day 2 (P < 0.001), and day 7 (P = 0.023). On day 1, most patients in the IJV group reported grade 1 (29.8%) or grade 2 (43.5%) discomfort, whereas most patients in the AxV/SCV group reported grade 0 (66.9%) or grade 1 (24.2%) discomfort. Similar patterns favoring the AxV/SCV group were observed on days 2 and 7. Procedure duration was significantly shorter in the AxV/SCV group (27.14 ± 3.29 mins) compared with the IJV group (28.92 ± 2.54 mins, P < 0.001). Early complications were significantly more frequent in the AxV/SCV group than in the IJV group (8.87% vs. 1.61%, P = 0.019), which was primarily attributable to a higher rate of catheter misplacement in the AxV/SCV group (4.84% vs. 0%, P = 0.029). Among the six catheter misplacements in the AxV/SCV group, the catheter tips were located at the right internal jugular vein in two cases, the right subclavian vein in two cases, and the left brachiocephalic vein in two cases. No significant differences were observed in the rates of artery puncture or subcutaneous hematoma, and no severe complications such as pneumothorax or hemothorax occurred in either group.

Ultrasound-guided AxV/SCV puncture for IVAP placement in breast cancer patients offers better postoperative comfort and shorter procedure duration compared with IJV puncture. However, the higher rate of catheter misplacement associated with the AxV/SCV approach highlights the need for careful intraoperative verification of catheter tip position.
