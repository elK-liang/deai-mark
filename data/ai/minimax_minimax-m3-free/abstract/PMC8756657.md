Breast cancer survivors (BCSs) often experience diminished health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and psychological factors such as coping styles, perceived social support, and resilience have been implicated in this process; however, the mechanisms underlying these relationships remain unclear. This study aimed to examine the associations of coping styles (confrontation, avoidance, and resignation) and perceived social support with HRQoL, and to investigate whether resilience mediates these relationships among Chinese BCSs. Methods: A cross-sectional study was conducted with 240 eligible BCSs recruited from a tertiary hospital in China, of whom 231 (96.3%) completed the questionnaires with no missing data and were included in the final analysis. Participants completed standardized measures of coping styles, perceived social support, resilience, and HRQoL. Normality of the data was verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and Pearson correlation analyses were performed to examine bivariate relationships among the study variables. Mediation analyses were conducted to determine whether resilience mediated the relationships between coping styles, perceived social support, and HRQoL. Results: The findings indicated that perceived social support and resilience were positively associated with confrontation coping and negatively associated with resignation coping, while avoidance coping showed no significant association with these variables. Both perceived social support and resilience emerged as significant correlates of HRQoL, with resilience demonstrating a mediating role in the relationships between coping styles, perceived social support, and HRQoL among BCSs. Conclusion: Resilience serves as a mediator between psychological predictors (coping styles and perceived social support) and HRQoL in breast cancer survivors, highlighting the importance of resilience-focused interventions to enhance the quality of life in this population.
