Abstract

Background
Psychiatric comorbidity (PC) is common in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet its genetic architecture and causal relationship with IBD remain poorly characterized. This study aimed to investigate the polygenic basis of PC in IBD and to explore causal inference between the two conditions among individuals of European ancestry.

Methods
Demographic and clinical characteristics of IBD patients with and without PC were compared, with covariates including sex, age at diagnosis, marital status, and disease subtype considered as potential confounders. A genome-wide association study (GWAS) of PC status in IBD was performed adjusting for these covariates and the top five multidimensional scaling (MDS) components. Risk single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were identified at multiple P-value thresholds (5×10⁻², 5×10⁻³, and 5×10⁻⁴), followed by linkage disequilibrium (LD) pruning and matching with an external IBD GWAS dataset. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted on genes mapped to the matched risk SNPs. Polygenic risk scores (PRS) were estimated based on the IBD GWAS at the different thresholds, and causal inference between IBD and PC was assessed using Mendelian randomization approaches.

Results
No SNPs reached genome-wide significance (5×10⁻⁸) for PC in IBD, although 37 SNPs reached suggestive significance (5×10⁻⁵), distributed across multiple chromosomes, supporting a polygenic architecture for the phenotype. After LD pruning and matching with the external IBD dataset, 9620, 471, and 33 SNPs remained at the three P-value thresholds, respectively; all individuals carried at least one risk allele among the 33 SNPs. The top enriched GO terms among genes associated with the risk SNPs were pyrimidine ribonucleotide metabolic process (GO:0009218), toll-like receptor 2 signaling pathway (GO:0034134), and protein localization to axon (GO:0099612), each with an enrichment P-value of 0.008 (adjusted P-value = 0.11). Patients with IBD and PC showed slightly higher mean standardized PRS compared with those without PC.

Conclusions
PC in IBD displays a polygenic genetic architecture without single loci of genome-wide significance. Shared risk variants with IBD and modestly elevated PRS in patients with PC suggest partial genetic overlap, although the limited sample size restricts statistical power. These findings provide a foundation for larger studies aimed at clarifying the genetic and causal relationships between IBD and psychiatric comorbidity.
