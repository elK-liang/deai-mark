Abstract

Colorectal carcinoma (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, and lipid metabolism has been increasingly recognized as a key contributor to tumor initiation and progression. However, the prognostic value of lipid metabolism-related genes (LMRG) and their relationship with the tumor immune microenvironment in CRC remain poorly characterized. This study aimed to develop and validate a lipid metabolism-related gene prognostic biomarker and to explore its association with the tumor immune microenvironment in colorectal carcinoma.

LMRG expression data were extracted from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts, and 945 intersecting LMRG were identified. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between normal and tumor tissues were identified in the training cohort (FDR < 0.05). Univariate Cox regression analysis was used to identify prognosis-related LMRG, and overlapping DEGs and prognostic genes were subjected to functional enrichment analysis, including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. A lipid metabolism-related risk signature was constructed using LASSO Cox regression analysis. The predictive performance of the signature was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to examine expression patterns between risk groups.

A total of 729 DEGs were identified, comprising 365 upregulated and 364 downregulated genes. Univariate Cox analysis identified 57 prognosis-related LMRG, of which 47 overlapped with DEGs. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these shared LMRG were significantly involved in lipid metabolism-related biological processes, including glycerolipid metabolic process, glycerophospholipid metabolic process, lipid modification, phospholipid metabolic process, and regulation of lipid metabolic process, as well as KEGG pathways such as glycerophospholipid metabolism, phosphatidylinositol signaling system, PPAR signaling pathway, fatty acid degradation, and fatty acid metabolism. Using LASSO Cox regression, a four-gene risk signature comprising PROCA1, CCKBR, CPT2, and FDFT1 was established. The risk score was calculated as: risk score = (PROCA1 × 0.03071) + (CCKBR × 0.58956) + (CPT2 × 0.00972) + (FDFT1 × 0.01381). The signature yielded area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.6901, 0.6776, and 0.5945 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival (OS) in the training set. Patients stratified into high- and low-risk groups according to the median risk score showed significantly different OS, with high-risk patients exhibiting shorter survival (P < 0.001). PCA based on the four LMRG effectively distinguished the two risk groups.

We established a lipid metabolism-related four-gene prognostic signature (PROCA1, CCKBR, CPT2, and FDFT1) that effectively stratifies CRC patients into distinct risk groups with significantly different overall survival outcomes. This biomarker is associated with the tumor immune microenvironment and may provide new insights into prognostic evaluation and therapeutic strategies for colorectal carcinoma.
