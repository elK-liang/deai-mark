Abstract

Synovial sarcoma is an aggressive soft-tissue malignancy characterized by the pathognomonic SYT-SSX fusion gene, most commonly SYT-SSX1. However, the molecular mechanisms by which SYT-SSX1 drives tumor progression remain incompletely understood. In this study, we investigated the role of SYT-SSX1 in enhancing the invasiveness and maintaining stem-like cell properties in synovial sarcoma cells.

The SYT-SSX1 fusion gene was overexpressed in SW982 synovial sarcoma cells via lentiviral transduction, with a transduction efficiency of approximately 93.86%. Overexpression was confirmed by RT-PCR, which detected the 118 bp SYT-SSX1 fusion transcript specifically in transduced cells. Migratory and invasive abilities were assessed using transwell migration/invasion assays and wound healing assays. Cancer stem-like cell (CSC) populations were enriched through serum-free suspension sphere-forming culture. The involvement of TGF-β1/Smad signaling was examined.

SYT-SSX1-overexpressing cells exhibited morphological changes consistent with increased malignancy, including a large nucleus, clear cytoplasm, and enhanced proliferation. Transwell assays demonstrated that SYT-SSX1 cells had significantly higher migratory and invasive capacities compared with parental SW982 cells (P < 0.0001). Wound healing assays confirmed significantly enhanced migration at both 24 h (P < 0.0001) and 48 h (P = 0.002). In addition, SYT-SSX1 cells displayed markedly greater sphere-forming ability under serum-free conditions, indicating enrichment of CSC-like populations.

These findings demonstrate that SYT-SSX1 enhances the invasive phenotype and promotes stem-like cell properties in synovial sarcoma cells, and suggest that induction of TGF-β1/Smad signaling underlies these effects. Targeting this pathway may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for synovial sarcoma.
