Gliomas are the most common primary malignant tumors in the central nervous system, and despite advances in surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, the prognosis of glioma patients remains poor. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as important regulators in various cancers, but their roles and mechanisms in gliomas are still largely unknown. Methods: We identified differentially expressed circRNAs during early neural differentiation using a bioinformatics approach based on a previously published murine P19 embryonic carcinoma model. The expression of circ-MAPK4 (hsa_circ_0047688) was examined in glioma tissues and normal brain tissues by qPCR with divergent primers, and its clinical relevance was analyzed in a validation cohort. Bioinformatics analysis combined with luciferase reporter assays, RNA immunoprecipitation, and functional assays in glioma cells was performed to identify downstream miRNA targets and signaling pathways. Results: We found that circ-MAPK4 was significantly downregulated on the 4th day of retinoic acid-induced neural differentiation of P19 cells. In contrast, circ-MAPK4 was significantly overexpressed in glioma tissues compared with normal brain tissues, whereas the parental MAPK4 mRNA showed no significant difference. Upregulation of circ-MAPK4 was confirmed in GBM using the MiOncoCirc database, and among other circRNAs identified in the neural differentiation model, none showed more significant overexpression in gliomas than circ-MAPK4. Clinically, circ-MAPK4 was elevated in patients with advanced-stage gliomas (III + IV) compared with early-stage gliomas (I + II). Mechanistically, circ-MAPK4 acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) by sponging miR-125a-3p, thereby relieving the repression of downstream components of the p38/MAPK signaling pathway and inhibiting cell apoptosis in gliomas. Conclusions: circ-MAPK4 is overexpressed in gliomas and associated with advanced tumor stage. Through the miR-125a-3p/p38/MAPK axis, circ-MAPK4 inhibits glioma cell apoptosis, suggesting that circ-MAPK4 may serve as a novel diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for glioma patients.
