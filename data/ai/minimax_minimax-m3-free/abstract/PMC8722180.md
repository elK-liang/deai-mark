Abstract

Paratesticular leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a rare malignant mesenchymal tumor arising from the smooth muscle of the spermatic cord, epididymis, or tunica. Owing to its rarity, optimal surgical management and the prognostic significance of clinicopathological features remain poorly defined. This study aimed to clarify optimal surgical treatment and identify prognostic factors in a large cohort of reported cases.

We retrospectively analyzed 217 patients with paratesticular LMS reported in the literature. Clinicopathological variables including tumor depth (cutaneous vs. subcutaneous), surgical procedure (high inguinal orchiectomy vs. simple tumorectomy), surgical margin status, tumor grade, and adjuvant therapy were extracted and correlated with oncologic outcomes. Survival analyses were performed using Kaplan-Meier methods for local recurrence (LR)-free survival, distant metastasis (DM)-free survival, and disease-specific survival (DSS).

The mean patient age was 59.3 years (range 1–89), with a mean follow-up of 41 months. Of the 217 tumors, 42 (20.2%) were cutaneous LMS and 166 (79.8%) were subcutaneous LMS. As definitive surgery, 150 patients (73.2%) underwent high inguinal orchiectomy and 55 (26.8%) underwent simple tumorectomy. Subcutaneous LMS was significantly larger than cutaneous LMS and, despite being more frequently treated with high inguinal orchiectomy (88.1% vs. 17.9%, p < 0.001), was associated with a significantly higher rate of positive surgical margins (18.2% vs. 0%, p = 0.049). Cutaneous LMS showed slightly better LR-free survival, DM-free survival, and DSS than subcutaneous LMS, with the difference reaching significance only for DM-free survival (p = 0.033). Higher tumor grade (Grade 3 vs. Grade 1) was significantly associated with increased risk of DM and disease-specific mortality (both p < 0.001). Microscopic positive margins were significantly associated with higher risk of LR and DM (p < 0.001 and p = 0.018, respectively). Adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy were administered in 31 (16.0%) and 13 (6.7%) of 194 patients with available data, respectively.

Paratesticular LMS exhibits distinct clinicopathological behavior according to tumor depth. Subcutaneous LMS is associated with larger tumor size and higher rates of positive surgical margins despite more aggressive surgical resection, whereas cutaneous LMS generally demonstrates more favorable outcomes. High tumor grade and positive microscopic margins are significant predictors of recurrence and mortality. These findings support complete surgical resection with negative margins as the cornerstone of treatment, and highlight the need for risk-adapted adjuvant strategies, particularly for high-grade subcutaneous tumors.
