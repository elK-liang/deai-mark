Abstract: Skin cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide, and primary school students in rural areas are particularly vulnerable due to limited awareness and frequent outdoor sun exposure. This study applied the Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) to examine and promote skin cancer preventive behaviors among fifth-grade primary school students in rural areas of Fasa city, Iran. The research comprised a cross-sectional phase involving 400 students (135 males, 33.75%; 265 females, 66.25%) with a mean age of 11.11 ± 0.79 years, followed by a semi-experimental phase with experimental and control groups (mean ages 11.26 ± 0.72 and 11.18 ± 0.80 years, respectively). Data were collected using a validated self-administered questionnaire assessing PMT constructs (perceived sensitivity, perceived severity, rewards, fear, protection motivation, self-efficacy, response efficacy, response costs), knowledge, and self-reported preventive behaviors. In the cross-sectional phase, significant positive correlations were observed between preventive behaviors and perceived sensitivity (r = 0.178, p = 0.007), perceived severity (r = 0.124, p = 0.016), protection motivation (r = 0.184, p = 0.004), response efficacy (r = 1.20, p = 0.022), self-efficacy (r = 0.180, p = 0.018), fear (r = 0.144, p = 0.009), and knowledge (r = 0.102, p = 0.037), while rewards (r = −0.126, p = 0.025) and response costs (r = −0.115, p = 0.016) were negatively correlated. Logistic regression analysis identified protection motivation (β = 0.228), self-efficacy (β = 0.219), and perceived sensitivity (β = 0.206) as the strongest predictors of preventive behaviors, with the model explaining 58.6% of the variance in behavior. Findings from this study support the applicability of PMT as a theoretical framework for understanding and promoting skin cancer preventive behaviors among rural primary school students and can inform the design of theory-based educational interventions targeting this population.
