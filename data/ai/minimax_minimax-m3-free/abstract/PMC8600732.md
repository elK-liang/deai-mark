Abstract

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disorder whose molecular pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of gene expression through competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks, but their roles in MG are largely unclear. This study aimed to identify MG-associated risk genes and to elucidate the regulatory role of lncRNA HCG18 using integrated bioinformatics and experimental approaches.

MG risk genes were retrieved from public databases, and a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed. Module analysis was performed to identify functionally coherent gene clusters, followed by Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses. A lncRNA-mediated module-associated ceRNA (LMMAC) network was constructed by integrating lncRNA–miRNA and miRNA–gene interactions, and a random walk with restart (RWR) algorithm was applied to prioritize candidate regulatory lncRNAs. The expression and regulatory function of the top-ranked lncRNA, HCG18, were further validated by experimental analyses.

The PPI network contained 250 genes and 4211 edges, from which 9 functional modules were identified (ranging from 3 to 54 genes per module). Enrichment analysis yielded 3257 GO terms and 364 KEGG pathways (p < 0.05), with the most prominent terms relating to immune and inflammatory responses, including T cell differentiation, T cell activation, interleukin-12 production, and positive regulation of cytokine production, as well as pathways such as Th17 cell differentiation, T cell receptor signaling, and natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity. The LMMAC network comprised 25 genes, 50 miRNAs, 64 lncRNAs, and 228 edges. Among the lncRNAs, HCG18 was identified as a key regulatory candidate through RWR analysis. Subsequent experimental validation confirmed the regulatory role of HCG18 in MG.

This study constructed a comprehensive PPI-based module framework and an lncRNA-mediated ceRNA network for MG, highlighting immune- and inflammation-related pathways as central to MG pathogenesis. Integrated bioinformatics prioritization and experimental validation identified lncRNA HCG18 as an important regulatory molecule in MG, providing new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the disease.
