Abstract: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is characterized by persistent airflow limitation and is associated with alterations in the respiratory microbiome; however, the specific sputum microbiome signatures during acute exacerbations and their correlations with clinical indices remain incompletely defined. In this study, we enrolled a total of 76 participants, including 10 healthy controls, 28 patients with acute exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD), 15 patients in the recovery phase, and 23 patients with stable COPD, and analyzed sputum samples using 16S ribosomal RNA (16S rRNA) gene sequencing. The demographics and baseline clinical characteristics of the four groups were largely comparable, with no significant differences observed in age, gender, body mass index (BMI), current smoking status, comorbid conditions, medications, or exacerbation frequency. As expected, lung function indices, including FEV1% predicted and FEV1/FVC, were significantly decreased in all COPD patients compared with healthy controls (p < 0.001), and patients in the AECOPD group exhibited higher modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnoea scores than those with stable COPD (p = 0.023). Notably, blood routine parameters, glycemia, arterial blood gas indices, inflammatory markers (procalcitonin [PCT] and C-reactive protein [CRP]), and GOLD classification did not differ significantly among the four groups. These findings establish the clinical and demographic baseline for subsequent microbiome profiling, which may help elucidate the microbial signatures associated with COPD exacerbations and their relationships with clinical indices, thereby providing a foundation for future investigations into microbiome-based biomarkers and therapeutic targets in COPD.
