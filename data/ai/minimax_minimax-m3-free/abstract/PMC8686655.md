Human interleukin-33 (hIL-33) is an alarmin cytokine implicated in age-related macular degeneration (AMD) and asthma, yet reliable quantification in biological matrices remains challenging due to low endogenous concentrations and interference from the soluble decoy receptor hsST2. Methods: To establish a fit-for-purpose biomarker assay, we compared multiple commercial assay reagents and platforms reported in the literature for hIL-33 detection and pre-screened anti-hIL-33 antibodies for sensitivity in buffer. Four selection criteria were defined for a qualified biomarker assay: (1) lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) in vitreous humor (VH) < 4 pg/mL to capture non-to-moderate pathology conditions; (2) tolerance of at least 10 ng/mL hsST2, reflecting mean concentrations of 2.05 ng/mL in VH and 19.8 ng/mL in serum; (3) specificity, defined as the ability to immunodeplete 100% ± 20% of hIL-33 from hIL-33-containing human VH samples; and (4) compatibility with a maximum sample volume of 50 µL, owing to the limited availability of ocular matrices. Results: Several platforms, including the Invitrogen Human IL-33 ELISA kit, the Pepro/R&D rabbit anti-hIL-33 antibody pair, the U-PLEX Human IL-33 MSD kit, and the Bio-Plex Pro Human Th17 cytokine 15-Plex Luminex kit, achieved low LLOQs in VH ranging from 0.25 to 3.2 pg/mL. However, none of the commercial kits or reagent combinations evaluated simultaneously satisfied all four predefined criteria, with hsST2 tolerance representing the principal limitation. Conclusion: Existing commercial assays for hIL-33 lack sufficient sensitivity, hsST2 tolerance, specificity, and/or sample volume compatibility to serve as a robust biomarker assay for human matrices in AMD and asthma drug development, underscoring the need for a purpose-built ultra-sensitive hIL-33 assay.
