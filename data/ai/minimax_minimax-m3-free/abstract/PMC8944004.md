Bladder cancer (BLCA) is the most common malignancy of the urinary tract and remains a major clinical challenge due to limited therapeutic options and poor outcomes for patients with advanced disease. While cisplatin-based chemotherapy is a standard of care for advanced BLCA, intrinsic and acquired chemoresistance frequently limits its efficacy, highlighting the urgent need for novel combination strategies. DNA damage repair (DDR) pathways are frequently dysregulated in BLCA, and analysis of TCGA data confirmed a high prevalence of mutations in DDR genes, including ATM, ERCC2, BRCA2, ATR, and TP53, which may render tumors particularly vulnerable to PARP inhibition (PARPi). In this study, we evaluated the relative efficacy of clinically available PARPi (olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib, veliparib, and talazoparib) against BLCA cells. IC50 analysis revealed differential sensitivities, with talazoparib being the most potent (1.0989 μM), followed by olaparib (8.2312 μM), niraparib (8.6093 μM), rucaparib (15.5063 μM), veliparib (39.4209 μM), and cisplatin (3.163 μM). Further analysis showed that talazoparib, niraparib, olaparib, and rucaparib significantly reduced survival of both BLCA cell lines (UM-UC-3 and T-24) and normal urothelial cells (SV-HUC-1), whereas veliparib failed to achieve greater than 30% inhibition even at high concentrations. Talazoparib and niraparib were particularly effective, achieving >50% reduction in BLCA cell survival at low concentrations. Together, these findings provide rationale for the further investigation of PARPi, particularly in combination with cisplatin, as a potential therapeutic strategy to improve outcomes for patients with bladder cancer.
