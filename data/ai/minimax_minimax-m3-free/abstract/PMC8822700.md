Abstract

Pregnancy-associated breast cancer (PABC) is defined as breast cancer diagnosed during pregnancy or within the postpartum period. However, the clinical characteristics and prognosis of PABC remain controversial. This retrospective age-matched study aimed to compare the clinical characteristics, pregnancy outcomes, ovarian function, and survival between PABC and non-PABC patients.

A total of 63 women diagnosed with PABC (15 diagnosed during pregnancy and 48 diagnosed within the postpartum period) and 126 age-matched non-PABC patients (1:2 ratio) were included in this retrospective study. Clinical characteristics, treatment regimens, pregnancy outcomes, ovarian function, recurrence, and survival were compared between the two groups.

There were no significant differences between PABC and non-PABC patients in age at diagnosis, age at menarche, family history of breast or ovarian cancer, or childbearing history. PABC patients were more likely to be diagnosed at an advanced tumor stage (stage III: 33.3% vs. 19.8%, p = 0.006), with larger tumor size (T2: 68.3% vs. 45.2%; T3: 6.3% vs. 2.4%, p < 0.001) and more axillary lymph node metastasis (N2: 22.2% vs. 7.1%, p = 0.016) than non-PABC patients. Estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, HER-2 status, and molecular subtypes were comparable between the two groups. A higher proportion of PABC patients received sequential neoadjuvant and adjuvant chemotherapy (39.7% vs. 10.3%, p < 0.001), while fewer received endocrine therapy. No significant differences were observed in rates of breast-conserving surgery, radiotherapy, or targeted therapy. The recurrence rate (25.4% vs. 12.7%) and death rate (20.6% vs. 7.9%) were higher in PABC patients than in non-PABC patients, and Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated that PABC patients had significantly worse progression-free survival (log-rank p = 0.0138).

Compared with age-matched non-PABC patients, PABC patients presented with more advanced tumor stage, larger tumor size, and more frequent axillary lymph node metastasis at diagnosis, and had a significantly worse prognosis despite receiving more intensive chemotherapy.
