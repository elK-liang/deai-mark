Abstract

The BCL7 family, including BCL7A, BCL7B, and BCL7C, has been implicated in various malignancies; however, their expression patterns and prognostic significance in glioma remain largely unclear. This study aimed to systematically characterize the expression of BCL7 family members in glioma and evaluate their clinical relevance, with a particular focus on BCL7A as a potential prognostic biomarker.

BCL7 family gene expression was analyzed in glioma using multiple public datasets, including cBioportal, Oncomine, Gliovis, GEO, CGGA, and TCGA. Expression of BCL7A, BCL7B, and BCL7C was compared between glioma tissues and non-tumor brain tissues (NBT), as well as across different WHO grades. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was performed on 108 glioma tissues to validate the expression patterns of BCL7 family members at the protein level.

Analysis of public datasets revealed that BCL7A expression was significantly downregulated in glioma compared to NBT in 7 out of 8 GEO datasets, and low BCL7A expression was observed in 12 of 16 brain and CNS cancer studies. Conversely, BCL7C was highly expressed in glioma tissues in 6 out of 8 GEO datasets and elevated in 11 of 12 brain and CNS cancer studies. BCL7B showed no consistent differential expression between glioma and NBT. BCL7A expression decreased progressively with increasing glioma grade, with the lowest levels observed in glioblastoma (GBM), whereas BCL7B and BCL7C were elevated in GBM compared to lower-grade glioma. IHC validation in 108 glioma tissues confirmed that BCL7A protein expression was significantly lower, and BCL7C expression was significantly higher in glioma compared to NBT, while BCL7B showed no significant difference.

BCL7 family members are differentially expressed in glioma, with BCL7A showing consistent downregulation and progressive loss with increasing tumor grade. These findings establish BCL7A as a novel prognostic biomarker candidate for glioma patients, warranting further investigation into its functional role and clinical utility.
