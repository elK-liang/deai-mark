Abstract

Obesity is characterized by excessive accumulation of adipose tissue, which is composed of mature adipocytes (ACs) and their precursor cells, preadipocytes (preACs). Inflammatory processes have been implicated in the pathogenesis of obesity and its metabolic complications. However, the molecular alterations occurring specifically within purified ACs and preACs under obese versus lean conditions, and how these alterations differ between lean and obese adipogenesis, remain incompletely defined.

We purified ACs and preACs from subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) samples of lean and obese individuals and performed mRNA-Seq to characterize their transcriptomes. Four classes of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined: Class I (AC-DEGs) from comparing lean ACs vs. obese ACs; Class II (preAC-DEGs) from comparing lean preACs vs. obese preACs; Class III (Lean_Ag-DEGs) from comparing lean preACs vs. lean ACs (lean adipogenesis); and Class IV (Obese_Ag-DEGs) from comparing obese preACs vs. obese ACs (obese adipogenesis). DEGs were identified using multiple statistical thresholds (P < 0.01 and Q < 0.05), and unsupervised clustering analyses (e.g., heatmaps) were performed to assess sample separation. As validation, our findings were compared with a publicly available microarray dataset (GEO: GSE80654) reporting DEGs between lean and obese ACs.

A total of 1,198 and 314 genes were identified as Class I AC-DEGs at P < 0.01 and Q < 0.05, respectively. Class II, III, and IV DEGs were similarly identified using the same thresholding strategy. Notably, lean AC samples could not be distinguished from obese AC samples by unsupervised clustering based on Class I DEGs, indicating limited transcriptomic separation between these groups. The comparative analyses of lean versus obese adipogenesis (Class III vs. Class IV DEGs) revealed distinct patterns of gene expression alterations associated with AC differentiation in each condition.

Our findings suggest a putative positive role for inflammatory genes in fat deposition, supported by altered gene expression profiles in purified human ACs and preACs from lean and obese adipose tissues. The comparison between our RNA-Seq results and the previously published microarray dataset (GSE80654) supports the robustness of our conclusions and highlights the influence of inflammatory pathways on adipocyte biology in the context of obesity.
