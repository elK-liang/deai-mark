Abstract

Gene therapy using adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors holds great promise for treating cardiovascular disease. However, efficient delivery and transduction of cardiac cells, particularly cardiomyocytes (CMs), remain a major challenge. Organotypic heart slice culture has emerged as a valuable ex vivo model that preserves the three-dimensional architecture, cellular composition, and extracellular matrix of native myocardium, making it an ideal platform for evaluating viral vectors for cardiac gene transfer. To date, a systematic comparison of different AAV serotypes for transduction efficiency in this model system has not been performed.

Myocardial slices of left ventricular tissue were prepared from transgenic homozygous αMHC-mCherry mice using a vibrating microtome and cultured under air-liquid interface conditions. To optimize the model, slice thickness (100–300 µm) was evaluated by mCherry fluorescence intensity and MTS metabolic activity assays. Slice viability under normoxic and hypoxic conditions was assessed by calcein-AM staining, and cardiac specificity of mCherry expression was confirmed by co-staining for cardiac troponin T (cTNT). Different AAV serotypes were then applied to the slices to compare their transduction efficiency in cardiac cells.

We established that 300-µm-thick slices provide the strongest mCherry signal and highest metabolic activity, while remaining easy to handle, and were therefore selected for all subsequent experiments. mCherry expression in αMHC-mCherry slices co-localized with cTNT, confirming cardiomyocyte-specific expression. Slices maintained viability for at least 5 days under normoxic culture conditions, although cardiomyocyte survival was reduced by approximately 25% after the first day in culture and remained stable thereafter. Exposure to hypoxia significantly reduced slice viability, establishing the system's capacity to model ischemic injury.

We have established and validated an organotypic heart slice culture system from αMHC-mCherry mice that preserves myocardial architecture and cardiomyocyte viability for at least 5 days under normoxic conditions and responds to hypoxic challenge. This model provides a robust ex vivo platform for the comparative evaluation of AAV serotypes for cardiac gene transfer.
