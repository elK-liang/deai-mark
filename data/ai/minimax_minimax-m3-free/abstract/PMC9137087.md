Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, and a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms that sustain the malignant phenotype of CRC cells is required to develop novel therapeutic strategies. Here, we identified the transcription factor AP4 encoded by the TFAP4 gene as a crucial suppressor of DNA damage, chromosomal instability and oncogene-induced senescence in CRC cells. AP4-deficient DLD-1 and SW480 cells displayed an enlarged and flattened morphology, increased senescence-associated β-galactosidase activity, reduced colony-forming capacity, elevated apoptosis and impaired proliferation and viability. Ectopic re-expression of AP4 largely reversed these phenotypes, confirming that the observed effects are specifically caused by loss of AP4 function. AP4-deficient cells accumulated high levels of endogenous DNA damage, as shown by increased γH2AX-positive foci, elevated γH2AX protein levels and longer comet tails, indicating that AP4 is required for efficient maintenance of genomic integrity. Mechanistically, AP4 induced the expression of MDC1 (Mediator of DNA Damage Checkpoint 1), a key mediator of the DNA damage response and homologous recombination repair, while repressing the long non-coding RNA MIR22HG and its derivative miR-22-3p, which would otherwise suppress MDC1. Through this dual regulation, AP4 ensures proper activation of DNA damage checkpoint signaling and homologous recombination-mediated repair, thereby limiting chromosomal instability and preventing premature senescence. Collectively, our data establish AP4 as a central node connecting transcriptional regulation of the DNA damage response with suppression of senescence in colorectal cancer cells, and highlight the AP4/MDC1/MIR22HG/miR-22-3p axis as a potential therapeutic target.
