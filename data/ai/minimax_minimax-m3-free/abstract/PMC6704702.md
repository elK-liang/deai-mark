Abstract

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a class of endogenous noncoding RNAs that have been increasingly recognized as important regulators in cancer progression. However, the expression pattern and functional role of circRNAs in gastric cancer metastasis remain poorly understood.

RNA-sequencing was performed to identify differentially expressed circRNAs between gastric cancer tissues with and without metastasis. The expression of circNHSL1 was validated in gastric cancer cell lines and 93 pairs of fresh frozen gastric cancer tissues with matched normal tissues by qRT-PCR. The circular nature of circNHSL1 was verified by Sanger sequencing, divergent and convergent primer amplification, genomic DNA (gDNA) analysis, and RNase R resistance assay. Subcellular localization was determined by nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation. The downstream miR-1306-3p/SIX1/vimentin axis was further investigated through loss-of-function assays and mechanistic studies.

RNA-sequencing identified 38 differentially expressed circRNAs (fold change > 2.0, p < 0.05), of which 37 were up-regulated and 1 was down-regulated in gastric cancer tissues with metastasis. circNHSL1 (hsa_circ_0006835), a 335-nt sense-overlapping circular transcript spliced from the NHSL1 gene, was the most highly expressed circRNA among the top 10 up-regulated candidates. Sanger sequencing confirmed the head-to-tail splicing of circNHSL1. circNHSL1 was expressed at higher levels in multiple gastric cancer cell lines than in GES-1 cells, with the highest expression observed in MKN-28 and AGS cells. circNHSL1 was detected only in cDNA but not in gDNA, and resisted RNase R digestion, confirming its circular structure. Subcellular fractionation revealed that circNHSL1 is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm. Functional and mechanistic studies demonstrated that circNHSL1 promotes gastric cancer progression through the miR-1306-3p/SIX1/vimentin axis.

circNHSL1 is a highly stable, cytoplasm-localized circular RNA that is up-regulated in gastric cancer and promotes tumor progression via the miR-1306-3p/SIX1/vimentin axis, suggesting its potential as a diagnostic or prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for gastric cancer.
