REST (RE1 silencing transcription factor) is a transcriptional repressor that plays complex roles in cancer, with loss of REST function implicated in tumor progression in multiple cancer types. However, the specific mechanisms by which REST loss contributes to breast cancer pathogenesis, particularly across different molecular subtypes, remain poorly understood. Methods: We analyzed gene expression profiling data from TCGA using FireBrowse to identify cancers with dysregulated REST target genes, with a focus on breast cancer subtypes including normal-like, invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC), and ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). We evaluated REST target gene expression patterns based on hormone receptor status, comparing ER+ versus ER- tumors and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) versus non-TNBC samples. Results: Our analysis revealed that several putative REST target genes, including PLK1, ADAM12, TNNT1, and CEMIP (KIAA1199), were highly dysregulated across cancers. In breast cancer, REST target genes showed differential regulation across molecular subtypes, with the more invasive IDC subtype exhibiting higher REST target gene expression. Notably, we identified distinct REST target gene subsets associated with hormone receptor status: ER- and TNBC tumors showed upregulation of VEGFa, CDKN2a, SFRP1, EGFR, MYC, LIF, MMP9, and CEMIP, while ER+ and non-TNBC tumors showed upregulation of ADAM12, COL1A2, IGF1, AR, MMP2, CAV1, SMAD2, and GATA3. Conclusion: These findings demonstrate that loss of REST in breast cancer leads to subtype-specific dysregulation of target genes involved in tumor progression, with distinct gene signatures in ER- versus ER+ tumors that may reflect differential hormone sensitivity. The identification of a RESTless gene signature could have important implications for predicting tumor behavior and guiding hormone-based treatment strategies in breast cancer patients, particularly those with ER- or TNBC subtypes where REST target gene dysregulation may promote more aggressive disease through pathways involving CEMIP and MMP-mediated processes.
