Gastric cancer (GC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, largely because early-stage disease is typically asymptomatic and lacks reliable, non-invasive screening tools. Salivary biomarkers have attracted growing interest as a convenient and cost-effective means of detecting systemic and local pathological changes. Cystatin B (CSTB) and Deleted in Malignant Brain Tumors 1 (DMBT1) are involved in protease inhibition, inflammation, and epithelial defense, but their combined diagnostic potential in saliva for GC has not been clearly established. Methods: In this case–control study, unstimulated whole saliva samples were collected from 31 patients with newly diagnosed GC and 31 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Salivary concentrations of CSTB and DMBT1 were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Demographic, clinical, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics were obtained through structured interviews and medical records, and group differences were compared using appropriate statistical tests. A machine learning approach was applied to evaluate the combined discriminatory performance of the two biomarkers together with selected clinical variables. Results: GC patients and healthy controls differed significantly in educational level, occupational stress, history of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), history of gastric ulcers, vegetable consumption, and salty taste preference (all p ≤ 0.04). Salivary CSTB levels were significantly lower in GC patients than in healthy controls (115.55 ± 7.06 vs. 128.30 ± 18.06 ng/mL; p = 0.001), whereas salivary DMBT1 levels were significantly higher in GC patients (171.88 ± 39.67 vs. 139.76 ± 39.05 ng/mL; p = 0.002). Conclusion: Salivary CSTB and DMBT1 levels are altered in patients with GC, with CSTB being downregulated and DMBT1 being upregulated relative to healthy controls. These findings suggest that the combined assessment of CSTB and DMBT1 in saliva, particularly when integrated with relevant clinical and lifestyle variables using machine learning algorithms, may serve as a promising non-invasive adjunct for the detection of gastric cancer. Further large-scale prospective studies are warranted to validate these results and to define clinically meaningful cut-off values.
