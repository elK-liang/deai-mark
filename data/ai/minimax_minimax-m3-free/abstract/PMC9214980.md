This study aimed to develop and validate models based on reassigned cut-off values of three tumor markers (TMs)—carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cytokeratin 19 fragment (CYFRA21-1) and neuron-specific enolase (NSE)—for lung cancer (LC) diagnosis and prognosis prediction. A total of three datasets were analyzed: Data 1 comprised patients meeting high-risk LC screening criteria, including 391 early-stage LC patients and 772 healthy individuals; Data 2 included 68 LC patients tested within one month before diagnosis and 208 benign lung disease (BLD) patients with at least two TM test records; Data 3 contained 4,351 LC patients with defined stage and 2,094 LC patients with undefined stage. We found that 50% of early-stage LC patients and 25% of advanced-stage LC patients had TM values below the current reference cut-offs, indicating limited discriminatory ability. After logarithmic transformation, CEA, CYFRA21-1 and NSE levels showed significant progressive increases from healthy individuals to early-stage and advanced-stage LC patients, with statistically significant differences among groups. Moreover, the three TMs exhibited heterogeneous distributions across LC histological subtypes—CEA was markedly elevated in lung adenocarcinoma, while CYFRA21-1 and NSE were significantly elevated in squamous cell carcinoma and small cell lung cancer, respectively. In LC patients, TM levels decreased after surgery and increased significantly at relapse or before death. Based on these findings, we constructed a prognostic model combining a composite cp-score derived from the reassigned TMs with age and tumor stage, which achieved favorable predictive performance for 12-month survival in both the training and test sets. These results suggest that reassigned TM-based models have potential value in improving LC screening and prognostic assessment.
