Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease whose molecular pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. In this study, we sought to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and key biological processes associated with TAA formation and progression. By comparing gene expression profiles between TAA and normal aortic tissues and applying a log2 fold-change threshold of |log2FC| > 0.5, we identified 85 downregulated and 171 upregulated genes in the TAA group. Enrichment analyses of Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathways revealed that the most significantly enriched biological processes included regulation of the immune effector process, cell–cell adhesion, immune cell proliferation, and immune receptor activity, indicating a central role for immune responses in TAA. KEGG pathway analysis further implicated Th17, Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, natural killer (NK) cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and the B cell receptor signaling pathway in disease pathogenesis, while also suggesting potential associations with bacterial or viral infections such as Staphylococcus aureus, tuberculosis, influenza A, and Epstein–Barr virus. Among the top 30 most differentially expressed genes, several immune- and chemotaxis-related genes were prominently upregulated, including CX3CR1, HLA-DPA1, HLA-DRA, CD74, CD53, FCGR3A, C3AR1, MS4A4A, EVI2B, HLA-DPB1, CYBB, C1QC, ALOX5AP, PTPRC, TLR7, CXCR4, and LCP1, whereas genes related to cell adhesion and axon guidance, such as SEMA3D, PCDH20, CDH8, CNTN1, DND1, PROM1, CNTN4, RYR2, SEMA3E, and SFRP4, were downregulated. Collectively, these findings suggest that chemotaxis and complement-related immune processes may contribute to the formation and progression of TAA, providing novel insights into its molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets.
