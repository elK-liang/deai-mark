Abstract

Acute lung injury (ALI) is a critical condition characterized by increased lung permeability, inflammatory cell infiltration, and excessive production of pro-inflammatory mediators. Microsomal prostaglandin E synthase-1 (mPGES-1) is a key enzyme in the synthesis of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) and has been implicated in inflammatory processes. The present study evaluated the therapeutic potential of the selective mPGES-1 inhibitor BI1029539 in murine models of ALI, in direct comparison with the cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor celecoxib.

ALI was induced in mice by intratracheal lipopolysaccharide (LPS) injection or by cecal ligation and puncture (CLP). Mice were treated with BI1029539, celecoxib, or vehicle. Lung permeability was assessed by bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid protein content and Evans blue dye extravasation, and pulmonary edema was evaluated by lung wet/dry ratio. Myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity was measured in lung tissue. Lung histopathology was scored, and expression of mPGES-1, COX-2, and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1) was examined by immunofluorescence. Inflammatory cell counts and levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), PGE2, and 6-keto prostaglandin F1α (a stable metabolite of prostacyclin, PGI2) were quantified in BAL fluid.

Intratracheal LPS administration caused marked increases in lung permeability, pulmonary edema, MPO activity, and inflammatory cell infiltration, together with elevated BAL concentrations of TNF-α, IL-1β, and PGE2, and increased parenchymal expression of mPGES-1, COX-2, and ICAM-1. Treatment with BI1029539 significantly attenuated each of these LPS-induced alterations, with reductions in total BAL cellular infiltrates, neutrophils, and macrophages of 80.7%, 93.6%, and 67%, respectively. Celecoxib produced comparable reductions in inflammatory cell influx (78.6%, 93.5%, and 74%, respectively) and similarly attenuated LPS-induced increases in lung permeability, MPO activity, edema, cytokine levels, and prostanoid/ICAM-1 expression. However, BAL levels of 6-keto prostaglandin F1α were significantly reduced only in celecoxib-treated mice. In the CLP model, BI1029539 also reduced lung damage and total BAL cell numbers.

Selective inhibition of mPGES-1 by BI1029539 ameliorates acute lung injury in mice to a degree comparable to that achieved with COX-2 inhibition by celecoxib, while sparing PGI2 production. These findings identify mPGES-1 as a promising therapeutic target in ALI.
