Abstract

Alkylation of RNA by methyl methanesulfonate (MMS) generates methylated ribonucleosides, including 1-methyladenosine (m1A) and 3-methylcytosine (m3C), which are repaired by the AlkB family of Fe(II)/2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases. ALKBH3 has been identified as a key enzyme for the removal of MMS-induced methyl lesions from mRNA, and its activity is supported by partner proteins. However, the molecular role of these ALKBH3 partners in mediating alkylation damage repair and clearance of methylated RNA bases from mRNA, as well as the dynamics of these modifications following MMS exposure, have not been characterized in human mRNA.

To investigate MMS-induced methylation in mRNA, HeLa cells were labeled for over 10 generations with a 1:1 mixture of non-labelled and deuterated (CD3) methionine, enabling distinction between endogenous (enzymatic) and MMS-induced (non-enzymatic) methylation based on CH3/CD3 ratios. Following treatment with a non-lethal dose of MMS (1 mM, 1 h), total RNA was extracted and mRNA was enriched by oligo(dT) capture. Levels of methylated ribonucleosides (m1A, m3C, m5C, m6A, and m7G) in total RNA and mRNA were quantified by LC–MS/MS. The role of the ALKBH3 partner ASCC3 in the repair process was examined, including its contribution to P-body formation and selective clearance of MMS-induced methylation from mRNA.

MMS treatment at a dose mediating ~90% relative survival induced a clear S-phase delay that resolved within 24 h. Mass spectrometric analysis revealed essentially equal CH3 and CD3 ratios for all five methylnucleosides in untreated cells, indicating steady-state endogenous methylation. After MMS treatment, CH3/CD3 ratios were markedly increased for m1A, m3C, and m7G in both total RNA and mRNA, whereas m5C and m6A ratios remained essentially unchanged, consistent with the known substrate specificity of MMS. The ALKBH3 partner ASCC3 was found to mediate P-body formation and to be required for the selective clearance of MMS-induced m1A and m3C from mRNA.

This study establishes that MMS induces quantifiable, non-enzymatic methylation at m1A, m3C, and m7G in human mRNA, and identifies ASCC3 as an essential component of the ALKBH3-dependent repair pathway that promotes P-body formation and selective removal of MMS-induced m1A and m3C lesions from mRNA. These findings advance our understanding of the cellular response to alkylation damage in mRNA.
