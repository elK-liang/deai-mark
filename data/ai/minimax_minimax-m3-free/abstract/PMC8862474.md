Abstract

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is characterized by a profoundly immunosuppressive microenvironment, in which T cell dysfunction plays a central role. BCL-2, an anti-apoptotic protein frequently overexpressed in CLL B cells, has been implicated in immune regulation, yet its role within the T cell compartment remains poorly defined.

We analyzed BCL-2 expression in peripheral T cells from 266 patients with CLL and healthy controls using flow cytometry. A subset of 110 patients was evaluated for BCL-2 expression specifically within CD4+ and CD8+ T cell subsets. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was performed on peripheral blood mononuclear cells from six newly diagnosed CLL patients to dissect the transcriptional landscape of BCL-2-positive versus BCL-2-negative T cells. Differential gene expression was assessed using the limma package, followed by KEGG pathway enrichment and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). Associations between BCL-2 expression in T cells and clinical characteristics were examined.

BCL-2 expression, assessed both as the proportion of positive cells and mean fluorescence intensity (MFI), was significantly elevated in total T cells, as well as in CD4+ and CD8+ T cell subsets, from patients with CLL compared with healthy controls. Cytobank-based comparative analysis confirmed higher BCL-2 expression in CLL-derived T cells. Higher BCL-2 expression in T cells positively correlated with serum β2-microglobulin (β2-MG) levels (χ² = 3.916, P < 0.05). scRNA-seq analysis revealed that BCL-2-positive T cells were transcriptionally enriched for gene signatures associated with cell–cell adhesion and T cell activation. GSEA demonstrated that BCL-2-positive T cells exhibited an increased Treg signature and a decreased Th1-type signature, with a greater proportion of Tregs among BCL-2-positive compared with BCL-2-negative T cells.

BCL-2 is overexpressed in T cells of patients with CLL and is associated with adverse clinical features such as elevated β2-MG. At the transcriptional level, BCL-2 expression in T cells is linked to enhanced regulatory T cell differentiation and reduced Th1 polarization, suggesting that BCL-2 contributes to the immunosuppressive T cell phenotype characteristic of CLL.
