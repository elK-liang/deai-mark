Abstract

Necroptosis is a regulated form of necrotic cell death that has been increasingly implicated in cancer biology, yet its comprehensive role in colorectal cancer (CRC) remains poorly defined. In this study, we sought to systematically characterize the genetic and transcriptional landscape of necroptosis-related genes (NRGs) in CRC and to leverage these features for molecular subtyping, tumor microenvironment characterization, and prognostic prediction.

We analyzed 33 NRGs across multiple CRC cohorts. Somatic mutations, copy number variations (CNVs), and differential expression of NRGs were examined in TCGA CRC samples. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to assess the discriminative capacity of NRGs between tumor and normal tissues. A necroptosis interaction network was constructed to explore gene–gene relationships and prognostic significance, with univariate Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier analyses used to identify prognostic NRGs. Consensus clustering was applied to stratify CRC patients into distinct necroptosis-based molecular subtypes, and the tumor microenvironment infiltration patterns were characterized across subtypes. Prognostic models were subsequently constructed and validated.

GO enrichment analysis confirmed that the 33 NRGs were predominantly involved in cell death and necroptosis-related biological processes. Genetic analysis revealed that 111 of 502 (22%) TCGA CRC samples harbored NRG alterations, with CASP8 showing the highest mutation rate (4%), while FADD, TRADD, TNF, and AURKA displayed no mutations. CNV analysis identified widespread copy number alterations, and the corresponding chromosomal locations were mapped. PCA based on NRG expression effectively separated CRC tumors from normal tissues. Genes with CNV amplification (e.g., MYC, FADD, AURKA, TRAF2, and PGAM5) were significantly upregulated in tumors, whereas TLR3, CHUK, RIPK1, FAS, NFKB1, and AXL were markedly downregulated, consistent with their CNV deletion patterns. Integration of five CRC cohorts (n = 1581) enabled construction of a necroptosis network, from which seven prognostic NRGs (TLR3, TLR4, BIRC2, TRAF2, CASP8, NFKB1, and TNFRSF10B) were identified. Consensus clustering based on these genes stratified CRC patients into distinct molecular subtypes with differing tumor microenvironment infiltration characteristics and clinical outcomes.

This study provides a comprehensive landscape of necroptosis-related genetic and transcriptional alterations in CRC. The seven-gene necroptosis-based classification captures distinct tumor microenvironment features and enables effective prognostic stratification, highlighting the potential of necroptosis as a candidate framework for CRC molecular subtyping and clinical risk prediction.
