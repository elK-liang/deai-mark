HER2-positive breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease with a complex tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) that evolves through treatment and metastatic progression. Understanding the immunologic differences between primary and metastatic lesions, particularly in non-liver sites such as brain and soft tissue, is critical for developing more tailored immunotherapeutic strategies. We employed the NanoString IO360 gene expression panel in conjunction with digital spatial profiling (DSP) to characterize the immune landscape of matched primary and metastatic HER2-positive breast tumors. Methods: Fifteen specimens from eight patients with HER2-positive breast cancer were analyzed, including 10 primary breast tumors, 2 soft tissue metastases, 2 brain metastases, and 1 lung metastasis. Eight patients were receiving cancer-directed therapy at the time of sample collection, while seven were not. Two patients contributed three matching timepoints, three patients contributed two matching timepoints, and three patients had a single timepoint. Bulk tumor gene expression analysis was performed to assess immune-oncology signatures, the 18-gene Tumor Inflammation Signature (TIS), PAM50 subtyping, and individual immune-related genes. Spatial proteomic profiling was used to evaluate immune cell populations and checkpoint molecule expression within the tumor and stromal compartments. Results: Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of IO360 signatures separated primary breast tumors from metastatic lesions, with 8 of 10 primary tumors exhibiting high TIS scores. The B cell abundance signature was significantly higher in primary compared to metastatic tumors, while several lymphocyte-related signatures—including those for T cells, CD8 T cells, PD-1, TIGIT, CTLA4, exhausted CD8, PD-L2, B7-H3, TH1, natural killer cells, and cytotoxic cells—showed a trend toward higher expression in primaries but did not reach significance with adjusted p-values. Individual genes associated with immune activation and trafficking were similarly enriched in primary lesions. Conclusions: The immune microenvironment of HER2-positive breast cancer differs between primary and metastatic sites, with primary tumors demonstrating a more inflamed, lymphocyte-rich profile. These findings underscore the importance of site-specific and time-resolved immune profiling to inform immunotherapy development in HER2-positive disease.
