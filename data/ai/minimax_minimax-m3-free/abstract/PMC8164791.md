Stroke remains a leading cause of mortality and long-term disability worldwide, with intracerebral hemorrhagic stroke representing a particularly severe subtype. While alcohol consumption is a well-established modifiable risk factor for hemorrhagic stroke, inter-individual variability in ethanol metabolism—influenced by genetic polymorphisms in alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) and aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH) enzymes—may modulate susceptibility. The ADH1B rs1229984 polymorphism, which encodes a faster-metabolizing enzyme subunit, has been linked to altered drinking behavior and alcohol-related disease risk; however, its contribution to stroke, particularly hemorrhagic stroke, remains incompletely characterized in Asian populations. Methods: We conducted a case-control study leveraging data linkage between the Taiwan Biobank (TWB) and the National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD), encompassing 19,500 participants. After applying quality-control criteria, 890 individuals with ischemic stroke, 70 with hemorrhagic stroke, and 16,837 controls were included. Genotyping for ADH1B rs1229984 and ALDH2 rs671 was performed, and alcohol consumption was self-reported. Logistic regression models were used to estimate associations between ADH1B rs1229984 genotype, alcohol intake, and stroke risk, adjusting for demographic, lifestyle, and clinical covariates including hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Results: Participants with ischemic stroke were older than controls (58.47 ± 8.17 vs. 48.33 ± 10.90 years, p < 0.0001), whereas those with hemorrhagic stroke did not differ significantly in age (50.54 ± 10.60 years, p = 0.0897). The prevalence of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, and alcohol intake differed significantly between stroke cases and controls. ADH1B rs1229984 genotype distributions were similar between cases and controls for both stroke subtypes. The ADH1B rs1229984 variant was not associated with ischemic stroke when stratified by alcohol intake. However, significant associations with diabetes, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia were observed among individuals carrying the rs1229984 TT and TC genotypes. Conclusion: In this Taiwanese population, ADH1B rs1229984 polymorphism alone did not demonstrate a significant association with either ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke, even when accounting for alcohol consumption. Nonetheless, the variant's interaction with established cerebrovascular risk factors—particularly hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes—suggests potential gene–environment interplay warranting further investigation in larger cohorts.
