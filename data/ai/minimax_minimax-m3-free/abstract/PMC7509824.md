Between February 28 and May 31, 2020, we analyzed 40,730 nasopharyngeal swabs collected in the Brescia area for the presence of SARS-CoV-2 RNA, and 11,344 (28.8%) tested positive. The majority of positive samples were identified in the first weeks of the epidemic, with 8,398 (62.0%) of the 13,547 swabs collected in March testing positive, while only 611 of 12,705 (4.8%) specimens analyzed in May were positive. Comparison of Ct values in randomly selected positive samples showed that viral RNA amounts were significantly higher in March than in May for all three molecular targets (E, RdRP and N genes) of the Seegene diagnostic assay, indicating that the two epidemic phases differed not only in the number of infected individuals but also in the viral load of positive specimens. Notably, one sample (GZ69) collected on May 8 from an asymptomatic subject during a systematic healthcare worker screening was strikingly different from all the other swabs analyzed in the late epidemic period, displaying very low Ct values (E gene Ct: 11.6; RdRP gene Ct: 13.9; N gene Ct: 15.5) that fell in the lower quartile of samples obtained during the early epidemic peak. This outlier sample was used to isolate a persistently replicating SARS-CoV-2 variant derived from an asymptomatic individual, suggesting that individuals without symptoms can harbor high viral loads and may act as reservoirs of viral variants capable of sustained replication, with potential implications for SARS-CoV-2 transmission, persistence and evolution.
