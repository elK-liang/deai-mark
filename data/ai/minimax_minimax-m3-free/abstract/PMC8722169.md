Abstract

Liver metastasis (LM) remains a major cause of treatment failure in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer (RC) undergoing neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NACRT) followed by surgery. Reliable pre-treatment predictors are needed to identify patients at high risk of LM. The apolipoprotein B to apolipoprotein A-I (apoB-to-apoAI) ratio has been associated with carcinogenesis and metastasis, but its prognostic value for LM in this setting is unclear.

Consecutive patients with untreated locally advanced RC who received NACRT followed by R0 resection between January 2007 and April 2016 were retrospectively analyzed. The apoB-to-apoAI ratio and other candidate variables were dichotomized using optimal cutoff values derived from receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Five-year liver metastasis-free survival (LMFS), overall survival (OS), and other metastasis-free survival (OMFS) were estimated. Variables significantly associated with outcomes in univariate analysis were entered into multivariate Cox regression models to identify independent predictors.

A total of 599 patients were eligible after exclusions, with a median follow-up of 71 (range, 10–162) months. During follow-up, 167 (27.9%) patients developed distant metastases, including 104 (17.4%) with liver metastases, and 130 (21.7%) patients died. ROC analysis yielded optimal cutoff values for LM prediction of 67 years for age, 1.22 g/L for apoAI, 1.06 g/L for apoB, 0.63 for the apoB-to-apoAI ratio, 6.1 ng/ml for CEA, and 26.3 U/ml for CA19-9. In univariate analysis, CA19-9 ≥ 26.3 U/ml, apoB-to-apoAI ratio ≥ 0.63, tumor regression grade (TRG) 5−3, pT4, and pN+ stage were associated with poorer 5-year LMFS. In multivariate analysis, the apoB-to-apoAI ratio emerged as an independent predictor of LM together with the established clinicopathological factors.

A pre-treatment apoB-to-apoAI ratio ≥ 0.63 is an independent predictor of liver metastasis in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer receiving NACRT plus surgery, and may help risk stratification and individualized treatment planning.
