LAGE3 (L Antigen Family Member 3) has been recently recognized as a candidate oncoprotein, however, its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains largely unknown. The current study aimed to clarify the clinical significance and biological function of LAGE3 in HCC. Methods: The expression patterns and prognostic value of LAGE3 in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) were analyzed using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) datasets and the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2 (GEPIA2) tool. LAGE3 expression in HCC cell lines and clinical tissue specimens was examined by qRT-PCR and immunohistochemistry (IHC), respectively. The oncogenic roles of LAGE3 were investigated in vitro through siRNA-mediated knockdown in SMCC-7721 and Huh-7 cell lines, and the underlying molecular mechanisms were explored using western blotting and transcriptomic analyses, with in vivo validation performed in xenograft models. Results: Analysis of TCGA and GEPIA2 datasets revealed that LAGE3 overexpression was significantly associated with poor overall survival (OS, p = 0.001) and disease-free survival (DFS, p = 0.019) in LIHC patients. LAGE3 mRNA was markedly upregulated in SMCC-7721, Huh-7, and BEL-7404 cell lines compared with other HCC cell lines and normal liver cells (p < 0.001), and IHC staining of tissue microarrays confirmed elevated LAGE3 protein expression in 79 HCC tissues relative to adjacent non-tumor and normal liver tissues. Functional studies demonstrated that LAGE3 knockdown significantly suppressed HCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and promoted apoptosis. Mechanistically, LAGE3 silencing inhibited the activation of both the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and Ras/RAF/MAPK signaling pathways, which are well-established drivers of tumor progression. Conclusion: Taken together, these findings identify LAGE3 as a potential prognostic biomarker and a promising therapeutic candidate for HCC, with mechanistic involvement of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and Ras/RAF/MAPK pathways in its tumor-promoting effects.
