Both lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] and fibrinogen (Fib) have been implicated in atherosclerotic and thrombotic processes, yet their combined prognostic value in stable coronary artery disease (CAD) remains incompletely defined. We evaluated the joint utility of baseline Lp(a) and Fib concentrations for risk stratification of cardiovascular events (CVEs) in a large prospective cohort of stable CAD patients. Methods: A total of 8,417 patients with stable CAD were consecutively enrolled and followed for a median of 37.1 months (interquartile range 22.5–55.4 months). Baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory data—including Lp(a), Fib, D-dimer, and a standard lipid panel—were collected, and medication use at admission and discharge was recorded. The primary outcome was a composite of all-cause death, non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), and stroke. Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for Lp(a) and Fib, individually and in combination, adjusting for established cardiovascular risk factors. Results: During follow-up, 395 CVEs occurred, comprising 160 deaths, 78 non-fatal MIs, and 157 strokes. Patients who experienced CVEs were significantly older (p < 0.001) and had a higher prevalence of hypertension (p = 0.005), diabetes mellitus (p < 0.001), and lower body mass index (BMI; p = 0.016). Lipid profiles (total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, apolipoprotein A1, apolipoprotein B) were comparable between groups (all p > 0.05), but Lp(a) levels were markedly elevated in the event group [19.24 (9.01–45.58) vs. 15.00 (6.66–36.26) mg/dL, p = 0.001]. Similarly, both Fib (3.35 ± 0.81 vs. 3.23 ± 0.78 g/L, p = 0.003) and D-dimer (0.55 ± 0.66 vs. 0.42 ± 0.62 µg/mL, p < 0.001) were higher in patients who suffered CVEs. Notably, statin use at admission was lower in the event group (68.3% vs. 75.8%, p = 0.005), although this difference disappeared at discharge. In multivariable analysis, higher Lp(a) and higher Fib were each independently associated with increased CVE risk, and their combination identified a subgroup of patients at substantially higher risk than either marker alone, suggesting an additive prognostic effect. Conclusion: In patients with stable CAD, elevated baseline levels of both Lp(a) and fibrinogen are independently associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes and, when considered together, provide incremental prognostic information beyond conventional risk factors and lipid profiles, supporting their combined use for risk stratification in this population.
