Glioblastoma (GBM) remains the most aggressive and lethal primary brain tumor, with limited therapeutic options and a median survival of only 12–15 months. IKBKE has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in GBM due to its frequent overexpression and oncogenic role in promoting tumor progression. In this study, we investigated the antitumor effects of CYT387, a potent IKBKE inhibitor, in human glioblastoma cells and explored the underlying molecular mechanism. Two IKBKE-high glioblastoma cell lines, U87-MG and LN-229, were treated with CYT387, and cytotoxicity was evaluated using CCK-8 assays, which showed time- and dose-dependent inhibition of cell viability, with 72 h IC50 values of 6.395 ± 1.127 μM for U87-MG cells and 5.139 ± 0.501 μM for LN-229 cells. Long-term proliferation assays and colony formation assays further demonstrated that CYT387 significantly suppressed glioblastoma cell proliferation and colony-forming ability compared with blank control and DMSO groups (P < 0.001). In addition, wound healing assays revealed that CYT387 markedly inhibited tumor cell migration. Mechanistically, CYT387 treatment activated the Hippo signaling pathway, a critical tumor-suppressive cascade that regulates cell growth and invasion. Collectively, our findings demonstrate that CYT387 suppresses glioblastoma progression by inhibiting IKBKE and reactivating the Hippo pathway, suggesting that CYT387 may represent a promising candidate for the treatment of glioblastoma.
