Abstract

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a major global health burden with a highly variable clinical course. Accurate risk stratification of CKD progression is essential for guiding clinical decision-making and patient counseling. This study aimed to develop and validate an easy-to-operate, web-based calculator for predicting CKD progression.

A total of 1,045 patients with CKD were included in the analysis after excluding 93 patients with missing data. Patients were randomly divided into a training set (N = 696) and a validation set (N = 349) at a 6:3 ratio. Clinical and demographic variables were analyzed using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses to identify independent prognostic factors for CKD progression, defined as > 50% estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) loss or initiation of dialysis. Variable selection was guided by the Akaike information criterion (AIC). A predictive nomogram was constructed based on the selected factors and deployed as a web-based calculator (https://ncutool.shinyapps.io/CKDprogression/). Model performance was evaluated in both training and validation sets.

The mean age of the overall cohort was 67.31 ± 13.6 years, and 69.9% of participants were male. The majority of patients had proteinuria and a history of hypertension. Disease progression occurred in 260 patients across the entire study cohort. Univariate Cox regression analysis identified multiple variables associated with CKD progression, and multivariate analysis confirmed that etiology, hemoglobin level, creatinine level, proteinuria, and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR) were independent prognostic factors. These five factors were used to construct the predictive nomogram. Based on the median risk score, patients were stratified into low- and high-risk groups, which showed significantly different progression-free survival in the training set (p < 0.001).

A prognostic nomogram incorporating etiology, hemoglobin, creatinine, proteinuria, and UPCR effectively stratifies CKD patients according to their risk of disease progression. The corresponding web-based calculator provides a practical, user-friendly tool to facilitate individualized risk prediction and may support clinicians in optimizing the management of CKD patients.
