Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) is a highly heterogeneous malignancy with distinct etiological subtypes, yet comparative data on recurrence and prognosis following radical resection across these subtypes remain limited. This multi-institutional investigation aimed to characterize recurrence patterns and survival outcomes in ICC patients stratified by hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, hepatolithiasis, and no identifiable cause (Con-ICC). Methods: A total of 448 patients who underwent radical resection for histologically confirmed ICC between 2010 and 2018 were retrospectively analyzed, including 261 (58.2%) with Con-ICC, 102 (22.8%) with HBV-associated ICC, and 85 (19.0%) with stone-associated ICC. Clinicopathological characteristics, recurrence patterns, and prognostic outcomes were compared across the three etiological subtypes. Results: The three subtypes showed significant differences in sex distribution, age, presence of obstructive jaundice, CA19-9 levels, Child–Pugh grade, tumor location, morphologic type, perineural invasion, liver capsule involvement, and AJCC 8th edition N stage (all P < 0.05). Notably, stone-associated ICC demonstrated a higher proportion of CA19-9 > 39.0 U/ml, perineural invasion, and periductal infiltrating and intraductal growth patterns compared with HBV-associated ICC (all P < 0.05). Conclusions: ICC patients with different etiological backgrounds—particularly those associated with hepatolithiasis—exhibit distinct clinicopathological features following radical resection, underscoring the importance of etiological classification in prognostic stratification and individualized management of ICC.
