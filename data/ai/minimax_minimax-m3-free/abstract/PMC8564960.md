Gallbladder cancer (GBC) is a highly aggressive malignancy of the biliary tract, and circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of tumorigenesis. However, the role and underlying molecular mechanisms of circSMAD2 in GBC remain largely unclear. Methods: The expression of circSMAD2 in GBC tissues and adjacent normal tissues, as well as in GBC cell lines (GBS-SD and G415) and the normal gallbladder epithelial cell line HIBEpic, was detected by RT-qPCR. CircSMAD2 was knocked down in GBC cells using three circSMAD2-specific siRNAs, and the most efficient siRNA (si-circSMAD2-3) was selected for subsequent experiments. Cell viability, apoptosis, migration, and invasion were assessed by CCK-8 assay, flow cytometry, and transwell assays, respectively. RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) was performed to investigate the interaction between circSMAD2 and eIF4A3. Results: CircSMAD2 expression was significantly upregulated in GBC tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues, and was also markedly higher in GBS-SD and G415 cells than in HIBEpic cells, although its expression level did not correlate with patient sex, age, or TNM stage. Knockdown of circSMAD2 significantly inhibited cell viability, induced apoptosis, and suppressed the migration and invasion of GBC cells. Furthermore, RIP assays revealed an interaction between circSMAD2 and eIF4A3. Conclusions: Knockdown of circSMAD2 inhibits the tumorigenesis of gallbladder cancer, at least in part through binding with eIF4A3, suggesting that circSMAD2 may serve as a potential therapeutic target for GBC.
