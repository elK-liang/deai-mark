Monocytes are innate immune cells that play a pivotal role in inflammation and tissue repair, and their recruitment into hypoxic tissue regions has been associated with a shift toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype. In this study, we investigated the directed migration of human naïve monocytes in response to oxygen gradients using a commercially available chemotaxis chamber combined with an enzymatic hypoxia model. The addition of glucose oxidase and catalase to the chamber rapidly generated stable oxygen gradients within approximately 15 minutes, exposing cells in the observation area to oxygen levels ranging from 10 to 80 mmHg. Live-cell tracking over 5 hours revealed a statistically significant directed migration of monocytes toward lower oxygen concentrations (p < 0.05), whereas normoxic conditions and gradients of heat-inactivated enzymes produced erratic, non-directed movement (p > 0.05), confirming that the oxygen gradient itself rather than the enzyme components drives monocyte migration. Hypoxic conditions also attenuated cytokine release from monocytes, and further analysis identified CCL26 as a key mediator linking oxygen sensing to this attenuated inflammatory response. Together, these findings indicate that hypoxia actively directs monocyte migration and modulates their secretory profile, with CCL26 emerging as a central player in this process and a potential therapeutic target in hypoxia-associated inflammatory diseases.
