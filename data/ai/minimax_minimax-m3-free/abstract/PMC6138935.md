Emerging evidence indicates that the androgen receptor (AR) plays a critical role in oestrogen receptor (ER)-negative breast cancer (BC), yet the underlying molecular mechanisms remain poorly understood. The prostate-derived Ets factor (PDEF) has been implicated in tumour progression, but its function in ER-negative BC has not been elucidated. Here, we investigated the clinicopathological significance of PDEF and AR/PDEF co-expression in ER-negative BC and explored a novel AR–PDEF signalling axis that drives tumour proliferation through MYC-mediated transcription. Methods: PDEF and AR expression were examined by immunohistochemistry in 100 ER-negative BC tissues, and their associations with clinicopathological parameters and patient survival were analysed. PDEF protein levels in tumour and adjacent normal tissues were compared by western blotting. Functional and mechanistic studies were performed to characterise the AR–PDEF pathway and its regulation of MYC transcriptional activity. Results: Of the 100 ER-negative BC samples, 60 (60%) showed positive nuclear PDEF expression (PDEF+) and 69 (69%) showed positive nuclear AR expression (AR+), with 55 (55%) cases co-expressing both AR and PDEF. PDEF expression was significantly associated with tumour grade (P = 0.032), pTNM stage (P = 0.011), lymphatic metastasis (P < 0.001) and AR expression (P < 0.001), whereas AR+PDEF+ co-expression was associated with pTNM stage (P = 0.047) and HER2 expression (P = 0.039). PDEF protein levels were higher in ER-negative BC tissues than in matched adjacent normal tissues. Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated that high PDEF expression was associated with poor overall survival (OS; P = 0.040) and relapse-free survival (RFS; P = 0.031), and that AR and PDEF co-expression was similarly associated with poor OS (P = 0.043) and RFS (P = 0.027). Mechanistically, AR cooperated with PDEF to promote tumour proliferation and upregulate MYC-mediated gene transcription by facilitating the degradation of the MYC transcriptional repressor MAD1. Conclusions: Our findings reveal that the AR–PDEF pathway is clinically and mechanistically important in ER-negative breast cancer. The high frequency of AR/PDEF co-expression and its association with aggressive clinicopathological characteristics and poor patient outcomes identify the AR–PDEF–MYC axis as a potential therapeutic target in ER-negative BC.
