Abstract

Fatigue is one of the most prevalent and distressing symptoms reported by breast cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. Its etiology remains poorly understood, with clinical, biological, and genetic factors all hypothesized to contribute. This study aimed to explore the associations between fatigue and a range of clinical, biological, sleep-related, mental, and genetic factors in breast cancer patients receiving chemotherapy.

A cross-sectional study was conducted including 67 women with breast cancer (mean age = 56.22 ± 11.96 years; mean body surface area = 1.76 ± 0.17 m²). Fatigue was assessed using the EORTC QLQ-C30 fatigue subscale and analyzed both as a continuous score and as a dichotomized variable (clinically significant fatigue vs. not). Bivariate analyses examined associations between fatigue and clinical characteristics, biological parameters (hemoglobin, leukocytes, platelets), pain, sleep and mental scales, and candidate genetic polymorphisms (including DRD2 and CLOCK). Variables with p < 0.20 in bivariate analyses were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, with the dichotomized fatigue score as the dependent variable.

Nearly 46% of patients exhibited clinically significant fatigue, with a mean fatigue score of 42.12 ± 32.10. In bivariate analyses, patients with metastases—particularly bone metastases—had significantly higher fatigue scores (80.00 ± 18.26 vs. 38.70 ± 31.39 in those without metastases). Patients receiving palliative chemotherapy and those treated with a capecitabine-based regimen also reported higher fatigue. Lower hemoglobin, leukocyte, and platelet counts, as well as a higher chemotherapy cycle number, were significantly associated with higher fatigue scores, whereas pain was not (p = 0.124). Neither sleep and mental scales nor genetic factors were significantly associated with fatigue in bivariate analyses. In the multivariable model, a higher cycle number (adjusted OR = 1.51) and a lower hemoglobin level (adjusted OR = 0.67) were independently associated with higher odds of clinically significant fatigue. Regarding genetic factors, carrying at least one C allele for the DRD2 single-nucleotide polymorphism (CC and CT genotypes) was significantly associated with 4.09-fold higher odds of fatigue compared with TT carriers (p = 0.047). The CLOCK SNP showed a trend toward significance, with TT carriers exhibiting lower odds of fatigue than TC carriers.

Fatigue was highly prevalent in this sample of breast cancer patients receiving chemotherapy. Disease burden (metastases, palliative treatment setting), hematological parameters (particularly lower hemoglobin), treatment intensity (higher cycle number), and the DRD2 polymorphism emerged as the main correlates of fatigue. These findings highlight the multifactorial nature of fatigue in breast cancer and suggest that both clinical and biological factors, along with selected genetic variants, should be considered in its assessment and management.
