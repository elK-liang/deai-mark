Patients with cancer have been prioritized for SARS-CoV-2 screening and containment measures, yet most studies have focused on symptomatic COVID-19, leaving the burden and immunologic features of apyretic SARS-CoV-2 infection in this population poorly characterized. Methods: Between April 14th and June 23rd 2020, 466 patients with solid tumors accessing the U01H and H04 units of the Ospedale Policlinico San Martino Cancer Center were screened, and 446 underwent an initial serological assessment for SARS-CoV-2. After exclusion of patients with pre-enrollment COVID-19 suggestive symptoms, hematologic malignancies, prior nasopharyngeal swab-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 detection, or exclusive domiciliary antineoplastic therapy, the remaining cohort underwent longitudinal serological follow-up. Demographic and tumor characteristics were recorded, and patients with detectable SARS-CoV-2 immunoglobulins were further characterized. Results: The recruited population comprised 146 patients with breast cancer, 103 with colorectal cancer, 42 with prostate cancer, 28 with pancreatic cancer, 25 with ovarian cancer, 18 with renal cancer, 17 with GIST, 14 with gastric cancer, 12 with hepatobiliary cancer, 11 with lung cancer, 8 with sarcoma, 5 with endometrial cancer, 4 with cervical cancer, 3 with esophageal cancer, 3 with urothelial cancer, and 7 with other neoplasms, all diagnosed before January 2020. Patient age ranged between 27 and 90 years, with the majority aged between 50 and 80 years. Longitudinal serological testing was available for at least two determinations in 397 patients, at least three in 312, and four in 122. A total of 14 patients (3.14%) tested positive for at least one SARS-CoV-2 immunoglobulin during the observation period extending through August 23rd 2020, and their serological profiles, together with available nasopharyngeal swab results, are detailed. Conclusions: Apyretic SARS-CoV-2 infection, as captured by longitudinal serology, occurred in a small but non-negligible fraction of solid tumor patients followed during the first wave of the pandemic, underscoring the value of serological surveillance in this vulnerable population and providing a basis for subsequent immunomic characterization of the antibody response.
