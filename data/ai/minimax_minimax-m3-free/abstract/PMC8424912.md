Abstract

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as important regulators in cancer biology, yet their roles in metabolic reprogramming of colorectal cancer (CRC) remain poorly understood. In this study, we identified a novel circRNA, circMYH9 (hsa_circ_0092283), derived from the MYH9 gene, and investigated its functional significance in CRC.

Differentially expressed circRNAs were screened by intersecting circRNA expression profiles from a GEO dataset (GSE126095, 10 paired CRC/adjacent tissues) with microarray data from 3 paired CRC tissues. The circular nature of circMYH9 was validated by divergent primer amplification, RNase R resistance assay, and Sanger sequencing. circMYH9 expression in CRC tissues and cell lines was assessed by qRT-PCR and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Clinicopathological correlations were examined in 148 CRC cases, and prognostic significance was evaluated by five-year follow-up data. Loss-of-function and gain-of-function studies, along with mechanistic investigations, were performed to elucidate the role of circMYH9 in regulating serine metabolism and redox homeostasis in a p53-dependent manner.

Nine overlapping upregulated circRNAs were identified, among which circMYH9 showed the highest upregulation (logFC = 3.63 in 10 paired tissues; FC = 4.51 in 3 paired tissues). circMYH9 was confirmed as a bona fide circRNA and was significantly overexpressed in CRC tissues compared with peritumoral tissues. High circMYH9 expression was significantly associated with tumor size, distant metastasis, lymph node metastasis, advanced TNM stage, and p53 status, and correlated with poorer patient prognosis. Functional studies demonstrated that circMYH9 drives CRC growth by regulating serine metabolism and redox homeostasis in a p53-dependent manner.

circMYH9 is a stably expressed circRNA that is upregulated in CRC and associated with adverse clinicopathological features and poor prognosis. It promotes colorectal cancer growth by modulating serine metabolism and redox homeostasis through a p53-dependent mechanism, highlighting its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in CRC.
