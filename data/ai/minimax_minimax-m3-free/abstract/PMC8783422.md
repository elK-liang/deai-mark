Histological response to preoperative chemotherapy is considered a prognostic factor in osteosarcoma, but its significance in soft tissue sarcoma (STS) remains unclear, partly due to the lack of standardized evaluation methods. The JCOG0304 trial investigated the efficacy of preoperative chemotherapy with doxorubicin and ifosfamide for high-grade non-round cell STSs. We aimed to standardize the histological evaluation method for assessing response to preoperative chemotherapy and to clarify its prognostic significance using specimens from this trial. Methods: Patients enrolled in the JCOG0304 trial who underwent preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgical resection were included. Histological response was evaluated by central review using a standardized method that distinguished viable tumor area, necrotic area with irreversible nuclear changes, and degenerative area with stromal fibrosis, hyalinization, or cystic change. The percentage of viable tumor area was calculated for each resected specimen, and its association with survival outcomes was analyzed. Results: Of the 72 patients enrolled in the JCOG0304 trial, 64 eligible patients were included in this accompanying study. The cohort comprised 30 males and 34 females with a median age of 47.5 years (range 21–66). The most frequent tumor location was the thigh (n = 32), and the most common histological subtypes were undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (n = 20), synovial sarcoma (n = 17), and leiomyosarcoma (n = 12). The median tumor size was 7.4 cm. In all 64 assessed cases, the percentage of viable tumor area ranged from 0.1% to 97.0%, with a median value of 35.7%. Using this standardized three-component classification, histological evaluation was feasible across diverse STS subtypes. Conclusions: We established a standardized histological evaluation method for response to preoperative chemotherapy in high-grade non-round cell STSs and demonstrated its applicability in a multicenter trial cohort. The wide range of viable tumor proportions observed underscores the need for such standardization, and further analysis of this cohort will clarify the prognostic significance of histological response in STS.
