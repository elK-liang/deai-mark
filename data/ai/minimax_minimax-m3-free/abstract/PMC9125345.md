Abstract

Tumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLNs) are considered a potential source of tumor-reactive T cells for adoptive cell therapy, particularly in patients with limited tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) availability. However, whether non-metastatic lymph nodes harbor tumor-reactive T cells and whether these cells can be enriched by stimulation with autologous tumor cells remain unclear, especially in colorectal cancer (CRC).

Autologous tumor-cell lines were established from colorectal tumor tissues of 7 CRC patients, and paired TDLNs were collected. For 3 cases (C126, C149, and C152), lymphocytes were isolated from both non-metastatic and metastatic lymph nodes and evaluated for tumor reactivity by IFNγ ELISPOT assay after stimulation with autologous tumor cells. For an additional 4 cases (C165, C177, C207, and C215), lymphocytes from non-metastatic lymph nodes were stimulated with autologous tumor cells either immediately after preparing single-cell suspensions or after a 2-week co-culture with autologous tumor cells. Tumor-reactive T cells were assessed by IFNγ ELISPOT assay before ("Pre") and after ("Post") co-culture, and the phenotype of expanded T cells was analyzed.

Tumor-reactive T cells were detected in lymphocytes from non-metastatic lymph nodes of one case (C126), while tumor-reactive T cells were detected in lymphocytes from metastatic lymph nodes of the remaining two cases (C149 and C152). Among the 4 cases in which non-metastatic lymph node lymphocytes were evaluated, tumor-reactive T cells were detected prior to co-culture only in case C207, a patient diagnosed with Lynch syndrome. After 2 weeks of co-culture with autologous tumor cells, IFNγ ELISPOT–positive tumor-reactive T cells were detected in all 4 cases. CD8+ T cells were dominantly expanded in three cases (C165, C177, and C207), whereas CD4+ T cells were dominantly expanded in all four lymph nodes of case C215, a patient with two genetically independent primary tumors in the sigmoid colon and rectum.

Tumor-reactive T cells are present in non-metastatic TDLNs of CRC patients but are often too rare to be detected directly ex vivo. Co-culture of TDLN-derived lymphocytes with autologous tumor cells is essential to expand these rare tumor-reactive T cells, supporting the feasibility of using TDLNs as an alternative source of tumor-reactive lymphocytes for adoptive cell therapy in CRC.
