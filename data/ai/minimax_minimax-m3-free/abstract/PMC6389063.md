Abstract

Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, and early diagnosis is critical for improving patient outcomes. Exosomal long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as promising non-invasive biomarkers for cancer detection. HOTTIP, a well-characterized lncRNA, has been implicated in cancer progression; however, its diagnostic and prognostic value in the exosomal compartment of GC patients remains unclear.

Exosomal HOTTIP expression was initially examined in the culture medium of the GC SGC7901 cell line at different incubation time points. Serum exosomal HOTTIP levels were then measured by RT-qPCR in a cohort of 126 GC patients and 120 healthy controls. The stability of exosomal HOTTIP in serum was assessed following prolonged exposure to room temperature and multiple freeze-thaw cycles. The diagnostic performance of exosomal HOTTIP was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and compared with the conventional serum tumor markers CEA, CA 19-9, and CA 72-4. Associations between exosomal HOTTIP expression and clinicopathological features of GC patients were also analyzed.

Exosomal HOTTIP was detectable in the culture medium of SGC7901 cells, and its levels increased with prolonged incubation time. Serum exosomal HOTTIP levels were significantly upregulated in GC patients compared with healthy controls (P < 0.001), and the expression remained stable under harsh conditions. Exosomal HOTTIP expression was positively associated with invasion depth (P = 0.0298) and TNM stage (P < 0.001), but not with age, gender, tumor size, pathological differentiation, or lymph node metastasis. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for exosomal HOTTIP was 0.827, which was significantly higher than that of CEA (0.653), CA 19-9 (0.685), and CA 72-4 (0.639) (all P < 0.001).

Exosomal HOTTIP is a stable, non-invasive biomarker that outperforms conventional serum tumor markers (CEA, CA 19-9, and CA 72-4) in distinguishing GC patients from healthy individuals. Its expression levels are also associated with invasion depth and TNM stage, suggesting its potential utility as a novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for GC.
