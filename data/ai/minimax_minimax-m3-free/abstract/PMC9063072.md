FGFR3 fusions drive a subset of bladder cancers, and although FGFR inhibitors such as erdafitinib have shown clinical efficacy, the emergence of acquired resistance limits their long-term benefit. To investigate mechanisms of resistance to FGFR inhibition, we established BGJ398-resistant bladder cancer cell lines (SW780-RS/RD and RT4-RS/RD) by continuous culture in the presence of BGJ398. Resistant cells displayed cross-resistance to erdafitinib and the irreversible FGFR inhibitor TAS-120 (futibatinib), but retained sensitivity to the MEK inhibitor trametinib, indicating that resistance was FGFR-specific. Phospho-RTK profiling of parental and resistant cells revealed a consistent upregulation of phosphorylated ERBB3, with minimal changes in EGFR and ERBB2 phosphorylation. The increase in pERBB3 was confirmed by immunoblot in both resistant cell lines. These findings identify ERBB3 activation as a potential adaptive mechanism underlying acquired resistance to FGFR inhibition in FGFR3 fusion-driven bladder cancer and provide rationale for co-targeting FGFR3 and ERBB3 to overcome resistance and improve therapeutic efficacy.
