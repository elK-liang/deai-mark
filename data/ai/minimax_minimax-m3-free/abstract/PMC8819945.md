Abstract

Cisplatin-based chemotherapy is the first-line treatment for bladder cancer; however, the development of cisplatin resistance remains a major clinical challenge. Identifying the genetic determinants of cisplatin resistance is essential for developing strategies to overcome chemoresistance.

A genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 knockout screen using the GeCKO library (123,411 sgRNAs targeting 19,050 human genes) was performed in T24 bladder cancer cells, which exhibited the highest cisplatin IC50 (7.637 µM) among six bladder cancer cell lines tested. Surviving cells following cisplatin treatment were analyzed by high-throughput sequencing to identify enriched sgRNAs. Candidate genes were validated using individual sgRNAs and high-content screening (HCS) analysis. The fold change in sensitivity enhancement (FSE) was calculated to evaluate the effect of gene knockout on cisplatin sensitivity.

Genome-wide CRISPR screening identified 327 genes significantly altered under cisplatin treatment (46 genes on day 7 and 284 genes on day 14). GO analysis revealed enrichment in tissue development, organonitrogen compound metabolism, and ion transport, as well as associations with cancer stem cell-related processes. Validation of 21 top candidate genes identified that sgRNAs targeting HNRNPU, OCLM, PRKCDBP, and CCDC146 significantly decreased cisplatin resistance in T24 cells, with an FSE of 1.4.

Our findings identify HNRNPU and additional candidate genes as potential therapeutic targets to overcome cisplatin resistance in bladder cancer.
