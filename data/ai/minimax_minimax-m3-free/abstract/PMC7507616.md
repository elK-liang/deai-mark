Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is among the most lethal malignancies, and cancer stem cells (CSCs) have been implicated in its aggressive behavior and poor outcomes. Reliable CSC-related prognostic biomarkers are urgently needed to guide risk stratification and clinical decision-making. Methods: CSC-related genes were systematically identified using integrated transcriptomic and clinical data. Differentially expressed CSC-related genes were filtered through stringent criteria (adjusted p < 0.01 and |log2FC| > 2), followed by univariate Cox regression analysis across three independent PDAC cohorts to identify genes consistently associated with overall survival (OS). Protective genes (HR < 1) that were significantly upregulated in CSCs and risky genes (HR > 1) that were significantly downregulated in CSCs were excluded to enhance biological relevance. The overlapping survival-associated genes were combined into a prognostic signature, and a risk score was calculated as a weighted linear combination of gene expression values. The signature was then validated in multiple independent PDAC cohorts for its prognostic performance using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, calibration curves, and comparisons of risk scores between tumor and normal tissues as well as across PDAC subtypes. Results: A total of 334 CSC-related genes were initially identified, from which four genes—DCBLD2, GSDMD, PLOD2, and PMAIP1—were consistently associated with unfavorable prognosis across three independent datasets and constituted the prognostic signature. The risk score formula was: Risk score = (0.31113 × DCBLD2) + (0.293903 × GSDMD) + (0.514119 × PLOD2) + (0.19192 × PMAIP1). PDAC tissues exhibited significantly higher risk scores than adjacent normal tissues and other pancreatic cancer subtypes. In six independent PDAC cohorts, the signature robustly stratified patients into distinct prognostic groups, with higher risk categories corresponding to lower OS probability. Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival probabilities. Conclusions: We developed and validated a four-gene CSC-related prognostic signature that reliably predicts OS in PDAC patients, offering a promising tool for individualized risk stratification and potentially informing future therapeutic strategies targeting CSC-driven tumor progression.
