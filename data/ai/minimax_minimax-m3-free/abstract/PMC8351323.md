Gastric cancer (GC) is a highly prevalent and lethal malignancy worldwide, and the DNA damage response (DDR) plays a critical role in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. BCLAF1 (Bcl-2-associated transcription factor 1) has been implicated in DNA damage signaling, but its phosphorylation status and functional relevance in GC remain largely unexplored. This study aimed to identify dysregulated protein phosphorylation events in GC tissues using a modified label-free phosphoproteomic workflow and to investigate the role of BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation in DDR regulation and radiosensitivity. Methods: A label-free phosphoproteomic approach combining deep fractionation, high-resolution LC–MS/MS, and the multi-blind spectral alignment algorithms MODa and Byonic was applied to paired tumor and para-cancerous tissues from GC patients. Phosphorylation sites with significant differential abundance were identified, followed by functional enrichment and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to characterize associated biological pathways. Candidate phosphorylation sites were validated, and the functional consequences of BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation were examined through in vitro and in vivo assays evaluating DDR signaling and cellular responses to ionizing radiation. Results: Phosphoproteomic profiling identified 4243 phosphorylation events corresponding to 832 unique phosphorylated sites across 382 proteins, with 25 sites upregulated and 52 sites downregulated in GC tissues compared with adjacent non-tumor tissues (FC > 2, p < 0.05). Bioinformatic analyses revealed that upregulated phosphorylations were predominantly associated with nucleotide, DNA, and RNA interactions and DDR-related pathways, whereas downregulated phosphorylations were linked to regulation of muscle contraction. GSEA further confirmed enrichment of DNA replication (p < 0.0001) and cell cycle (p < 0.0001) pathways among differentially expressed proteins. Notably, BCLAF1 was identified as the most prominently upregulated phosphoprotein, and its Ser290 phosphorylation was validated as a key event associated with DDR activation in GC. Functional studies demonstrated that BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation modulates DNA damage signaling and critically regulates radiosensitivity in gastric cancer cells. Conclusions: This study establishes a rapid label-free phosphoproteomic workflow for profiling phosphorylation dysregulation in GC and identifies BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation as a novel regulatory event in the DNA damage response. These findings provide new mechanistic insights into GC pathogenesis and suggest BCLAF1 Ser290 phosphorylation as a potential target for enhancing radiotherapy efficacy in gastric cancer.
