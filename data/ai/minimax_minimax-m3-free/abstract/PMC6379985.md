Bladder carcinoma (BC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide; however, the functional roles and clinical implications of circular RNAs (circRNAs) in BC remain largely unknown. In this study, we analyzed circRNA expression profiles in three pairs of BC and matched non-tumor bladder tissues using a circRNA microarray and identified cTFRC as a consistently and significantly up-regulated circRNA in BC tumor tissues. This finding was validated by quantitative RT-PCR (qPCR) in an expanded cohort of 57 BC patients, confirming that cTFRC expression was elevated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues. Further analysis in a cohort of 220 BC patients with recurrence data demonstrated that cTFRC expression was significantly increased in recurrent BC tissues relative to primary BC tissues, and that higher cTFRC expression was significantly associated with higher tumor grade, advanced T stage, and lymphatic metastasis. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that BC patients with higher cTFRC expression had poorer overall survival. Collectively, these results indicate that the up-regulation of cTFRC is correlated with aggressive clinicopathological features and poor prognosis in BC patients, suggesting that cTFRC may serve as a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in bladder carcinoma.
