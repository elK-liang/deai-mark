Abstract

Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) are rare in patients with localized, non-metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and stable models that permit their expansion and molecular characterization are lacking. Patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models established from primary tumor tissue can be used to generate CTCs in vivo, but whether these CTCs retain the phenotypic features of patient CTCs and can themselves give rise to stable, transplantable tumors has not been determined.

Primary tumor tissues from non-metastatic NSCLC patients were implanted subcutaneously into NOD scid gamma (NSG) mice to generate patient-tumor PDX (ptPDX) models. CTCs isolated from ptPDX mouse blood were characterized by immunostaining and compared with patient-matched CTCs. ptPDX-derived CTCs were reinjected into naïve NSG mice to establish CTC-derived xenograft (CDX) models. Single nuclear RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) was performed on matched ptPDX and CDX tumors to profile their transcriptomic landscapes at single-cell resolution.

Ten ptPDX models were successfully generated, and their CTCs phenotypically matched patient CTCs. ptPDX-derived CTCs developed into stable CDX tumors in 2/10 (20%) patients (MU150 and MU197), and high numbers of CTC clusters in ptPDX blood were associated with successful CDX establishment. Pathology-reported biomarker expression was conserved across patient primary tumors and matched ptPDX and CDX tumors. snRNA-seq identified nine cell type clusters across all samples, with one ptPDX (MU197) showing ten clusters; most clusters were of epithelial origin, consistent with xenograft tumors. CDX tumors uniquely contained two AT2-like epithelial populations, whereas matched ptPDX tumors contained only one, a finding confirmed by SFTPB feature plots and AT2 canonical marker expression.

ptPDX-derived CDX models provide a feasible platform for stable ex vivo expansion of CTCs from non-metastatic NSCLC patients. These models recapitulate patient tumor biomarker expression and reveal distinct single-cell heterogeneity, including the emergence of additional AT2-like populations in CDX tumors. This CTC expansion approach may serve as a valuable liquid biopsy-derived tool for molecularly studying and predicting the risk of recurrence and metastasis after curative resection of localized NSCLC.
