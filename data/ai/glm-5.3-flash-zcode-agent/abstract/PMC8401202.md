Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is diagnosed more often in women than in men, and plasma lipid metabolism may differ by both sex and disease status. We analyzed plasma lipid profiles of ME/CFS patients and controls of similar age and body mass index, including 40 females and 38 males among Whites, 1 female and 6 males among Asians, and 9 females and 6 males among African Americans (both non-Caucasian groups), with all analyses stratified by sex. Relative to healthy males, healthy females showed a significant increase in total phosphatidylcholine (PC) content (p < 0.05). Among males, total hexosylceramide (HexCer) levels were significantly increased in ME/CFS patients compared with controls (p < 0.05), whereas among females, ME/CFS patients had lower levels of phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and HexCer compared with controls (p < 0.05). Examination of lipid unsaturation status showed no differences in female ME/CFS compared with female controls, but among males, PE-MUFA, PI-MUFA and TG-SFA were significantly higher in ME/CFS patients than in controls (p < 0.05). Profiling of arachidonic acid (AA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)-containing lipids showed that AA- and DHA-containing PC and CE were elevated whereas CAR-AA was decreased in healthy females compared with healthy males (p < 0.01); PE-AA were decreased in female ME/CFS patients compared with controls (p < 0.01), and CE-AA levels were elevated in female compared with male ME/CFS patients. These sex-specific plasma lipid alterations are associated with pain, fatigue and cognitive symptoms in ME/CFS and underscore the importance of sex-stratified analyses in biomarker discovery for this disease.
