Abstract: The accumulation of senescent cells contributes to aging, and immune-mediated clearance of these cells has emerged as a potential anti-aging strategy. We evaluated the effect of adoptive natural killer (NK) cell infusion on senescent cells in humans and in aged mice. Twenty-six volunteers who received NK cell infusion were recruited, with blood collected approximately 37 (25-57) days after transfusion. The proportion and absolute number of NK cells in peripheral blood were inversely related to the age of the volunteers, as was the purity of the NK cell product reinfused into each individual. Compared with the senescence markers and senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) factors of peripheral blood CD3+ T cells before autologous NK cell infusion, the proportion of SA-β-gal-positive T cells was significantly decreased after infusion, and the mRNA levels of P16, P21 and plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (Pai1) were significantly decreased, whereas changes in monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (mcp-1) and IL-6 were not significant; no significant differences were observed in peripheral blood biochemical indexes. In adipose tissue from three obese individuals, perforin and CD69 expression in NK cells was significantly upregulated after co-incubation with senescent adipose tissue, NK cell treatment significantly decreased the expression of p16 and p21 in senescent adipose tissue, and key SASP components in the conditioned medium were significantly reduced. In aged mice, combining adoptive NK cell infusion with a dopamine-releasing peptide reduced senescent cells, supporting NK cell-based approaches for eliminating senescent cells during aging.
