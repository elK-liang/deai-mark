We evaluated the role of the deubiquitinase USP15 in human cancers and in lung cancer progression. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas data across 33 human cancer types showed that USP15 was significantly downregulated in five cancer types, including lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma, skin cutaneous melanoma and testicular germ cell tumors, whereas no significant change was observed in the other 28 cancer types. USP15 expression was markedly lower in lung cancer than in normal controls (27.29 vs 64.76), and the survival of combined LUAD and LUSC patients decreased with low USP15 expression, indicating a role in lung cancer patient survival. In a validation cohort of 41 primary non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients, including 25 LUAD, 8 LUSC and 8 other lung cancers, USP15 was significantly downregulated in LUAD. To explore its functional role, USP15-knockout human lung adenocarcinoma A549 and USP15-knockout H1299 cells were generated using CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing. Loss of USP15 promoted lung cancer cell invasion and progression, and mechanistically USP15 negatively regulates lung cancer progression through the TRAF6-BECN1 signaling axis for autophagy induction. These findings identify USP15 as a downregulated, tumor-suppressive deubiquitinase in lung adenocarcinoma and suggest that the USP15-TRAF6-BECN1 autophagy axis may offer therapeutic opportunities for NSCLC.
