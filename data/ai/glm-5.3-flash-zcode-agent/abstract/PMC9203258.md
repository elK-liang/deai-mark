We performed global lipidomic profiling to characterize the host cell lipid response to SARS-CoV-2 infection. Two cell lines were analyzed 24 hours after infection in biological quintuplicate: HEK293T cells overexpressing ACE2 and the more physiologically relevant A549-ACE2 cells. Total cellular lipids were extracted using the Bligh-Dyer method and analyzed by liquid chromatography electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS), with abundances normalized to internal standards. In HEK293T-ACE2 cells, 514 unique lipids spanning the glycerolipid, phospholipid, sphingolipid and acylcarnitine categories were identified, of which 409 (79.6%) were statistically altered between SARS-CoV-2 and mock infection (Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05, ANOVA), changing between 2- and 64-fold in response to infection. In A549-ACE2 cells, 443 unique lipids spanning the same categories were identified, and 227 were statistically altered, changing between 1.1- and 19.7-fold. Principal component analysis confirmed that infection status accounted for most of the observed changes, with the five infected and five mock samples falling into two distinct groups in both cell lines. The resulting global lipid map defines the extent and specificity of SARS-CoV-2-induced lipid remodeling across distinct host cell contexts and reveals host lipid-associated dependency factors conserved across SARS-CoV-2 variants, providing candidates for antiviral therapeutic development.
