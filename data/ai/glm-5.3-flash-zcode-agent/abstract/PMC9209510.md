Abstract: Spatially resolved measurement of carrier behavior is essential for understanding the optoelectronic properties of two-dimensional semiconductors at mesoscopic scales. We combined scanning near-field optical spectroscopy with carrier transport-based analysis to investigate carrier diffusion and recombination in mesoscopic regions of a two-dimensional semiconductor. Carriers excited in the semiconductor crystal diffuse and recombine at each point, and spectra recorded at each point were used to plot the distributions of emission energy and emission intensity across the measurement range. The emission intensity in the bottom regions was higher than that in the surrounding hill regions, indicating that carriers generated in the semiconductor quantum well tend to be collected at the bottoms, i.e., the stable points, as they diffuse down to low-energy areas. Higher intensity was also observed in areas where the contour lines are widely spaced, which is attributed to the increased time taken by carriers to migrate through these gently sloped regions and the associated probability of recombination. In two-probe measurements, excited carriers generated at a local excitation point diffused in a way that detoured regions of energy higher than the excitation point, suggesting that the existence of saddle-shaped potentials influences the path selection of the spreading carriers, which branch at the saddle-shaped potential and diffuse toward stable points. The diffusion pathways are therefore influenced by the energy of the local excitation point and the surrounding energy topography. These results demonstrate that scanning near-field optical spectroscopy with carrier transport analysis can resolve carrier diffusion and recombination in mesoscopic regions of two-dimensional semiconductors.
