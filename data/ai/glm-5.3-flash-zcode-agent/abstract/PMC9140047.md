Malignant progression of neurofibromin-deficient cells in neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) involves cooperating genetic events that remain incompletely defined. We investigated the role of PTPN11, encoding the SHP-2 tyrosine phosphatase, in this process. The human NF1-mutant cell line sNF96.2-GFP, which harbors an NF1 frameshift mutation (c.3683delC, p.Asn1229MetfsTer11), generally shows low tumorigenic activity and did not form tumors after subcutaneous or sciatic nerve injection in immunodeficient mice. When injected under the kidney capsule, however, a tumor developed 154 days after cell injection and manifested as a high-grade, atypical, spindle-shaped cell sarcoma with necrotic areas, diagnosed as spindle cell sarcoma consistent with an MPNST. Many mitotic cells were observed, with a Ki-67 labeling rate exceeding 60%, and tumor cells had spread outside of the kidney and infiltrated skeletal muscle. Immunostaining for S-100, Sox10, GFAP and CD57, proteins commonly examined for the diagnosis of human MPNST, was negative, as in about half of all human MPNST cases. Cells established from this tumor, designated A-1 cells, showed an STR profile identical to the parental sNF96.2-GFP cells. Compared with parental cells, A-1 cells displayed significantly higher proliferative activity in vitro, much higher phosphorylation levels of Akt and MEK, greater sensitivity to the MEK inhibitor trametinib, and much higher tumorigenic activity upon subcutaneous injection into nude mice. These findings indicate that PTPN11 mutation promotes MEK activation and malignant progression in neurofibromin-deficient cells in a manner sensitive to BRAP mutation.
