Abstract: Systems-level characterization of microRNA-mediated interactions in kidney transplant rejection (KTR) may reveal biomarkers for diagnosis and subtyping. We integrated differentially expressed miRNAs and mRNAs, miRNA-mRNA interactions, and protein-protein interaction (PPI) data to construct a KTR-specific miRNA-mediated PPI network (miR-PPIN). The network contained 949 miRNA-mRNA pairs and 1,447 PPIs among 31 miRNAs and 642 abnormally expressed genes, whose products were significantly enriched in T cell-related biological processes, including NK T cell proliferation and T cell antigen processing and presentation, indicating the specificity of the identified network in KTR development. Degree distributions at both the miRNA-mRNA and PPI levels followed a power law with a slope of -1.34, with fewer miRNAs regulating more targets and most targets located at peripheral positions in the PPI network. Target genes were prioritized by degree, closeness and betweenness to define a CORE gene set, and miRNAs regulating unusually many CORE genes, quantified by newly defined topological parameters (NTGA and NTGC), were nominated as candidate biomarkers. Three miRNAs met these criteria: miR-145-5p and miR-23b-3p were downregulated in KTR, whereas miR-155-5p was overexpressed, especially in cell-mediated rejection (CMR). Receiver operating characteristic analyses on both a training dataset and an independent validation dataset evaluated the potential of the individual miRNAs and their combination for KTR diagnosis and subtyping. This multi-omics network approach thus reveals novel microRNA biomarkers and mechanisms for the diagnosis and subtyping of kidney transplant rejection.
