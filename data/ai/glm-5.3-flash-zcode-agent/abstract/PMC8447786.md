Abstract: We report a real-world multicentre experience of primary breast double-hit lymphoma (DHL), describing its clinical characteristics, management and outcomes. A total of 48 patients were enrolled. The median age was 48 years (range, 24-61 years), and a painless unilateral breast mass was present in 38 patients (79.2%), with the left breast involved more often than the right (45.8% vs 33.3%). The median tumor size was 2.6 cm (range, 0.9-11.5 cm), with 17 tumors exceeding 5 cm. Thirteen patients were classified as Ann Arbor stage IE, 21 as IIE and 14 as IV; by age-adjusted International Prognostic Index, 27 patients fell into the low- and low-intermediate risk categories and 21 into the high-intermediate and high-risk categories. According to the Hans algorithm, 40 patients (83.3%) were of the germinal center B-cell type and 8 of the non-GCB type. All cases were positive for C-MYC protein, 43 were positive for both BCL2 and BCL6, and chromosomal abnormalities were observed in 12 of 48 patients. Next-generation sequencing applied to 14 samples identified TP53 mutations in 4 patients, BCL6, BCL2, CD79B and IGHD mutations in 2 patients each. Fourteen patients were treated with DA-EPOCH-R/MA, 16 with R-HyperCVAD and 18 with DA-EPOCH-R, and 18 underwent autologous stem cell transplantation after initial therapy. After a median follow-up of 47 months (range, 9.5-79 months), 28 patients had died. These findings document the presentation, treatment patterns and outcomes of primary breast DHL in routine multicentre practice.
