The intrasaccular flow disruptor WEB and endovascular coiling are established treatment options for intracranial aneurysms, but comparative data for basilar tip aneurysms are limited. We compared angiographic outcomes and complication rates of WEB embolization versus coiling for unruptured basilar tip aneurysms. The study population comprised 40 patients treated with WEB (61.9 ± 12.6 years) and 35 treated with coiling (63.2 ± 10.4 years). Baseline patient and aneurysm characteristics were similar between groups, except that aneurysm neck width was significantly larger in the WEB group (4.7 ± 1.4 mm vs 3.9 ± 1.7 mm; p = 0.028), resulting in a lower dome-to-neck ratio (p = 0.035). Additional stent implantation was significantly more often necessary for coiled aneurysms (71%, 25/35) than for WEB embolization (2.5%, 1/40; p < 0.001), while 23% (8/35) of aneurysms were treated by stand-alone coiling. There was no treatment failure in either group. Treatment duration was shorter in the WEB group (111 ± 57 min) than in the coiling group (152 ± 81 min; p = 0.048), and patient radiation exposure and contrast dye application were also lower with WEB, although without reaching statistical significance (p = 0.088 and p = 0.071). In the WEB group, two procedure-related thromboembolic events occurred (5.0%); in one patient, multiple posterior cortical infarcts probably due to thromboembolism caused numbness in the left lower leg that resolved within a few weeks, with no permanent morbidity. These findings indicate that WEB embolization is a feasible and time-efficient alternative to coiling for unruptured basilar tip aneurysms, with a markedly reduced need for adjunctive stenting.
