We investigated the role of the unfolded protein response (UPR) effector XBP1 in acetaminophen (APAP)-induced liver injury. XBP1 expression was examined in liver tissue sections obtained from patients undergoing emergency liver transplantation for APAP-induced acute liver failure (N = 13). Although most hepatocyte nuclei expressed XBP1, cytoplasmic expression of XBP1 became more evident as hepatocyte architecture was altered. To model the injury, we studied the mycoplasma-free human hepatic cell line HepaRG and a preclinical mouse model. Splicing of Xbp1 mRNA was observed under high concentrations of APAP in vitro 24 hours after challenge and in wild-type mice after high-dose APAP injection, indicating activation of the UPR. Consistently, immunohistochemical staining of liver sections from mice treated with high-dose APAP (500 mg/kg) revealed significantly increased XBP1 expression in the liver parenchyma 24 hours after treatment, specifically in hepatocyte cytosols, compared with vehicle-treated controls. These results demonstrate that transcriptional activation of XBP1 is directly related to the UPR in both human and murine APAP-induced liver injury. Furthermore, genetic and pharmacological inhibition of XBP1 protects against APAP hepatotoxicity through the activation of autophagy, identifying XBP1 as a potential therapeutic target for APAP-induced acute liver failure.
