Early tumor shrinkage (ETS) has been reported to predict favorable outcomes in metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), but its association with patient-reported outcomes deserves attention. In the prospective Phase II QUACK trial, we analyzed a dataset from 128 of 140 mCRC patients treated with first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy to evaluate the impact of ETS on quality of life. The median age was 66 years (range, 27-89 years), 87 patients (68.0%) were male, and 107 (83.6%) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status of 0. At baseline, 51 patients (39.8%) were symptomatic, reporting "very much" or "quite a bit" for at least one of the eight symptom items (appetite loss, pain, diarrhea, constipation, dyspnea, fatigue, insomnia, and nausea/vomiting) on the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire. ETS was achieved in 82 patients (64.1%), consistent with previously reported ETS rates of 61.5-80%. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics were well balanced between patients with and without ETS, except for differentiation. ETS was associated with a higher overall response rate (76.8% vs 21.7%; P < .001), and the rate of surgical resection of metastases was significantly higher among patients with ETS than among those without ETS (28.6% vs 11.4%; P = .031). These findings indicate that ETS during first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy identifies mCRC patients with higher response rates and a greater likelihood of metastatic resection, supporting its evaluation as an early marker of treatment benefit alongside patient-reported quality of life.
