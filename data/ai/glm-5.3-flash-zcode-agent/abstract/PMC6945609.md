Abstract: To characterize the immune environment of the peritoneal cavity in endometriosis, we analyzed peritoneal fluid cells (PFCs) and peripheral blood cells (PBCs) from endometriosis patients and endometriosis-free controls using mass cytometry (CyTOF). A panel of 33 antibodies was designed to identify major innate and adaptive immune cell types, including cells of the mononuclear phagocytic system (macrophages, monocytes and dendritic cells), NK cells, neutrophils, T cells and B cells, together with markers defining their differentiation and plasticity states. Cells collected during laparoscopic surgery were isotopically labelled and processed for CyTOF acquisition, and immune profiles were visualized by viSNE and principal component analysis. The majority of PFCs were CD45+ immune cells, with non-immune CD45− cells averaging only 1.85% of total cells. Members of the mononuclear phagocytic system, namely macrophages and dendritic cells, constituted the largest proportion of cells in PFC samples compared with PBCs, whereas T cells, NK cells, B cells and neutrophils were also detected in peritoneal fluid, albeit at much lower frequencies. Peritoneal immune profiles differed substantially from the circulating blood compartment, and principal component analysis showed a clear separation between peritoneal fluid and blood samples, with PBCs displaying less variation than PFCs. These findings reveal a distinct immune environment in peritoneal fluid in endometriosis and provide a reference map of peritoneal immune cell populations for future studies of disease mechanisms and biomarkers.
