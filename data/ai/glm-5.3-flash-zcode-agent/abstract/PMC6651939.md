Abstract: Glucocorticoid receptor (GR) agonists have been suggested to inhibit estrogen receptor (ER)-driven breast cancer cell proliferation, and we examined the mechanisms by which GR modulation suppresses ER-positive breast cancer growth. In GR+/ER+ MCF-7 and T-47D cells, co-treatment with the synthetic glucocorticoid dexamethasone (Dex) and estradiol (E2) significantly reduced cell proliferation compared with E2 alone, whereas GR liganding with Dex in the absence of E2 was not growth suppressive, indicating that GR specifically reduces E2-mediated proliferation. Because long-term glucocorticoid treatment is not well tolerated in patients, we tested selective GR modulators (SGRMs). Although C134 and C335 are GR antagonists with respect to canonical GR target gene expression such as FKBP5, they, similarly to Dex, significantly inhibited E2-mediated proliferation, suggesting that specific GR ligand binding per se slows E2-mediated proliferation. The reduced cell number observed after several days of GR-selective liganding was not accompanied by increased cell death; instead, cell cycle analysis showed slowing of progression from S to G2/M at 18 hours in MCF-7 cells, reflected by increased S-phase accumulation. The effect was GR-specific: neither Dex nor the SGRMs CORT108297 or C335 decreased E2-driven proliferation in the GR-negative T47D-Y cell line, and a radioactive E2 competition assay using recombinant ER ligand-binding domain confirmed that SGRMs do not interact directly with ER. Mechanistically, GR modulation decreases ER-positive breast cancer cell proliferation and suppresses wild-type and mutant ER chromatin association, supporting GR ligands as a therapeutic strategy for ER-positive breast cancer.
