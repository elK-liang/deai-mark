Saliva-derived exosomal small RNAs offer a non-invasive liquid biopsy route for cancer detection, and we evaluated them as predicting biomarkers for esophageal carcinoma in a multicenter prospective study. In a pilot study of 3 esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients and 3 healthy volunteers, salivary exosomes were confirmed by transmission electron microscopy and immunoblotting for the exosomal markers Alix, TSG101, CD63 and CD9, with absence of the intracellular protein Calnexin; nanoparticle tracking showed an average exosome diameter of 95 nm. Small RNA sequencing identified 1366 differentially expressed saliva-derived exosomal small non-coding RNAs (sesncRNAs, excluding miRNAs) in ESCC patients (p < 0.05), of which 32 were highly expressed with log2 fold changes > 2, and the top five candidates with the most significant fold changes were selected. In the CHSUMC cohort of 33 ESCC patients and 33 controls, quantitative RT-PCR showed that two of the five sesncRNAs met the predetermined significance level of 0.01: the tsRNA tRNA-GlyGCC-5, derived from the 5' end of TRG-GCC, and a previously uncharacterized small non-coding RNA located on chromosome 1 between SSX2IP and lncRNA LOC102724892, which we named sRESE. Both tRNA-GlyGCC-5 and sRESE were highly expressed in exosomes secreted into conditioned media and in ESCC cell lines compared with immortalized esophageal epithelial cells, and ROC analysis evaluated their diagnostic performance in the CHSUMC cohort. A signature of saliva-derived exosomal small RNAs therefore serves as a predicting biomarker for esophageal carcinoma and a candidate pre-operative biomarker for clinical decision-making.
