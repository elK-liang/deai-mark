Abstract: Estimating the abundance of tissue-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations is essential for studying the microenvironment in murine models of health and disease. We developed the murine Microenvironment Cell Population counter (mMCP-counter), a method that estimates the abundance of tissue-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations in murine samples from gene expression data. mMCP-counter relies on the identification of specific transcriptomic markers for each analyzed population, defined as transcripts with high expression in a given cell population, including all its subpopulations, and zero expression in any other cell population. The hierarchy of murine cell populations was completed from the ImmGen hematopoietic tree using a survey of the literature, and transcriptomic profiles of sorted cell populations were collected from the ImmGen Microarray datasets, supplemented with additional datasets from non-immune, non-stromal negative controls, including epithelial and hypothalamic cell lines, melanoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and breast cancer cell lines, myoblasts and hepatocytes. For each of the 55 represented populations, all available transcripts were screened for log2 fold change (FC), specific fold change (sFC) and area under the ROC curve (AUC), and features qualified as transcriptomic markers if they met three criteria: FC > 2.1, sFC > 2.1 and AUC > 0.97. For example, the probe for Tpsb2 qualified as a mast cell marker with a log2 FC of 6.48 and a log2 sFC of 5.94. mMCP-counter thus provides a gene expression-based tool to estimate murine tissue-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations across murine samples.
