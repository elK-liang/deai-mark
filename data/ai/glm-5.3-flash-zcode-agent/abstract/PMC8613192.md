Abstract: Gastric cancer (GC) arises within a complex microenvironment in which both microbes and metabolites participate, and we examined the interactions between gastric microbiota and metabolites in GC. Gastric tumor and paired non-tumor tissues from 37 GC patients (Cohort 1) were profiled by 16S rRNA gene sequencing, producing a median of 80,110 clean reads per sample. Tumor tissues showed significantly higher microbial alpha diversity than non-tumor tissues, with higher observed OTUs (464.00 vs 231.00; P < 0.001) and Shannon index (5.20 vs 2.98; P < 0.001). Beta diversity analysis by weighted UniFrac principal coordinate analysis revealed significant clustering between the two groups (PERMANOVA, R2 = 0.211, P = 0.001), and 2222 and 3961 OTUs were detected in non-tumor and tumor tissues, respectively, with 1832 shared. LEfSe analysis identified 64 discriminative taxa (LDA > 3.5, Q < 0.05): at the phylum level, Proteobacteria was increased in non-tumor tissues, whereas Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Actinobacteria, Fusobacteria and Spirochetes were enriched in tumor tissues; at the genus level, Helicobacter was elevated in non-tumor tissues, while Lactobacillus, Streptococcus, Acinetobacter, Prevotella, Sphingomonas, Bacteroides, Fusobacterium, Comamonas, Empedobacter and Faecalibacterium were increased in tumor tissues. Untargeted metabolomic analysis of the same paired samples by UHPLC-MS/MS quantified 1198 metabolites, and partial least squares-discriminant analysis separated tumor from non-tumor tissues. These results demonstrate markedly different gastric microbiota and metabolic profiles between tumor and non-tumor tissues, revealing interactions between gastric microbiota and metabolites in gastric cancer.
