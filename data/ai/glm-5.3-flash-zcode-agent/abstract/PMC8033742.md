Abstract: Targeted therapy of acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) is limited by the rapid development of drug resistance, and alternating inhibitors may delay or overcome it. We examined the effects of the kinase inhibitors quizartinib and pexidartinib and of drug rotations between them in AML cell lines. Incubation of MOLM-14 and MV4-11 cells with quizartinib and pexidartinib for 48 hours reduced the number of viable cells in a concentration-consistent manner, with half-maximally inhibitory coefficients (IC50) in the sub-nanomolar range for quizartinib and in the low-nanomolar range for pexidartinib, consistent with previous studies. To model the development of resistance, cells were incubated with each inhibitor and followed for 6 days, after which the drug was removed by washing and treatment was re-initiated; six cycles (generations) of grow-treat-wash were performed, and growth rates were estimated at days 3 and 6. Washing was necessary to avoid exposing the cells to the two drugs simultaneously, since their metabolism in these cells is limited. With both drugs, cells grew slowly during the first days but then became resistant, as evidenced by comparison of the number of cells and growth rates at days 3 and 6, and none of the cell populations were impaired by the washing procedure, as shown by continued growth at subsequent generations. When MV4-11 cells were treated with quizartinib, there was a gradual increase in the number of cells at day 6 upon subsequent cycles. These results characterize the dynamics of resistance development under quizartinib and pexidartinib and support drug rotations as a strategy to combat drug resistance in AML.
