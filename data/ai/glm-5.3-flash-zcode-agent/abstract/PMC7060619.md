Distinguishing true resistance mutations from phylogenetically informative benign mutations is essential for accurate prediction of antibiotic resistance from whole-genome sequencing of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex (MTBC). We analyzed a collection of 405 MTBC isolates, comprising 296 evolutionary "modern" strains (lineages 2-4) and 109 "ancestral" strains, ranging from lineages 1 and 7 (M. tuberculosis, n = 60) and lineages 5 and 6 (M. africanum, n = 35) to 14 animal-adapted species, including one M. bovis BCG vaccine strain. Only 40 isolates (9.9%) carried a mutation in the rifampicin resistance-determining region of rpoB and were rifampicin resistant. A maximum likelihood phylogeny based on 42,760 single nucleotide polymorphisms, excluding repetitive regions and 92 genes implicated in antibiotic resistance, was constructed with 1000 resamples, and all internal nodes and branches with bootstrap support of 0.9 or higher (n = 334) were investigated for group-specific mutations in these 92 genes, which include genes implicated in antibiotic resistance, confirmed resistance mechanisms, and genes compensating for the reduced fitness of resistance mutations. We identified a total of 557 group-specific mutations; 26 were excluded because they were found in groups comprising strains with a pairwise distance of up to five SNPs, suggesting recent transmission, with the exception of three H37Rv-specific mutations and the gyrA A90G change, which were retained given their phylogenetic importance. This catalog of phylogenetically informative mutations in antibiotic resistance genes provides a resource for distinguishing benign lineage markers from resistance-conferring mutations and improving genotypic resistance prediction in the MTBC.
