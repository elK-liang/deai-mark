Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) is a debilitating condition whose epidemiological features at the population level are incompletely documented in many countries. Using nationwide claims data, we characterized the incidence and prevalence of physician-diagnosed CFS in South Korea between 2010 and 2020. The mean number of patients newly diagnosed with CFS each year was 22,720 ± 3284, with a consistent female predominance of 1.49-fold (males 9122 ± 1152 vs females 13,598 ± 2179) over the 11-year period. The annual number of new diagnoses increased 1.57-fold between 2010 (16,175) and 2020 (25,403), with a slightly higher increase in females (1.66-fold) than in males (1.44-fold). The absolute number of CFS diagnoses peaked in the 50- to 59-year-old population in both sexes, and females predominated across all age groups except those younger than 10 years. The average annual incidence of CFS was estimated at 44.71 ± 6.10 cases per 100,000 individuals (95% CI: 40.57-48.76), increasing 1.53-fold between 2010 (32.43) and 2020 (49.47). Females (53.49 ± 8.05 per 100,000) were 1.49-fold more likely than males (35.93 ± 4.30 per 100,000) to be diagnosed, a female-predominant pattern consistent across the population aged 10 to 79 years; the peak relative frequency was observed in men older than 80 years (70.7 patients) and in women aged 60 to 69 years (70.7 patients). These findings provide a nationwide epidemiological characterization of CFS in South Korea and document a rising disease burden with a marked female predominance.
