We investigated the role of APPL, the Drosophila amyloid precursor protein homologue, in brain health of adult flies. APPL null (appld) flies died significantly earlier than genetic background controls in a sex-independent manner, starting at 2 to 3 weeks of age, suggesting that loss of APPL compromises survival at an early age. To assess neuronal health, we quantified cell death in whole-mount brains of appld flies and controls at 2, 7, 21 and 45 days of age by staining for the caspase Dcp-1, the homologue of human Caspase 3. Both genotypes showed significant cell death at 2 days, reflecting ongoing brain remodeling. By 7 days of age, apoptotic cells dropped sharply in controls, whereas this drop was significantly reduced in appld flies, which maintained an average of 7 to 8 apoptotic cells per brain at any given time point; counterstaining with the neuronal marker Elav and the glial marker Repo showed that all dying cells were neurons. At 21 and 45 days, both genotypes showed a similar age-related increase in apoptotic cells, indicating that loss of APPL renders neurons particularly vulnerable during the first week of adult life. Accordingly, pan-neuronal RNAi knockdown of APPL resulted in significantly more apoptotic neurons in 7-day-old flies, similar to appld mutants, and neuronal re-expression of APPL rescued this phenotype. These findings demonstrate that the Drosophila APP homologue mediates neuronal survival and neuroglial interactions, illuminating conserved physiological functions of the APP protein family.
