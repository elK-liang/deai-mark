Abstract: When a new infectious disease emerges, outcome prediction models are typically delayed by the scarcity of disease-specific training data. Transfer learning from existing diseases with similar clinical vocabularies may close this gap. We developed a hierarchical multi-modal BERT model for COVID-19 outcome prediction using transfer learning from an existing respiratory disease (SRD) cohort, motivated by the strong vocabulary overlap between the two cohorts: 88.8% of diagnosis codes, 94.04% of drug codes, 63.2% of procedure codes and 90.57% of laboratory measurement codes in the COVID-19 cohort were also found in the SRD cohort. The SRD cohort provided strong coverage of severe outcomes, with 14.6% of patients requiring ventilation versus 11.4% in the COVID-19 cohort, proportions of ICU days of 23.4% versus 5.76%, and mortality of 8.34% versus 6.46%. The cohorts differed significantly in demographics, including age, sex and race, with Hispanic or Latino ethnicity significantly over-represented in the COVID-19 cohort. The model was evaluated on two binary classification tasks on the COVID-19 dataset, with each patient's stay duration segmented into intervals of fixed length serving as aggregation windows for the multimodal visit data. These results demonstrate the feasibility of preparing outcome prediction models for a new pandemic via transfer learning from existing diseases using hierarchical multi-modal BERT, providing a framework for COVID-19 outcome prediction and future outbreak preparedness.
