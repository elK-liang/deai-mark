Abstract: Osteosarcoma (OS) is characterized by genomic and immune alterations that drive oncogenesis and shape prognosis, and we sought to define the transcriptional networks involved. Four GEO datasets containing both normal control and osteosarcoma tissues were analyzed, reflecting complementary control samples ranging from normal primary osteoblasts to osteoblasts derived from mesenchymal stem cells. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs; |log2(Fold Change)| > 0.58 and adjusted p < 0.05) were identified in the four OS datasets independently, and DEGs overlapped in at least three datasets were considered credible for functional enrichment analysis, yielding 124 upregulated and 98 downregulated genes. Upregulated genes were enriched in collagen-containing extracellular matrix, smooth muscle cell proliferation, cell-substrate adhesion and the p53 signaling pathway, whereas downregulated genes were enriched in immune-related functions including MHC class II protein complex and complex binding and lymphocyte/leukocyte proliferation, indicating a significant loss of MHC class II protein complex and immune cell activation capacity in OS samples compared with controls. A protein-protein interaction network constructed with Cytoscape based on the STRING database identified the top 10 hub genes by degree: FN1, TYROBP, EGFR, CSF1R, C1QB, PLEK, LCP2, C1QA, CD86 and VWF, enriched in osteoclast differentiation, integrin binding, collagen-containing extracellular matrix and glial cell activation. Within this landscape, the SPI1-TYROBP-FCER1G network plays a role in the oncogenesis and prognosis of osteosarcoma and is associated with immune infiltration, providing candidate prognostic markers and therapeutic targets in OS.
