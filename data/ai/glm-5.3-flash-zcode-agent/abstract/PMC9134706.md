DLEU1 is a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), and we characterized its role in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). By analyzing two independent lncRNA + mRNA microarray profiles comprising 119 and 60 pairs of ESCC samples with matched adjacent normal tissues, we screened for differentially expressed lncRNAs and selected four of the most frequently overexpressed candidates in cancer tissues for functional analysis: LINC01614, LOC284930, KCNMB2-AS1 and DLEU1. Among these, knocking down DLEU1 with a pool of three siRNAs had a potent suppressive effect on ESCC cell growth. DLEU1 expression was higher in 118 (99.2%) and 59 (98.3%) tumors than in their matched normal counterparts in the two independent cohorts, and ESCC patients with high DLEU1 expression tended to have decreased overall survival. The Cancer Genome Atlas confirmed significantly higher DLEU1 expression in tumors and a correlation between DLEU1 upregulation and worse prognosis, and overexpression of DLEU1 was positively correlated with lymph node metastasis and advanced clinical stages. RNAscope assays confirmed upregulated DLEU1 expression in ESCC compared to adjacent nontumor tissues. Gene set enrichment analysis showed enrichment of genes upregulated in breast cancer stem cells, metastatic prostate cancer and liver cancer with poor survival, as well as genes regulating mitochondrial membrane permeability involved in apoptosis, suggesting a contribution of DLEU1 to poor prognosis and mitochondrial apoptosis. Mechanistically, DLEU1 promotes cell survival by preventing DYNLL1 degradation in ESCC, identifying DLEU1 as an oncogenic lncRNA and a candidate therapeutic target.
