Accurate non-invasive diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains challenging, particularly in populations without overt high-risk features. This study developed and evaluated a non-invasive diagnostic strategy for HCC in a low-risk population across two institutions. In total, 681 patients were enrolled, 463 from the first institution and 218 from the second. Patients were allocated to a training cohort (n = 324), an internal validation cohort (n = 139), and an external validation cohort (n = 218). HCC accounted for 174 (37.6%) and 103 (48.0%) of patients in the two institutions, respectively. The remaining non-HCC patients comprised malignancies, predominantly intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, mixed HCC-cholangiocarcinoma and sarcoma, and benign lesions, most commonly focal nodular hyperplasia, inflammatory pseudotumor, angioleiomyolipoma and liver abscess. Median ages ranged between 53 and 60 years, and over 50% of patients were male (58%-68%). Nearly 80% of nodules exceeded 3 cm in diameter. Significant differences were observed among the three cohorts in age, family history, cigarette smoking, alcohol consumption, cardiovascular disease, HBcAb and HBeAb seropositivity, and serum AST, ALP, GGT, CA125 and CA19-9 (all p < 0.05), whereas characteristics such as sex, diabetes, platelet count, ALT and AFP showed comparable distributions. These results establish a well-characterized, multi-institutional cohort of hepatic nodules in a low-risk population and provide the demographic and clinical foundation for developing and validating a non-invasive diagnostic strategy for HCC in this setting.
