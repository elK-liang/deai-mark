Necroptosis is a form of regulated necrotic cell death that shapes tumor behavior and the immune microenvironment, and we systematically analyzed necroptosis-related genes (NRGs) and derived long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) to build a prognostic signature in breast cancer. Among 67 NRGs, genetic changes occurred in 145 (14.75%) of 983 patients, with ATRX showing the highest alteration frequency (2%), and most variations were copy number amplifications. Differential expression analysis showed that, except for RIPK1, RIPK3, TNF, MAP3K7 and STAT3, all other NRGs were significantly differentially expressed in breast cancer, with TERT showing the highest upregulation (log FC = 2.627) and KLF9 the greatest downregulation (log FC = -1.601). Using clinical and transcriptome data of 1,096 breast cancer specimens and 112 normal specimens from TCGA, Spearman correlation analysis identified 1,520 necroptosis-related lncRNAs (NRlncRNAs) with correlation coefficients > 0.4 and p < 0.001, of which 46 prognostic NRlncRNAs were extracted by univariate Cox analysis. LASSO-penalized Cox regression based on the expression of 22 NRlncRNAs established a necroptosis-related lncRNA signature in the training cohort. Consistently, LMNTD2-AS1, AC007608.3 and LINC02446 were significantly higher, while LINC00377, AL450322.1, MEF2C-AS1 and AC079298.3 were significantly lower in breast cancer tissues than in normal breast tissue. The necroptosis-related lncRNA signature and associated molecular subtypes predict prognosis and reveal the immune microenvironment of breast cancer, offering potential biomarkers for prognostic stratification and immunotherapy decision-making.
