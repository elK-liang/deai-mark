We investigated the expression and oncogenic function of DEAD-box helicase 10 (DDX10) in colorectal cancer (CRC). Analysis of 50 paired cancer and paracarcinoma tissues from the TCGA database showed that DDX10 mRNA expression was evidently higher in CRC tissues than in paracarcinoma tissues, a finding reproduced in 30 pairs of samples from the GEO database (GSE74604) and verified by GEPIA2 and ONCOMINE database analyses showing significantly different DDX10 mRNA expression between tumor and normal tissues. Consistent with the mRNA data, DDX10 protein expression was significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared with paracarcinoma tissues by immunohistochemistry (n = 35, H-score, P < 0.05). Survival analysis of disease-free survival in colon adenocarcinoma revealed that DDX10 expression was associated with patient prognosis, and the mutation rate of DDX10 in TCGA CRC was 3.4%, observed in twelve cases. Compared with normal intestinal epithelial cells (NCM460), higher expression of DDX10 was shown in multiple CRC cell lines, including LoVo, HCT116, RKO and SW480. Functional analyses demonstrated that DDX10 promotes the proliferation and metastasis of colorectal cancer cells via splicing RPL35. These findings identify DDX10 as an upregulated oncogenic factor in CRC and highlight the DDX10-RPL35 splicing axis as a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target in colorectal cancer.
