Abstract: Reducing energy losses is a key route to higher efficiency in organic solar cells (OSCs), and the molecular design of non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) critically influences these losses. Based on the Y-type NFA molecular backbone, we synthesized two acceptors, Qx-1 and Qx-2, by fusing quinoxaline (Qx) derivatives into the heteroaromatic cores. Both molecules dissolved well in common solvents, with solubilities of 36.2 mg/ml for Qx-1 and 13.9 mg/ml for Qx-2 in chloroform. Replacing the BTZ core of the Y6 acceptor with the weaker electron-withdrawing Qx core weakened the intramolecular charge transfer effect and increased the bandgap, while the enhanced electron-donating ability of the Qx core increased both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energies; the higher LUMO energy level facilitates a higher open-circuit voltage in devices. Furthermore, the energy offsets between the HOMOs of donor and acceptor for hole transfer and between the LUMOs for electron transfer were largely decreased, which reduces the voltage loss of exciton dissociation. In both solutions and films, the Stokes shifts from Y6 to Qx-1 and Qx-2 were decreased, suggesting smaller excited-state relaxations, consistent with the small reorganization energy of these acceptors and beneficial for reducing voltage losses. Moving from solution to solid films, the absorption spectra were red-shifted, indicating strong intermolecular π-π interactions. These results demonstrate that small reorganization energy acceptors enable low energy losses in non-fullerene organic solar cells and provide a molecular design strategy for high-efficiency devices.
