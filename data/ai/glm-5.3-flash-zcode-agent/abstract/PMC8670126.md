African American (AA) patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) have been shown to be associated with increased inflammation, but quantitative characterization of their tumor immune microenvironment remains limited. We performed a case-control study quantitatively assessing the immune microenvironment of TNBC in AA versus non-AA (NAA) patients using multiplex quantitative immunofluorescence. Quantitative scoring with the pan-leukocyte marker CD45 showed that infiltration with CD45+ immune cells was higher in AA TNBC samples (p = 0.0102), including higher levels of lymphoid CD45+ CD14− cells (p = 0.0081) and myeloid CD45+ CD14+ cells (p = 0.0864). Quantification of CD68, CD206, CD4, CD83, CD8 and CD20 revealed no significant differences among individual lymphoid and myeloid cell subtypes, although CD3+, CD8+ and CD20+ cells were on average numerically higher among AA TNBC samples. Using a definition of activated T cells previously associated with response to immunotherapy in lung cancer, CD3+ cells of AA TNBCs were more likely to be in an active than dormant state (p = 0.0077), suggesting more active immunosurveillance in the AA tumor microenvironment. Ki67 expression was significantly higher in AA TNBCs (p = 0.0066), and granzyme B level was also higher, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.0912), whereas no significant differences were detected in regulatory T cells (FOXP3+ CD4+). These findings demonstrate quantitative differences in the immune microenvironment of AA TNBC that may inform the design of immunotherapy strategies for this population.
