ESR1 mutations emerge under estrogen deprivation and are associated with aromatase inhibitor (AI) resistance in breast cancer, but the timing of their clonal emergence during treatment is incompletely characterized. We assessed circulating ESR1 mutations by digital PCR in a cohort of 42 hormone receptor-positive breast cancer patients treated with adjuvant AI, sampling plasma at the end of adjuvant treatment, at relapse, and at progression on first-line metastatic treatment. The median age at diagnosis was 60.7 years, all 42 patients received adjuvant AI, and the median duration of adjuvant AI treatment was 60.0 months (range, 41-85 months). None of the 42 patients had a circulating ESR1 mutation detectable at the end of AI adjuvant therapy. Plasma samples at relapse were available for 38 patients (90%), of whom 2 (5.3%) had a detectable circulating ESR1 mutation (Y537C and D538G) at the time of relapse. Plasma samples at progression on first-line metastatic treatment were available for 31 patients (74%), including 21 treated with AI; a circulating ESR1 mutation was detected in 7 of these 21 patients (33%) progressing on AI, whereas none of the 10 patients progressing on chemotherapy or non-AI endocrine treatment had a detectable circulating ESR1 mutation. Of note, in none of the 3 patients with early relapse (< 6 months) after the end of adjuvant AI was an ESR1 mutation detected. These findings indicate that circulating ESR1 mutations are rare at the end of adjuvant AI treatment and emerge preferentially after re-exposure to AI in the metastatic setting, supporting longitudinal ctDNA monitoring during endocrine therapy.
