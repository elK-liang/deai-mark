Abstract: The immune phenotype of breast cancer metastases may differ substantially from that of primary tumors, with direct implications for immunotherapy. We performed immunohistochemistry for CD8, PD-1 and LAG-3 on whole tissue sections of primary breast tumors (PBTs) and metastases (METs) and translated the frequency and spatial distribution of CD8+ T cells into three immune phenotypes corresponding to the clinical categories "hot" and "cold". In PBTs, the frequencies of immune-excluded and inflamed cases were similar, whereas METs turned "cold" regardless of breast cancer molecular subtype, clinicopathological parameters or anatomical site. METs of "cold" PBTs always remained "cold" at their matched metastatic site, while "hot" PBTs either stayed "hot" or turned "cold". Among distant metastatic sites, brain and soft tissue METs remained more commonly inflamed. LAG-3 expression occurred only in PD-1-positive cases (p < 0.01) with an overall low frequency (5% intratumoral and 31% stromal positive cases in PBTs, lower for METs), and PD-1+/LAG-3+ expression correlated significantly with a "hot" immune phenotype in both PBTs and METs, regardless of molecular subtype. Brain and soft tissue METs more commonly displayed PD-1 expression, with the same tendency for LAG-3. Dichotomization of PBTs into inflamed/"hot" or excluded-desert/"cold" tumors revealed improved disease-free survival (DFS) for "hot" PBTs, and combined analysis of stromal PD-1/LAG-3 expression and immune phenotype further improved DFS discrimination: "hot" but PD-1− PBTs showed significantly better DFS, while "hot" PD-1+ PBTs had the same reduced DFS as "cold" tumors. These findings demonstrate differential PD-1/LAG-3 expression and immune phenotypes across metastatic sites of breast cancer.
