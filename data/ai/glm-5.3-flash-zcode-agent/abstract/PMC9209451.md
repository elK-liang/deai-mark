Abstract: Junctophilin-2 (JP2) is proteolytically cleaved by calpains in the heart, but the spectrum of cleavage fragments and sites remains incompletely characterized because of alternative proteolytic pathways, antibody limitations and the anomalous migration of JP2 in SDS-PAGE gels. To identify Calpain-specific JP2 fragments in a systematic and directly controlled substrate-reaction manner, we expressed recombinant mouse full-length JP2 in E. coli, purified it, and exposed it to purified Calpain-1 or Calpain-2 across four orders of neutral protease concentrations (0.01 through 10 U/ml) under constant reaction conditions. Coomassie Blue R250 staining captured the complete Calpain-2-specific cleavage pattern. At the lowest Calpain-2 concentration (0.01 U/ml), a single major cleavage event produced the N- and C-terminal fragments NT1 and CT1, migrating at apparent molecular weights of ~75 kDa and ~25 kDa, respectively. At 0.1 U/ml, three additional cleavage events were evident: N-terminally, two cleavage reactions produced NT2 and NT1-NT2 (~20 and ~55 kDa) and NT3 and NT1-NT3 (~30 and ~45 kDa), while C-terminally CT1 was cleaved to the ~10 kDa fragment CT2. Notably, the computationally predicted CT1-CT2 and NT3-NT2 fragments were not detected. These results demonstrate that calpain cleavage of Junctophilin-2 generates a spectrum of calcium-dependent cleavage products, defining the fragment pattern of JP2 proteolysis and providing a foundation for understanding calpain-dependent JP2 fragmentation and NT1-fragment domains in cardiomyocytes.
