Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), the inflammatory form of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), involves dysregulated long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) expression, and we profiled lncRNAs across disease stages to identify mediators of NASH inflammation. Using the Mouse LncRNA Microarray V2.0, we analyzed lncRNA and mRNA expression profiles in mice with NAFLD and NASH, with pathological sections confirming significantly increased fat accumulation in DB/DB mice fed a normal diet (NAFLD model) and significant inflammatory cell infiltration in DB/DB mice fed an MCD diet (NASH model); serum ALT and AST were 2.98-fold and 1.94-fold higher in the NASH group than in controls, and F4/80 immunohistochemistry showed significant inflammation in NASH mice. Microarray analysis identified 3111 differentially expressed lncRNAs between NAFLD and control mice (1915 upregulated and 1196 downregulated), 1394 upregulated and 1283 downregulated lncRNAs between NASH and control mice, and 1513 upregulated and 1824 downregulated lncRNAs between NASH and NAFLD mice. Venn analysis revealed 63 lncRNAs changed in all three groups, and series cluster analysis identified 381 lncRNAs that were highly expressed in NAFLD and even higher in NASH, among which we focused on the novel lncRNA Gm9795. Functional analysis demonstrated that lncRNA-Gm9795 promotes inflammation in NASH via the NF-κB/JNK pathway through endoplasmic reticulum stress, identifying Gm9795 as a candidate driver of NASH progression and a potential therapeutic target for inflammatory liver disease.
