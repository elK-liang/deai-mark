Glial cells vary greatly in size, yet the mechanisms that determine glial dimensions are poorly understood. Using the nematode C. elegans, we conducted an unbiased forward genetic screen for mutants with abnormally sized amphid sheath (AMsh) glial cells and isolated the yad70 mutant. In yad70 animals, the enlarged AMsh cell phenotype increased in penetrance from larval stages to day 3 adults, reaching 100% by day 2 of adulthood, and the volume of AMsh cell bodies progressively increased at a faster rate, so that AMsh cells were roughly four times the size of control counterparts by day 3. The phenotype was not secondary to overall animal size, as yad70 animals were slightly shorter than controls, and starvation did not suppress the enlarged AMsh cells. Phasmid sheath glia (PHsh) also exhibited an enlarged cell size phenotype, whereas the 12 amphid neurons stained by DiI showed no noticeable change in size or number. Consistent with AMsh dysfunction, yad70 mutants exhibited reduced chemotaxis toward benzaldehyde and pyrazine, behaviors mediated by the AWC and AWA neurons respectively, and impaired long-range avoidance of the repulsive odorant 1-octanol, a behavior partly mediated by ADL neurons. Our results identify eicosapentaenoic acid as a regulator of glial size acting through a novel Golgi apparatus mechanism and establish a genetic entry point for studying the control of glial cell dimensions.
