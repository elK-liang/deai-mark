Abstract: The tumor immune microenvironment of hepatoblastoma, the most common pediatric liver malignancy, is incompletely characterized at the genomic and epigenomic levels. We integrated transcriptomic and methylation data to develop genomic and epigenomic signatures associated with the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatoblastoma. Hepatoblastoma-related genes were retrieved from the GSE75271 and GSE81928 datasets. After normalization of the GSE75271 microarray profiles by RMA and removal of three outlier samples identified by principal component analysis, 1364 differentially expressed genes were screened between hepatoblastoma and normal samples, including 756 up- and 608 down-regulated genes. In the RNA-seq data of GSE81928, standardized by log2(TPM + 1) with background removal, 528 up- and 274 down-regulated genes were identified. Intersection of the two datasets yielded 134 commonly up-regulated and 191 commonly down-regulated genes in hepatoblastoma. Methylation profiles from the GSE78732 dataset, after removal of one outlier sample, revealed 1125 hypermethylated and 2337 hypomethylated sites in hepatoblastoma, with a distinct difference in methylation between hepatoblastoma and normal specimens. Integration of differential expression and differential methylation identified 83 differentially methylated and expressed genes in hepatoblastoma, including TNFRSF19, SP5, NOTUM, NKD1, BMP4, ROBO1 and AXIN2. These genomic and epigenomic signatures characterize the tumor immune microenvironment of hepatoblastoma and provide candidate biomarkers and therapeutic targets for this pediatric cancer.
