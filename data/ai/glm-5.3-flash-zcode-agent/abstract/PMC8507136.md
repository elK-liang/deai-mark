We set out to systematically characterize the gene network regulated by the transcription factor CREB1 and its relevance to cancer. Because CREB1 knockout has been difficult to establish in cells that depend on CREB1 function, we used the experimentally robust cervical cancer cell line HeLa, which has CREB1 expression comparable to many CREB1-sensitive cancer cell lines. Two sgRNAs were designed to target CREB1 intron 1 and the proximal promoter, including the TATA box, and after screening 14 clones, three parallel HeLa CREB1 knockout (KO) clones with deletion of all alleles were obtained. CREB1 expression was rescued in all three KO clones using a lentiviral CREB1 expression construct, and western blotting confirmed the absence and presence of CREB1 protein in the KO and rescue cell lines, respectively. RNA sequencing was then performed to compare the expression patterns of wild-type, KO and rescue cells and to identify the CREB1-mediated gene network in HeLa cells. Systematic analysis of the resulting CREB1-regulated genes, including rank-rank hypergeometric overlap comparisons and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses across cancer datasets, revealed a strong cancer relevance of CREB1-regulated genes. These findings establish a practical strategy for dissecting transcription factor-regulated networks and demonstrate that genes regulated by CREB1 are strongly relevant to cancer, providing a resource for further functional studies of CREB1 in tumorigenesis.
