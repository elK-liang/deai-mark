Abstract: Activation of fibroblasts in distant organs contributes to the formation of the pre-metastatic niche (PMN), which prepares fertile ground for metastatic colonization. We developed an MMP2-activatable self-assembled branched peptide, FR17, consisting of the self-assembling Phe-Phe-Lys-Tyr (FFKY) backbone, derived from the Aβ36-related Phe-Phe (FF) domain, with the immunomodulatory pentapeptide thymopentin (TP5, RKDVY) conjugated to the side chain of lysine through the MMP2-cleavable linker GPLGLAGG. When specifically cleaved by MMP2, FR17 releases the self-assembled monomer FG8, which spontaneously folds into a membrane-like structure, the peptide nano-blanket, under both neutral and weakly acidic conditions simulating the pH of the inflammatory microenvironment in PMN. Transmission electron microscopy and cryo-transmission electron microscopy showed lamellar self-assemblies with an average diameter of approximately 500 nm, and the nano-blanket was observed in the intercellular substance of PMN lung in vivo, with sizes ranging from hundreds to thousands of nanometers depending on the intercellular space. An aggregation-induced emission assay monitored the spontaneous aggregation of FG8 in aqueous systems, and fluorescent dots in organ sections revealed the responsive in vivo assembly of the peptide nano-blanket at PMN lung sites. A scrambled peptide in which TP5 was replaced by the inactive pentapeptide DVYKR (sFD17) served as the assembly control. By impeding fibroblast activation, the peptide nano-blanket impedes the subsequent formation of the pre-metastatic niche and offers a peptide-based strategy against metastasis.
