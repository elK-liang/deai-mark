Abstract: Antiviral RNA interference (RNAi) is a major defense mechanism in plants, and we set out to identify its positive and negative regulators in Arabidopsis thaliana. Natural variation in quantitative resistance to CMV-∆2b, a mutant of the highly virulent subgroup I CMV strain Fny rendered susceptible to antiviral RNAi by preventing VSR-2b expression, was assessed across 496 fully re-sequenced A. thaliana accessions by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay of the viral coat protein two weeks after mechanical inoculation. Virus titers displayed a nearly normal distribution after log-transformation. Genome-wide association analysis with the easyGWAS pipeline found that none of the significantly associated SNPs mapped to any of the known antiviral RNAi genes; the most significantly associated SNP resided in a region of chromosome 4 coding for At4g11040, corresponding to Reduced Dormancy 5 (RDO5)/Delay of Germination 18 (DOG18). CMV-∆2b titers differed significantly between accessions classified as haplotypes T and G at this SNP. Northern and western blot analyses verified that CMV-∆2b accumulated to lower levels in resistant haplotype T accessions, including Col-0, and to much higher levels in the An-1 accession carrying a single base-pair frameshifting deletion in RDO5 than in Col-0 or in An-1 transgenically complemented with the Col-0 RDO5 gene. T-DNA insertion mutants rdo5-4 and rdo5-5 and their complementation lines were generated to verify the role of RDO5 in Col-0 plants. These results identify RDO5 as a regulator of antiviral RNA interference and reveal both positive and negative regulators of antiviral defense in A. thaliana.
