We investigated the expression and function of junctional adhesion molecule-like protein (JAML) in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Quantitative real-time PCR and western blotting showed that JAML expression was significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared with paired peritumor tissues in 15 pairs of LUAD specimens, and immunohistochemistry confirmed significant upregulation of JAML in another 30 pairs of LUAD tissues. When samples were sorted by the median JAML immunohistochemical score into high- and low-expression groups, high JAML expression was significantly correlated with advanced pT stage and pTNM stage, but not with age, sex, pN stage or pM stage. Among four LUAD cell lines (A549, H1299, PC9 and H1975), JAML was relatively upregulated in A549 and H1299 cells and downregulated in PC9 and H1975 cells, and immunofluorescence showed that JAML was expressed in the cytoplasm of LUAD cells. A549 and H1299 cells were therefore selected for knockdown studies, and PC9 and H1975 cells for overexpression studies. Functional assays demonstrated that JAML promotes LUAD cell migration and invasion, and that JAML promotes tumor progression via the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. These findings identify JAML as an oncogenic protein that is elevated in LUAD and drives tumor progression through Wnt/β-catenin signaling, suggesting that JAML and its downstream pathway may represent therapeutic targets for LUAD patients.
