Active surveillance testing has been widely adopted by institutions to control SARS-CoV-2 transmission, and scalable sampling strategies are essential for its success. The University of Padua implemented a salivary-based SARS-CoV-2 surveillance program for its employees during the second and third pandemic waves. Of 8183 subjects invited in September 2020, including 6764 employees and 1419 temporary staff members, 5580 (68%) started the program by providing a first salivary sample in October-November 2020. A second invitation launched in January 2021 led a further 556 subjects to start the program between January and March 2021. On average, 4790 employees participated each month from October 2020 to May 2021, ranging from a minimum of 3965 subjects in May 2021 to a maximum of 5447 in November 2020. For evaluation, two cohorts were defined: employees under surveillance, comprising 5605 subjects from October to December 2020 and 6284 subjects from January to May 2021, and employees not under surveillance, comprising 2047 and 1368 subjects in the same periods, whose clinical status was traced by the Preventive Medicine of the University of Padua. The high and sustained participation achieved by the program demonstrates that salivary-based active surveillance is feasible at institutional scale and, by allowing frequent, non-invasive testing of a large workforce, contributed to minimizing viral transmission during the second and third pandemic waves, providing a practical model for controlling SARS-CoV-2 spread in workplace settings.
