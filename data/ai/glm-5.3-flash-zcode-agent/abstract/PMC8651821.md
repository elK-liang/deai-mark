High-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC) frequently develops chemoresistance, and cell cycle regulators represent candidate targets to restore platinum sensitivity. We identified genes involved in the tumorigenesis and chemoresistance of ovarian cancer from two independent GEO datasets (GSE14407 and GSE10971) containing gene expression profiles of primary serous ovarian carcinoma and normal controls, yielding 258 overlapping upregulated differentially expressed genes (log2 fold change > 1, P value < 0.001). KEGG pathway analysis revealed the cell cycle pathway as the most enriched, and among the 22 cell cycle-related DEGs, TTK was the only gene with a clinical-grade inhibitor investigated in a clinical trial that had not been studied in HGSOC. TTK was expressed at higher levels in ovarian cancer and other cancers than in the corresponding normal controls in several open-access databases, and the TTK mRNA and protein levels detected in our patient samples (11 fallopian tube tissues and 20 HGSOC tissues) were consistent with the online databases. TTK expression was increased in cisplatin-resistant A2780 and SKOV3 cells, and progression-free survival and overall survival analyses with Kaplan-Meier plotter showed that patients with high TTK expression had a poor prognosis. Collectively, TTK is a key cell cycle regulator contributing to the oncogenesis, chemoresistance and poor prognosis of HGSOC, and TTK inhibition increases cisplatin sensitivity in HGSOC through the mTOR/autophagy pathway, supporting TTK inhibitors as a novel targeted therapy for patients with HGSOC.
