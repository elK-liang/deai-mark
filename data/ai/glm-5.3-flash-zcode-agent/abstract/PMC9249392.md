Abstract: Profilin 1 (PFN1) functions through conserved binding sites for PIP lipids, actin monomers, PLP motifs and microtubules, and directly visualizing its molecular behavior requires fluorescent versions that preserve function. We engineered two genetically encoded versions of human PFN1 visible with either an mApple fluorescent probe or self-labeling ligands that bind Halo-tags. mApple was chosen over standard fluorescent proteins because it is bright, relatively stable, and avoids overlap with the excitation and emission ranges of well-characterized actin labels used in biochemical assays, such as Oregon-Green and pyrene-labeled actin. Each tag was fused to the N-terminus of PFN1 with a linking sequence, and recombinant untagged PFN1 and mApple-PFN1 were expressed and purified for comparison in several biochemical assays. SDS-PAGE and gel filtration analyses showed that both the tag-free and tagged versions were of the expected size and highly pure. Liposome pelleting assays assessed binding to PI(3,5)P2 and PI(4,5)P2, PIP lipids whose PFN1 interactions inhibit actin polymerization to regulate plasma membrane morphology and facilitate trafficking of late endosomes to the lysosome; bound tagged and untagged PFN1 were detected in liposome pellets by western blotting with PFN1-specific antibodies, including in pellets containing phosphatidylserine control or PI(3,5)P2- and PI(4,5)P2-containing lipids. These results validate fluorescently tagged PFN1 as a functional molecule, enabling visualization of human profilin and supporting cell biological and biochemical studies of its interactions with actin, lipids and other binding partners.
