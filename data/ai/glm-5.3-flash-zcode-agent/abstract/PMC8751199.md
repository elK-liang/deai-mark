Abstract: Staphylococcus aureus forms biofilms on implanted devices and is a common cause of healthcare-associated MRSA infections, including implant infections. We investigated the reactivity of human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against S. aureus surface antigens with S. aureus biofilms in vitro and in vivo. Five mAbs previously found to recognize surface components of planktonic S. aureus cells were studied: two recognizing cell wall teichoic acids (4461-IgG and 4497-IgG), one recognizing surface proteins of the SDR family (rF1-IgG), one against clumping factor A (T1-2-IgG), and one of which the exact target is yet unknown (CR5132-IgG). F598-IgG against PNAG served as a positive control, and antibodies recognizing the hapten dinitrophenol (G2a-2-IgG) and HIV protein gp120 (b12-IgG) as negative controls. Variable heavy and light chain sequences were cloned into expression vectors to produce full-length human IgG1 (kappa) antibodies in Expi293F cells. Because PNAG is produced by only a subset of S. aureus biofilms, we used Wood46, which produces PNAG with low surface expression of IgG-binding staphylococcal protein A, as a model strain for PNAG-positive biofilm, and LAC∆spa∆sbi, a USA300 lineage mutant capable of forming robust biofilm with no detectable PNAG, as a model for PNAG-negative biofilm. Using these reagents, we demonstrate that human monoclonal antibodies against S. aureus surface antigens recognize S. aureus biofilms both in vitro and in vivo. These findings extend the utility of anti-staphylococcal mAbs to the biofilm context and inform antibody-based strategies against implant-associated S. aureus infections.
