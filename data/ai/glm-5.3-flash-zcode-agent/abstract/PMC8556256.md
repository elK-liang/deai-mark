Abstract: Through screening of an ENU-mutagenized mouse cohort for craniofacial phenotypes, we identified the egl1 mutant, which displays bulging eyes and elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). Optical coherence tomography revealed optic neuropathy characterized by optic nerve cupping and severe retinal nerve fiber layer loss in mutant retinas, and histological examination of retinas from 12-month-old mice confirmed an enlarged optic disc cup with thinning of the inner retinal layer. Whole-exome sequencing identified a homozygous G to T transversion in Pitx2, c.G344T, resulting in the p.R115L missense substitution (NM_001042502.2) at a residue located in the homeodomain region of the PITX2 protein that is highly conserved across species; the mutation was confirmed by allele-specific PCR and Sanger sequencing in a segregating cross. To establish causality, a Pitx2R115L knock-in (KI) mouse model was generated using CRISPR/Cas9 technology. Homozygous KI mice were born at the expected Mendelian ratio, and approximately 59% of mutants (20 out of 34) exhibited a bulging and distended appearance of the eye with mild corneal opacity as early as 2 months of age, roughly 80% of these cases being bilaterally affected. Rebound tonometry confirmed elevated IOPs in mutants, which progressed with age. These findings identify a novel missense mutation in Pitx2 that causes early-onset glaucoma via the NRF2-YAP1 axis, providing new insight into the mechanisms of glaucoma pathogenesis.
