Abstract: White-opaque switching in the human fungal pathogen Candida albicans is a stochastic transition between two heritable cell states that has typically been studied by scoring colony sectors after plating single cells, an approach that registers past switching events but provides no insight into how the cell that founded a sector differed from surrounding cells. We set out to observe switching at the single-cell level using microfluidic plates with specialized barrier traps containing switching-competent C. albicans a/Δ diploid cells. As the experiment begins, traps contain a few cells that divide and eventually fill the trap; after approximately 12 hours, each trap holds approximately 400 cells, and imaging is continued for an additional 12 hours, during which cells continue to divide and push excess cells out of the trap, where they flow away. This setup enables observation of thousands of divisions in each trap and, by monitoring many traps, captures detailed instances of stochastic white-opaque switching despite the rarity of these events. Switching events were followed in a strain homozygous for a Wor1-green fluorescent protein fusion protein; Wor1 is a master regulator of white-opaque switching whose expression is 40-fold higher in opaque cells compared to white cells. Experiments beginning with cells from white colonies showed no visible GFP signal, while those beginning with cells from opaque colonies had uniformly high levels of GFP signal, allowing unambiguous identification of switching events and quantification of the increase in Wor1 expression during transitions. These single-cell observations reveal that multiple molecular events underlie stochastic switching between the two heritable cell states in fungi.
