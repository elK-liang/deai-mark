We characterized the landscape of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) regulators in small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and their molecular characteristics, immuno-oncology features and clinical relevance. We curated a catalog of 30 m6A regulators, including 11 writers, two erasers and 17 readers. Somatic mutations of the 30 regulators were explored in 110 SCLC samples: 28 samples exhibited mutations of m6A regulators, with a mutation frequency of 25.5%, and readers showed relatively high mutation frequencies, with FMR1 showing the highest, whereas no erasers exhibited mutations; co-occurrent mutations were noted between METTL3 and YTHDC2 and between IGF2BP2 and YTHDC2. Copy number variation (CNV) analysis in 53 SCLC cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia showed universal CNV alterations, with most readers (11/17) displaying widespread amplification and all erasers showing more frequent loss. Principal component analysis revealed a dramatically different expression pattern between normal lung and SCLC samples, and nearly all writers and readers were significantly upregulated in SCLC while eraser expression tended to decline, indicating that SCLC is associated with abundant m6A modification; combining CNV and expression patterns suggests that CNV alterations contribute to perturbations in m6A regulator expression. IGF2BP3 was significantly higher in SCLC, extending to this tumor a phenomenon found in multiple solid tumors. Most writers and readers also showed an upward distribution in metastasis-derived compared with primary-derived SCLC cell lines. These findings define the m6A regulator landscape of SCLC and support m6A regulators as biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets.
