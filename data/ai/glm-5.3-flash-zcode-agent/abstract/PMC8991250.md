Glioblastoma (GBM) is characterized by molecular features such as amplification of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), often in association with the EGFRvIII variant, which may cause drug and apoptosis resistance hindering therapeutic success. N6-isopentenyladenosine (iPA), a naturally occurring modified nucleoside, was investigated here for its antiproliferative and cytotoxic activity in GBM cells representing these backgrounds: U87MG, U87MG engineered to overexpress wild-type EGFR (U87EGFRwt), and U87MG overexpressing EGFRvIII (U87EGFRvIII). MTT and BrdU incorporation assays showed that iPA inhibited cell viability and DNA synthesis in a dose- and time-dependent manner compared with vehicle-treated cells, with IC50 values of 3 µM for U87MG, 4.3 µM for U87EGFRwt and 7.1 µM for U87EGFRvIII after 48 hours of treatment. No significant cytotoxic effects were found at 1 and 2.5 µM, and iPA did not elicit a significant reduction in the proliferation of normal human astrocytes, indicating selectivity toward tumor cells. Cell cycle analysis revealed that exposure to 10 µM iPA caused an increase in the G0/G1 fraction at 24 hours, whereas at 48 hours cells were reduced in G0-G1 and significantly accumulated in the G2/M phase compared with untreated cells. Together, these findings show that iPA effectively counteracts the growth of EGFR-expressing GBM cells, including cells harboring the EGFRvIII resistance variant, by inducing cell death through necroptosis, and support iPA as a candidate therapeutic strategy for glioblastoma.
