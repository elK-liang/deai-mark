Hepatoblastoma is the most common primary malignant liver tumor of childhood, typically arising in the first three years of life. Treatment combines platinum-based chemotherapy with surgical resection or transplantation, and survival has improved substantially over recent decades. Nevertheless, patients with high-risk disease, including metastatic or refractory tumors, continue to fare poorly, and current risk stratification relies mainly on clinical stage, alpha-fetoprotein levels, and histology. Molecular markers that refine risk assessment and reveal actionable biology are therefore needed, and attention has increasingly turned to the genomic and epigenomic features of these tumors.

An emerging body of work in adult cancers has established that the tumor immune microenvironment is a determinant of prognosis and of the response to immunotherapy. In pediatric tumors, however, and in hepatoblastoma in particular, the composition and regulation of the immune microenvironment remain poorly characterized. Whether hepatoblastomas display immune-excluded or immune-inflamed phenotypes, and which genetic and epigenetic features underlie these differences, is unknown. Addressing this question is timely, as immunotherapeutic approaches are beginning to be explored in children with refractory solid tumors.

Both genetic alterations and epigenetic dysregulation have been implicated in hepatoblastoma pathogenesis. Transcriptomic studies have delineated molecular subclasses of the disease, and aberrant DNA methylation is increasingly recognized as a driver of pediatric tumor biology, shaping developmental gene expression programs and potentially modulating the interaction of tumor cells with their microenvironment. Integrating gene expression and methylation data from public repositories offers an efficient strategy to identify tumor-specific molecular alterations and to connect them with immune-related signatures.

In this study, we integrated multiple public microarray and sequencing datasets to define the genomic and epigenomic landscape of hepatoblastoma. By comparing tumor and normal liver samples from two independent expression datasets, we identified 134 commonly upregulated and 191 commonly downregulated genes. Methylation profiling of an additional dataset revealed 1125 hypermethylated and 2337 hypomethylated sites in tumors, from which we derived 83 differentially methylated and expressed genes. Building on this integrated molecular characterization, we developed and validated genomic and epigenomic signatures associated with the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatoblastoma, providing new molecular tools for risk stratification and a foundation for microenvironment-targeted therapeutic strategies in this rare pediatric cancer.
