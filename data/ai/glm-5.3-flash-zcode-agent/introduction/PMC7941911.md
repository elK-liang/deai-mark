Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) has become the most common chronic liver disorder worldwide, paralleling the global rise in obesity and metabolic syndrome. The disease encompasses a spectrum ranging from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), the inflammatory form characterized by hepatocellular injury, lobular inflammation, and progressive fibrosis. NASH can progress to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma, and no approved pharmacological therapy is yet available. Understanding the molecular mechanisms that drive the transition from bland steatosis to inflammatory steatohepatitis is therefore a central goal of hepatology research.

Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress is increasingly recognized as a key event in this transition. Lipid overload disrupts ER homeostasis, triggering the unfolded protein response and downstream signaling through c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), pathways that promote inflammation, hepatocyte apoptosis, and insulin resistance. In animal models and human NASH, ER stress markers correlate with disease severity, and pharmacological relief of ER stress attenuates hepatic inflammation. However, the regulatory networks that control ER stress signaling in the fatty liver, and their dysregulation in NASH, remain incompletely defined.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are emerging as important regulators of inflammatory and metabolic liver disease. lncRNAs modulate gene expression at transcriptional, post-transcriptional, and epigenetic levels, and hepatic lncRNA expression is extensively altered in models of steatosis and steatohepatitis. Nevertheless, the majority of differentially expressed lncRNAs in NAFLD and NASH are functionally unannotated, and their contribution to the inflammatory activation that distinguishes NASH from simple fatty liver has not been systematically explored. Genome-wide lncRNA profiling across healthy, steatotic, and steatohepatitic states offers a route to identify candidates whose expression tracks disease progression.

In this study, we performed lncRNA and mRNA microarray profiling of liver tissue from mouse models of NAFLD and NASH, using DB/DB mice on a normal diet as a model of hepatic steatosis and DB/DB mice on a methionine- and choline-deficient diet as a model of steatohepatitis, confirmed by histology, F4/80 immunohistochemistry, and elevated serum transaminases. Among thousands of differentially expressed lncRNAs, we identified a subset whose expression increased progressively from normal liver to NAFLD to NASH and focused on the novel lncRNA Gm9795. We demonstrate that lncRNA-Gm9795 promotes hepatic inflammation in NASH by activating the NF-κB/JNK pathway through induction of ER stress, establishing it as a candidate mediator and biomarker of steatohepatitis progression.
