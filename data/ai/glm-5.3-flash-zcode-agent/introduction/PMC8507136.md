Transcription factors organize gene expression into coherent programs, and identifying the set of genes controlled by a given transcription factor is a central step toward understanding its biological function. Comparing genome-wide expression patterns between cells in which the factor is present and cells in which it has been removed is an effective and widely used strategy for this purpose. The advent of CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing has made such perturbations feasible in many systems, allowing transcription factor-regulated gene networks to be delineated directly in human cells rather than inferred from binding motifs or indirect correlations.

CREB1, the cAMP response element-binding protein 1, is a stimulus-responsive transcription factor that regulates transcription following its phosphorylation by cAMP-dependent kinases. It controls genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, metabolism, and differentiation, and its activity has been linked to tumor biology in a growing number of cancer types. Dysregulated CREB signaling has been associated with oncogenic phenotypes, and CREB1-sensitive transcriptional programs have been proposed to contribute to tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance. However, the genome-wide set of genes regulated by CREB1 in human cells, and the relationship of this set to cancer-associated gene signatures, has not been systematically characterized.

An important practical obstacle to defining CREB1-regulated networks is the apparent essential function of CREB1 in normal cells and in some cancer cell lines, which has hampered the successful establishment of CREB1 knockout cells via CRISPR/Cas9. This difficulty has left the field reliant on partial perturbations or indirect evidence, and motivates an experimental system in which the factor can be removed completely and its expression restored under controlled conditions.

In this study, we established a robust experimental platform for defining the CREB1-regulated gene network. Using the cervical cancer cell line HeLa, whose CREB1 expression is comparable to that of many CREB1-sensitive cancer cell lines, we generated three independent CREB1 knockout clones by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated deletion of all alleles, targeting sequences in intron 1 and the proximal promoter region containing the TATA box, and verified the absence of CREB1 protein by western blot. Lentiviral rescue lines re-expressing CREB1 were generated for each knockout clone. We then performed RNA sequencing across these matched knockout and rescue lines to delineate the CREB1-mediated gene network, and systematically compared this network with publicly available cancer gene expression datasets. Our analysis reveals a strong and previously underappreciated relevance of CREB1-regulated genes to human cancer.
