Metastasis is responsible for the great majority of cancer deaths, and its lethal outcome is largely determined before tumor cells ever colonize a distant organ. Two decades ago it was recognized that primary tumors can systemically instruct distant tissues to become permissive for future colonization, a state termed the pre-metastatic niche (PMN). In organs destined to receive disseminating tumor cells, such as the lung, the PMN is characterized by inflammatory signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, vascular leakiness, and the accumulation and activation of stromal cells, most prominently fibroblasts. Activated fibroblasts deposit and reorganize matrix components and secrete cytokines and growth factors, thereby constructing a microenvironment that supports the survival, arrest, and outgrowth of arriving tumor cells. Interfering with the formation of the PMN, and with fibroblast activation in particular, has therefore emerged as a strategy to prevent metastatic relapse.

Nanomaterials that localize to pathological tissues offer a means of intervening in this process at the microenvironmental level. Among them, self-assembling peptides are attractive because of their programmability, biocompatibility, and responsiveness to disease-associated cues. Matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP2) is prominently overexpressed in inflammatory and remodeling tissues, including pre-metastatic sites, and can be exploited as a trigger for site-specific assembly or drug release. We hypothesized that an MMP2-activatable peptide could be designed to assemble selectively within the PMN and, by coating intercellular structures in situ, impede the activation of resident fibroblasts that drives niche formation.

Here we report the design of such a molecule, termed FR17, a branched peptide that combines three functional elements: a self-assembling Phe-Phe-Lys-Tyr backbone derived from the amyloidogenic core motif, the immunomodulatory pentapeptide thymopentin (TP5), which is used in the clinic as an adjunctive agent to prevent postoperative infection and to activate immune responses, and an MMP2-cleavable linker connecting the two. Upon cleavage by MMP2, FR17 releases a self-assembling monomer that spontaneously folds into membrane-like lamellar structures, which we term the peptide nano-blanket, under both neutral and mildly acidic conditions mimicking the inflammatory PMN microenvironment. Electron microscopy revealed nanoscale sheets with an average diameter of approximately 500 nm, and in vivo imaging confirmed the responsive assembly of the nano-blanket in pre-metastatic lung tissue.

In this study, we characterize the formation of the peptide nano-blanket and demonstrate that it impedes fibroblast activation and the subsequent establishment of the pre-metastatic niche, leading to reduced metastatic burden. Our results establish MMP2-triggered peptide assembly as a viable approach for microenvironment-targeted antimetastatic intervention.
