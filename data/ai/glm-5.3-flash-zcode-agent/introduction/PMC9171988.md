Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common histological subtype of non-small cell lung cancer and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Despite advances in molecularly targeted therapy and immunotherapy, many patients are diagnosed at advanced stages and eventually develop progression and treatment resistance. Identification of molecules that drive LUAD invasion, migration, and metastasis therefore remains essential both for prognostic stratification and for the development of new therapeutic targets.

Junctional adhesion molecule-like protein (JAML) is a member of the carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule (CAR) family of junctional adhesion molecules, a group of immunoglobulin-like transmembrane proteins involved in cell adhesion and leukocyte activation. JAML was initially characterized in epithelial tight junctions and on activated myeloid cells, where it participates in barrier organization and inflammatory signaling. Given that classical adhesion molecules have repeatedly been shown to influence tumor cell motility, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and metastatic behavior, the possible involvement of JAML in cancer has attracted increasing attention. Its expression pattern and functional role in lung cancer, however, have remained unexplored.

The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is a central regulator of development and homeostasis, and its aberrant activation is a well-established driver of tumor initiation and progression in many malignancies, including lung cancer. Abnormal β-catenin accumulation and nuclear translocation promote the transcription of genes controlling proliferation, survival, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and invasion. Whether JAML interacts with this pathway in LUAD cells has not been investigated.

In this study, we examined JAML expression in LUAD tissues and cell lines and defined its clinical and functional significance. Quantitative RT-PCR and western blotting demonstrated that JAML was significantly elevated in 15 pairs of LUAD tumor tissues compared with matched peritumor tissues, and immunohistochemical analysis of 30 pairs of samples confirmed its upregulation at the protein level. High JAML expression was significantly correlated with advanced pT stage and pTNM stage. In vitro, JAML was expressed in the cytoplasm of LUAD cells, and knockdown or overexpression experiments in appropriately selected cell lines showed that JAML promotes LUAD cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Mechanistically, we found that JAML activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, identifying JAML as an oncogenic regulator of tumor progression in lung adenocarcinoma.
