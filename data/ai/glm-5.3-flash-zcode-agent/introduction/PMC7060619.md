Tuberculosis remains the leading infectious cause of death worldwide, and the emergence and spread of drug-resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis threatens the control of the epidemic. The diagnosis of drug resistance has therefore moved rapidly toward molecular and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) approaches, which detect resistance-associated mutations in days rather than weeks and are increasingly used to guide individualized therapy. The accuracy of these approaches, however, depends fundamentally on the completeness and reliability of the catalog of mutations used to predict resistance.

Genes implicated in antibiotic resistance in the M. tuberculosis complex contain two kinds of sequence variation. Some mutations directly cause resistance, while others are merely polymorphisms that have accumulated along particular phylogenetic lineages without any phenotypic consequence, so-called benign or phylogenetically informative mutations. When such lineage-specific variants are misinterpreted as resistance determinants, molecular diagnostics produce false-positive resistance calls, leading to unnecessary regimen changes, prolonged ineffective therapy, and inflated estimates of resistance prevalence. Distinguishing causal mutations from phylogenetic bystanders is thus a central task in the interpretation of WGS-based resistance prediction, and the ancestors of the M. tuberculosis complex, including human-adapted lineages 1, 5, 6, and 7 and the animal-adapted species, carry a disproportionately large share of undercharacterized variation because most resistance catalogues have been built from modern lineage 2-4 strains.

Comparative genomics across the full breadth of the M. tuberculosis complex offers a systematic route to this distinction. Mutations shared by all members of a monophyletic group, particularly groups defined by well-supported internal branches of a robust phylogeny, are unlikely to have arisen repeatedly under drug pressure and can be flagged as phylogenetically informative, while mutations occurring on terminal branches in diverse genetic backgrounds are more consistent with causal resistance determinants. Constructing such a resource requires a globally representative collection spanning ancestral and modern lineages, animal-adapted species, and resistant isolates.

Here, we analyzed 405 M. tuberculosis complex genomes representing seven major human lineages and 14 animal-adapted species, including 296 evolutionary modern and 109 ancestral strains. Using a maximum likelihood phylogeny based on 42,760 chromosomal SNPs, we identified 334 well-supported internal branches and systematically cataloged 557 group-specific mutations within 92 genes implicated in antibiotic resistance, excluding mutations attributable to recent transmission. We classify these mutations as candidate causal resistance determinants or phylogenetically informative variants, providing a refined reference for WGS-based drug resistance prediction across the M. tuberculosis complex.
