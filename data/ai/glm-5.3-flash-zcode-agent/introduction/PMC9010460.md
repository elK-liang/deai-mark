Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for the majority of cases. Although molecularly targeted therapies and immune checkpoint inhibitors have improved outcomes for selected patients, most lung cancers still progress, and the molecular determinants of invasion and metastasis in lung adenocarcinoma remain incompletely understood. A better definition of the pathways controlling tumor cell invasiveness is therefore essential for the development of new prognostic markers and therapeutic targets.

Post-translational modification of proteins by ubiquitin controls virtually every cellular process, and its reversal by deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) has emerged as a critical layer of regulation in cancer biology. Among the roughly one hundred human DUBs, ubiquitin-specific protease 15 (USP15), a member of the USP family, has attracted increasing attention. USP15 participates in the regulation of TGF-beta signaling, chromatin dynamics, protein stability, and autophagy, and both tumor-suppressive and oncogenic functions have been ascribed to it in different contexts. Recent pan-cancer analyses suggest that USP15 expression is altered in several malignancies, but its role in lung cancer, where its expression and functional relevance have not been systematically examined, remains unclear.

Autophagy occupies a complex position in tumor progression, acting as a survival mechanism under metabolic stress but also suppressing early tumor development and invasive behavior in various settings. The initiation of autophagy is controlled by the Beclin1 (BECN1) protein complex, whose activity is regulated by post-translational modifications. TRAF6, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, has been shown to ubiquitinate BECN1 and thereby promote autophagy induction. Whether a deubiquitinating enzyme counteracts this modification, and whether such an axis operates in lung cancer cells, has not been established.

Here we show that USP15 is significantly downregulated in lung adenocarcinoma and lung squamous cell carcinoma, among a small number of cancer types identified by pan-cancer analysis of TCGA data, and that low USP15 expression is associated with reduced survival of lung cancer patients. Analysis of primary NSCLC specimens confirmed reduced USP15 levels in lung adenocarcinoma. Using CRISPR/Cas9-generated USP15-knockout A549 and H1299 cells, we demonstrate that USP15 deficiency promotes lung cancer cell invasion, and we identify a TRAF6-BECN1 signaling axis through which USP15 induces autophagy and negatively regulates lung cancer progression.
