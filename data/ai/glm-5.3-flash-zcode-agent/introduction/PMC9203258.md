SARS-CoV-2, the causative agent of COVID-19, has caused a global pandemic of unprecedented scale, and the continuing emergence of variants has repeatedly challenged vaccines and antiviral drugs that target viral components. Because the virus encodes only a limited number of proteins, it depends extensively on host cell machinery at every step of its life cycle. Host-oriented factors are attractive therapeutic targets in principle, since they are less prone to escape mutations than viral proteins and may offer activity that is conserved across variants. Identifying such dependency factors therefore remains a central goal of SARS-CoV-2 research.

Among host processes co-opted by RNA viruses, lipid metabolism occupies a prominent position. Viruses remodel cellular membranes to build replication organelles, alter lipid composition to facilitate entry and egress, and perturb lipid-derived signaling to shape the antiviral immune response. Prior work on SARS-CoV-2 and related coronaviruses has reported reprogramming of fatty acid synthesis and cholesterol handling, but these observations have typically been confined to selected lipid classes, individual cell models, or specific viral variants. A systematic, quantitative, and global view of how infection reshapes the host lipidome, and of which changes are shared rather than variant-specific, is still lacking.

To address this gap, we performed global lipidomic profiling in two human cell lines expressing ACE2, collected 24 hours after SARS-CoV-2 infection and in matched mock-infected controls. Cellular lipids were extracted and quantified by liquid chromatography coupled to electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry, with abundance normalization to internal standards. In HEK293T-ACE2 cells we identified 514 unique lipids spanning the glycerolipid, phospholipid, sphingolipid, and acylcarnitine categories, of which 409, corresponding to 79.6 percent, were significantly altered upon infection, changing between 2- and 64-fold. In the more physiologically relevant A549-ACE2 cells, 443 unique lipids were detected and 227 were significantly altered, with fold changes between 1.1 and 19.7. Principal component analysis confirmed that infection status, rather than technical variation, accounted for most of the observed differences in both cell lines.

Building on this lipid map, we reasoned that lipids and lipid-modifying enzymes whose perturbation is shared across viral variants would represent candidate host dependency factors relevant to all circulating strains. In the following, we characterize the architecture of the SARS-CoV-2-induced lipid landscape, integrate it with functional perturbation approaches, and prioritize conserved host factors as potential targets for broad-spectrum antiviral intervention.
