Junctophilin-2 (JP2) is an essential junctional protein of cardiomyocytes that physically tethers the plasma membrane to the junctional sarcoplasmic reticulum, stabilizing the dyadic architecture on which excitation-contraction coupling depends. By anchoring L-type calcium channels and ryanodine receptors in close apposition, JP2 ensures efficient calcium-induced calcium release, and its downregulation is a consistent feature of hypertrophy and heart failure, where loss of JP2 disrupts dyadic organization and contributes to contractile dysfunction and arrhythmogenesis.

In stressed myocardium, JP2 becomes a substrate for calpains, calcium-activated neutral proteases whose activity is elevated under conditions of calcium overload. Calpain-mediated cleavage of JP2 has been documented in animal models and human heart disease and is generally regarded as pathogenic, because truncation of the protein compromises its membrane-anchoring function. A growing body of work suggests that the cleavage fragments themselves may be biologically active: N-terminal fragments of JP2 have been reported to translocate to the nucleus, where they may influence gene expression. Accurate identification of the cleavage fragments generated in vivo is therefore a prerequisite for understanding the consequences of JP2 proteolysis.

Existing knowledge of the JP2 cleavage pattern is incomplete, for methodological reasons. Most studies have relied on immunoblotting against individual JP2 epitopes or on fusion tagging, approaches that are confounded by alternative proteolytic pathways, by species-limited and affinity-biased antibody choices, and by the anomalous migration of the highly charged JP2 protein in SDS-PAGE gels. Computational tools that predict calpain cleavage sites, such as DeepCalpain, indicate that the joining and divergent regions of JP2 contain multiple additional candidate sites beyond those identified experimentally, implying that the full spectrum of calpain-generated fragments has not been characterized.

In this study, we established a directly controlled, substrate-reaction system to define the calpain cleavage pattern of JP2 comprehensively. Full-length recombinant mouse JP2 was expressed and purified, molecularly confirmed, and exposed to purified Calpain-1 and Calpain-2 across four orders of magnitude of protease concentration under constant reaction conditions, with total protein staining capturing the complete cleavage pattern. We found that Calpain-2 cleaves JP2 at a hierarchy of calcium-dependent sites, generating a spectrum of N-terminal (NT1, NT2, NT3) and C-terminal (CT1, CT2) fragments whose composition depends on protease concentration. We further demonstrate that the NT1 fragment accumulates in DNA-rich nuclear domains in cardiomyocytes, implicating specific JP2 cleavage products in nuclear signaling during cardiac stress.
