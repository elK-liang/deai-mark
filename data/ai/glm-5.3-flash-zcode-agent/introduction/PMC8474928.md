N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant internal modification of eukaryotic messenger RNAs and a central layer of post-transcriptional gene regulation. The m6A landscape is installed by writers, chiefly the METTL3-METTL14 complex, removed by erasers such as FTO and ALKBH5, and interpreted by readers including the YTH domain family, which determine the fate of modified transcripts in splicing, export, stability, and translation. Through these mechanisms, m6A modification controls virtually every aspect of cellular physiology, and its dysregulation has been increasingly implicated in cancer initiation, progression, metastasis, and the response to therapy.

Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is the most aggressive histological subtype of lung cancer, characterized by rapid doubling time, early and widespread metastasis, and initial sensitivity followed by swift acquisition of chemotherapy resistance. Despite modest improvements with the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors and targeted agents in selected molecularly defined subsets, the prognosis of SCLC remains poor, and the molecular determinants of its aggressive behavior are incompletely understood. In contrast to non-small cell lung cancer, SCLC has been largely excluded from systematic analyses of RNA modification regulators, and the landscape of m6A biology in this disease has never been comprehensively characterized.

Emerging evidence indicates that m6A modification also shapes the immune phenotype of tumors. Epigenetic regulators influence the expression of immune modulators, the infiltration of immune cell populations, and the response to immunotherapy, raising the possibility that the m6A machinery contributes to the immuno-oncological features of SCLC, a tumor type in which durable immunotherapy benefit remains uncommon. Defining the alterations of m6A regulators and their relationship to immune characteristics and clinical outcome could therefore open new therapeutic avenues.

Here, we curated a catalog of 30 m6A regulators, comprising 11 writers, 2 erasers, and 17 readers, and systematically characterized their genomic and transcriptomic alterations in SCLC using patient samples and cell line data. We found a mutation frequency of 25.5 percent for m6A regulators in SCLC samples, universal copy number variation in SCLC cell lines, and widespread upregulation of writers and readers in tumors. We further define the association of the m6A regulator landscape with molecular characteristics, immune infiltration, immuno-oncology features, and clinical outcomes, establishing the m6A machinery as a biologically and clinically relevant axis in small cell lung cancer.
