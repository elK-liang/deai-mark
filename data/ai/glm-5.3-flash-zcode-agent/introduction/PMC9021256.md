Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a myeloproliferative neoplasm driven by the BCR-ABL fusion tyrosine kinase, the product of the Philadelphia chromosome. Targeted therapy with ABL tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), first with imatinib (IM) and subsequently with second- and third-generation inhibitors, has transformed CML from a fatal disease into a chronic condition for most patients. Nevertheless, a clinically important fraction of patients develop resistance, most frequently through point mutations in the BCR-ABL kinase domain. The T315I mutation, the so-called gatekeeper mutation, confers resistance to most available TKIs and remains one of the most challenging problems in CML management, motivating the search for therapeutic strategies that act independently of kinase binding.

Increasing evidence indicates that oncogenic transformation imposes characteristic metabolic requirements on cancer cells, and that drug resistance is frequently accompanied by metabolic reprogramming. Our previous work suggested that BCR-ABL mutation, especially T315I, causes metabolic alterations associated with changes in drug sensitivity, raising the possibility that the resistant phenotype is sustained not only by reduced drug binding but also by a rewired metabolic state that could be targeted pharmacologically. Among metabolic vulnerabilities, enhanced glycolytic flux, the long-recognized Warburg-type dependence of cancer cells on glucose fermentation, is particularly amenable to inhibition with agents such as 2-deoxyglucose (2-DG), which targets hexokinase at the entry point of glycolysis.

Inhibition of energy metabolism engages cellular stress responses, foremost among them macroautophagy, a lysosomal degradation process that recycles cytoplasmic material and buffers cells against energetic stress. Autophagy has dual roles in cancer treatment: it can promote survival under stress, but its induction can also contribute to cell death in defined contexts, and metabolic interventions that trigger autophagy have been linked to sensitization of resistant leukemia cells to therapy. How glycolytic inhibition interacts with autophagy in BCR-ABL-mutant, TKI-resistant CML cells, and whether such interaction can be exploited to restore drug sensitivity, has not been established.

In this study, we examined the impact of glycolytic inhibition on IM sensitivity of CML cells carrying wild-type BCR-ABL or the T315I mutation. We show that 2-DG and IM act synergistically in both contexts, with combination indexes below 1.0, and that similar 2-DG IC50 values in sensitive and resistant cells indicate the absence of co-resistance, supporting the use of 2-DG to overcome IM resistance. We further demonstrate that the combination induces autophagy, identify the contribution of autophagy to the synergistic killing of resistant cells, and establish modulation of energy metabolism as a strategy to overcome drug resistance in CML.
