The rapid expansion of clinical genome and exome sequencing has generated an ever-growing volume of sequence variants whose clinical significance must be assessed before results can be used in patient care. Variant interpretation has consequently become a principal bottleneck of genomic medicine: classification requires the integration of clinical, genetic, population, and functional evidence according to defined rules, and inconsistency between laboratories and clinicians has been widely documented. Standardized, transparent, and scalable curation infrastructure is therefore essential for the practice of medical genetics.

In 2015, the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) published a framework for the interpretation of sequence variants, specifying standard categories of pathogenicity together with criteria for combining evidence from different sources. Implementation of this framework, however, depends on tools that support curators in applying and documenting each criterion, on gene- and disease-specific adaptations of the general rules, and on mechanisms for expert review and public dissemination of results. The Clinical Genome Resource (ClinGen) program addresses these needs through expert panels that develop specifications for evidence evaluation in particular disease-gene contexts, and its curation process has received recognition from the US Food and Drug Administration.

To operationalize this process at scale, ClinGen developed the Variant Curation Interface (VCI), a web-based platform that guides curators through the assessment of evidence for individual variants, supports both individual and group curation, associates pertinent evidence with each classification according to variant curation disease- and gene-specific protocols, allows provisional records to be saved, supports expert review, and automatically publishes classifications and underlying criteria assessments to a public repository. Given the regulatory significance of variant classifications, the platform also maintains a complete audit trail of all curation actions in support of FDA recognition requirements.

Here we describe the development, architecture, and current state of the ClinGen Variant Curation Interface. The current version of the platform holds over 6300 variant classifications in different curation stages, and its modernized architecture is designed to scale to support over one million future classifications. We outline planned enhancements, including task management, bulk curation workflows, automated ingestion of variant evidence with monitoring of data changes, and customized curation experiences based on expert panel specifications, which together aim to increase the scale, throughput, and reproducibility of variant interpretation for the medical genetics community.
