Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is one of the major histological subtypes of esophageal cancer and a leading cause of cancer death worldwide, with particularly high incidence in parts of East Asia and Africa. Despite combined modality treatment with surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, the five-year survival of patients with advanced disease remains poor, largely because of early metastasis and treatment resistance. The molecular events underlying ESCC development and progression are incompletely characterized, and new molecular markers and therapeutic targets are urgently needed.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of cancer biology, participating in the control of proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, and metastasis through interactions with DNA, RNA, and proteins. Among the lncRNAs implicated in human cancer, DLEU1, located within a region of frequent deletion on chromosome 13q14 initially characterized in B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukemia, has attracted increasing attention. DLEU1 has been reported to be overexpressed in several solid tumors, where it contributes to cell proliferation and survival, but its expression pattern, clinical significance, and mechanism of action in ESCC have not been defined.

Evasion of apoptosis is a hallmark of cancer and a key determinant of treatment resistance. Dynein light chain LC8-type 1 (DYNLL1) is a highly conserved dimeric protein originally identified as a component of the dynein motor complex and subsequently shown to act as a hub that binds diverse pro-apoptotic and pro-survival proteins, thereby regulating apoptotic signaling. Whether lncRNAs control the abundance of DYNLL1, and whether such regulation contributes to the survival of carcinoma cells, has not previously been reported.

In this study, we screened differentially expressed lncRNAs in two independent cohorts of ESCC patients with matched adjacent normal tissues and identified DLEU1 as one of the most frequently overexpressed lncRNAs in ESCC, with DLEU1 upregulated in approximately 99 percent of tumors. High DLEU1 expression correlated with lymph node metastasis, advanced clinical stage, and reduced overall survival, findings confirmed in The Cancer Genome Atlas and by RNA-scope analysis. Functionally, we show that DLEU1 promotes ESCC cell survival and proliferation, and we elucidate the underlying mechanism: DLEU1 interacts with and prevents the degradation of DYNLL1, thereby inhibiting apoptosis and supporting tumor cell survival. Our findings identify the DLEU1-DYNLL1 axis as a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target in ESCC.
