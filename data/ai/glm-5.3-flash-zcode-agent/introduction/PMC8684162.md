Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is among the most common urological malignancies, and clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) constitutes its predominant histological subtype. Although localized disease can often be cured by surgical resection, a considerable proportion of patients present with or develop metastatic disease, for which the prognosis remains poor despite advances in targeted therapy and immune checkpoint blockade. A better understanding of the mechanisms driving ccRCC migration, invasion, and metastatic dissemination is therefore of substantial clinical importance.

Tumors are not merely collections of malignant cells but complex tissues shaped by their microenvironment. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are among the most abundant infiltrating immune cells in solid tumors and have a well-documented role in tumor progression. Macrophages exhibit remarkable plasticity and are commonly classified along a spectrum from the M1 phenotype, associated with inflammatory and antitumor activity, to the M2 phenotype, which supports tissue remodeling, immunosuppression, angiogenesis, and tumor progression. High M2 macrophage infiltration has been linked to adverse clinicopathological features in a range of malignancies, but the direct interactions between M2 macrophages and renal cancer cells, and the mediators responsible, remain incompletely characterized in ccRCC.

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), the process by which epithelial tumor cells lose polarity and cell-cell adhesion and acquire migratory and invasive properties, is a central mechanism of cancer dissemination. A reduction in the epithelial marker E-cadherin, together with the gain of mesenchymal markers, is a hallmark of this transition. Inflammatory stromal cells, including macrophages, can induce EMT in neighboring tumor cells through secreted factors, among which chemokines have emerged as important messengers. CXCL13 is a chemokine with recognized roles in lymphoid tissue organization and B-cell trafficking, and its potential contribution to the cross-talk between M2 macrophages and renal cancer cells has not been defined.

In this study, we show that M2 macrophage infiltration in ccRCC, assessed by immunohistochemical staining for the M2 marker CD206 in 55 tumor samples, is positively correlated with pathological grade and T stage. Using an in vitro co-culture system in which THP-1-derived macrophages are polarized toward the M2 phenotype, we demonstrate that M2 macrophages promote the proliferation, migration, and invasion of Caki-1 and ACHN ccRCC cells and induce EMT, as reflected by reduced E-cadherin expression. We further identify CXCL13, secreted by M2 macrophages, as a key mediator of these effects, implicating the M2 macrophage-CXCL13 axis in ccRCC progression.
