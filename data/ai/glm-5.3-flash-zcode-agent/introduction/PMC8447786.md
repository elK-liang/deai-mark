Primary breast lymphoma is a rare entity, accounting for a small fraction of all extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphomas and of breast malignancies. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is by far its most common histological subtype, and the breast represents a distinctive presentation site in which diagnostic work-up, staging, and treatment considerations differ from both nodal lymphoma and primary breast carcinoma. Because of its rarity, most available evidence derives from small single-center series and case reports, leaving many management questions unresolved.

Among patients with aggressive B-cell lymphoma, a subgroup defined by chromosomal rearrangements involving MYC together with BCL2 and/or BCL6, the so-called double-hit lymphomas, carries a particularly poor prognosis. These lymphomas respond poorly to standard immunochemotherapy, and intensified regimens such as dose-adjusted EPOCH-R or R-HyperCVAD, with or without consolidative autologous stem cell transplantation, have been proposed. However, the optimal treatment strategy remains contentious, and patients with double-hit lymphoma are generally underrepresented or excluded from prospective randomized trials. When such lymphomas arise in the breast, the evidence base is thinner still, and clinical practice is guided largely by extrapolation from studies of nodal disease.

Real-world multicenter data are therefore needed to describe how primary breast double-hit lymphoma is actually managed and with what outcomes. Such data are also essential for characterizing the biological features of this entity in the breast, including cell-of-origin distribution, the pattern of accompanying genetic aberrations detectable by next-generation sequencing, and the frequency of advanced-stage presentations, all of which inform risk stratification and the design of future trials.

In this study, we report the management and outcomes of 48 patients with primary breast double-hit lymphoma collected across multiple centers in a real-world setting. The cohort was characterized by a median age of 48 years and a predominance of germinal center B-cell-like disease, with 83.3 percent of patients classified as GCB type. Patients were treated with heterogeneous approaches, including DA-EPOCH-R-based regimens, R-HyperCVAD, and consolidation with autologous stem cell transplantation in a subset. After a median follow-up of 47 months, 28 patients had died. Here we analyze the clinical characteristics, treatment patterns, and survival outcomes of this cohort to provide a reference for the counseling and treatment of this uncommon but aggressive disease.
