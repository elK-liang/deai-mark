Unruptured intracranial aneurysms are increasingly detected due to the widespread availability of non-invasive vascular imaging, and the decision to treat requires weighing the risk of future rupture against the procedural risks of intervention. Aneurysms of the basilar tip represent one of the most challenging locations for treatment, as they arise at a deep-seated bifurcation of the vertebrobasilar circulation, are often oriented away from the parent vessels, and lie in close proximity to perforating arteries supplying the brainstem and thalamus. Both surgical clipping and endovascular therapy are technically demanding in this region, and endovascular treatment has become the most common approach.

Detachable coil embolization has long been the standard endovascular option for basilar tip aneurysms. However, bifurcation aneurysms frequently have an unfavorable dome-to-neck configuration, and coil packing in wide-necked lesions can be unstable, often requiring adjunctive techniques such as stent- or balloon-assistance. Stent-assisted coiling, while effective, necessitates dual antiplatelet therapy and adds procedural complexity. The Woven EndoBridge (WEB) device was developed specifically for wide-necked bifurcation aneurysms. As an intrasaccular flow-disrupting device, it is delivered within the aneurysm dome and spares the parent arteries and branch vessels, potentially avoiding the need for adjunctive stenting and its associated antithrombotic burden.

Most evidence on the safety and efficacy of WEB derives from large registries and single-arm studies that aggregate aneurysms across multiple locations, with basilar tip lesions forming only a subset. Comparisons between WEB embolization and standard coiling are often confounded by differences in aneurysm morphology, since unfavorable configurations tend to be selected for WEB treatment. Focused analyses restricted to a single anatomical location, in which the two techniques are compared under homogeneous conditions, remain scarce, particularly with respect to angiographic outcomes, procedural characteristics, and complication rates.

In this study, we compared WEB embolization with coil embolization for the treatment of unruptured basilar tip aneurysms in 75 patients. We analyzed baseline patient and aneurysm characteristics, procedural details including the need for adjunctive devices, treatment duration, radiation exposure and contrast use, as well as angiographic results and procedure-related complications. The aim was to determine whether the theoretical advantages of intrasaccular flow disruption translate into measurable procedural and safety benefits in this specific and demanding anatomical setting.
