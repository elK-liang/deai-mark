Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a debilitating, multisystem chronic illness characterized by profound fatigue, post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, pain, and cognitive impairment. The disease affects millions of people worldwide, yet its biological basis remains obscure, diagnosis rests on clinical criteria with exclusion of other conditions, and no approved treatment exists. The absence of objective molecular markers is a major obstacle to diagnosis, drug development, and recognition of the disease.

Accumulating evidence indicates that ME/CFS is accompanied by systemic disturbances of metabolism, immunity, and inflammation. Among metabolic alterations, lipid abnormalities are biologically plausible and increasingly documented: lipids are central to energy storage, membrane composition, and signaling, and lipid mediators derived from polyunsaturated fatty acids such as arachidonic acid and docosahexaenoic acid include both pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory eicosanoids and endocannabinoid-related species. Previous metabolomic and lipidomic studies in ME/CFS have reported perturbations of phospholipids, sphingolipids, and fatty acids, but findings have been inconsistent across cohorts, in part because influential determinants of the lipidome have not been systematically accounted for.

Sex is one such determinant. ME/CFS is diagnosed substantially more often in women than in men, and the plasma lipidome itself differs profoundly between the sexes in health, with women exhibiting higher phosphatidylcholine content and distinct patterns of lipid unsaturation and arachidonic acid metabolism. Most prior studies of ME/CFS lipidomics have combined sexes or analyzed small, predominantly female cohorts, potentially masking sex-specific disease signatures. A sex-stratified design, with age- and body mass index-matched cases and controls, is required to resolve disease-associated lipid alterations and to relate them to the clinical phenotype, including the cardinal symptoms of pain, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction.

In this study, we performed plasma lipidomic profiling in a cohort of ME/CFS patients and matched healthy controls comprising both sexes and multiple ancestral backgrounds, with all analyses stratified by sex. We found sex-specific disease signatures: in males, ME/CFS was associated with increased total hexosylceramides and elevated monounsaturated phosphatidylethanolamine and phosphatidylinositol species and saturated triglycerides, whereas in females, ME/CFS was associated with decreased phosphatidylethanolamine and hexosylceramides and reduced arachidonic acid-containing phosphatidylethanolamine. Several of these lipid species correlated with symptom severity, including pain, fatigue, and cognitive symptoms. These findings establish sex-specific plasma lipid signatures in ME/CFS and support lipidomics as a source of candidate biomarkers for the disease.
