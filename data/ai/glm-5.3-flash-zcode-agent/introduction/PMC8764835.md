Esophageal cancer is one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) accounts for the majority of cases globally, with a markedly uneven geographic distribution concentrated in parts of Asia and Africa. Despite advances in surgery, chemoradiotherapy, and perioperative systemic therapy, the overall prognosis remains poor, largely because most patients are diagnosed at advanced stages. Endoscopic surveillance can detect early disease, but its applicability is limited by cost and capacity in high-incidence regions, and existing serological markers lack adequate sensitivity and specificity. Minimally invasive biomarkers that enable earlier detection and inform treatment decisions therefore represent a significant unmet clinical need.

Body fluids contain abundant nucleic acid information released by tumors, and among liquid biopsy sources, saliva has distinctive advantages for cancers of the upper aerodigestive tract: it is completely noninvasive, can be collected repeatedly without trained personnel, and bathes the esophageal mucosa, making it a plausible reservoir of tumor-derived material. Exosomes, small membrane vesicles released by cells into extracellular fluids, protect their cargo from degradation and carry a rich repertoire of small RNAs, including microRNAs and, increasingly recognized, non-miRNA small RNAs such as tRNA-derived fragments and other small non-coding RNAs. Exosomal small RNAs in saliva may thus capture molecular signatures of esophageal tumors, but their systematic characterization in ESCC has not been performed.

Beyond diagnosis, molecular biomarkers have an expanding role in treatment planning. For patients with locally advanced ESCC, decisions about adjuvant therapy after resection or about pre-operative systemic treatment depend on risk estimation that currently relies on pathological staging alone. A pre-operative, noninvasive biomarker capable of identifying patients at higher risk could personalize these decisions, an application that has not been addressed by existing genomic signatures.

Here, we report the identification and validation of a signature of saliva-derived exosomal small non-coding RNAs for ESCC. In a pilot discovery phase, we profiled exosomes isolated from the saliva of ESCC patients and healthy volunteers, confirmed their identity by electron microscopy, marker protein analysis, and nanoparticle tracking, and identified 1366 differentially expressed non-miRNA small RNAs. Two candidates, the tRNA-derived fragment tRNA-GlyGCC-5 and a previously uncharacterized small RNA we termed sRESE, were validated by RT-qPCR in independent cohorts and emerged as a predictive biomarker signature. In a multicenter prospective study, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of this signature and its value as a pre-operative biomarker for guiding adjuvant therapy in ESCC.
