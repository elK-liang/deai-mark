Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS), also known as myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), is a debilitating, multisystem illness characterized by profound fatigue that is not relieved by rest, post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive difficulties. The condition severely impairs occupational and social functioning and is associated with marked reductions in quality of life. Despite decades of research, the etiology and pathophysiology of CFS remain poorly understood, diagnosis continues to rely on clinical criteria and the exclusion of other conditions, and no curative treatment is available. These uncertainties extend to basic epidemiology: estimates of disease frequency vary widely across countries, health care systems, and diagnostic criteria, and reliable population-based data are scarce.

Accurate epidemiological characterization is a prerequisite for research planning and health policy. Incidence and prevalence estimates inform the allocation of medical resources, guide clinicians who are often unfamiliar with the condition, and provide the demographic backbone for etiological studies. Nationwide claims databases offer a unique opportunity to obtain such estimates at the level of an entire population, because they capture physician-assigned diagnoses across all ages, sexes, and regions over long time periods, thereby avoiding the selection biases inherent in single-center or registry-based studies. South Korea maintains a single-payer national health insurance system with comprehensive claims records, which makes it well suited for this purpose.

Previous studies of CFS epidemiology have reported varying figures, with several suggesting a female predominance, but systematic nationwide data from East Asian populations are particularly limited. Differences in diagnostic practice, health-seeking behavior, and coding habits across countries further complicate comparisons, underscoring the value of longitudinal nationwide data from a single, unified health care system.

In this study, we used the Korean national health insurance claims database to characterize the epidemiological features of physician-diagnosed CFS in South Korea over an 11-year period from 2010 to 2020. We estimated annual incidence and prevalence rates for the whole population and stratified by sex, age, and calendar year. The mean annual number of newly diagnosed patients was 22,720, with a female-to-male ratio of 1.49, and the average annual incidence rate was 44.71 per 100,000 individuals, increasing 1.53-fold between 2010 and 2020. The absolute number of cases peaked in the 50-59 year age group in both sexes, while the highest age-specific rates were observed in the oldest male and the 60-69 year female populations. These data provide the most comprehensive nationwide picture to date of CFS in Korea and a reference point for international comparison.
