Colloidal quantum dots (QDs) are among the most promising solution-processed optical gain materials. Their size-tunable emission, high photoluminescence quantum yields, and compatibility with low-cost solution deposition make them attractive for lasers and light-emitting diodes spanning the visible spectrum. While optically pumped amplified spontaneous emission and lasing from QD films have been demonstrated for many years, the realization of electrically driven QD devices operating in the high-current regime required for optical gain remains a central challenge in the field.

Two interrelated obstacles stand in the way. First, under electrical pumping the gain threshold is reached only at very high current densities, at which multiexciton states dominate the emission dynamics and nonradiative Auger recombination rapidly depopulates the gain-active states. Second, high current densities cause substantial Joule heating in the device: heat buildup raises the lattice temperature, shifts the emission spectrum, degrades quantum yield, and can ultimately destroy the device. Progress therefore requires both QD materials engineered for fast optical gain and device architectures that allow very high current densities to be sustained without destructive overheating.

Recent developments on the material side have produced compact, shell-engineered QDs, so-called continuously graded QDs (cg-QDs), whose asymmetrically compressed cores modify the fine structure of band-edge excitonic states and shorten the single-exciton lifetime, while a thick radially graded shell prolongs the biexciton lifetime and provides a large electrical cross-section. These characteristics act together to reduce optical gain and lasing thresholds compared with standard, non-engineered QDs. Translating these material advantages into electrically driven devices, however, demands strategies for heat management at extreme current densities.

Here, we address this challenge by combining two complementary heat-management approaches: current focusing, in which the injection area is spatially confined to reduce heat-generating power and enhance heat exchange, and pulsed excitation, which limits heat accumulation according to the ratio of pulse duration to the characteristic heat dissipation time. Embedding one to four layers of CdSe/CdxZn1−xSe/ZnSe0.5S0.5 cg-QDs into an inverted p-i-n LED architecture, we demonstrate ultrabright electroluminescence at current densities approaching 1000 A cm−2 and, in the same material system, resolve optical gain in both single-exciton and biexciton bands. These results establish a route toward electrically pumped colloidal QD lasers and define the device and material parameters that govern high-current QD electroluminescence.
