Metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related death, and the primary goal of first-line treatment for most patients with unresectable disease is to prolong survival while preserving quality of life. For patients with RAS wild-type tumors, the anti-EGFR antibody cetuximab combined with chemotherapy is an established first-line option, offering high response rates. Response to treatment, however, has traditionally been assessed late, by conventional radiological criteria at eight or more weeks, and the relationship between the speed of tumor regression and the patient's own experience of the disease and its treatment has received comparatively little attention.

Early tumor shrinkage (ETS), defined as a rapid reduction in tumor burden observed at the first radiological assessment, has emerged as an early and easily measurable indicator of treatment activity. In patients receiving first-line chemotherapy with or without biologics, ETS has been associated with higher objective response rates, greater feasibility of subsequent metastatic resection, and favorable survival outcomes. Based on this evidence, early depth of response and tumor shrinkage are increasingly used to guide clinical decision-making. What remains largely unknown is whether patients whose tumors shrink early also feel better sooner: symptom relief and quality of life are central endpoints for patients with metastatic disease, yet the association between ETS and patient-reported outcomes has rarely been examined prospectively.

Quality of life in colorectal cancer is most commonly evaluated with the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire, which captures global health status, functioning scales, and symptom items including pain, fatigue, and diarrhea, symptoms that are particularly relevant for patients with chemotherapy-related toxicities and tumor-related burden. Prospective patient-reported outcome data collected from the start of treatment allow the trajectory of symptoms to be compared between patients who achieve early tumor shrinkage and those who do not.

The QUACK trial was a prospective phase II study of first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy in patients with unresectable mCRC, in which ETS was assessed at the first radiological evaluation and health-related quality of life was measured serially using the EORTC QLQ-C30. In the present analysis of 128 patients, ETS was achieved in 64.1 percent of cases and was associated with a markedly higher objective response rate and a higher rate of subsequent metastatic resection. Here we examine whether early tumor shrinkage was also reflected in the quality-of-life trajectories of these patients during the first months of treatment.
