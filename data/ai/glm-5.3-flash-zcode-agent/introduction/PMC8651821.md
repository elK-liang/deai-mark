High-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC) is the most lethal gynecologic malignancy. Although most patients respond initially to cytoreductive surgery combined with platinum-based chemotherapy, the majority relapse within a few years, and acquired platinum resistance is the central obstacle to long-term survival. Novel therapeutic targets, particularly those identified through systematic analysis of the molecular alterations that drive chemoresistance, are urgently needed for this disease.

The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is a surveillance mechanism that ensures accurate chromosome segregation during mitosis, and its key kinase, TTK (also known as Mps1), is essential for checkpoint signaling. By delaying anaphase until all chromosomes achieve proper kinetochore attachment, TTK safeguards genomic integrity, and inhibiting TTK forces cells through mitosis with unattached kinetochores, causing catastrophic chromosome missegregation and death, especially in genomically unstable cancer cells. Potent, selective TTK inhibitors have been developed and are being investigated in clinical trials, making TTK one of the few SAC components with a clinical-grade pharmacological inhibitor. Elevated TTK expression has been reported in several tumor types and associated with poor prognosis, but its role in ovarian cancer has not been systematically examined.

Beyond its mitotic function, TTK inhibition has recently been connected to autophagy, the lysosomal degradation pathway that recycles cytoplasmic components and is regulated upstream by the mTOR signaling network. Autophagy occupies a dual role in chemotherapy response: it can protect tumor cells from cytotoxic stress, and its modulation influences sensitivity to platinum agents. Whether TTK status influences autophagic activity and platinum sensitivity in HGSOC has not been explored, despite the potential therapeutic implications for combination strategies in chemoresistant disease.

In this study, we integrated gene expression profiles from two public GEO datasets of serous ovarian carcinoma and normal controls and identified 258 overlapping upregulated differentially expressed genes, with the cell cycle pathway as the most enriched. Among the cell cycle genes identified, TTK was the only one with a clinical-grade inhibitor under clinical investigation that had not previously been studied in HGSOC. We show that TTK is overexpressed in ovarian cancer tissues, that its expression is further increased in cisplatin-resistant cell lines, and that high TTK expression predicts poor progression-free and overall survival. Functionally, we demonstrate that TTK inhibition increases cisplatin sensitivity in HGSOC through modulation of the mTOR/autophagy pathway, supporting TTK as a therapeutic target and candidate biomarker in this disease.
