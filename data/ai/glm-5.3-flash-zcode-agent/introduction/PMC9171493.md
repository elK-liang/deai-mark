Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed malignancy and a leading cause of cancer death in women worldwide. Although advances in early detection and systemic therapy have improved survival, patients differ substantially in prognosis and treatment response, and better molecular tools for risk stratification, particularly tools that reflect tumor biology rather than clinicopathological stage alone, remain an important goal. In addition, growing interest in immunotherapy for breast cancer has sharpened the need for markers that characterize the immune microenvironment of individual tumors.

Cell death programs are increasingly recognized as determinants of tumor behavior and antitumor immunity. Beyond apoptosis, regulated necrotic cell death, most prominently necroptosis, mediated by the RIPK1, RIPK3, and MLKL signaling axis, releases intracellular contents that can stimulate inflammation and modulate immune infiltration. Necroptosis has been implicated in tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and response to therapy in multiple malignancies, and necroptosis-related genes have emerged as candidate prognostic markers. How necroptosis pathways are altered in breast cancer, and whether their activity can be harnessed for prognostication and immune phenotyping, has not been systematically examined.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are master regulators of gene expression involved in chromatin remodeling, transcription, and post-transcriptional control, and their dysregulation contributes to cancer development and progression. Because lncRNAs exhibit disease- and tissue-specific expression patterns and are detectable in clinical specimens, they constitute attractive candidates for prognostic signatures. lncRNAs involved in necroptosis regulation may therefore link a clinically relevant cell death pathway to the molecular classification of breast cancer, but such a connection has not been established.

In this study, we comprehensively analyzed necroptosis-related genes and lncRNAs in breast cancer using The Cancer Genome Atlas data comprising 1,096 breast cancer specimens and 112 normal specimens. We confirmed a set of 67 necroptosis-related genes from the literature, characterized their genomic alterations and differential expression, and identified 1,520 necroptosis-related lncRNAs significantly correlated with these genes, of which 46 were prognostic. Using LASSO-penalized Cox regression in a training cohort, we constructed a 22-lncRNA necroptosis-related signature that stratifies patients by risk. We further classified breast cancers into molecular subtypes based on necroptosis-related lncRNA expression, associated these subtypes with clinicopathological features, immune cell infiltration, and predicted immunotherapy response, and validated the prognostic performance of the signature, providing a necroptosis-centered framework for prognosis and immune microenvironment characterization in breast cancer.
