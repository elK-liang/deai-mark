The COVID-19 pandemic exposed a critical weakness in the clinical informatics infrastructure available at the onset of a novel disease outbreak. Predictive models that could anticipate which patients would deteriorate, require ventilation, or die would have supported triage and resource allocation during the earliest and most dangerous phase of the pandemic. Yet at that time, labeled data for the new disease were scarce, patient cohorts were small and institution-specific, and models trained on them generalized poorly. Each new pandemic threatens to recreate this cold-start problem, and preparing for it requires approaches that can leverage the vast clinical data already accumulated for existing diseases.

Electronic health records (EHRs) contain longitudinal, multi-modal information, including diagnoses, medications, procedures, and laboratory measurements, from which patient trajectories can be modeled. In recent years, deep learning architectures adapted from natural language processing, notably BERT-style models, have been applied to structured medical codes, treating a patient's record as a sequence of tokens in a language of clinical concepts. Such models capture temporal dependencies and higher-order interactions among clinical events and can be pretrained on large corpora before being fine-tuned on specific tasks. Hierarchical representations add the ability to organize medical concepts at multiple levels of granularity, matching the structure of medical coding systems.

Transfer learning provides the principled bridge between these capabilities and the pandemic cold-start problem. If the vocabulary of clinical concepts used by patients with a severe respiratory viral disease substantially overlaps that of COVID-19, a model pretrained on cohorts of similar existing diseases may transfer effectively to outcome prediction for the new disease, even before large COVID-specific datasets accumulate. Quantifying this vocabulary overlap and the behavior of transferred models across outcome tasks, patient subgroups, and care settings is a prerequisite for establishing transferability as a pandemic preparedness strategy.

In this study, we develop and evaluate a hierarchical multi-modal BERT model for COVID-19 outcome prediction and investigate transfer learning from cohorts of similar respiratory diseases (SRD). We quantify the similarity between the SRD and COVID-19 cohorts in terms of shared diagnosis, medication, procedure, and laboratory codes, demonstrate strong coverage of severe outcomes in the source cohort, and characterize demographic differences between the populations. We then show that pretraining on SRD data and fine-tuning on COVID-19 data yields effective models for two binary outcome classification tasks, supporting transfer learning from existing diseases as a viable strategy for preparing predictive infrastructure ahead of the next pandemic.
