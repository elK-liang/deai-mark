Melanoma is the most aggressive form of skin cancer, and its lethality is driven by the propensity of tumor cells to invade surrounding tissue and to metastasize early to distant organs. Although targeted therapies and immune checkpoint inhibitors have improved outcomes for advanced disease, many patients still progress, and the mechanisms that govern the transition of melanoma cells from a proliferative, in situ phenotype to an invasive, metastatic one remain incompletely understood. Characterizing the molecular regulators of this phenotypic switch is therefore of considerable clinical importance.

BORIS, also known as CTCFL, is a paralog of the ubiquitously expressed chromatin insulator protein CTCF and a canonical member of the cancer-testis antigen family. Under physiological conditions, BORIS expression is restricted to germ cells of the testis, where it participates in the regulation of imprinted gene methylation and spermatogenesis. In somatic tissues, BORIS is silent, but its aberrant reactivation has been documented in a wide range of human cancers, including lung, breast, prostate, and melanoma. Because BORIS can bind to CTCF sites and remodel chromatin architecture, its ectopic expression has the potential to rewire gene regulatory networks controlling proliferation, differentiation, and cell adhesion, but the functional consequences of BORIS expression in melanoma have not been defined.

Melanoma progression is characterized by dynamic shifts between cellular states. Proliferative melanoma cells expand within the epidermis and dermis, whereas invasive cells adopt motile, extracellular-matrix-remodeling properties that facilitate dissemination. This plasticity is governed by transcriptional and epigenetic reprogramming, and genes that reactivate embryonic or germline programs in cancer, such as cancer-testis antigens, are plausible candidates for promoting such state transitions. Establishing whether BORIS merely marks melanoma progression or actively drives it requires controlled perturbation experiments in appropriate cellular models.

Here, we first confirm that BORIS is aberrantly expressed in human melanoma tumor samples and cell lines, with significantly higher expression in metastatic than in primary melanoma, and its absence from non-malignant congenital nevi. We then establish a doxycycline-inducible system for BORIS expression in the MM057 melanoma cell line, which expresses low endogenous BORIS, and demonstrate that induced BORIS expression reduces cell proliferation in a time- and dose-dependent manner while promoting phenotypic features of invasion. Our findings indicate that BORIS promotes a switch from a proliferative towards an invasive phenotype in melanoma cells, implicating this cancer-testis antigen as a driver of melanoma progression.
