Kidney transplantation is the most effective renal replacement therapy for patients with end-stage renal disease, offering superior survival and quality of life compared with dialysis. Despite advances in immunosuppression, allograft rejection remains a major threat to long-term graft function and patient survival. The current gold standard for diagnosing rejection is core needle biopsy of the graft, interpreted according to standardized histological criteria. Biopsy, however, is an invasive procedure with inherent risks, is subject to sampling error, and cannot be repeated frequently enough to monitor the dynamic process of immune injury. These limitations have motivated an intensive search for non-invasive biomarkers that can detect rejection early, guide immunosuppressive adjustments, and reduce reliance on repeated biopsies.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as attractive candidates in this search. These small non-coding RNAs are stable in plasma and urine, participate in the post-transcriptional regulation of immune responses, and their expression patterns can reflect pathological processes in the graft. Although several miRNAs have been associated with rejection, most studies have evaluated individual miRNAs or isolated differential expression, which overlooks the fact that miRNAs act through coordinated regulation of large sets of target genes embedded in complex protein interaction networks. A single miRNA rarely operates in isolation; its functional importance depends on how many and which key targets it regulates within the cellular interaction landscape.

Systems-level approaches that integrate multiple omics layers offer a way to address this limitation. By combining differentially expressed miRNAs and mRNAs with miRNA-mRNA regulatory relationships and protein-protein interaction (PPI) data, it becomes possible to construct miRNA-mediated regulatory networks and to prioritize candidate biomarkers according to their topological position, for example their control over structurally important target genes. Such network-based prioritization provides biologically interpretable criteria for biomarker selection that go beyond fold-change statistics alone, and it can be tailored to the specific clinical context of kidney transplant recipients.

In this study, we applied a multi-omics network strategy to kidney transplant rejection. We constructed a kidney transplant recipient-specific miRNA-PPI integrated network by combining differentially expressed miRNAs and genes, miRNA-mRNA interactions, and PPI information. Using newly defined topological parameters, we identified miR-145-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-23b-3p as candidate biomarkers and evaluated their individual and combined performance for the diagnosis and subtyping of rejection in both a training dataset and an independent validation dataset. Our work provides a network-based framework for discovering miRNA biomarkers of rejection and offers mechanistic insight into the miRNA-mediated regulatory events underlying allograft injury.
