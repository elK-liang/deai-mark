Genomic sequencing of SARS-CoV-2 has been central to the global response to the COVID-19 pandemic. Real-time genomic surveillance underpinned the detection of emerging variants, informed vaccine and therapeutic development, and enabled the reconstruction of transmission chains in outbreak investigations. The scale of this effort, with millions of genomes generated worldwide, is unprecedented in public health genomics and was made possible by a coordinated framework of national sequencing consortia and standardized laboratory protocols.

The dominant approach to SARS-CoV-2 genome sequencing is based on multiplex PCR tiling, as implemented in the ARTIC protocol, followed by library preparation and sequencing on nanopore or short-read platforms. This strategy performs well at moderate scale, but the requirements of pandemic-scale surveillance expose its limitations. Library preparation steps that require individual handling of samples, quantification, and normalization constrain throughput, consume staff time, and introduce opportunities for error and contamination. As case numbers surged, laboratories needed methods that could process large batches of samples quickly, cheaply, and uniformly, while remaining compatible with both high-throughput sequencing instruments and rapid, small-batch workflows for time-critical outbreak samples.

Nanopore sequencing on the MinION offers long read lengths and rapid time-to-answer, and its utility for SARS-CoV-2 surveillance has been demonstrated widely. However, standard nanopore library preparation was designed for individual samples and adapts poorly to high-sample numbers. A method that combines the low input cost of transposase-based adapter tagging with highly multiplexed barcoding could in principle overcome this bottleneck, allowing dozens of samples to be processed simultaneously without per-sample manipulations, and could additionally be switched between nanopore and Illumina sequencing depending on throughput needs.

Here we present CoronaHiT, a high-throughput library preparation method for SARS-CoV-2 genome sequencing that couples transposase-based introduction of adapters with symmetric PCR barcoding of up to 96 samples per run on a MinION, with the option to switch the same barcoded products to Illumina sequencing for higher throughput. CoronaHiT requires no individual sample washes and allows samples to be processed uniformly without quantification or normalization. We validated the method on routine surveillance samples and on rapid response outbreak samples sequenced within 24 hours of receipt, compared coverage and accuracy against the ARTIC LoCost protocol on both nanopore and Illumina platforms, and demonstrate its suitability for large-scale, cost-effective SARS-CoV-2 genomic surveillance.
