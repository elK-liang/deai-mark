Cell size is a fundamental property of every cell type, and its regulation is essential for tissue architecture, organ function, and metabolic homeostasis. Although much has been learned about how proliferating cells couple biosynthesis and division to maintain size, the mechanisms that determine the size of terminally differentiated, non-dividing cells remain incompletely understood. Glial cells are a particularly instructive case: in the nervous system, glia envelop neurons, regulate their extracellular environment, and can attain remarkable sizes and complex morphologies, yet the genetic and molecular determinants of glial size have been largely unexplored.

The nematode Caenorhabditis elegans offers an experimentally tractable system in which to address this question. Its glia comprise a small number of individually identifiable cells, including the amphid sheath (AMsh) glia, which enwrap the sensory endings of the amphid organ and are required for the function of olfactory and thermosensory neurons. Because AMsh glia can be visualized in living animals and manipulated genetically with ease, perturbations in their size can be detected directly and linked to functional consequences for sensory behavior. Forward genetic screens in C. elegans have repeatedly proven effective at uncovering unanticipated biology, and we applied this unbiased approach to search for genes that control glial cell size.

We report the identification of a mutant, yad70, in which AMsh glia become progressively and dramatically enlarged during postembryonic development, eventually reaching roughly four times the size of their wild-type counterparts, with complete penetrance by early adulthood. The phenotype was specific to glia: neighboring amphid neurons retained normal size and number, the animals themselves were not proportionally enlarged, and nutritional conditions did not suppress the phenotype, indicating that enlarged AMsh glia reflect a cell-autonomous defect in size control rather than systemic overgrowth. Consistent with a functional role of AMsh glia in sensory neuron support, the mutant animals exhibited defects in chemotaxis and odorant avoidance behaviors mediated by the amphid sensory neurons.

Here we identify the gene disrupted in yad70 and show that eicosapentaenoic acid, an omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid, regulates glial size through a previously unrecognized mechanism centered on the Golgi apparatus. Our findings establish a link between fatty acid metabolism, Golgi organization, and the size control of differentiated glial cells, and provide a genetic entry point into the broader question of how non-dividing cells establish and maintain their size.
