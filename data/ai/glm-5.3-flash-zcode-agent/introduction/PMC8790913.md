Osteosarcoma is the most common primary malignant bone tumor in children and adolescents. Despite the introduction of multi-agent neoadjuvant chemotherapy several decades ago, which substantially improved survival, patients with metastatic or recurrent disease continue to face a dismal prognosis, and recent therapeutic advances have been limited. A deeper understanding of the molecular networks that drive osteosarcoma development and progression, and of how the tumor interacts with the immune system, is therefore needed to identify new prognostic markers and therapeutic targets.

Unlike many epithelial malignancies, osteosarcoma has long been considered immunologically inert, and it responds poorly to immune checkpoint blockade in most patients. An accumulating body of evidence nevertheless indicates that osteosarcoma is not devoid of immune infiltration, but rather exhibits a suppressed or skewed immune contexture. Transcriptomic studies of osteosarcoma consistently report downregulation of immune-related functions, including antigen presentation machinery, suggesting that the loss of immunological visibility, rather than the absence of immune cells, characterizes the osteosarcoma microenvironment. Characterizing the genes and regulatory networks responsible for this immune silencing, and their relationship to tumor behavior and patient prognosis, is a key step toward rational immunotherapeutic strategies.

Publicly available gene expression datasets provide the scale needed to dissect these networks reproducibly. Multiple independent osteosarcoma cohorts with matched normal osteoblastic controls now exist, and integrated analysis across them can separate reproducible disease-associated signatures from dataset-specific effects. Network-based prioritization of the resulting differentially expressed genes further allows transcription factors, adaptor molecules, and effector genes to be assembled into functional modules rather than treated as isolated markers.

In this study, we systematically analyzed four GEO datasets containing osteosarcoma tissues and normal controls, identified credible differentially expressed genes replicated in at least three datasets, and characterized their functional enrichments. The downregulated genes were prominently enriched in immune-related functions, including MHC class II protein complex and immune cell activation, confirming a loss of immune activity in osteosarcoma samples. Protein-protein interaction network analysis identified hub genes and functional clusters, from which we define a SPI1-TYROBP-FCER1G regulatory network and demonstrate its role in osteosarcoma oncogenesis and prognosis and its association with immune infiltration. Our findings link an immune adaptor-centered network to the clinical behavior of osteosarcoma and highlight it as a candidate target for immunotherapeutic intervention.
