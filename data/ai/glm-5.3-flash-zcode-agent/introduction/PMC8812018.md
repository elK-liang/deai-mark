Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies and a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide. Although screening and improved treatments have reduced mortality, a large proportion of patients still develop recurrence and metastasis, which remain the principal causes of death from this disease. The identification of molecular drivers of CRC proliferation and metastatic spread is therefore essential both for prognostic stratification and for the development of targeted therapies.

DDX10 belongs to the DEAD-box family of RNA helicases, a large class of RNA-dependent ATPases implicated in every aspect of RNA metabolism, from splicing and ribosome biogenesis to translation. DDX10 is best known for its involvement in ribosomal RNA processing and its association with Williams-Beuren syndrome, a developmental disorder, when deleted. Accumulating evidence from cancer genomics indicates that ribosome biogenesis factors and RNA helicases are frequently dysregulated in tumors, reflecting the elevated biosynthetic demands of malignant cells. However, the expression pattern, clinical relevance, and functional role of DDX10 in human cancer, and in colorectal cancer in particular, have remained unexplored.

Ribosomal proteins themselves have emerged as regulators of tumorigenesis beyond their canonical structural roles in the ribosome. Perturbation of individual ribosomal proteins can activate p53-dependent stress responses, alter translation programs, and influence cell proliferation and migration. Whether RNA helicases such as DDX10 influence cancer behavior through the regulation of specific ribosomal protein transcripts is an open question that connects ribosome biogenesis to malignant phenotypes.

Here, we show that DDX10 is consistently upregulated in colorectal cancer across multiple independent sources: matched tumor-normal analysis of 50 paired samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas, an additional GEO dataset, and several online databases; immunohistochemistry in clinical specimens confirmed elevated protein expression. High DDX10 expression was significantly associated with poorer disease-free survival, and DDX10 was more highly expressed in CRC cell lines than in normal intestinal epithelial cells. Functionally, we demonstrate that DDX10 promotes CRC cell proliferation and metastasis by regulating the splicing of the ribosomal protein transcript RPL35, revealing a pro-tumorigenic RNA helicase-ribosomal protein axis in colorectal cancer.
