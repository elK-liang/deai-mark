Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) is one of the most common inherited tumor predisposition syndromes, caused by loss-of-function mutations in the NF1 gene encoding neurofibromin, a negative regulator of RAS signaling. Patients with NF1 develop benign cutaneous and plexiform neurofibromas, and a major clinical concern is the transformation of plexiform neurofibromas into malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors (MPNSTs). MPNSTs are aggressive soft tissue sarcomas with a poor prognosis and limited response to conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and they represent a leading cause of mortality in the NF1 population. The molecular events that cooperate with neurofibromin loss to drive malignant progression remain incompletely defined, and effective targeted therapies are lacking.

Activation of the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK cascade is a hallmark of NF1-deficient cells, but the level of MAPK output varies substantially between benign and malignant lesions, implying that additional lesions amplify signaling during transformation. Members of the RAS regulation network, including the protein tyrosine phosphatase SHP-2 encoded by PTPN11, act upstream of RAS and RAF and have been implicated in oncogenic signaling in multiple tumor types. Similarly, BRAP, a BRCA1-associated modulator of RAS pathway activity, may influence the dependence of tumor cells on this axis. Defining how such alterations reshape MAPK signaling in a neurofibromin-deficient background could reveal actionable vulnerabilities.

Experimental modeling of MPNST progression has been constrained by the limited tumorigenic capacity of available NF1-mutant cell lines. The human MPNST cell line sNF96.2, which carries a frameshift mutation in the NF1 gene, generally shows low tumorigenic activity in mice and fails to form tumors upon subcutaneous or sciatic nerve injection, providing a baseline model of a minimally transformed, neurofibromin-deficient state. Whether a single additional genetic lesion in the RAS signaling network suffices to endow such cells with malignant behavior has not been established.

In this study, we show that mutation of PTPN11, encoding the SHP-2 phosphatase, promotes MEK activation and malignant progression of neurofibromin-deficient cells. Following implantation of sNF96.2 cells under the kidney capsule of an immunodeficient mouse, we derived the A-1 cell line, which displayed markedly increased proliferative activity, elevated phosphorylation of Akt and MEK, enhanced sensitivity to the MEK inhibitor trametinib, and substantially greater tumorigenicity than the parental cells, while retaining an identical STR profile. We further demonstrate that the oncogenic effect of SHP-2 activation is modulated by BRAP mutation status, indicating that the context of the RAS regulation network determines the response of NF1-deficient cells to pathway amplification.
