Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors are aggressive sarcomas arising in the context of neurofibromatosis type 1, and the molecular events that drive the transformation of indolent neurofibromin-deficient cells into high-grade sarcoma remain incompletely defined. In this study, we developed an experimental model of this transformation and identified mutation of PTPN11, encoding the SHP-2 tyrosine phosphatase, as an event that promotes MEK activation and malignant progression in neurofibromin-deficient cells, in a manner sensitive to BRAP mutation status.

The model we established from the sNF96.2 human NF1-mutant MPNST cell line recapitulates key features of the disease. Parental sNF96.2 cells, which harbor a frameshift mutation in NF1, generally show low tumorigenic activity and did not form tumors after subcutaneous or sciatic nerve injection. Only implantation under the kidney capsule of an immunodeficient mouse gave rise to a tumor, which developed slowly, appearing 154 days after injection, and manifested as a high-grade, atypical, spindle-shaped cell sarcoma with necrotic areas, a Ki-67 labeling rate exceeding 60%, and a histological appearance consistent with MPNST. The tumor was negative for S-100, Sox10, GFAP, and CD57, a pattern seen in about half of all human MPNST cases, which supports the diagnostic classification. The derived A-1 cell line, whose identity was confirmed by STR profiling to be identical to the parental line, displayed markedly increased proliferative activity in vitro and greatly enhanced tumorigenic activity in vivo, providing paired low- and high-malignancy models from a common origin.

The molecular comparison between A-1 and parental cells revealed constitutively elevated phosphorylation of both MEK and Akt, indicating activation of the Ras-MEK pathway, which is the expected signaling consequence of neurofibromin loss but is intensified in the progressed derivative. The demonstration that PTPN11 mutation drives this MEK activation is biologically coherent: SHP-2 is a well-established positive regulator of Ras signaling, and its oncogenic activation would compound the loss of neurofibromin-mediated Ras inhibition. The concurrent involvement of BRAP, a modulator of Ras pathway assembly, indicates that the intensity of MEK activation in neurofibromin-deficient cells depends on cooperating mutations within the same signaling module, an observation with implications for how genetic heterogeneity shapes aggressiveness in NF1-associated tumors.

The most direct translational implication of our findings concerns MEK inhibition. A-1 cells were more sensitive to the MEK inhibitor trametinib than the parental sNF96.2 cells, showing that the transformed state created by PTPN11 mutation is accompanied by an increased dependence on MEK signaling. MPNSTs are notoriously resistant to conventional chemotherapy, and effective systemic options are lacking; our results suggest that the oncogenic configuration of an individual tumor, specifically the presence of SHP-2 pathway activation, could identify patients more likely to benefit from trametinib or related agents. This supports the broader strategy of molecularly stratifying MPNSTs according to their Ras-pathway lesions rather than treating them as a single entity.

Several limitations should be noted. Our model derives from a single cell line, and the generality of PTPN11 mutation as a driver of progression requires validation in cohorts of primary MPNSTs and in additional NF1-deficient models. The xenograft setting does not reproduce the microenvironment of nerve sheath tumors, and the kidney capsule implantation, although necessary to obtain the progressed derivative, does not recapitulate the anatomical context of MPNST development. The mechanistic dissection of how SHP-2 and BRAP cooperate at the biochemical level, and the in vivo efficacy of trametinib in models established from A-1 cells, remain to be demonstrated. Nevertheless, by capturing a spontaneous progression event from a defined NF1-mutant background and linking it to MEK dependence and MEK inhibitor sensitivity, this study provides both a tractable experimental system for malignant transformation in neurofibromatosis and a rationale for MEK-directed therapy in a molecularly defined subset of these tumors.
