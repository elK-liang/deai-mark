Unruptured aneurysms of the basilar tip represent a technically demanding subgroup of posterior circulation lesions, in part because the perforating arteries arising from the basilar apex make adjunctive stenting undesirable and because surgical access is limited. In this study we compared WEB embolization with conventional coiling in 75 patients, 40 treated with the WEB device and 35 treated with coiling. The two groups were well matched with respect to age, sex, and aneurysm dimensions, with the notable exception that aneurysms treated with WEB had a significantly wider neck (4.7 versus 3.9 mm) and a lower dome-to-neck ratio. In other words, the WEB device was applied to anatomically less favorable lesions, which is an important consideration when interpreting the observed outcomes.

The procedural profiles of the two techniques differed substantially. Additional stent implantation was required in 71% of coiled aneurysms but in only one WEB-treated patient (2.5%), and 39 of the 40 WEB procedures were completed with the device alone. Treatment duration was significantly shorter in the WEB group (111 versus 152 minutes), and patient radiation exposure and contrast volume were numerically lower, although these differences did not reach statistical significance. Avoidance of stent assistance has particular relevance at the basilar tip, where stenting obligates dual antiplatelet therapy and raises concern for compromising perforator flow. The ability to treat wide-necked aneurysms without adjunctive devices is therefore a meaningful practical advantage of the intrasaccular approach in this location.

Regarding safety, two procedure-related thromboembolic events occurred in the WEB group (5.0%). One patient developed multiple posterior cortical infarcts and experienced numbness in the left lower leg, which resolved within a few weeks without permanent morbidity. No treatment failure was observed in either group. Thromboembolic complications are a recognized risk of intrasaccular flow disruption, and the observed rate falls within the range reported for this device class, although the small sample size precludes precise estimation of complication frequencies or of any difference between the two techniques.

Taken together, these findings support the view that WEB embolization is a feasible single-modality option for unruptured basilar tip aneurysms, including lesions with wider necks and less favorable dome-to-neck ratios that would frequently require stent-assisted coiling. The shorter procedure time and the numerical reductions in radiation exposure and contrast administration, if confirmed in larger samples, would further favor the technique from the standpoint of patient comfort, renal protection, and operator safety. These considerations are relevant not only for the treatment decision itself but also for counseling patients who are candidates for either modality.

Several limitations of this study must be acknowledged. The sample size is modest, the comparison was not randomized, and the choice of treatment modality was made by the treating physicians, introducing the potential for selection bias; the wider necks in the WEB group illustrate that the two populations were not strictly comparable. Follow-up was limited, and delayed recurrence after WEB occlusion, which has been described for intrasaccular devices in other territories, cannot be excluded on the basis of the present data. In addition, the study was restricted to unruptured aneurysms, and conclusions about ruptured lesions at the basilar tip, where the avoidance of dual antiplatelet therapy is even more consequential, would require separate evaluation. Larger, ideally prospective and multicenter comparisons with core-lab-adjudicated angiographic outcomes and standardized follow-up protocols will be needed to define the respective roles of WEB embolization and coiling at this challenging anatomical site.
