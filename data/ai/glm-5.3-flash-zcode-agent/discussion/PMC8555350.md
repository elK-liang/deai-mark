Hepatoblastoma is the most common malignant liver tumor of childhood, yet its molecular characterization lags behind that of adult liver cancers, and the contribution of its tumor immune microenvironment to clinical behavior is poorly understood. In this study, we integrated transcriptomic, epigenomic, and copy number data from public repositories to define genomic and epigenomic signatures associated with the tumor immune microenvironment of hepatoblastoma. From the GSE75271 and GSE81928 datasets we identified 1364 differentially expressed genes (756 upregulated and 608 downregulated) and 528 upregulated and 274 downregulated genes respectively, and their intersection yielded 134 commonly upregulated and 191 commonly downregulated genes. Parallel analysis of methylation data identified 1125 hypermethylated and 2337 hypomethylated sites in hepatoblastoma, and the convergence of differential methylation and differential expression defined 83 differentially methylated and expressed genes.

The identity of these genes provides biological coherence to the analysis. The list includes components of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway such as NOTUM, NKD1, BMP4, BAMBI, AXIN2, and LAMB1, which is consistent with the established centrality of CTNNB1 pathway activation in hepatoblastoma pathogenesis, as well as developmental regulators such as TNFRSF19, SP5, HOXA3, TBX3, and MSX1 that reflect the embryonal character of this tumor. Equally notable are the liver function genes that appear among the downregulated, hypomethylated-associated set, including CYP2C8, CYP1A2, GLS2, SLC10A1, XDH, HAO2, TDO2, PGLYRP2, and TTC36, whose silencing recapitulates the loss of hepatocytic differentiation that accompanies hepatoblastoma. The joint analysis of methylation and expression, rather than either dimension alone, distinguishes genes whose dysregulation is potentially epigenetically driven from those whose expression changes reflect other mechanisms, and therefore prioritizes candidates for functional follow-up.

The immunological dimension of this study addresses an underexplored aspect of hepatoblastoma. By characterizing the tumor immune microenvironment through the lens of these integrated signatures, the analysis situates hepatoblastoma within the emerging appreciation that pediatric embryonal tumors are immunologically distinct from adult cancers, generally described as cold tumors with low mutational burden and limited immune infiltration. Documenting the immune-related features of hepatoblastoma in a systematic, multi-omic manner establishes a baseline for evaluating whether any component of the immune microenvironment correlates with clinical variables such as stage, histology, or chemoresponsiveness, and whether immunotherapeutic concepts applicable to other pediatric tumors might extend to this disease. The copy number and methylation patterns documented here also serve as a reference against which future sequencing cohorts can be compared.

The methodological approach deserves comment. Analyzing multiple independent datasets, with normalization, outlier removal by principal component analysis, and intersection of results, guards against the cohort-specific artifacts that beset single-dataset analyses of rare tumors. For an entity as uncommon as hepatoblastoma, no single repository provides adequate sample sizes, so the disciplined combination of public datasets is often the only route to adequately powered discovery. The removal of outlier samples by PCA in both expression and methylation datasets, before differential analysis, prevented contaminated normal samples from diluting the tumor-specific signals.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis is entirely dependent on public microarray and methylation datasets generated in different laboratories, on different platforms, and under different preprocessing conventions, and despite normalization, residual batch effects cannot be excluded; this is partially mitigated by the requirement for concordance across datasets. The clinical annotation of the public samples is limited, precluding robust association of the identified signatures with prognosis, treatment response, or outcome, which will require dedicated cohorts with linked clinical data. The immune microenvironment analysis inferred from bulk omics data cannot resolve the spatial organization or the functional state of immune cells, and deconvolution-based estimates are constrained by the reference signatures available for pediatric liver tumors. Finally, causality between the methylation changes and expression differences was not tested experimentally, and the functional role of the 83 differentially methylated and expressed genes in hepatoblastoma biology remains to be established. With these caveats, our study provides an integrated genomic and epigenomic map of hepatoblastoma, highlights Wnt pathway and differentiation genes as epigenetically modulated determinants of its phenotype, and offers a systematic characterization of its tumor immune microenvironment as a foundation for future clinical and functional studies.
