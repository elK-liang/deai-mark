The scaling of SARS-CoV-2 genome sequencing has been a defining constraint on genomic surveillance throughout the pandemic. In this study, we developed CoronaHiT, a library preparation method that combines cheap transposase-based adapter introduction with symmetric PCR barcoding of up to 96 samples on a MinION flowcell, with the option of switching the same barcoded libraries to Illumina sequencing when higher throughput or accuracy is required. We evaluated the method against the ARTIC LoCost protocol on both routine surveillance samples and a rapid response set sequenced within 24 hours of receipt, using 30 hours of sequencing data for the routine set and 18 hours for the rapid response set on ONT platforms.

The yield comparison demonstrates that CoronaHiT achieves throughput comparable to the established protocol while reducing hands-on complexity. For the routine samples, CoronaHiT-ONT produced 9.6 Gbases of sequence data with an average of 1145X coverage per sample, ARTIC LoCost produced 8.0 Gbases at 1719X, and CoronaHiT-Illumina yielded 15.7 Gbases at 4649X. For the rapid response set, the corresponding outputs were 5.7 Gbases at 1104X, 4.5 Gbases at 1421X, and 7.3 Gbases at 3010X. The lower total yield of CoronaHiT-ONT relative to ARTIC reflects the shorter read length inherent to the transposase-based library design, but the important observation is that the coverage per sample remained ample for consensus genome generation, and that coverage was more uniform between samples on CoronaHiT-ONT runs, with a lower standard deviation relative to the mean than in the ARTIC runs. Uniformity matters as much as total yield in multiplexed surveillance sequencing, because uneven sample coverage wastes sequencing capacity and can leave the least concentrated samples without usable genomes.

The practical advantages of CoronaHiT derive from its streamlined workflow. The method does not require individual sample washes and allows all samples to be processed uniformly without quantification or normalization, which is a substantial operational saving at public health scale where hundreds of samples are processed daily. Symmetric barcoding means that the same library design serves both ONT and Illumina platforms, so laboratories can flexibly match sequencing capacity to demand, retaining MinION runs for rapid local response and switching to Illumina when accuracy or batch size warrants it. The rapid response evaluation, in which 59 samples including 18 query outbreak samples were sequenced with results available the following day, illustrates the use case that motivated the design: epidemiologically urgent samples can be barcoded alongside routine material without dedicated workflows.

These results should be viewed in the context of genomic surveillance strategy. The marginal cost of sequencing, dominated by library preparation labor and flowcell use, determines how comprehensively a country can sample its epidemic. A method that increases samples per flowcell while reducing hands-on time directly extends surveillance capacity, and the dual-platform compatibility protects against supply chain disruptions affecting either sequencer type. The ability to process samples without normalization is particularly valuable for field laboratories receiving samples of variable quality and quantity, a common reality during epidemic surges.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The evaluation was performed on a defined sample set with comparison to a single alternative protocol, and performance on highly degraded clinical material or on samples with very high Ct values, where amplicon-based approaches also struggle, was not systematically characterized. The transposase-based design yields shorter reads, which affects the fraction of the genome covered by individual reads and may influence sensitivity to strand-specific artifacts, although consensus accuracy was maintained by depth. Coverage uniformity, while improved relative to ARTIC on ONT runs, still varied between samples, and laboratories handling particularly diverse sample concentrations may need to combine CoronaHiT with a light normalization step. Finally, the method was validated for SARS-CoV-2 amplicon schemes of the era, and adaptation to other viral genomes or to evolving primer schemes requires revalidation. Within these boundaries, CoronaHiT provides a scalable, cost-conscious, and platform-flexible approach to viral genome sequencing that supports both routine surveillance and rapid outbreak response.
