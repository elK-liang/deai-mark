Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease spans a spectrum from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and the mechanisms that drive the transition to inflammatory, progressive disease are incompletely understood. In this study, we profiled long non-coding RNA expression across mouse models of NAFLD and NASH and identified lncRNA-Gm9795 as a promoter of hepatic inflammation acting through the endoplasmic reticulum stress-mediated NF-κB/JNK pathway. The disease models were validated histologically and biochemically: DB/DB mice fed a normal diet showed increased fat accumulation consistent with NAFLD, while DB/DB mice fed a methionine- and choline-deficient diet developed significant inflammatory cell infiltration, confirmed by F4/80 immunohistochemistry, with serum ALT elevated 2.98-fold and AST 1.94-fold relative to controls in the NASH group.

The microarray analysis provides a global view of lncRNA dysregulation across the NAFLD-NASH axis. A total of 3111 lncRNAs were differentially expressed between NAFLD and control mice, 1915 upregulated and 1196 downregulated, while 1394 upregulated and 1283 downregulated lncRNAs distinguished NASH from controls, and 1513 upregulated and 1824 downregulated lncRNAs distinguished NASH from NAFLD. Venn analysis identified 63 lncRNAs changed in all three comparisons, and series clustering highlighted 381 lncRNAs that were not only highly expressed in NAFLD but increased further in NASH. This expression pattern, in which a subset of lncRNAs tracks with disease severity rather than with steatosis alone, is biologically meaningful because it enriches for transcripts associated with the inflammatory and progressive phase of the disease rather than with benign fat accumulation. From this subset, we focused on lncRNA-Gm9795, a previously uncharacterized transcript, for detailed investigation.

Our functional analyses support a pro-inflammatory role for lncRNA-Gm9795 operating through endoplasmic reticulum stress. ER stress is a recognized feature of NASH, driven by lipotoxicity, and its downstream signaling through the NF-κB and JNK pathways connects the stressed hepatocyte to inflammatory cytokine production and to apoptosis. The finding that lncRNA-Gm9795 promotes inflammation via this axis integrates three strands of NASH pathogenesis, lipid overload, ER stress, and inflammatory signaling, into a single regulatory circuit, and it positions a non-coding RNA as an upstream node in a pathway usually analyzed at the protein level. This matters therapeutically: inflammation is the key determinant of progression from steatosis to fibrotic, clinically significant disease, and a mediator that is specifically upregulated with disease severity represents a candidate target for interrupting that progression, as well as a potential circulating or tissue biomarker for distinguishing NASH from simple steatosis, a distinction that currently requires liver biopsy.

The study also contributes to the broader cataloging of lncRNA involvement in liver disease. The large number of differentially expressed lncRNAs observed across the three comparisons indicates that non-coding transcription is extensively remodeled in fatty liver disease, as has been reported for coding genes, and the specific focus on transcripts that increase progressively from control through NAFLD to NASH provides a resource for other investigators interested in severity-associated non-coding regulators. The use of two complementary models, a genetic obesity model for NAFLD and a dietary deficiency model for NASH, captures different facets of human disease, although it also means that the lncRNA changes common to both comparisons may reflect model-specific rather than human-relevant biology.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The murine models, whether genetic or dietary, incompletely reproduce the metabolic context of human NASH, which develops over years in the setting of insulin resistance and obesity rather than weeks, and the MCD diet in particular induces weight loss and mitochondrial toxicity that are atypical of human disease. The microarray platform detects annotated transcripts and may miss unannotated or low-abundance lncRNAs. The mechanistic findings for lncRNA-Gm9795 derive from murine models and cell-based systems, and the relevance of this specific lncRNA to human NASH, including whether a human homolog exists and behaves similarly, requires validation in human liver samples. Finally, the upstream regulation of lncRNA-Gm9795 expression, whether by lipotoxic stress, inflammatory cytokines, or transcription factors, was not established. With these caveats, our study identifies lncRNA-Gm9795 as a severity-associated, inflammation-promoting non-coding RNA in NASH, delineates an ER stress-NF-κB/JNK mechanism for its action, and adds to the evidence that lncRNAs participate in the progression of fatty liver disease.
