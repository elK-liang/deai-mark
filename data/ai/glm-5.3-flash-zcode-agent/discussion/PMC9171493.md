Necroptosis, a form of regulated necrotic cell death, is increasingly recognized as a modulator of the tumor immune microenvironment, and long non-coding RNAs are emerging as regulators of cell death pathways in cancer. In this study, we systematically characterized necroptosis-related genes and lncRNAs in breast cancer and constructed a necroptosis-related lncRNA signature for predicting prognosis and revealing the immune microenvironment. Among the 67 necroptosis-related genes analyzed, genetic alterations were common, occurring in 145 of 983 patients (14.75%), with copy number amplification as the predominant variation type, and differential expression analysis showed that all but five of these genes were significantly differentially expressed between tumor and normal breast tissue, including upregulation of TERT with the highest log fold change of 2.627.

The lncRNA landscape associated with necroptosis proved extensive. By correlating lncRNA and gene expression across 1096 breast cancer specimens and 112 normal specimens from TCGA, we identified 1520 necroptosis-related lncRNAs, of which 46 were prognostic by univariate analysis, and LASSO-penalized Cox regression condensed these into a 22-lncRNA signature for the training cohort. The individual members of the signature include lncRNAs both upregulated in tumors, such as LMNTD2-AS1, AC007608.3, and LINC02446, and downregulated, such as LINC00377, AL450322.1, MEF2C-AS1, and AC079298.3. This bidirectional composition is consistent with the dual roles that necroptosis can play in tumors, where death pathway activity may either suppress tumor growth through inflammatory cell death or promote it through the secretion of pro-tumoral factors, so that the prognostic information resides in the balance of the entire program rather than in any single component.

The clinical value of such a signature depends on its relationship to the immune microenvironment, which our analysis addresses. Necroptotic death is immunologically distinct from apoptosis: it releases damage-associated molecular patterns and inflammatory mediators that shape the infiltration and activation of immune cells in tumors. A necroptosis-related lncRNA signature therefore plausibly captures, in transcriptomic form, an axis of tumor-immune interaction that is relevant both to prognosis and to the response to immunotherapy. In breast cancer, where immune checkpoint blockade benefits only a subset of patients, transcriptomic classifiers that reflect the inflammatory state of the tumor could assist in patient selection, and the association of our signature with immune infiltration provides a mechanistic rationale for its prognostic performance.

Our analytical strategy has methodological strengths worth noting. Anchoring lncRNA selection in correlations with curated necroptosis-related genes, rather than in an unguided screen, focuses the signature on a biologically coherent program and reduces the risk that the model captures batch or proliferation artifacts. The use of LASSO-penalized Cox regression guards against overfitting relative to univariate selection alone, and the characterization of mutation and copy number landscapes of the underlying genes provides a genomic context for the transcriptomic findings.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The signature was constructed and evaluated within TCGA data, and independent external validation in additional cohorts, ideally with matched clinical outcomes and treatment information, is required before any clinical application; internal cross-validation does not substitute for external replication. TCGA breast cancer samples encompass heterogeneous molecular subtypes, and the performance and composition of the signature may differ across luminal, HER2-positive, and triple-negative disease. The analysis is correlative: no functional experiments were performed to demonstrate that the signature lncRNAs regulate necroptosis or immune infiltration causally, and the direction of regulation for many lncRNAs remains unknown. Batch effects between transcriptomic platforms and differences in sample handling may also influence lncRNA measurements. Finally, necroptosis-related lncRNAs identified by expression correlation may act through mechanisms unrelated to necroptosis, and the annotation of their functions is largely unexplored. With these caveats, our study maps the genomic and transcriptomic landscape of necroptosis-related lncRNAs in breast cancer, provides a multi-lncRNA prognostic signature with links to the immune microenvironment, and offers a foundation for experimental work aimed at validating necroptosis-related lncRNAs as biomarkers or therapeutic targets.
