In this study, we combined global lipidomic profiling with infection experiments in two human cell lines to define how SARS-CoV-2 reshapes the lipid landscape of its host cell. Twenty-four hours after infection, the composition of the cellular lipidome was extensively altered: in HEK293T-ACE2 cells, 409 of 514 identified lipid species (79.6%) changed significantly between infected and mock-infected samples, with abundances shifting between 2- and 64-fold, and in A549-ACE2 cells, 227 of 443 identified lipids were significantly altered, changing between 1.1- and 19.7-fold. Principal component analysis confirmed that infection status accounted for most of the observed variation, with infected and mock samples separating cleanly into two distinct groups. Together, these findings establish SARS-CoV-2 infection as a systemic perturbation of host lipid metabolism rather than a change confined to one or a few lipid classes.

The breadth of the response across glycerolipids, phospholipids, sphingolipids, and acylcarnitines is consistent with the known demands of coronavirus replication. SARS-CoV-2, like other coronaviruses, builds an extensive network of replication organelles derived from host membranes, and expanding this intracellular membrane surface requires a coordinated supply of phospholipids and neutral lipids. Alterations in sphingolipid metabolism may additionally influence membrane curvature and signaling, whereas changes in acylcarnitines and glycerolipid storage point to a rerouting of fatty acid utilization toward energy production and lipid synthesis. The magnitude of the changes, reaching tens of fold for individual species, suggests that lipid remodeling is not a passive by-product of infection but an actively orchestrated program of the virus-host interaction.

A comparison of the two cell lines revealed both convergence and divergence. The more pronounced remodeling in HEK293T-ACE2 cells, which overexpress the viral receptor and provide a convenient and controllable system, may partly reflect their high permissiveness, whereas the more physiologically relevant A549-ACE2 background displayed a substantial but narrower response. Importantly, the shared fraction of altered lipids across the two systems defines a core lipid response that is robust across cellular contexts. This conserved fraction is arguably the most valuable output of the analysis, because host lipid functions recruited irrespective of cell type are more likely to represent genuine dependency factors of the virus than cell-line-specific epiphenomena.

The identification of host lipid dependency factors that are conserved across SARS-CoV-2 variants has direct translational relevance. Viral proteins evolve under immune pressure and are the usual targets of antiviral drugs, which makes resistance a recurring concern as variants emerge. Host pathways that the virus requires are constrained differently: an intervention that raises the metabolic cost of membrane remodeling is less likely to be circumvented by point mutations in viral genes. The lipid map presented here therefore provides a rational starting point for selecting lipid-metabolizing enzymes and regulatory nodes whose inhibition could be evaluated as variant-agnostic antiviral strategies, either alone or in combination with directly acting antivirals. Such host-directed approaches could also retain utility against future coronaviruses that rely on similar membrane biology.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis was performed in cell culture at a single time point, 24 hours after infection, and therefore captures a snapshot of what is certainly a dynamic process; time-resolved lipidomics may reveal earlier events that initiate the remodeling program. The ACE2-overexpressing HEK293T model, although experimentally powerful, does not fully reproduce the lipid metabolism of primary respiratory epithelium, and A549-ACE2 cells, while more physiologically relevant, remain an immortalized line. Finally, lipidomic profiling identifies coordinately altered species but does not by itself establish causality; each candidate dependency factor will require genetic or pharmacological validation in appropriate infection models. Despite these caveats, the global lipid map provides a resource for understanding coronavirus-host interactions and a basis for exploring host-directed therapeutics against SARS-CoV-2.
