Antiviral RNA interference is the principal small RNA-based defense in plants and invertebrates, yet the host factors that regulate its antiviral output remain incompletely catalogued. In this study, we exploited natural variation among fully re-sequenced Arabidopsis thaliana accessions to identify regulators of antiviral RNAi, using quantitative resistance to CMV-∆2b, a mutant of the virulent Fny strain of Cucumber mosaic virus rendered susceptible to RNAi by the disruption of its viral suppressor VSR-2b, as the quantitative phenotype. Virus accumulation in upper non-inoculated leaves, measured by ELISA of the viral coat protein two weeks after mechanical inoculation, displayed a nearly normal distribution across 496 accessions after log-transformation, providing a suitable trait for genome-wide association.

The genetic architecture revealed by the GWAS is informative in two respects. First, none of the significantly associated SNPs mapped to any of the known antiviral RNAi genes, indicating that the standing natural variation in this population does not reside in the canonical core machinery such as Dicers, Argonautes, or RNA-dependent RNA polymerases, presumably because variation in these essential components is deleterious and has been purged. Second, the most significant association localized to At4g11040, corresponding to RDO5/DOG18, a gene previously characterized in seed dormancy and germination but not previously connected to antiviral defense. Accessions carrying the resistant haplotype T, including Col-0, accumulated significantly lower CMV-∆2b titers than accessions of haplotype G, a difference confirmed by northern blot, and comparison of the An-1 accession, which carries a single base-pair frameshifting deletion in RDO5, with Col-0 and with An-1 lines transgenically complemented with the Col-0 RDO5 allele demonstrated that RDO5 from Col-0 suppresses CMV-∆2b accumulation. The consistent behavior of independent T-DNA mutants and complementation lines establishes RDO5 as a positive regulator of antiviral RNAi.

The involvement of RDO5 raises mechanistic questions that extend beyond this virus. RDO5 belongs to a family of purine-binding proteins, and its dual role in seed dormancy and antiviral defense suggests a function at the interface of RNA metabolism and developmental regulation. Because CMV-∆2b is a VSR-deficient virus, RDO5 acts on the RNAi pathway itself rather than against viral suppression; its activity may therefore be masked or overwhelmed in infections by wild-type viruses carrying functional suppressors, and testing RDO5-dependent resistance against suppressor-intact viruses will be important. The study's broader contribution lies in the demonstration that quantitative resistance traits in natural populations can pinpoint both positive and negative regulators of antiviral RNAi, complementing candidate-gene approaches that are biased toward known pathway components. Negative regulators identified by this approach are of particular interest because their loss-of-function alleles can enhance antiviral resistance without perturbing the core machinery, offering a potential route to engineered resistance.

The population-genetic framing of this work has additional advantages. Quantifying virus accumulation across hundreds of accessions captures the allelic richness present in nature, including rare variants that laboratory mutagenesis screens rarely sample in a controlled way, and the near-normal distribution of the phenotype indicates that resistance is polygenic, with RDO5 being the strongest single contributor detected. This architecture implies that antiviral RNAi output is fine-tuned by many loci of modest effect, an expectation that the GWAS data support and that future analyses of additional associations may address.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The association mapping identifies genomic regions and was validated for the leading locus, but the biochemical function of RDO5 in antiviral RNAi, including whether it acts directly on viral RNA, on siRNA stability, or on amplification of the silencing signal, remains to be determined. The phenotype was measured for a single virus under controlled growth conditions, and host-by-environment interactions, as well as variation in viral suppressor complements, may alter the genetic basis of resistance in the field. Furthermore, the CMV-∆2b system deliberately removes the major viral suppressor, so the loci identified here represent the RNAi-permissive layer of defense rather than the full resistance landscape. With these caveats, our study establishes RDO5 as a natural-variation-derived regulator of antiviral RNA interference in plants and demonstrates the power of association genetics in large diversity panels for uncovering previously unknown components of innate antiviral immunity.
