Long non-coding RNAs are increasingly recognized as regulators of cancer cell survival, but the molecular mechanisms through which individual lncRNAs exert their effects remain incompletely defined. In this study, we identified DLEU1 as a frequently overexpressed lncRNA in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and demonstrated that it promotes cell survival by preventing the degradation of DYNLL1. The discovery strategy combined two independent lncRNA and mRNA microarray profiles comprising 119 and 60 pairs of ESCC samples with matched adjacent normal tissues, from which the four most frequently overexpressed lncRNAs, LINC01614, LOC284930, KCNMB2-AS1, and DLEU1, were selected for functional screening. Knockdown experiments showed that DLEU1 had the most potent suppressive effect on ESCC cell growth, directing the subsequent analysis.

The clinical evidence establishes DLEU1 as a robustly dysregulated and prognostically relevant transcript in ESCC. DLEU1 expression was higher in tumors than in matched normal counterparts in 118 of 119 (99.2%) and 59 of 60 (98.3%) comparisons across the two cohorts, a consistency of dysregulation that is unusual even among established cancer markers. High DLEU1 expression was associated with decreased overall survival, and this relationship was reproduced in the TCGA cohort, while overexpression correlated with lymph node metastasis and advanced clinical stage. RNAscope in situ hybridization confirmed the upregulation at the tissue level, demonstrating that the signal localizes to the tumor compartment rather than to infiltrating cells. This concordance across discovery cohorts, an external database, spatial transcript detection, and clinicopathological correlations provides a strong foundation for the biological relevance of DLEU1 in this disease.

Mechanistically, our data define a survival pathway in which DLEU1 stabilizes DYNLL1, the dynein light chain that participates in apoptotic signaling. Gene set enrichment analysis showed that genes regulating mitochondrial membrane permeability involved in the apoptotic process were significantly enriched in ESCC with high DLEU1 expression, together with signatures of cancer stem cells, metastatic prostate cancer, and liver cancer with poor survival, and this enrichment pattern oriented the mechanistic work toward apoptosis regulation. The demonstration that DLEU1 prevents DYNLL1 degradation, thereby maintaining the anti-apoptotic machinery of the cell, provides a concrete molecular explanation for the growth-suppressive effect of DLEU1 knockdown. This mechanism is coherent with the known involvement of ubiquitination-dependent protein turnover in apoptosis control, and it positions a non-coding RNA as an upstream guard of a cell death effector, an arrangement that offers a specific therapeutic logic: disrupting the DLEU1-DYNLL1 interaction would lower the apoptotic threshold of ESCC cells specifically, an effect that normal cells, lacking DLEU1 overexpression, would tolerate better.

The high prevalence of DLEU1 overexpression also carries biomarker implications. A transcript upregulated in more than 98% of tumors, detectable by RNAscope in archival tissue, and correlated with nodal metastasis and stage could complement existing pathological staging in risk assessment, particularly for identifying patients at risk of occult nodal disease. The association with advanced stage and worse survival suggests that DLEU1 participates in the biology of progression rather than being a passenger of transformation, and its expression may therefore track with clinically aggressive disease states.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The microarray-based discovery, while supported by qPCR, RNAscope, and TCGA validation, was conducted on cohorts of limited size, and the prognostic significance of DLEU1 requires validation in larger, prospective, multicenter cohorts with standardized treatment. The mechanistic model, although supported by the enrichment and degradation data, would benefit from direct demonstration of the ubiquitination events involved and of the domain requirements for the DLEU1-DYNLL1 interaction, and the possibility that DLEU1 acts through additional mechanisms, as lncRNAs frequently do, was not excluded. Our functional studies were performed in cell lines, and the in vivo importance of the axis in xenograft or genetically engineered models remains to be established. Finally, the upstream drivers of DLEU1 overexpression in ESCC, whether copy number gain, epigenetic derepression, or transcriptional activation, were not identified. With these caveats, our study establishes DLEU1 as a near-universally overexpressed, prognostically relevant lncRNA in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and defines a survival mechanism based on the stabilization of DYNLL1, nominating this axis as a candidate therapeutic target in a cancer with substantial unmet need.
