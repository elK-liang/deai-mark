N6-methyladenosine is the most abundant internal modification of eukaryotic mRNA, installed by writers, removed by erasers, and interpreted by readers, and its dysregulation is increasingly implicated in cancer. Small cell lung cancer, a highly lethal neuroendocrine malignancy with limited treatment options, has not previously been characterized in this respect. In this study, we curated a catalog of 30 m6A regulators, comprising 11 writers, two erasers, and 17 readers, and characterized their genomic, transcriptomic, and immuno-oncological features in SCLC.

The genomic landscape of m6A regulators in SCLC exhibits distinctive features. Somatic mutations were present in 28 of 110 samples, a frequency of 25.5%, with readers showing relatively high mutation frequencies and FMR1 the highest, while none of the erasers exhibited mutations. Copy number variation, assessed across 53 SCLC cell lines from the CCLE, was universal, with most readers (11 of 17) showing widespread amplification and all erasers showing predominantly loss. Co-occurrence analysis identified paired alterations between METTL3 and YTHDC2 and between IGF2BP2 and YTHDC2, suggesting that combinations of writer and reader changes are selected during tumor evolution. At the expression level, principal component analysis separated normal lung from SCLC samples dramatically, and nearly all writers and readers were significantly upregulated in SCLC while eraser expression tended to decline, a pattern that, combined with the CNV data, indicates that SCLC is associated with abundant m6A modification activity and that copy number alterations contribute to the perturbation of regulator expression.

The direction of these changes is biologically coherent with the aggressive phenotype of SCLC. Enhanced writer activity would increase global m6A deposition, and reader upregulation, particularly of IGF2BP family members that stabilize their target transcripts, would extend the half-life of oncogenic mRNAs; IGF2BP3, known to be elevated in multiple solid tumors, is extended to SCLC in this study. The simultaneous decline of erasers, which remove methylation marks, reinforces the shift toward a hyper-methylated transcriptome. Because m6A modification governs mRNA stability, translation efficiency, and splicing, a systematic increase in modification potential provides SCLC with a post-transcriptional mechanism for rapid proteome reshaping, an attribute suited to a tumor type defined by high proliferation, early metastasis, and genomic catastrophe. The finding that most writers and readers showed an upward distribution in metastasis-derived compared with primary-derived cell lines further connects the m6A apparatus to the metastatic behavior that dominates the clinical course of this disease.

The clinical and therapeutic implications of this landscape are considerable. First, the expression patterns of m6A regulators define molecular subgroups within SCLC that may align with the emerging transcriptional classification of the disease and could carry prognostic or predictive information. Second, the immuno-oncology features associated with regulator expression patterns, examined in this study, are relevant to the ongoing evaluation of immunotherapy in SCLC, where responses occur but are uncommon, and m6A modification has been implicated in antitumor immune responses in other cancers, including through the regulation of interferon pathways and immune cell infiltration. Third, the druggability of the system is improving: pharmacological inhibitors of METTL3 and of reader RNA-binding activities are in development, and our data suggest that SCLC, with its widespread upregulation of writers and readers, is a rational indication for such agents, particularly in tumors with copy number amplification of these regulators.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis is largely correlative, resting on curated gene sets and public datasets, and the functional consequence of the observed alterations for global m6A levels in SCLC was inferred rather than directly measured, since m6A quantification across the cohorts was not performed. The use of cell line panels for CNV and metastasis analyses introduces the biases of established lines, including adaptation to culture, and the mutation analysis reflects a modest sample size for rare event detection. The integration with immuno-oncology features relies on bulk transcriptomic inference rather than on matched tumor specimens with immune phenotyping. Finally, the identity of the mRNAs whose methylation and stability are altered by these regulator changes, and their contribution to SCLC phenotypes, requires experimental dissection. With these caveats, our study provides the first systematic landscape of m6A regulators in small cell lung cancer, demonstrates their pervasive genomic and transcriptomic dysregulation in a direction consistent with enhanced m6A activity, and nominates the m6A machinery as both a biomarker axis and a therapeutic vulnerability in this recalcitrant disease.
