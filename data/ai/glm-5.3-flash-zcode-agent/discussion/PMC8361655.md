In this study we constructed a kidney transplant rejection (KTR)-specific microRNA-protein interaction network by integrating differentially expressed miRNAs and mRNAs, experimentally supported miRNA-mRNA interactions, and protein-protein interaction data. The resulting miR-PPIN contained 949 miRNA-mRNA regulatory pairs and 1,447 protein-protein interactions among 31 differentially expressed miRNAs and 642 abnormally expressed genes. Functional characterization showed that the genes in the network were significantly enriched in T cell-related biological processes, including NK T cell proliferation, T cell antigen processing and presentation, and positive regulation of memory T cell activation. This enrichment is consistent with the central role of T cell-mediated alloimmunity in rejection and supports the biological plausibility of the network as a model of the rejection process rather than a purely statistical construct.

Analysis of the network's global architecture revealed a scale-free organization, with node degrees following a power-law distribution with a slope of -1.34. miRNAs with more targets were correspondingly rare, and most targets occupied peripheral positions within the protein interaction network. This topology allowed us to prioritize target genes according to their degree, closeness, and betweenness properties, defining a CORE gene set whose perturbation would be expected to propagate broadly through the network. Only a small number of miRNAs regulated a disproportionately large number of CORE genes, a property that provided a rational, structure-based criterion for biomarker discovery and differs from the conventional practice of selecting candidates solely on the magnitude of differential expression.

Applying these criteria identified three candidate biomarkers: miR-145-5p and miR-23b-3p, which were downregulated in KTR, and miR-155-5p, which was overexpressed in KTR and particularly in the CMR subgroup. The convergent dysregulation of these three miRNAs suggests that rejection involves coordinated remodeling of miRNA-mediated regulation rather than isolated alteration of a single transcript. Receiver operating characteristic analyses, performed on both the training dataset and an independent validation dataset, supported the potential of the individual miRNAs and their combination for diagnosing KTR and for discriminating between rejection subtypes, addressing the twin clinical needs of detection and classification.

The behavior of the three candidates is consistent with known biology. miR-155 has been repeatedly linked to lymphocyte activation and inflammatory signaling, which accords with its elevation in rejection, particularly in the cell-mediated form of the disease. The downregulation of miR-145-5p and miR-23b-3p, which have been implicated in the control of fibroproliferative and inflammatory responses, may reflect a loss of regulatory restraint on injury-response pathways within the graft. Read together with the network enrichment in T cell-related processes, these patterns support the view that the identified miRNAs occupy positions of regulatory importance in the rejection program rather than representing random fluctuations in expression.

A strength of this approach is that network topology is used as an explicit filter, which reduces the false-positive burden that affects single-omics biomarker studies and simultaneously provides mechanistic context for each candidate. Because the prioritized miRNAs regulate structurally central genes, they are also plausible points of therapeutic modulation, and their downstream networks suggest hypotheses about the pathways that drive rejection that can be tested experimentally. The multi-omics framing further allows the same analytical pipeline to be applied to other transplant-related phenotypes, such as chronic allograft dysfunction, where biomarkers are equally needed.

Several limitations warrant consideration. The network was constructed from expression data and curated interaction databases and therefore inherits their incompleteness and biases. Biopsy remains the gold standard for diagnosing rejection, and any non-invasive miRNA panel would require prospective evaluation against biopsy-proven diagnoses before clinical implementation. Pre-analytical factors, including sample handling and normalization strategy, can substantially influence miRNA measurements and must be standardized across centers. Finally, although the ROC analyses were performed on an independent validation dataset, larger multicenter cohorts with longitudinal sampling will be needed to determine whether these miRNAs can detect rejection before irreversible injury has occurred and can reliably distinguish rejection from other causes of graft dysfunction such as infection or drug toxicity.
