Glaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible blindness, and the genetic determinants of early-onset, high-pressure forms remain incompletely characterized. In the present study, an ENU mutagenesis screen in mice identified the egl1 line, whose homozygotes display bulging eyes and markedly elevated intraocular pressure. Whole-exome sequencing revealed a G-to-T transversion in Pitx2 producing a c.G344T, p.R115L missense change, and the causality of this variant was established by generating a Pitx2R115L knock-in mouse with CRISPR/Cas9 technology that reproduced the glaucoma-like phenotype of the original mutant. The concordance between the chemically induced mutant and the precisely engineered knock-in provides strong genetic evidence that this single amino acid substitution is sufficient to drive the disease.

The ocular phenotype of the knock-in mice recapitulates key features of human glaucomatous neuropathy. Elevated IOP was detectable from an early age and progressed over time, and optical coherence tomography revealed optic nerve cupping together with severe retinal nerve fiber layer loss, findings that were confirmed histologically at 12 months by enlargement of the optic disc cup and thinning of the inner retinal layers. Externally, approximately 59% of mutants (20 of 34) exhibited a bulging, distended ocular appearance with mild corneal opacity as early as two months of age, and roughly 80% of these cases were bilaterally affected, a pattern resembling the frequently bilateral presentation reported for Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome patients.

The molecular characterization of the mutation provides several clues to its mechanism. The affected arginine at position 115 lies within the homeodomain of PITX2 and is highly conserved across species, suggesting that the substitution perturbs DNA binding or target gene recognition rather than protein stability, a conclusion supported by the observation that total protein content and subcellular localization of PITX2 were unaltered in mutant retinas. PITX2 was strongly expressed in the ganglion cell layer, where it colocalized with the retinal ganglion cell marker Brn3a, implicating the transcription factor directly in retinal ganglion cell function. The findings presented here implicate the NRF2-YAP1 axis as the pathway through which the R115L substitution produces its pathogenic effects, linking a developmental transcription factor to the oxidative stress response and to Hippo pathway signaling, both of which have established roles in the survival of retinal ganglion cells and in the regulation of intraocular pressure.

These results have implications beyond the mouse model. PITX2 is best known for its role in Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome, a developmental disorder in which ocular hypertension is a frequent feature, but a direct mechanistic connection between a PITX2 missense mutation, elevated IOP, and progressive optic neuropathy has been difficult to study in vivo. The egl1 and knock-in lines now provide a genetically defined platform on which the sequence of events leading from PITX2 dysfunction to axonal degeneration can be dissected, and on which interventions targeting the NRF2-YAP1 pathway can be tested for their ability to lower pressure or protect the nerve. Given that elevated intraocular pressure remains the only modifiable risk factor in glaucoma, models that connect a defined genetic lesion to pressure dysregulation are of particular value.

Some caveats apply. The phenotype was studied primarily in homozygous animals, whereas human Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome is typically inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, so the relevance of this recessive model to heterozygous carriers requires further investigation. Penetrance was incomplete, with roughly 59% of mutants showing the external ocular phenotype, which points to the influence of genetic modifiers and offers an additional angle for identifying protective loci. Finally, as in all murine models, differences in aqueous humor outflow anatomy between mice and humans must be considered when extrapolating pressure-regulating mechanisms to clinical glaucoma, and the contribution of corneal and anterior segment changes to the measured pressures deserves separate attention.
