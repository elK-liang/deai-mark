The mechanics of large earthquakes on normal faults depends on fault geometry in ways that are difficult to capture with instrumental records alone. In this study, we integrated approximately 370 field measurements of fault and slip attitude with fault traces from the literature to produce a detailed structural map of the regional-scale CMF, a complex normal fault system crossing the Monti della Maddalena ridge in the southern Apennines. The field data, each georeferenced and quality-ranked, were grouped into fourteen composite sites along continuous and homogeneous portions of the fault, from which we calculated average fault planes, average slip vectors, rakes, and the orientation of the maximum tension axes, and represented the average kinematics at each site as pseudo-focal mechanisms.

The kinematic analysis reveals a fault system with systematic along-strike variability. The slickenside data depict predominantly normal dip-slip kinematics, but with a progressively higher dextral trans-tensive component towards the southeast. This gradient in kinematics correlates with geometric complexity: the fault strike rotates from approximately N120° between Caggiano and Montemurro, deviating from the N160° trend of the Vallo di Diano basin to follow a trend sub-parallel to the Val d'Agri basin, and the overall structure displays a well-defined right en-échelon pattern. The agreement between the T-axes of deformation derived from our structural data and geodetic strain indicators provides independent validation that the field-measured kinematics reflect the active stress field rather than relict deformation.

Structurally, the mapping delineates a highly segmented but substantially continuous tectonic structure that connects the SSW-dipping border fault of the Auletta basin at the northern termination of the Vallo di Diano basin with the eastern Val d'Agri fault system, crossing the Monti della Maddalena ridge. This connectivity is the key result with respect to seismic hazard. Geometric complexities between fault strands, including gaps and overlaps, are traditionally regarded as barriers that arrest rupture propagation, but our data show that in this system the segments are arranged en-échelon along a substantially continuous structure, a configuration in which stepovers may be crossed during rupture rather than stopping it. For a fault system capable of large earthquakes, the along-strike continuity therefore implies that rupture length, and hence earthquake magnitude, may exceed what would be predicted from the dimensions of individual segments.

These findings contribute to a broader recognition that the segmentation of active normal faults must be demonstrated rather than assumed. The kinematic partitioning we document, with trans-tensive behavior concentrated toward the southeast and influenced by the fault's segmentation, indicates that deformation is distributed according to the three-dimensional geometry of the fault system. Pseudo-focal mechanisms constructed from field data offer a bridge between geological observations and seismological moment tensor solutions, allowing pre-instrumental and instrumental earthquake behavior to be compared on a common kinematic footing. Where the field-derived kinematics are consistent with the geodetic deformation field, they can be used with some confidence to characterize faults whose seismic potential is not fully captured by the short instrumental record.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Field measurements sample only the exposed portions of the fault, and the subsurface geometry at depth, including dip variations and linkage between strands, remains inferred rather than directly imaged. Slickenside populations integrate deformation over geological time, and although the consistency with geodetic data supports an active signal, distinguishing cumulative long-term kinematics from the slip distribution of the most recent earthquakes is not always possible. The classification of outcrop quality introduces a degree of subjectivity, which we addressed through standardized ranking, and the number of composite sites, while sufficient to resolve the along-strike trend, constrains the spatial resolution of kinematic variability. Finally, translating geometric continuity into quantitative rupture scenarios requires integration with paleoseismological data and earthquake simulators. With these caveats, our study provides a robust structural basis for reassessing the earthquake-generating potential of the CMF system and illustrates how dense field structural data can refine the fault models on which seismic hazard assessment depends.
