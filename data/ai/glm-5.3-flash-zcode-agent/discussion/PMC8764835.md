Esophageal carcinoma is among the most lethal malignancies worldwide, and its detection at an early, treatable stage remains a clinical challenge. Non-invasive biomarkers accessible before surgery could improve both diagnosis and treatment planning. In this multicenter prospective study, we identified a signature of small non-coding RNAs in saliva-derived exosomes as a predicting biomarker for esophageal carcinoma, with the leading candidates being a transfer RNA-derived fragment, tRNA-GlyGCC-5, and a previously uncharacterized small RNA that we designated sRESE.

The rationale for interrogating salivary exosomal RNA is supported by our characterization of the vesicle population. Exosomes isolated from saliva were confirmed by transmission electron microscopy and by immunoblotting for canonical exosomal markers including Alix, TSG101, CD63, and CD9, with the absence of calnexin excluding cellular contamination, and nanoparticle tracking established an average diameter of approximately 95 nm, consistent with the exosomal size range. Saliva is a particularly attractive biofluid for esophageal carcinoma biomarkers because it is collected non-invasively and repeatedly, and because the anatomical proximity of the esophagus to the oral cavity plausibly enriches saliva for tumor-derived material relative to blood. The extracellular vesicle context is also meaningful: small RNAs packaged in exosomes are protected from RNase degradation, providing a stable analyte that can withstand collection, shipping, and storage conditions required for multicenter studies.

The discovery and validation pipeline identifies two robust candidates. In the pilot phase, 1366 differentially expressed salivary exosomal small non-coding RNAs, excluding microRNAs, distinguished esophageal carcinoma patients from controls, and the top candidates by fold change were advanced to RT-qPCR validation in an independent cohort of 33 patients and 33 controls at CHSUMC, in which two of the five candidates met the predetermined significance threshold of 0.01. Of these, tRNA-GlyGCC-5 is a 5'-derived fragment of a glycine tRNA, adding to the growing recognition that tRNA-derived fragments are active signaling molecules rather than degradation products, and sRESE is a small RNA encoded on chromosome 1 between SSX2IP and the lncRNA LOC102724892 that had no prior functional annotation. Both candidates were highly expressed in exosomes secreted into conditioned media and in esophageal carcinoma cell lines compared with immortalized esophageal epithelial cells, indicating that tumor cells themselves are a plausible source of the salivary signal. The subsequent ROC analyses and the confirmation in the independent ATH cohort demonstrate that the signature retains discriminatory performance across centers, which is the essential requirement for a clinically deployable biomarker.

Beyond diagnosis, the study was designed to address pre-operative prediction of adjuvant therapy benefit, a use case with direct clinical value. Decisions about adjuvant treatment in esophageal carcinoma are currently based on pathological staging after resection, and a biomarker measurable in saliva before surgery could, if prospectively validated, inform neoadjuvant treatment selection and clinical trial stratification. The multicenter prospective design of our study, encompassing multiple hospitals and independent cohorts, strengthens the generalizability of these findings relative to single-institution retrospective biomarker studies that dominate the liquid biopsy literature.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The validation cohorts, although independent and multicenter, remain modest in size, and the reported diagnostic performance requires confirmation in larger, ideally consecutive, patient series that include the full spectrum of benign esophageal disease, since inflammation and reflux, common in the target population, could elevate exosomal RNA levels and reduce specificity. The biological function of sRESE is entirely unknown, and although tumor origin is suggested by the cell line data, the cellular source of the salivary exosomal signal, whether tumor cells, exfoliated epithelium, or resident immune cells, has not been definitively traced. Pre-analytical variables in saliva collection, including circadian variation, oral intake, and oral hygiene, were standardized but may still influence measurements. Finally, the predictive value of the signature for adjuvant therapy response requires linkage to treatment outcomes, which will demand prospective trials with biomarker-driven stratification. With these caveats, this study establishes a saliva-based exosomal small RNA signature, anchored by tRNA-GlyGCC-5 and the novel RNA sRESE, as a non-invasive biomarker candidate for esophageal carcinoma and demonstrates the feasibility of multicenter prospective liquid biopsy development in this disease.
