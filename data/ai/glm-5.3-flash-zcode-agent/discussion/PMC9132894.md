The pre-metastatic niche is an orchestrated, tumor-directed transformation of distant organs into a hospitable soil for disseminating cancer cells, and fibroblast activation together with extracellular matrix remodeling is one of its defining features. In this study, we designed FR17, an MMP2-activatable self-assembling branched peptide that combines a self-assembled peptide domain derived from the FFKY backbone with thymopentin, a clinically used immunomodulatory pentapeptide, connected through an MMP2-cleavable linker. Upon cleavage by MMP2, the released self-assembling monomer spontaneously folds into a membrane-like structure, the peptide nano-blanket, under both neutral and weakly acidic conditions such as those found in the inflammatory pre-metastatic microenvironment. Electron microscopy confirmed the lamellar morphology of these assemblies, with an average diameter of approximately 500 nm, and the scrambled peptide control demonstrated that both the sequence-specific assembly and the TP5 component contribute to the observed activity.

A central strength of this design is that assembly is triggered by the disease microenvironment itself. MMP2 is abundantly present in pre-metastatic sites, and we directly visualized the in situ formation of the nano-blanket in the intercellular substance of the pre-metastatic lung, where the structures ranged from hundreds to thousands of nanometers depending on the local intercellular space. The aggregation-induced emission strategy allowed the spontaneous aggregation of the released monomer to be monitored, and fluorescent signals in organ sections confirmed the responsive assembly in vivo. This disease-site-triggered, in situ self-assembly contrasts with conventional nanomedicines that must be fully preformed and delivered systemically, and it confines material formation to the pathological tissue, which should reduce off-target exposure.

Functionally, the nano-blanket impedes the activation of fibroblasts and thereby the establishment of the pre-metastatic niche. This mechanism addresses a cell population that has been difficult to target therapeutically: activated fibroblasts are not genetically aberrant, so they evade approaches aimed at tumor-cell-intrinsic vulnerabilities, yet they supply much of the extracellular matrix, growth factors, and immunosuppressive architecture that incoming tumor cells exploit. Physically interposing a peptide blanket within the intercellular space offers a non-cytotoxic way of intervening in this stromal education process. The conjugated TP5 adds an immunological dimension, given its established use as an adjunctive agent to activate immune responses, so the construct couples a physical impediment to niche formation with support for immune competence rather than the immunosuppression often associated with stromal targeting.

The broader significance of this work lies in the therapeutic paradigm it illustrates. Rather than killing cells, the intervention reprograms the microenvironmental conversation between a future metastatic site and the primary tumor by blocking the material substrate of niche construction. Because the pre-metastatic niche is prepared before detectable metastases, this approach is inherently prophylactic or adjuvant in nature and is conceptually suited to settings such as post-resection therapy, where the goal is to prevent outgrowth of already disseminated cells. The biomaterial strategy may also be complementary to existing approaches, since it does not depend on the molecular vulnerabilities of any single tumor type but on a generic feature of niche formation, matrix-remodeling enzyme activity, which is shared across many cancers.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Our findings derive from preclinical models, and the efficiency of in situ assembly, the local concentration of active peptide, and the long-term biocompatibility of accumulated peptide material in humans remain unknown. MMP2 has physiological roles in wound healing and tissue homeostasis, so chronic engagement of this pathway requires careful safety evaluation. The heterogeneity of pre-metastatic niches across organs and tumor types may also limit the universality of a single design. Nevertheless, by demonstrating that a rationally designed, microenvironment-activatable peptide can physically interfere with fibroblast activation and pre-metastatic niche formation in vivo, this study establishes a viable material-based strategy against metastasis and provides a template for engineering additional microenvironment-responsive assemblies.
