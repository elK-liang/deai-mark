DEAD-box helicases comprise the largest family of RNA helicases and participate in essentially every aspect of RNA metabolism, yet the contribution of individual family members to colorectal cancer has been only fragmentarily explored. In this study, we identify DDX10 as an overexpressed, prognostically relevant gene in colorectal cancer that promotes tumor cell proliferation and metastasis through the splicing of RPL35. DDX10 mRNA expression was evidently higher in colorectal cancer tissues than in paired paracarcinoma tissues, as shown by analysis of 50 paired samples from TCGA and 30 pairs from the GEO dataset GSE74604, and this upregulation was independently verified by GEPIA2 and ONCOMINE database analyses. At the protein level, immunohistochemistry in 35 clinical samples confirmed significantly elevated DDX10 in tumor tissues.

The clinical associations of DDX10 expression support its functional importance. Disease-free survival analyses in colorectal cancer revealed that DDX10 expression is associated with patient prognosis, and the mutation frequency of DDX10 in TCGA was low, at 3.4%, indicating that the relevant alteration in this cancer is expression dysregulation rather than mutation. Consistent with the tissue findings, multiple colorectal cancer cell lines, including LoVo, HCT116, RKO, and SW480, expressed higher DDX10 than the normal intestinal epithelial cell line NCM460, establishing the cellular basis for functional studies. The convergence of evidence across independent cohorts, databases, protein-level validation, and cell lines strengthens the conclusion that DDX10 overexpression is a consistent feature of colorectal cancer rather than a cohort-specific observation.

The mechanistic link to RPL35 splicing connects DDX10 overexpression to ribosome biology. RPL35 is an essential component of the large ribosomal subunit, and its correct splicing is required for ribosome biogenesis. A helicase-dependent effect on the splicing of a ribosomal protein transcript provides a route by which an RNA helicase can influence the protein synthesis capacity of the cell: altered RPL35 splicing would perturb ribosome assembly, a perturbation that in proliferating tumor cells must be compensated or exploited. Ribosome biogenesis is a rate-limiting process for cell growth, and its upregulation is a hallmark of transformed cells, so an oncogenic helicase that enhances the production of a ribosomal protein is mechanistically plausible. This mechanism also situates DDX10 within the nucleolar stress and p53 surveillance network, since defects in ribosome biogenesis activate p53 through ribosomal protein-MDM2 interactions, and the interplay between DDX10-driven splicing changes and this checkpoint pathway merits further study.

The demonstration that DDX10 promotes both proliferation and metastasis extends its relevance beyond growth control. Metastasis is the principal cause of death in colorectal cancer, and genes that enhance motility and invasive capacity independently of proliferation are of particular translational interest. Whether the pro-metastatic effect of DDX10 operates through the same RPL35 splicing mechanism, through translation-dependent changes in the metastatic machinery, or through helicase activities on specific transcripts involved in cell adhesion and migration remains to be resolved. From a translational standpoint, DDX10 overexpression, being consistent across cohorts and detectable at both RNA and protein levels, is a candidate biomarker for risk stratification in colorectal cancer, although its incremental value beyond established staging and molecular markers requires dedicated validation. As a therapeutic target, RNA helicases have historically been considered challenging, but the growing availability of allosteric inhibitors and the enzymatic dependency of helicase function make this class increasingly tractable, and the dependence of tumor cells, but presumably not quiescent normal epithelium, on elevated DDX10 provides a therapeutic window.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The clinical correlation analyses were based on datasets of modest size, and the prognostic value of DDX10 must be validated in larger cohorts with multivariate adjustment for stage and other molecular markers. The functional studies rely on a panel of colorectal cancer cell lines, and the importance of DDX10 in vivo, in orthotopic or patient-derived xenograft models, was not established. The splicing mechanism, while supported by the experimental evidence, represents one facet of DDX10 function, and additional RNA targets may contribute to the observed phenotypes. Finally, the upstream drivers of DDX10 overexpression in colorectal cancer, whether transcriptional, epigenetic, or copy number-related, remain unidentified. With these caveats, our study establishes DDX10 as an overexpressed, prognosis-associated helicase in colorectal cancer and defines a RPL35 splicing mechanism through which it promotes tumor cell proliferation and metastasis.
