This study provides the first nationwide assessment of the epidemiological characteristics of chronic fatigue syndrome in South Korea, based on eleven years of physician-diagnosed cases. On average, 22,720 patients were newly diagnosed each year, and the average annual incidence was estimated at 44.71 cases per 100,000 individuals. The absolute number of new diagnoses increased 1.57-fold between 2010 and 2020, and the population-adjusted incidence increased 1.53-fold over the same period, indicating a genuine rise in diagnosed disease rather than a mere effect of population growth.

A consistent female predominance emerged as the most robust demographic feature. Women were 1.49-fold more likely than men to be diagnosed, and this pattern held across all age groups from 10 to 79 years and was preserved over the study period, with the female incidence increasing slightly faster (1.66-fold) than the male incidence (1.44-fold). This female predominance, of a magnitude close to that observed in many clinical and population-based studies from Western countries, adds to the evidence that biological sex is a major determinant of susceptibility to or diagnosis of this condition, whether through hormonal, immunological, psychosocial, or help-seeking differences that remain to be disentangled.

The age distribution offers several noteworthy observations. The absolute number of diagnoses peaked in the 50-59-year age group in both sexes, showing that chronic fatigue syndrome is not confined to young adults as sometimes assumed. The relative frequency, however, rose with age, peaking in men older than 80 years and in women aged 60-69. An elevated relative frequency in the oldest groups may reflect the cumulative burden of comorbidities, or a diagnostic tendency to attribute fatigue symptoms in the elderly to chronic fatigue syndrome when alternative explanations are not pursued. Distinguishing age-related physiological fatigue and comorbidity-associated fatigue from idiopathic chronic fatigue syndrome in older adults is a recognized challenge, and claims-based data cannot resolve it.

The rising incidence over the decade deserves careful interpretation. Increasing temporal trends in claims-derived incidence can reflect true changes in disease occurrence, but also changes in diagnostic awareness, coding practice, physician reimbursement incentives, and patient willingness to seek care. The growing public and professional recognition of chronic fatigue syndrome during this period makes ascertainment effects a plausible contributor, and the observed increase may therefore combine real and diagnostic components. Longitudinal studies with consistent case definitions, ideally supplemented by clinical validation of a sample of coded diagnoses, would be required to separate these influences.

Several limitations inherent to the data source should be acknowledged. The case definition relied on physician diagnosis recorded in claims data, without centralized validation of individual diagnoses, and such diagnoses are known to be heterogeneous and potentially inclusive of conditions such as depression, hypothyroidism, or unexplained chronic fatigue that would not meet research criteria for myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome. Underdiagnosis is also likely, given the absence of a definitive biomarker and the reluctance of some patients to seek care, which would bias absolute estimates downward; indeed, the incidence observed here is considerably lower than prevalence estimates from questionnaire-based international studies. The data provide no information on symptom duration, severity, functional status, or treatment. Despite these constraints, the large, population-representative scale of the analysis establishes reference figures for incidence and prevalence in an Asian population, documents a strong and stable female predominance, defines the age distribution of diagnosed disease, and demonstrates a rising diagnostic trend, all of which can inform health service planning and further etiological research into this still poorly understood condition.
