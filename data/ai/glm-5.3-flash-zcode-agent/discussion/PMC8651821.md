High-grade serous ovarian carcinoma remains the gynecologic malignancy with the highest mortality, and cisplatin-based chemotherapy, although initially effective, is limited by the emergence of resistance. In this study, we identified the spindle assembly checkpoint kinase TTK as a key determinant of cisplatin response in HGSOC and demonstrated that its inhibition increases cisplatin sensitivity through the mTOR/autophagy pathway. Mining two independent GEO datasets of primary serous ovarian carcinoma and normal controls yielded 258 overlapping upregulated differentially expressed genes, and KEGG pathway analysis identified the cell cycle pathway as the most enriched, from which 22 participating genes were extracted. Among these, TTK was the only gene with a clinical-grade inhibitor under investigation in clinical trials that had not previously been studied in HGSOC, which directed our attention to it.

The expression and clinical data establish TTK as a relevant target in this disease. TTK was expressed at higher levels in ovarian cancer than in normal controls across several open-access databases, and the levels detected in our own patient samples, measured by RT-qPCR in 20 HGSOC specimens against 11 fallopian tube controls and by western blotting, were consistent with the database findings. Patients with high TTK expression had poorer progression-free and overall survival in Kaplan-Meier plotter analyses, indicating that TTK overexpression carries prognostic weight. Critically, TTK expression was increased in cisplatin-resistant A2780 and SKOV3 cells, directly linking the kinase to the chemoresistant state rather than merely to tumor aggressiveness.

The mechanism we delineate connects mitotic checkpoint function to autophagic regulation through mTOR. TTK, also known as MPS1, is the essential kinase of the spindle assembly checkpoint, and its overexpression promotes the chromosomal instability that characterizes high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma, a tumor defined by near-universal TP53 mutation and profound aneuploidy. Our findings indicate that TTK activity impinges on the mTOR/autophagy pathway, such that TTK inhibition modulates mTOR signaling and thereby alters autophagic flux, and that this modulation underlies the increased cisplatin sensitivity observed upon TTK inhibition. The connection between spindle checkpoint stress, mTOR signaling, and autophagy is biologically plausible: mitotic perturbation imposes proteotoxic and energetic stress that cells attempt to buffer through autophagy, and the interplay between checkpoint kinase activity and mTOR provides a route by which a mitotic kinase can influence a central metabolic-survival axis. By interfering with this adaptive capacity, TTK inhibition renders cells more vulnerable to the DNA damage imposed by cisplatin.

The therapeutic implications of these findings are supported by the translational maturity of the target. Selective TTK inhibitors have entered clinical trials in other oncology indications, which lowers the barrier to testing TTK-directed combinations in ovarian cancer. The synergy with platinum chemotherapy is particularly attractive because it addresses the central clinical problem of platinum resistance rather than competing with existing standards, and the elevated TTK expression in resistant cells suggests that TTK status could serve as a biomarker for selecting patients likely to benefit from such combinations. The established association between TTK overexpression and poor survival further supports the inclusion of TTK expression in molecular risk assessments for this disease.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The identification of TTK relied on bioinformatic analysis of public datasets, and although validated in patient samples and cell lines, the causal role of TTK in cisplatin resistance was established in a limited set of models; confirmation in additional HGSOC models, including patient-derived organoids and xenografts with documented platinum response, would strengthen the conclusion. The relative contributions of the spindle checkpoint function and the mTOR/autophagy axis to the sensitization observed were not fully dissociated experimentally, and the precise molecular route from TTK to mTOR remains to be mapped. TTK inhibitors are known to cause myelosuppression, reflecting the checkpoint's role in normal hematopoiesis, so the therapeutic window for combination with myelosuppressive platinum chemotherapy requires careful evaluation in vivo. Finally, the biomarker value of TTK expression needs validation in prospective cohorts with annotated platinum outcomes. With these caveats, our study identifies TTK as an overexpressed, prognostically adverse, and chemoresistance-associated kinase in high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma and provides a mechanistic basis, through the mTOR/autophagy pathway, for combining TTK inhibition with platinum chemotherapy.
