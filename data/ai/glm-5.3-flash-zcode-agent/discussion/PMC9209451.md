Junctophilin-2 is the structural linker that stabilizes the apposition of the plasma membrane and sarcoplasmic reticulum in cardiomyocytes, and its proteolysis by calpains during calcium overload is implicated in the excitation-contraction remodeling that accompanies heart failure. Previous efforts to map calpain cleavage of JP2 have relied on immunoblotting against individual epitopes or on fusion tagging in cell lysates, approaches limited by alternative proteolytic pathways, species-limited and affinity-biased antibody choices, and the anomalous migration of JP2 in SDS-PAGE. In this study, we characterized the spectrum of calpain cleavage products under directly controlled substrate-reaction conditions, using purified recombinant full-length mouse JP2 as the substrate and purified Calpain-1 and Calpain-2 across four orders of protease concentration.

The use of a purified substrate-reaction system resolved ambiguities that lysate-based approaches could not. At the lowest Calpain-2 concentration (0.01 U/ml), a single major cleavage event generated two products, the N-terminal NT1 fragment of approximately 75 kDa and the C-terminal CT1 fragment of approximately 25 kDa, consistent with cleavage at the site implicated in prior studies. At a tenfold higher concentration (0.1 U/ml), three additional cleavage events became evident: two further N-terminal reactions produced the fragment pairs NT2 with NT1-NT2 (approximately 20 and 55 kDa) and NT3 with NT1-NT3 (approximately 30 and 45 kDa), while C-terminal cleavage of CT1 yielded the approximately 10 kDa fragment CT2. The computational predictions of DeepCalpain, which had suggested multiple additional cleavage sites in the joining and divergent regions of JP2, were thus substantially borne out, although two predicted fragments, CT1-CT2 and NT3-NT2, were not detected, indicating that predicted susceptibility does not guarantee detectable cleavage under these conditions. The fragment pattern observed with increasing Calpain-2 concentration constitutes a dose-dependent spectrum rather than a single cleavage event, and analogous analysis with Calpain-1 establishes the specificity of the two isoforms.

The biological significance of this fragment spectrum derives from the domain organization of JP2. The N-terminal region of JP2 carries the MORN motifs that bind the plasma membrane, while the C-terminal region anchors to the junctional sarcoplasmic reticulum, so each calpain cleavage event separates these two membrane-targeting functions in a different way. The NT1 fragment, which preserves the MORN-containing membrane-binding domain, accumulates as the dominant early product, and the formation of DNA-rich NT1-fragment domains in cardiomyocytes, as examined in this study, links this cleavage product to a nuclear phenotype. Progressive cleavage at higher calcium and calpain activity, as occurs during ischemia-reperfusion and pressure overload, would therefore generate a changing repertoire of fragments, each with distinct localization and functional consequences, rather than a single pathogenic species. This perspective reframes calpain proteolysis of JP2 as a graded, concentration-dependent process whose pathological output depends on the severity and duration of calcium dysregulation.

Methodologically, our approach demonstrates the value of purified substrate-reaction systems for mapping protease specificity in multidomain proteins. The Coomassie-based total protein staining captured the complete cleavage pattern independent of antibody availability, circumventing the affinity biases that have constrained prior analyses, and the direct comparison of Calpain-1 and Calpain-2 on the identical substrate provides an isoform-specific reference for interpreting fragment patterns detected in tissue. The concordance between computational prediction and experimental detection, including the instructive discrepancies, calibrates the reliability of in silico cleavage site prediction for future applications.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The recombinant substrate, expressed in bacteria and purified, lacks the post-translational modifications and membrane-associated conformational context of JP2 in cardiomyocytes, and cleavage susceptibility in situ may differ where JP2 is oligomerized at junctional membranes. The in vitro concentrations and incubation conditions, while systematically varied, do not reproduce the transient, localized calpain activation that occurs in the cardiomyocyte during injury. The identification of fragments relied on apparent molecular weight, and definitive assignment of cleavage sites by mass spectrometry for every band would strengthen the map. Finally, the functional consequences of individual fragments, particularly the NT1 domains observed in cardiomyocytes, require direct mechanistic study to determine whether they act as dominant-negative membrane-binding competitors, nuclear effectors, or degradation intermediates. With these caveats, our study provides the first systematically controlled spectrum of calpain cleavage products of junctophilin-2, demonstrates the differential behavior of Calpain-1 and Calpain-2, and establishes the foundation for interpreting JP2 fragmentation in cardiac disease states.
