In this study, we identified SNHG10 as a downregulated long non-coding RNA in epithelial ovarian cancer with tumor-suppressive properties and clarified the mechanism through which it acts. By intersecting the top downregulated lncRNAs from two independent GEO datasets, we narrowed a shortlist of five candidates and verified by qRT-PCR that all were expressed at low levels in EOC tissues, with SNHG10 showing the lowest expression. Analysis of the TCGA database independently confirmed that SNHG10 is significantly downregulated in EOC, and low SNHG10 expression was associated with shorter progression-free survival and overall survival. Multivariate analysis identified SNHG10 expression, together with intraperitoneal metastasis and lymph node metastasis, as significantly correlated with patient prognosis, supporting its potential as an independent predictive biomarker for EOC patients.

The functional mechanism we define places SNHG10 within the competing endogenous RNA framework. SNHG10 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-200a-3p, thereby relieving the repression of BIN1, a well-characterized tumor suppressor. This axis is biologically coherent: the miR-200 family is a central regulator of epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and its dysregulation promotes the acquisition of invasive and metastatic behavior in epithelial cancers. By sequestering miR-200a-3p, SNHG10 maintains BIN1 expression, and the loss of SNHG10 in EOC unleashes miR-200a-3p-mediated suppression of BIN1, favoring tumorigenesis and EMT. The identification of EMT as a downstream consequence is consistent with the clinicopathological correlations we observed, in which low SNHG10 tracks with adverse prognostic features such as intraperitoneal metastasis.

These findings have implications in two directions. As a biomarker, SNHG10 adds to the growing list of lncRNAs whose expression carries prognostic information in ovarian cancer. Because it was validated in independent datasets, in patient tissues, and by survival and multivariate analyses, SNHG10 expression is a candidate to be tested prospectively alongside established clinicopathological variables, potentially contributing to risk stratification beyond stage and residual disease. As a mechanistic target, the SNHG10/miR-200a-3p/BIN1 axis suggests that restoring SNHG10 expression, inhibiting the relevant miRNA, or protecting BIN1 from repression could counteract EMT and tumor progression. Delivery remains the principal obstacle for lncRNA-based therapeutics, but the tumor-suppressive direction of this axis is favorable in that it proposes reconstitution of a lost function rather than blockade of an oncogenic one.

Our study also illustrates the value of an unbiased, multi-dataset discovery strategy. Rather than selecting a candidate for its known associations, we systematically overlapped differentially expressed lncRNAs across public cohorts and then prioritized by expression level and prognostic correlation. This approach reduces the risk of single-cohort artifacts, although it does not eliminate them: GEO datasets are heterogeneous with respect to histological subtype, stage distribution, and treatment, and the two cohorts used for discovery may share biases. The consistency of the SNHG10 findings across discovery, TCGA validation, and our own patient samples mitigates this concern.

Several limitations should be noted. The clinical correlation analyses were conducted in a cohort of limited size, and the prognostic value of SNHG10 requires validation in larger, preferably prospective, multicenter cohorts before any clinical use. Our mechanistic experiments were performed mainly in cell lines, and the contribution of the SNHG10/miR-200a-3p/BIN1 axis within the complex microenvironment of ovarian cancer, including immune and stromal compartments, remains to be defined. Finally, lncRNAs frequently act through multiple mechanisms, and sponge activity toward miR-200a-3p may not be the sole function of SNHG10; additional protein-interaction or transcriptional roles could exist. Within these boundaries, our results establish SNHG10 as a clinically relevant tumor-suppressive lncRNA in epithelial ovarian cancer and provide a mechanistic rationale for targeting the miR-200a-3p/BIN1 node in this disease.
