Primary breast diffuse large B-cell lymphoma with concurrent MYC and BCL2 or BCL6 rearrangement, so-called double-hit lymphoma, is a rare and incompletely characterized entity. In this multicenter study, we report the management and outcomes of 48 patients, which to our knowledge represents one of the largest real-world experiences with this disease. The median age of 48 years, the predominance of painless unilateral breast masses, the greater involvement of the left breast, and the median tumor size of 2.6 cm are consistent with the clinical presentation of primary breast lymphoma, while the stage distribution, with 13 patients at stage IE, 21 at IIE, and 14 at stage IV, reflects the propensity of this lymphoma for regional and distant dissemination at presentation.

Several biological observations emerged from our cohort. The overwhelming majority of patients (83.3%) were of the germinal center B-cell type by the Hans algorithm, and all cases expressed C-MYC protein, with 43 cases co-expressing BCL2 and BCL6. Chromosomal abnormalities were documented in 12 of 48 patients, and the use of next-generation sequencing in a subset of 14 samples revealed recurrent mutations in TP53, BCL6, BCL2, CD79B, and IGHD. The predominance of the GCB phenotype is noteworthy because double-hit lymphoma arising at nodal sites more frequently displays a non-GCB phenotype; this discrepancy suggests that breast double-hit lymphoma may represent a biologically distinct subgroup within the double-hit spectrum. The high frequency of TP53 alterations in the sequenced samples is consistent with the aggressive clinical behavior of this disease and may interact with MYC-driven lymphomagenesis.

The treatment experience in our cohort was heterogeneous, reflecting the absence of a standard approach for this rare entity. Patients received DA-EPOCH-R-based regimens, R-HyperCVAD, or dose-adjusted strategies with maintenance or intensification, and 18 patients underwent autologous stem cell transplantation after initial therapy. After a median follow-up of 47 months, 28 of 48 patients had died. This outcome, with mortality affecting well over half of the cohort, confirms that primary breast double-hit lymphoma carries a poor prognosis even with intensive immunochemotherapy. The small numbers within each treatment stratum preclude meaningful between-regimen comparisons, but the overall experience argues that neither conventional R-CHOP-type therapy nor intensified approaches have solved the problem of this disease, and that strategies such as targeted agents directed at MYC, BCL2, or the B-cell receptor pathway, or novel antibody-drug conjugates and bispecific antibodies, deserve evaluation.

Our findings have implications for diagnosis and clinical management. The rarity of breast double-hit lymphoma means that it is frequently not considered in the differential diagnosis of a breast mass, and histological confirmation with adequate immunophenotyping and FISH testing for MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 rearrangements is essential. In our cohort, 38 patients were tested for MYC and BCL2 translocations and 10 for MYC and BCL6, reflecting variation in diagnostic practice across centers; standardized testing protocols would improve the comparability of future studies. For patients with stage IE or IIE disease, the role of consolidative radiation therapy and of upfront autologous transplantation remains undefined and should be addressed in prospective trials or at least in prospectively collected registries.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The study is retrospective and multicenter, with the inherent risks of selection bias, incomplete documentation, and treatment heterogeneity. The modest sample size, although substantial for this entity, limits the statistical power for prognostic factor analysis, and the number of patients assessed by next-generation sequencing was small. Central pathological review was not performed, so the application of double-hit criteria may have varied across institutions, particularly for cases in which FISH testing was incomplete. Follow-up duration, while reasonable, may underestimate late relapses. Despite these limitations, this real-world multicenter experience documents the demographic, pathological, and genetic characteristics of primary breast double-hit lymphoma, demonstrates its poor outcomes under current treatment strategies, and provides the baseline evidence needed to design collaborative prospective studies for this orphan disease.
