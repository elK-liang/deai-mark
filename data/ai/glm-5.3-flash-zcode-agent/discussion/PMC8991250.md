The results of this study indicate that N6-isopentenyladenosine exerts a marked antiproliferative effect on human glioblastoma cells irrespective of their EGFR background. Treatment with iPA reduced cell viability and DNA synthesis in a dose- and time-dependent manner in parental U87MG cells and in derivatives overexpressing either wild-type EGFR or the constitutively active variant EGFRvIII, with IC50 values at 48 hours of 3 µM, 4.3 µM, and 7.1 µM, respectively. Importantly, the same treatment did not significantly reduce the proliferation of normal human astrocytes, suggesting a degree of selectivity for transformed cells that is desirable for any candidate antiglioma agent.

The inclusion of EGFR-engineered models is particularly relevant to the biology of glioblastoma. Amplification of EGFR and its oncogenic variant EGFRvIII are hallmark molecular features of this tumor and are associated with resistance to apoptosis and to pharmacological treatment. In our experiments, cells expressing EGFRvIII were the least sensitive, showing the highest IC50 value, which is consistent with the contribution of this receptor to aggressive behavior. Nevertheless, the difference between the three lines was modest, and iPA retained activity against EGFRvIII-expressing cells at achievable concentrations. This observation supports the idea that iPA acts downstream of, or parallel to, EGFR-driven survival signaling, and that cell death induced by iPA does not rely on the apoptotic routes that EGFR signaling is known to subvert.

The cell cycle analysis provides further insight into the sequence of events triggered by iPA. At 24 hours of exposure, cells accumulated in the G0/G1 phase, whereas at 48 hours the fraction of cells in G0/G1 decreased and the G2/M population increased significantly. This temporal progression suggests that the initial growth arrest is followed by perturbation of mitotic progression as cells lose viability. Whether the cell cycle effects are a cause or a consequence of the activation of the death program remains to be clarified, but the close coupling between cell cycle disturbance and loss of proliferation is consistent with a sustained, progressive cytotoxic action rather than a transient cytostatic one.

A central conclusion of this work is that iPA kills glioblastoma cells through necroptosis. This mode of regulated necrosis is of special interest in the context of glioblastoma, where defective apoptosis is a well-recognized mechanism of therapeutic resistance. Because necroptosis does not depend on the canonical apoptotic machinery, it offers a way to eliminate tumor cells that would otherwise survive caspase-dependent death signals. Regulated necrosis is also generally viewed as a more inflammatory form of cell death than apoptosis, raising the possibility that necroptotic tumor cells could promote an antitumor immune response, although this hypothesis will require testing in appropriate models. Our finding that iPA is effective across EGFR backgrounds, including the apoptosis-resistant EGFRvIII context, fits well with the engagement of this alternative death pathway.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. The study relies on a panel of cell lines, however representative, and does not address the tumor microenvironment, hypoxia, or intratumoral heterogeneity that characterize glioblastoma in patients. The concentrations used are within a low micromolar range, but their relationship to clinically attainable plasma and brain exposures has not been established, and the blood-brain barrier remains a critical consideration for any systemic approach. Future work should therefore extend these observations to orthotopic and patient-derived models, dissect the molecular signaling that executes necroptosis downstream of iPA, and explore rational combinations with standard temozolomide-based therapy or with EGFR-directed agents. Within these boundaries, our data identify iPA as a selective cytotoxic compound for glioblastoma cells that engages a non-apoptotic death pathway and thus deserves further preclinical development.
