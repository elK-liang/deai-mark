Abstract

Kidney transplant rejection (KTR) remains a major cause of allograft failure, and reliable molecular biomarkers for early diagnosis and accurate subtyping are urgently needed. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as promising non-invasive biomarkers; however, their regulatory roles and mechanisms in KTR at a systems level have not been fully elucidated.

We constructed a KTR-specific miRNA-mediated protein–protein interaction network (miR-PPIN) by integrating differentially expressed miRNAs and mRNAs, miRNA–mRNA regulatory pairs, and protein–protein interaction data. Functional enrichment analysis was performed to assess the biological relevance of network genes. Topological features, including degree, closeness, and betweenness centrality, were used to prioritize hub genes (CORE gene set), and miRNAs were ranked based on the number of CORE targets they regulate (NTGA and NTGC). Candidate biomarkers were further evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis in both training and independent validation cohorts.

The miR-PPIN comprised 31 miRNAs and 642 genes connected through 949 miRNA–mRNA pairs and 1,447 protein–protein interactions. Network genes were significantly enriched in T cell-related biological processes, including NK T cell proliferation and T cell antigen processing and presentation, supporting the network's specificity for KTR. Node degrees followed a power-law distribution (slope = −1.34), indicating scale-free topology. Three miRNAs—miR-145-5p, miR-23b-3p, and miR-155-5p—were identified as candidate biomarkers based on significantly high NTGA and NTGC values. miR-145-5p and miR-23b-3p were downregulated in KTR, whereas miR-155-5p was overexpressed, particularly in chronic medullary rejection (CMR). ROC analyses demonstrated diagnostic and subtyping potential of these miRNAs individually and in combination.

Multi-omics network characterization revealed novel miRNA biomarkers and mechanistic insights for kidney transplant rejection. The identified miR-145-5p, miR-23b-3p, and miR-155-5p represent promising candidates for non-invasive diagnosis and subtyping of KTR, warranting further clinical validation.
