Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is one of the most common chronic liver diseases worldwide, and its progressive form, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), is characterized by hepatic inflammation and hepatocellular injury. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of inflammatory and stress responses; however, their expression landscapes and functional contributions to NAFLD-to-NASH progression remain incompletely defined. Methods: To comprehensively profile lncRNA and mRNA expression during disease progression, we employed the Mouse LncRNA Microarray V2.0 in db/db mice fed a normal diet (NAFLD model) or a methionine-choline-deficient (MCD) diet (NASH model), with db/db mice on chow as controls. Liver histopathology was assessed by hematoxylin–eosin staining, and inflammation was evaluated by immunohistochemistry for the macrophage marker F4/80. Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels were measured to confirm hepatocellular injury. Differentially expressed lncRNAs across the three groups were identified, followed by Venn and series-cluster analyses to pinpoint lncRNAs with progressive upregulation from normal liver to NAFLD to NASH. Results: NAFLD mice displayed marked hepatic steatosis, whereas NASH mice exhibited pronounced inflammatory cell infiltration, elevated serum transaminases (ALT 2.98-fold and AST 1.94-fold higher than controls), and increased F4/80 staining. Microarray profiling identified 3,111 differentially expressed lncRNAs between NAFLD and control mice (1,915 up- and 1,196 downregulated), 2,677 between NASH and control mice (1,394 up- and 1,283 downregulated), and 3,337 between NASH and NAFLD mice (1,513 up- and 1,824 downregulated). Venn analysis revealed 63 lncRNAs commonly altered across all three comparisons, and series-cluster analysis identified 381 lncRNAs that were upregulated in NAFLD and further increased in NASH. Among these progressively upregulated transcripts, we focused on the novel lncRNA-Gm9795 for further investigation. Conclusion: Our study establishes a comprehensive lncRNA expression atlas distinguishing NAFLD from NASH and identifies lncRNA-Gm9795 as a candidate regulator whose progressive upregulation parallels the transition from steatosis to steatohepatitis, providing a basis for mechanistic studies of lncRNA-driven inflammation in NASH.
