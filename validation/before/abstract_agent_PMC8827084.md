Abstract: Myocardin (MYOCD) is a master regulator of the vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) contractile phenotype, and we investigated whether it controls phenotypic modulation by regulating autophagy. In human aortic VSMCs (HA-VSMCs), knockdown of MYOCD with shRNA decreased the expression of the contractile proteins ACTA2 and SM22α, increased the proliferative markers osteopontin (OPN) and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), and significantly increased the number of EdU-positive cells. MYOCD knockdown simultaneously decreased the expression of p62, increased beclin1 expression and promoted the conversion of LC3-I to LC3-II, and cotreatment with the lysosomal inhibitor bafilomycin A1 led to higher accumulation of LC3-II and p62 compared with the shMYOCD group, indicating that knockdown of MYOCD increased the autophagic flux. Consistently, more GFP-LC3 puncta were observed in MYOCD-knockdown cells, and bafilomycin A1 further increased the number of GFP-LC3 dots. Treatment with the autophagy inhibitor 3-methyladenine inhibited the autophagy induced by MYOCD knockdown and restored the level of the contractile proteins ACTA2 and SM22α. Mechanistically, the Myocardin/microRNA-30a/Beclin1 signaling axis mediates this regulation. These results demonstrate that MYOCD controls the phenotypic modulation of vascular smooth muscle cells by regulating autophagy, linking the contractile phenotype to autophagic activity in the vessel wall.
