Abstract: Collagen architecture in breast tissue is thought to influence cancer risk, and we quantitatively examined mammary collagen architecture and its association with mammographic density and lesion severity among women undergoing image-guided breast biopsy. In this case-control analysis, 138 women were included: 65 with invasive or in situ cancer and 73 with benign breast disease (BBD), matched on age (mean 52.8 vs 51.9 years), BMI and postmenopausal status. A total of 243 regions of interest (ROIs) were identified and analyzed, including 133 ROIs among BBD controls (71 benign and 62 normal background tissue) and 110 ROIs among cases (56 cancer, 39 benign and 15 normal background tissue), encompassing approximately 3 million collagen fibers. Collagen fiber features including length, straightness, width, alignment, density and orientation were quantified for each ROI. In descriptive, unadjusted analyses, average fiber length, straightness and density were greater in normal ROIs for both cases and controls, followed by benign ROIs, followed by cancer ROIs in the cancer cases. Collagen fiber characteristics were associated with histologic measures of tissue composition extracted from H&E-stained sections, while they were not associated with participant characteristics among controls. These findings quantitatively characterize mammary collagen architecture across normal, benign and cancerous breast tissue and support the association of collagen organization with mammographic density and lesion severity, suggesting its potential as a marker of breast cancer risk and progression.
