Abstract: The sodium/myo-inositol co-transporter SLC5A3 controls cellular uptake of myo-inositol (MI), and its role in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) was examined here. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas showed that SLC5A3 transcript levels were significantly higher in NSCLC tumor tissues (n = 1037) than in normal lung tissues (n = 108), and SLC5A3 mRNA was also elevated in NSCLC tumor tissues compared with paired adjacent normal lung tissues (n = 106 each). In a local cohort of 13 NSCLC patients who underwent tumor resection, SLC5A3 mRNA levels were significantly higher in tumor tissues than in paired adjacent normal tissues, and western blotting and immunohistochemistry confirmed SLC5A3 protein upregulation in tumor specimens. Consistently, immortalized A549 cells and primary human NSCLC cells (pCan-1, pCan-2 and pCan-3) expressed significantly higher levels of SLC5A3 mRNA and protein than primary human lung epithelial cells and established BEAS-2B epithelial cells. In the NSCLC cells, ISYNA1 protein expression was low and even lower than in epithelial cells, whereas intracellular MI levels were significantly elevated compared with lung epithelial cells. Functional analyses further showed that SLC5A3 promotes NSCLC cell growth, supporting a pro-tumorigenic role of the transporter. Collectively, these results identify SLC5A3 as an upregulated protein in human NSCLC tissues and cells and highlight the SLC5A3-MI axis as a potential target for NSCLC therapy.
