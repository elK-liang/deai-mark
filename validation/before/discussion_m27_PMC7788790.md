Discussion

The assessment of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and immune checkpoint expression has become increasingly important in breast cancer management, particularly with the advent of immunotherapeutic approaches. While hematoxylin and eosin-based stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte scoring has demonstrated prognostic and predictive utility in early-stage breast cancer, the semi-quantitative nature of this methodology may limit its sensitivity for detecting subtle changes induced by immunotherapy. In this study, we developed a multiplex immunofluorescence approach to quantify stromal TILs and PD-L1 expression with the aim of achieving greater precision than conventional H&E scoring while maintaining clinical applicability. Our results demonstrate that the sTILmIF score, defined as the density of stromal T cells (CD4+, CD8+, and FoxP3+), correlates significantly with traditional H&E-based sTIL scoring across both pre-treatment biopsies and excisional specimens, with correlation coefficients of 0.59 and 0.63, respectively. These findings suggest that multiplex immunofluorescence can serve as a valid alternative to H&E scoring for the assessment of stromal TILs in early-stage breast cancer, while providing the additional benefit of simultaneous measurement of multiple immune parameters.

The moderate-to-good correlation between sTILH&E and sTILmIF scores is notable given the fundamental differences in how these metrics are derived. H&E scoring estimates the percentage of stromal area occupied by mononuclear cells, whereas our mIF-based approach specifically enumerates defined T cell subsets normalized to stromal area. The inclusion of helper, cytotoxic, and regulatory T cells in our sTILmIF metric provides a comprehensive assessment of the T cell compartment, though it necessarily excludes other mononuclear cells such as plasma cells and macrophages that contribute to the H&E-based score. This distinction may explain some of the variance between the two methodologies and highlights the complementary nature of these approaches. The strong correlation coefficients observed in our analysis nonetheless support the construct validity of our mIF-based sTIL measurement as a surrogate for conventional stromal TIL assessment.

Perhaps the most striking observation from our analysis is the substantial intra-sample heterogeneity in sTILmIF scores across regions of interest within individual tumors. The mean coefficient of variation of 0.61, with a range extending from 0.27 to 1.44, indicates that T cell infiltration is not uniformly distributed throughout the tumor microenvironment. This heterogeneity has important implications for both research and clinical applications, as it suggests that single-biopsy assessments may not accurately represent the immune landscape of an entire tumor. The substantial variability observed in our study underscores the value of multi-region sampling or whole-slide analysis when assessing tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, particularly in the context of monitoring treatment response where focal changes may be biologically significant but missed by limited sampling approaches.

The ability to simultaneously assess multiple immune parameters using multiplex immunofluorescence represents a significant advantage over traditional H&E-based TIL scoring. Beyond the sTILmIF metric, our panel enables quantification of CD8+ T cell density, PD-L1 expression on both tumor cells and infiltrating immune cells, and the relative proportions of different T cell subsets. This comprehensive approach allows for more nuanced characterization of the tumor immune microenvironment that may better predict response to immunotherapy. Given that IRX-2 treatment has been shown to upregulate PD-L1 expression and increase immune-related gene signatures, the precision offered by mIF quantification may prove particularly valuable for detecting these dynamic changes and understanding the mechanisms underlying treatment effects.

Several limitations of our study warrant consideration. The sTILmIF metric, as defined in our analysis, captures only T lymphocytes and does not include plasma cells, which contribute to the H&E-based sTIL score. While plasma cells are typically present in relatively sparse clusters within early-stage breast cancer, their exclusion may result in underestimation of total mononuclear cell infiltration in tumors with prominent plasma cell responses. Additionally, our analysis focused on specific T cell subsets, and the prognostic or predictive significance of individual populations may differ from the composite sTIL score. Future studies incorporating additional markers for plasma cells, macrophages, and other immune populations would provide a more complete picture of the tumor immune microenvironment. The spatial relationships between different immune cell populations, which may be crucial for understanding immune activation or suppression, also merit further investigation using the multiplex immunofluorescence platform described herein.

In conclusion, we have developed and validated a multiplex immunofluorescence approach for the quantitative assessment of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and PD-L1 expression in early-stage breast cancer. The significant correlation between sTILmIF and conventional H&E-based sTIL scoring supports the validity of this methodology, while the substantial intra-sample heterogeneity observed highlights the importance of comprehensive sampling strategies. The simultaneous measurement of multiple immune parameters afforded by multiplex immunofluorescence represents a powerful tool for characterizing the tumor immune microenvironment and may facilitate more precise monitoring of treatment-induced changes in future clinical trials investigating immunotherapeutic approaches in early-stage breast cancer.
