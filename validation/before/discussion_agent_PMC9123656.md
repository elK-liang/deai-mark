Melanoma subtypes prevalent in Asian populations, principally acral and mucosal melanoma, differ biologically from the cutaneous melanoma that dominates Western incidence, and they are typically underserved by therapies developed and tested in Western cohorts. In this phase I study, we evaluated the safety, activity, and pharmacokinetics of camrelizumab, a humanized anti-PD-1 antibody, in 36 patients with advanced melanoma in a Chinese population. The cohort reflected the Asian melanoma spectrum, with primary sites of acral origin in 50.0%, mucosal in 27.8%, cutaneous and unknown origin in 19.4%, and one choroidal case, and with the majority of patients having received at least two prior lines of chemotherapy or targeted therapy.

The safety profile established in this study was favorable and characterized by a distinctive toxicity. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed across the three dose groups, the maximum tolerated dose was not reached, and all 36 patients received at least one dose. Treatment-related adverse events occurred in 29 patients (80.6%), the most common being reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (RCCEP) at 63.9%, followed by hypothyroidism (25.0%), rash (13.9%), fever (11.1%), hyperthyroidism (8.3%), and vitiligo and fatigue (5.6% each). Grade 3 or higher events were rare, comprising one case of liver injury that recovered after symptomatic treatment and two cases of asymptomatic elevations of γ-glutamyltransferase and blood triglycerides. No administrations were suspended or terminated due to treatment-related adverse events, and there were no study drug-related deaths. RCCEP, a vascular proliferation observed predominantly on the face and trunk after the first cycle, is a toxicity characteristic of camrelizumab that has not been reported at comparable frequency with other PD-1 antibodies, and its mostly mild, reversible nature, along with the reported association between its occurrence and efficacy, makes it a phenomenon of both safety and scientific interest, potentially reflecting VEGF pathway engagement by this particular antibody.

The population studied is significant for the interpretation of these results. More than half of the patients were staged as M1c or M1d, 77.8% had an ECOG performance status of 1, and 55.6% had elevated lactate dehydrogenase, all features associated with poor prognosis in melanoma, and 66.7% had received at least two prior lines of therapy. BRAF V600E mutations were present in only 8.3% of patients, consistent with the low frequency of BRAF mutations in acral and mucosal melanoma, and confirming that targeted therapy is applicable to only a small minority of Asian melanoma patients. In this unmet-need population, immune checkpoint blockade represents the principal systemic option, and the tolerability documented here, together with the pharmacokinetic profile that supported the selected dose, establishes camrelizumab as a suitable agent for this population. The observation that seven patients remained on treatment for more than one year and four for more than two years speaks to the durability of disease control that can be achieved in a subset of heavily pretreated patients.

The study design, a phase I trial with dose escalation and expansion, has inherent constraints that qualify these conclusions. With 36 patients distributed across three dose groups, the cohort is too small to estimate response rates with precision or to identify predictive biomarkers, and the lack of a control arm prevents any assessment of the contribution of camrelizumab beyond historical expectations. The single-arm phase I context also means that the activity signals reported here require confirmation in the randomized phase II and III studies that have followed this program, and the modest number of patients with each melanoma subtype limits subtype-specific conclusions, although the inclusion of acral and mucosal patients, rather than their exclusion, is itself a strength relative to the Western trials that shaped checkpoint inhibitor development.

Several additional limitations should be acknowledged. The trial was conducted at a limited number of centers in China between 2016 and 2017, before the establishment of current standards for subsequent therapy, and the prior treatment landscape of the enrolled patients may differ from contemporary practice. The pharmacokinetic assessment in a heavily pretreated population may not fully represent patients with better organ function. Finally, RCCEP management, although generally supportive care, was still being codified at the time of this study, and its incidence may vary with dose and schedule. Within these boundaries, this phase I study establishes the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetic profile of camrelizumab in advanced Asian melanoma, documents the distinctive RCCEP toxicity, and provides the foundation on which the subsequent registration trials of camrelizumab in melanoma and other cancers were built.
