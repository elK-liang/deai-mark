Abstract: Complex, naturalistic behavioral tasks are needed to study how animals compose knowledge into flexible goal-directed behavior. We trained two monkeys to play an adapted Pac-Man (Namco) game in which they navigated Pac-Man through a maze to eat pellets and energizers while avoiding two ghost predators, Blinky and Clyde, each following a unique deterministic algorithm based on Pac-Man's and their own locations, with Blinky chasing Pac-Man more aggressively. If Pac-Man was caught, the monkeys received a time-out penalty, after which Pac-Man and the ghosts were reset to their starting locations. Eating an energizer cast the ghosts into a temporary scared mode that Pac-Man could capture for extra rewards. All game elements that yield points in the original game provided juice rewards instead, and completing a game with fewer attempts yielded additional juice. The game therefore functioned as a naturalistic foraging task requiring navigation with the risk of encountering predators, making it intuitive for the animals and crucial to training success, which proceeded from simple mazes without ghosts to the fully elaborated game. Behavior was analyzed over 74 testing sessions after all game elements had been introduced, with joystick movements, eye movements and pupil sizes recorded simultaneously. On average, the animals completed 33 ± 9 games per session, with each game taking 4.9 ± 1.8 attempts. These results establish Pac-Man as a rich behavioral platform and show that monkeys can engage in compositional strategies and hierarchical decision-making during complex video game play.
