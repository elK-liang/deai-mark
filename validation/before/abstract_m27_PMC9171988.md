Junctional adhesion molecule-like protein (JAML) is frequently upregulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and correlates with advanced tumor stage, but its functional role and mechanism remain unclear. In this study, we analyzed JAML expression in 15 paired LUAD and peritumor tissues by qRT‑PCR and Western blot, and validated its upregulation in 30 additional tissue pairs by immunohistochemistry. Statistical analysis revealed that high JAML expression significantly associated with advanced pT and pTNM stages (P < 0.001), but not with age, gender, nodal status, or distant metastasis. Immunofluorescence showed cytoplasmic localization of JAML in LUAD cells. Among four LUAD cell lines, JAML was relatively high in A549 and H1299 cells and low in PC9 and H1975 cells. Functional assays were performed by silencing JAML in high‑expressing cells using a specific siRNA (siJAML1) and by overexpressing JAML in low‑expressing cells. The results demonstrated that JAML positively regulates tumor cell invasion and migration, and mechanistic studies indicated that these effects are mediated through activation of the Wnt/β‑catenin signaling pathway. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that JAML functions as an oncogene in LUAD, promoting tumor progression via the Wnt/β‑catenin pathway, and suggest that JAML may be a potential therapeutic target for LUAD.
