Vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) are distinguished by their remarkable plasticity. In mature blood vessels they maintain a differentiated, contractile phenotype characterized by abundant contractile proteins such as alpha-smooth muscle actin (ACTA2) and SM22α, which supports their physiological roles in vasomotor tone and blood pressure regulation. In response to vascular injury or pathological stimuli, VSMCs undergo phenotypic modulation toward a synthetic, proliferative and migratory state, marked by loss of contractile markers and gain of proteins such as osteopontin (OPN) and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA). This phenotypic switching is a central event in the development of atherosclerosis, restenosis, and other proliferative vascular diseases, and the transcriptional mechanisms that govern it have therefore attracted intense interest.

Myocardin (MYOCD) is a master transcriptional coactivator of serum response factor and the principal driver of the VSMC contractile gene program. Consistent with this role, reduced MYOCD expression promotes the acquisition of a proliferative, synthetic phenotype in VSMCs. However, transcriptional reprogramming alone does not fully account for the metabolic and degradative remodeling that accompanies phenotypic modulation, and recent work has implicated autophagy, the lysosomal degradation pathway that recycles cytoplasmic components, as a regulator of VSMC behavior in vascular disease. Whether autophagy acts upstream or downstream of the contractile gene program, and how it is controlled during phenotypic modulation, remain open questions.

MicroRNAs have emerged as important post-transcriptional regulators of this interplay. Among them, microRNA-30a-5p has been identified as a regulator of autophagy through its capacity to target beclin1, an essential autophagy-initiation protein. How microRNA-30a-5p might be integrated with myocardin signaling, and whether such a regulatory module coordinates autophagy with the contractile phenotype of VSMCs, has not been established.

In this study, we investigated the relationship between MYOCD, microRNA-30a-5p, beclin1, and autophagy in human aortic VSMCs. We found that knockdown of MYOCD not only suppressed contractile marker expression and promoted proliferation, but also increased autophagic flux, as evidenced by reduced p62 levels, increased beclin1 expression, enhanced conversion of LC3-I to LC3-II, accumulation of GFP-LC3 puncta, and further LC3-II accumulation in the presence of the lysosomal inhibitor bafilomycin A1. Pharmacological inhibition of autophagy with 3-methyladenine restored the expression of contractile proteins in MYOCD-deficient cells, indicating that autophagy contributes functionally to phenotypic modulation. Our results define a MYOCD/microRNA-30a/beclin1 signaling axis that links autophagy regulation to the contractile-synthetic phenotype switch of vascular smooth muscle cells.
