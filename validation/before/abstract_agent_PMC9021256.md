Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) cells harboring the BCR-ABL T315I mutation are resistant to imatinib (IM), and metabolic alterations have been linked to this resistance. We tested whether modulation of energy metabolism with the glycolytic inhibitor 2-deoxyglucose (2-DG), which targets hexokinase, could overcome IM resistance in CML cells. Using CCK-8 assays and direct cell counting, the IC50 values of IM were 0.09 µM in BCR-ABL-positive KBM5 cells and 2.84 µM in KBM5-T315I cells, a 30-fold difference confirming that the T315I mutation confers significant IM resistance. In contrast, the IC50 values of 2-DG were similar in the two cell lines (1.62 mM and 1.28 mM), indicating no co-resistance between IM and 2-DG, likely due to their different mechanisms of action and suggesting the potential of 2-DG to overcome IM resistance. Combination treatment with 2-DG and IM resulted in a dose-dependent enhancement of cell growth inhibition in both KBM5 and KBM5-T315I cells compared with either drug alone, and direct cell counting consistently showed that 2-DG enhanced the suppressive effect of IM on CML cell proliferation. Quantitative analysis of drug combination indexes showed CI values mostly below 1.0 in both CML cell lines, indicating strong synergy between the two drugs. These results demonstrate that targeting glycolysis modulates energy metabolism to overcome drug resistance in CML cells through induction of autophagy, supporting the combination of metabolic inhibitors with tyrosine kinase inhibitors as a strategy against drug-resistant CML.
