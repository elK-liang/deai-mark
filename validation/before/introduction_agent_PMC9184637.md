Black phosphorus (BP), a two-dimensional elemental semiconductor, has attracted intense interest for optoelectronic applications owing to its layer-tunable bandgap, high carrier mobility, and, distinctively, its strong in-plane optical and electrical anisotropy between the armchair and zigzag crystal directions. These properties make BP a promising material for polarization-resolved photodetection, in which the polarization state of incident light is read out directly from the anisotropic photoresponse. Polarization-resolved detection is valuable for imaging, sensing, and communication applications, but photodetectors based on field-effect transistor geometries have been limited by trade-offs between sensitivity, dark current, and speed.

Photodetectors based on two-dimensional materials typically exploit either the photothermoelectric (PTE) or the photovoltaic (PV) effect. In conventional BP field-effect transistors, the PTE photocurrent arises at interfaces between regions of different Seebeck coefficients, which are usually created by the electrical doping imposed by the metal electrodes. When the doping level is spatially uniform, carriers driven by the temperature gradient transport in opposite directions and the net current vanishes, so the photoresponse depends critically on artificial asymmetries near the contacts. In addition, the conductive channel of a field-effect transistor inherently carries a large dark current, which degrades the detectivity and masks weak signals. A device geometry that combines a strong, internally defined photothermal asymmetry with a low dark current would substantially advance BP-based photodetection.

Ferroelectric materials provide a means of patterning electrostatically distinct regions within a single channel. By contacting a ferroelectric layer to few-layer BP, the local doping of the channel can be defined by the polarization state of adjacent ferroelectric domains, allowing p-type and n-type regions to be written without chemical doping or electrode-induced asymmetry. The thermoelectric conversion efficiency of the resulting junction is governed by the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, and thermal conductivities, which in BP differ markedly between the armchair and zigzag directions, and the Seebeck coefficient itself can be tuned by the external electric field, further shaping the photoresponse.

Here we design and fabricate a BP in-plane PN homojunction photodetector defined by ferroelectric domains. The junction combines the PTE and PV effects within a single device, with the built-in field providing a positive contribution to the photocurrent on both sides of the junction and suppressing dark current. We characterize the anisotropic photoresponse and demonstrate an ultra-sensitive, polarization-resolved detector whose performance follows the strong directional dependence of the BP band structure on the applied ferroelectric field, including bandgap narrowing in the armchair direction through the giant Stark effect while the zigzag band structure remains essentially unchanged.
