Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a lethal malignancy whose progression is frequently driven by aberrant long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs). To identify clinically relevant lncRNAs in ESCC, we analyzed two independent lncRNA + mRNA microarray datasets comprising 119 and 60 paired tumor‑normal tissues and screened for differentially expressed transcripts. Among the most consistently up‑regulated lncRNAs, DLEU1 (deleted in lymphocytic leukemia 1) stood out; knockdown of DLEU1 with a pooled siRNA strategy markedly suppressed ESCC cell growth, whereas the other candidates had weaker effects. Validation in the same cohorts showed that DLEU1 was over‑expressed in 118 of 119 (99.2 %) and 59 of 60 (98.3 %) tumors relative to matched normal epithelia. High DLEU1 levels were significantly associated with reduced overall survival, advanced clinical stage, and lymph‑node metastasis, an observation confirmed in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) ESCC dataset. RNAscope in‑situ hybridization corroborated higher DLEU1 abundance in tumor cells compared with adjacent non‑tumor tissue. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of DLEU1‑high tumors revealed significant enrichment of gene signatures characteristic of cancer stem cells, metastatic progression, and mitochondrial apoptosis, suggesting a pro‑survival, anti‑apoptotic function. Mechanistic experiments demonstrated that DLEU1 directly binds to the dynein light chain DYNLL1, protecting it from ubiquitination by the E3 ligase RNF114 and subsequent proteasomal degradation; stabilized DYNLL1 enhances anti‑apoptotic signaling downstream of BCL2, thereby promoting cell survival. Together, these findings establish DLEU1 as an oncogenic lncRNA that safeguards DYNLL1 from degradation, drives an anti‑apoptotic program, and predicts poor outcome in ESCC patients, highlighting DLEU1 as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for this disease.
