Non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains the leading cause of cancer‑related deaths worldwide, accounting for approximately 85 % of all lung malignancies. Despite advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the molecular mechanisms that drive NSCLC initiation and progression are still not fully elucidated. A deeper understanding of the key genetic and metabolic alterations that support tumor growth is essential for the development of more effective treatment strategies.

Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer cells, which often rely on altered nutrient acquisition to meet the demands of rapid proliferation. Myo‑inositol (MI) is a critical precursor for phosphatidylinositol and inositol phosphates, molecules that participate in a variety of signaling pathways regulating cell survival and division. The sodium‑coupled myo‑inositol transporter SLC5A3 (also known as SMIT) mediates the active uptake of MI across the plasma membrane and thus influences intracellular MI pools. While SLC5A3 is known to transport MI in several tissue types, its expression pattern and functional relevance in NSCLC have not been systematically investigated.

To begin to address this gap, we examined SLC5A3 levels in publicly available transcriptomic data and in a collection of clinically annotated NSCLC specimens. These analyses revealed a consistent up‑regulation of SLC5A3 mRNA and protein in tumor tissues relative to matched normal lung parenchyma. Complementary studies in a panel of NSCLC‑derived cell lines demonstrated that SLC5A3 expression is markedly higher than in non‑transformed lung epithelial cells, and that this elevation is accompanied by increased intracellular concentrations of MI. The myo‑inositol biosynthetic enzyme ISYNA1 was expressed at low levels in NSCLC cells. This suggests that enhanced uptake via SLC5A3 may compensate for limited de‑novo synthesis. Together, these observations raised the possibility that SLC5A3‑mediated MI acquisition contributes to the metabolic demands of NSCLC cells.

In the present study, we sought to comprehensively characterize SLC5A3 expression in clinical NSCLC samples and cellular models, to correlate its abundance with intracellular MI content, and to determine whether SLC5A3 influences NSCLC cell growth. The findings provide evidence that SLC5A3 is frequently overexpressed in NSCLC and may exert a pro‑tumorigenic effect by modulating myo‑inositol homeostasis.
