N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA modification plays a critical role in tumor biology, yet the landscape of m6A regulators in small cell lung cancer (SCLC) remains poorly characterized. Here, we curated a catalog of 30 m6A regulators, comprising 11 writers, 2 erasers, and 17 readers, and systematically examined their genetic and expression alterations in SCLC. Somatic mutation analysis of 110 SCLC samples revealed that 28 samples (25.5%) harbored mutations in m6A regulators, with readers showing relatively high mutation frequencies and FMR1 displaying the highest mutation frequency, whereas no erasers exhibited mutations. Co-occurrence analysis identified recurrent mutation pairs between METTL3 and YTHDC2 and between IGF2BP2 and YTHDC2. Copy number variation (CNV) profiling across 53 SCLC cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) demonstrated widespread CNV alterations, with most readers showing broad CNV amplification and erasers predominantly exhibiting copy number loss. Principal component analysis of expression profiles revealed a markedly distinct distribution pattern between normal lung and SCLC tissues, with nearly all writers and readers significantly upregulated in SCLC and erasers tending toward downregulation. This indicates an overall increase in m6A modification in SCLC. Notably, IGF2BP3 was confirmed for the first time to be substantially overexpressed in SCLC. Integration of CNV and expression data suggested that CNV alterations contribute to perturbations in m6A regulator expression. Furthermore, comparative analysis of 50 SCLC cell lines with different pathological origins showed that most writers and readers tended to be upregulated in metastasis-derived cell lines compared to primary-derived cell lines. Collectively, these findings delineate the molecular characteristics of m6A regulators in SCLC and provide a foundation for further investigation of their immuno-oncological and clinical relevance.
