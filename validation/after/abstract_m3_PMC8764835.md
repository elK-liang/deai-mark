Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is one of the most aggressive malignancies with poor prognosis, largely due to the lack of reliable biomarkers for early detection and treatment stratification. Emerging evidence has suggested that salivary exosomes carry tumor-derived molecular signatures that may serve as non-invasive liquid biopsy tools, yet the diagnostic and prognostic potential of small non-coding RNAs (sncRNAs) within these vesicles remains largely unexplored. In this multicenter prospective study, we performed small RNA sequencing of salivary exosomes from ESCC patients and healthy controls and identified a panel of differentially expressed salivary exosomal sncRNAs (sesncRNAs), from which five candidates with the most significant fold changes were selected for validation. Two sesncRNAs, tRNA-GlyGCC-5, a tRNA-derived fragment originating from the 5' end of tRNA-GlyGCC, and sRESE, a previously uncharacterized small non-coding RNA located on chromosome 1, met the predetermined significance threshold and were significantly upregulated in salivary exosomes of ESCC patients across two independent cohorts (CHSUMC and ATH). Both molecules were also enriched in exosomes secreted by ESCC cell lines and in ESCC cells themselves compared with immortalized esophageal epithelial cells, and functional assays indicated that they promote ESCC cell migration and invasion. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated that the combination of tRNA-GlyGCC-5 and sRESE achieved robust diagnostic performance for distinguishing ESCC patients from healthy controls in the CHSUMC cohort. This supports their utility as a liquid-biopsy signature. Collectively, our findings establish tRNA-GlyGCC-5 and sRESE as promising saliva-derived exosomal biomarkers for ESCC, with potential applications in non-invasive detection and preoperative biomarker-guided therapeutic decision-making.
