Mutations in the ligand-binding domain of estrogen receptor alpha, particularly Y537S and D538G, emerge under aromatase inhibitor therapy and mediate endocrine resistance in metastatic breast cancer. Selective estrogen receptor degraders such as fulvestrant were developed with these constitutively active receptors in mind, but their intramuscular route and modest potency leave room for alternative endocrine strategies. In this study, we evaluated lasofoxifene, a non-steroidal selective estrogen receptor modulator with high oral bioavailability and a history of clinical use in osteoporosis, against tumors expressing wild-type and mutant ER alpha in xenograft models.

The central finding is that lasofoxifene is effective against tumors driven by the two most clinically relevant ESR1 mutations and, for these mutants, outperforms fulvestrant. Tumors derived from Y537S and D538G mutant cells grew faster than wild-type MCF7 tumors, confirming the growth advantage conferred by ligand-independent receptor activity. In vivo luminescence imaging showed that lasofoxifene at 5 and 10 mg/kg reduced tumor mass compared with vehicle in xenografts expressing wild-type, Y537S, and D538G ER alpha, with effects similar to fulvestrant overall, but the quantification of photon flux over time demonstrated that lasofoxifene was more effective than fulvestrant at inhibiting growth of the two mutants, with the 10 mg/kg dose eliciting a superior inhibitory effect. Final tumor weights corroborated the imaging results, with statistically robust dose effects for lasofoxifene, especially against the Y537S mutant: reductions in tumor weight of approximately 60% for Y537S and 50% for D538G were observed at 5 and 10 mg/kg, and lasofoxifene at both doses produced significantly lower terminal tumor weights than fulvestrant for both ER alpha mutants.

The subtype selectivity of the effect deserves emphasis. Fulvestrant appeared more effective than lasofoxifene at inhibiting wild-type tumors, whereas lasofoxifene was superior against the mutants. This reversal is mechanistically informative: the Y537S and D538G substitutions alter the ligand-binding domain so that the coactivator interface is constitutively engaged, and the ability of lasofoxifene to antagonize these receptors, where the bulky side-chain fulvestrant achieves degradation, suggests that the mutant receptor retains sensitivity to a different conformational strategy. A selective estrogen receptor modulator that binds the mutant receptor in a conformation that disrupts coactivator recruitment can suppress mutant ER transcriptional activity without requiring receptor degradation, which may explain the superior activity against mutants and the retained, if lesser, activity against wild-type receptor. The clinical implication is appealing: an orally available agent with documented long-term safety data from osteoporosis trials, effective against ESR1-mutant tumors, could offer a convenient alternative or sequencing partner to injectable degraders in endocrine-resistant disease.

These preclinical results align with and support the ongoing clinical development of lasofoxifene in ER-positive metastatic breast cancer, particularly in molecularly stratified populations with ESR1 mutations detectable in circulating tumor DNA. The use of ctDNA to select patients whose tumors harbor Y537S or D538G, combined with an orally administered antagonist active against those mutants, represents a rational precision endocrine strategy, and the head-to-head superiority over fulvestrant in the mutant models provides the preclinical rationale for randomized comparisons against fulvestrant-based standard therapy.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The models used are MCF7-derived xenografts expressing single defined ER alpha mutants, which capture the dominant resistance mechanism but not the multiplicity of alterations present in clinical resistance, including copy number gains of mutant ESR1, co-mutations, and pathway activations downstream of the receptor. Tumor growth was monitored primarily by luminescence and terminal weight, and histological assessment, while revealing metastases to lung and other sites in some models, was not systematically quantified across treatment groups. The doses of lasofoxifene employed, 1 to 10 mg/kg, exceed the exposures used in osteoporosis settings, and the translation of the observed efficacy to clinically achievable exposures requires pharmacokinetic confirmation. Finally, the models lack an intact immune and endocrine milieu, and the long-term durability of response, the emergence of resistance to lasofoxifene itself, and combination strategies with CDK4/6 inhibitors were not addressed. With these caveats, our study demonstrates that lasofoxifene is active against tumors driven by the Y537S and D538G ER alpha mutations and exceeds fulvestrant's efficacy against them. These results support the clinical evaluation of this orally available SERM in patients with ESR1-mutant, endocrine-resistant metastatic breast cancer.
