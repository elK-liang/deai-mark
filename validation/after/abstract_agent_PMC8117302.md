Endocrine therapy resistance driven by mutations in the estrogen receptor alpha (ERα) ligand-binding domain, such as Y537S and D538G, limits treatment options in metastatic breast cancer, and we evaluated lasofoxifene (LAS), a selective estrogen receptor modulator, as a potential treatment for therapy-resistant ER-positive disease. Xenograft tumors derived from MCF7 cells expressing wild-type (WT), Y537S or D538G ERα were treated with LAS (1, 5 or 10 mg/kg) or fulvestrant (FUL). Tumors derived from the mutant Y537S and D538G cells grew faster than tumors from WT cells. In vivo real-time luminescence imaging showed that LAS at 5 and 10 mg/kg reduced tumor mass compared with vehicle control in xenograft tumors expressing WT, Y537S and D538G ERα, similarly to FUL. For the two mutants, total photon flux quantification of luminescence signal over time demonstrated that LAS was more effective than FUL at inhibiting tumor growth, with the highest dose of LAS (10 mg/kg) eliciting a superior inhibitory effect versus FUL. Final primary tumor weight at sacrifice correlated with the imaging results, with significantly lower terminal tumor weights for FUL and LAS relative to vehicle in the three mouse models, and reduction in tumor weight with LAS at 5 and 10 mg/kg of approximately 60% for Y537S and 50% for D538G. LAS at 5 or 10 mg/kg resulted in significantly lower tumor weights for both ERα mutants versus FUL, whereas FUL appeared more effective than LAS at inhibiting WT tumors. These findings support lasofoxifene as a potential treatment for therapy-resistant ER-positive metastatic breast cancer with ERα mutations.
