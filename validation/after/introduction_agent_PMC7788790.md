Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) have emerged as robust biomarkers in early-stage breast cancer. The density of stromal TILs assessed on routine hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides, formalized through the international guidelines of the TILs working group, is prognostic across breast cancer subtypes and predictive of benefit from neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with emerging evidence for predictive value in the setting of immunotherapy. Because of this evidence base, stromal TIL assessment by H&E has been incorporated into clinical trial protocols and is increasingly considered for routine diagnostic reporting.

H&E-based stromal TIL scoring, however, has inherent limitations. It is a semi-quantitative estimate of the percentage of stromal area occupied by mononuclear cells, it does not resolve the phenotype of the infiltrating cells, and it cannot capture the expression of immune checkpoints such as PD-L1 on immune or tumor cells. Moreover, the immune landscape of a tumor is spatially heterogeneous, and the dynamics of the immune response induced by neoadjuvant therapy, or by immune-modulating interventions, may involve changes in specific lymphocyte subsets that a global percentage cannot detect. Methods that quantify defined immune cell populations and checkpoint molecules in defined tumor compartments would therefore offer greater precision and mechanistic insight.

Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) provides such a capability. It allows simultaneous visualization of multiple immune cell markers and PD-L1 within a single tissue section and quantitative scoring across tumor and stromal compartments. For mIF-derived TIL metrics to complement the established H&E standard, they must be analytically validated against it: an mIF stromal TIL score should reproduce the H&E score closely while offering greater precision, and its relationship to intra-tumoral heterogeneity should be characterized. In addition, demonstrating the ability of mIF to detect treatment-induced dynamic changes, rather than static baseline differences, is a prerequisite for its use in trials of immune-modulating agents.

In this study, we developed and evaluated an mIF-based approach to measuring stromal TILs in early-stage breast cancer, applied to paired pre-treatment biopsies and surgical excisions from patients treated in a trial of the immune-modulating biologic IRX-2, an agent previously shown to increase H&E sTIL scores, upregulate PD-L1 expression, and upregulate immune-related gene signatures. We defined an mIF stromal TIL score as the density of stromal T cells per unit stromal area and show that it correlates closely with H&E-based scoring, quantify the substantial intra-sample heterogeneity of TIL distribution, and demonstrate that mIF detects dynamic changes in TIL subsets and PD-L1 during treatment.
