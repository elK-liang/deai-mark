Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) assessed on hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides are prognostic and predictive in breast cancer, but their semi-quantitative evaluation limits precision and the ability to measure dynamic changes. We developed a multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) method to measure stromal TILs (sTILs) in early-stage breast cancer, aiming to closely recapitulate the H&E sTIL score with greater precision. In line with the sTILs working group definition, the sTILmIF score was defined as the sum of counts of all stromal T cells (helper, cytotoxic and regulatory) divided by the stromal area. In the analyzed pre-treatment and excisional samples, sTILH&E and sample mean sTILmIF scores were correlated for both pre-treatment (r2 = 0.59, p < .001) and excisional specimens (r2 = 0.63, p < .001). These correlations support the mIF assay as an analog of the clinical H&E metric. sTILmIF scores varied substantially across regions of interest within a sample, with a mean coefficient of variation of 0.61 (range 0.27-1.44), indicative of marked intra-sample heterogeneity. Beyond the sTIL score, the mIF panel quantified CD8+ cell counts and densities and PD-L1 metrics at the cell and region level. This enables finer characterization of therapy-related immune changes in this setting, where IRX-2 treatment was previously shown to increase the mean H&E sTIL score and upregulate PD-L1 expression. Multiplex immunofluorescence thus provides a quantitative, spatially resolved platform to measure TILs and PD-L1 in early-stage breast cancer and their dynamic changes during systemic treatment.
