Endocrine therapy remains the cornerstone of treatment for estrogen receptor‑positive (ER⁺) breast cancer, yet a substantial proportion of patients develop resistance, especially in the metastatic setting. Acquired mutations in the ligand‑binding domain of the estrogen receptor α gene (ESR1), most frequently the Y537S and D538G substitutions, confer constitutive activity to the receptor and have been identified as key mechanisms that diminish the efficacy of selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) and degraders. These mutations render tumors less responsive to standard hormonal agents and are associated with a more aggressive clinical course. Novel therapeutic strategies that can effectively target mutant ERα are therefore needed.

Fulvestrant, a selective estrogen receptor down‑regulator (SERD), is widely used for patients with ER⁺ metastatic disease who have progressed on prior endocrine therapy. By binding the receptor and promoting its proteasomal degradation, fulvestrant can suppress ER signaling; however, data from preclinical models and clinical samples indicate that certain ESR1 mutations attenuate the ability of fulvestrant to down‑regulate ERα and to inhibit tumor growth. Consequently, there is considerable interest in identifying agents that retain activity against the constitutive, mutant forms of the receptor.

Lasofoxifene is a third‑generation SERM that interacts with ERα through a distinct binding mode compared with tamoxifen and other SERMs, leading to unique transcriptional profiles in estrogen‑responsive tissues. Its capacity to modulate ERα activity in a manner that differs from both agonists and classical anti‑estrogens suggests a potential utility against tumors harboring activating ESR1 mutations. Preclinical investigations have hinted that lasofoxifene may retain efficacy in contexts where conventional endocrine agents lose potency, yet systematic in vivo comparisons with established SERDs such as fulvestrant in mutant ERα models are limited.

To address this gap, we evaluated the anti‑tumor effects of lasofoxifene in xenograft models of human ER⁺ breast cancer engineered to express wild‑type (WT) ERα or the clinically relevant Y537S and D538G mutants. Mice bearing these tumors were treated with vehicle, fulvestrant, or escalating doses of lasofoxifene (1, 5, and 10 mg/kg), and tumor progression was monitored by longitudinal bioluminescence imaging and confirmed by terminal tumor weight and histologic analysis. The primary goal was to determine whether lasofoxifene can suppress growth of ERα‑mutant tumors more effectively than fulvestrant and to delineate dose‑response relationships that could inform future clinical development.
