Melanoma is the most lethal form of skin cancer, and its incidence continues to rise worldwide. While the epidemiology of melanoma in populations of European ancestry is dominated by cutaneous disease related to intermittent sun exposure, melanoma in Asian populations presents a markedly different spectrum: acral lentiginous melanoma and mucosal melanoma together account for roughly half of all cases, and these subtypes are characterized by low mutation burden and infrequent BRAF mutations. Because pivotal trials of immune checkpoint inhibitors have been conducted predominantly in Western patient populations with cutaneous melanoma, evidence for their efficacy and safety in Asian patients with acral and mucosal disease has historically been limited, even though such patients represent the majority of the melanoma burden in East Asia.

Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade is the standard of care for advanced melanoma, but the approved agents differ in dosing schedule, toxicity profile, and, importantly, in the pattern of immune-related adverse events observed across populations. Camrelizumab, a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody against PD-1 developed and approved in China, has drawn particular attention for its distinctive safety profile, most notably the frequent occurrence of reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (RCCEP), a reversible vascular lesion uncommon with other PD-1 inhibitors. Understanding the dose-response, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of camrelizumab specifically in Asian patients with advanced melanoma, in whom acral and mucosal subtypes predominate, was an essential step toward its clinical development in this disease.

Phase I studies fulfill this role by establishing safety and recommended dosing while providing early signals of activity in the specific population for which the drug is intended. For checkpoint inhibitors in melanoma, such studies additionally inform expectations regarding response durability and the management of immune-mediated toxicities in heavily pretreated patients, who constituted the standard treatment population before the checkpoint inhibitor era.

Here, we report the results of a phase I study of camrelizumab in Asian patients with advanced melanoma who had failed prior first-line systemic therapy. Thirty-six patients were enrolled in dose escalation and expansion across three dose groups. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed and the maximum tolerated dose was not reached; treatment-related adverse events occurred in 80.6 percent of patients, led by RCCEP in 63.9 percent, with grade 3 or higher events limited to three cases and no treatment-related deaths. We present the safety profile, antitumor activity, and pharmacokinetic characteristics of camrelizumab in this population and characterize the clinical course of RCCEP.
