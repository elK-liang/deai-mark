Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a debilitating chronic illness of unknown etiology that lacks objective diagnostic biomarkers, with growing evidence implicating metabolic and inflammatory dysregulation in its pathophysiology. Given that sex is a known influence on ME/CFS diagnosis and that lipid metabolism intersects with both inflammation and immune function, we conducted a sex-stratified lipidomic analysis of plasma from ME/CFS patients and healthy controls to characterize phospholipid and neutral lipid profiles and their relationships to pain, fatigue, and cognitive symptoms. Untargeted lipidomics revealed substantial sex-based variation in lipid profiles, with healthy females showing significantly higher total phosphatidylcholine (PC) content than healthy males. This underscores the importance of sex-stratified analyses. Among males, ME/CFS patients exhibited significantly elevated total hexosylceramide (HexCer) levels relative to controls, whereas female ME/CFS patients showed significantly reduced phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and HexCer compared with female controls. Assessment of lipid unsaturation, a feature linked to inflammatory status in chronic disease, identified no differences between female ME/CFS patients and female controls, but among males, PE-monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA), phosphatidylinositol (PI)-MUFA, and triglyceride (TG)-saturated fatty acid (SFA) species were significantly higher in ME/CFS patients than in controls. Profiling of arachidonic acid (AA)- and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)-containing lipid species, which respectively carry pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory potential, further demonstrated that healthy females had elevated AA- and DHA-containing PC and cholesteryl ester (CE) together with decreased CE-AA relative to healthy males. Within females, PE-AA was reduced in ME/CFS patients versus controls, while CE-AA was elevated in female ME/CFS compared with male ME/CFS patients. Together, these findings define sex-specific plasma lipid signatures in ME/CFS that involve sphingolipid, glycerophospholipid, and neutral lipid compartments and intersect with pathways relevant to inflammation and immunity. This provides a foundation for future studies linking lipid metabolism to the cardinal symptoms of pain, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction in this disease.
