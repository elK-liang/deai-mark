Black phosphorus has attracted attention as a photodetector material because of its thickness-dependent bandgap, high carrier mobility, and in-plane anisotropy, but conventional device architectures force a compromise between responsivity and dark current. In this study, we designed and fabricated an in-plane PN homojunction in few-layer black phosphorus defined by ferroelectric domains, in which the same flake is doped p-type and n-type by up-polarized and down-polarized ferroelectric domains. This architecture was conceived to obtain simultaneously a low dark current and a high polarization ratio, and to combine the photothermoelectric effect and the photovoltaic effect within a single device.

The rationale for the design follows from the physics of photocurrent generation in anisotropic materials. In a traditional black phosphorus field-effect transistor with uniform doping, carriers driven by a temperature gradient are transported in opposite directions, yielding no net photothermoelectric current; introducing a PN junction provides a positive contribution to the PTE current on both sides of the junction, so the two effects reinforce rather than cancel. The thermoelectric conversion efficiency, governed by the figure of merit ZT, is inherently larger along the armchair direction than along the zigzag direction, which is consistent with the electronic band structure and carrier transport anisotropy of black phosphorus and provides the physical basis for polarization-resolved detection. Crucially, the Seebeck coefficient can be tuned by the external electric field provided by the ferroelectric domains: the Seebeck coefficient of the n-doped area is larger than that of the p-doped area, which further promotes directional PTE current flow from the n-doped to the p-doped region.

The use of ferroelectric domains as the doping mechanism has several advantages over electrostatic or chemical approaches. Ferroelectric polarization defines the PN junction non-destructively, without permanent chemical doping that could degrade the sensitive surface of black phosphorus, and it produces a stable junction whose polarity can in principle be rewritten. Band structure calculations show that the applied field narrows the bandgap through the giant Stark effect in the armchair direction while leaving the zigzag direction essentially unchanged, which modulates the optical and electronic properties anisotropically and is consistent with the enhanced directionality of the photocurrent. Because the junction is created within a single flake, the device avoids the material interfaces and contact-induced doping variations of heterogeneous photodiodes, reducing dark current and noise sources that normally limit sensitivity.

The resulting photodetector, which achieves ultra-sensitive, polarization-resolved detection, has implications for imaging and sensing applications. Polarization sensitivity in the infrared is valuable for contrast enhancement in scenes where intensity alone carries little information, and the combination of low dark current with the intrinsic anisotropy of black phosphorus makes this device architecture a candidate for compact infrared polarimetric imagers. More broadly, the demonstration that ferroelectric domains can template functional junctions in two-dimensional materials suggests a general strategy for reconfigurable device architectures, in which the same material can be patterned into different electronic configurations after fabrication.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The device depends on the integrity of the few-layer black phosphorus flake, a material known to be sensitive to ambient degradation, and long-term stability under operating conditions requires further assessment. The ferroelectric layer and its domain pattern introduce constraints on device geometry and on the range of accessible doping profiles, and the switching endurance of the domain-defined junctions needs characterization for practical use. Our measurements characterize the device in a laboratory setting, and performance under varying temperatures, illumination intensities, and bias conditions, as well as the uniformity of response across devices, remains to be established. Finally, the speed of the photothermal contribution, which is limited by thermal diffusion, may constrain applications requiring very fast modulation, although the photovoltaic contribution can respond more rapidly. Within these considerations, this study demonstrates that ferroelectric-domain-defined PN homojunctions unlock the combination of low dark current, high responsivity, and polarization sensitivity in black phosphorus. This provides a template for anisotropic two-dimensional photodetectors and reconfigurable junction devices.
