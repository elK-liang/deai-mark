Abstract: We designed and prepared a black phosphorus (BP) in-plane PN homojunction photodetector defined by ferroelectric domains, aiming to combine the photothermoelectric (PTE) and photovoltaic (PV) effects in one device with low dark current and a high polarization ratio. Few-layer BP was transferred onto a SiO2/Si substrate and doped into p-type and n-type regions by up-polarized and down-polarized ferroelectric domains. In traditional BP field-effect transistors, the PTE photocurrent is affected by the electrical doping of the metal electrodes, and when the doping level is uniform, carriers driven by the temperature gradient are transported in opposite directions, leading to a zero net current. Introducing an in-plane PN junction in BP provides a positive contribution to the PTE current on both sides. The Seebeck coefficient is tuned by the external electric field, and because the Seebeck coefficient of the n-doped area is larger than that of the p-doped area for BP, the PTE current flows directionally from the n-doped to the p-doped area. With an external electric field, the bandgap of BP is narrowed owing to the giant Stark effect, and the band structure is remarkably tunable in the armchair direction while hardly changing in the zigzag direction, consistent with the anisotropic thermoelectric conversion efficiency of BP. The resulting ferroelectric-domain-defined BP homojunction achieves ultra-sensitive, polarization-resolved photodetection. This provides a route to high-performance anisotropic photodetectors based on two-dimensional materials.
