Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common primary liver malignancy, and the establishment of an accurate and practical non-invasive diagnostic strategy for HCC, especially in low-risk populations, is an unmet clinical need. The present study aimed to develop and validate a non-invasive diagnostic model for HCC based on widely available clinical, serological, and imaging features in patients considered at low risk for HCC. Methods: A total of 681 patients with hepatic nodules from two institutions were retrospectively enrolled. Patients from the first institution (n = 463) were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 324) and an internal validation cohort (n = 139), while patients from the second institution (n = 218) served as an external validation cohort. Clinical characteristics, serological tumor markers, and imaging features (including LI-RADS categories) were collected and compared among the three cohorts. A diagnostic model was constructed using the training cohort and subsequently evaluated in the internal and external validation cohorts. Diagnostic performance was assessed in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Results: Of the 681 patients, 277 (40.7%) were diagnosed with HCC, and the remaining patients comprised non-HCC malignancies (predominantly iCCA, mixed HCC-CC, and sarcoma) and benign lesions (predominantly FNH, inflammatory pseudotumor, angioleiomyolipoma, and liver abscess). Nearly 80% of nodules exceeded 3 cm in diameter. There were significant differences among the three cohorts in age, family history, cigarette smoking, alcohol consumption, cardiovascular disease, HBcAb and HBeAb seropositivity, AST, GGT, ALP, CA125, and CA19-9 (all p < 0.05). The proportion of HCC increased from the training cohort (35%) to the internal (45%) and external (48%) validation cohorts (p = 0.006). The constructed non-invasive diagnostic model demonstrated favorable discrimination and calibration in both the internal and external validation cohorts. Conclusions: The proposed non-invasive diagnostic strategy showed good performance for the identification of HCC in a low-risk population and may serve as a practical tool to assist clinical decision-making in this setting.
