Abstract

Treatment options for advanced melanoma remain limited, particularly in Asian populations where acral and mucosal subtypes predominate. Camrelizumab, a humanized anti–PD-1 monoclonal antibody, has demonstrated antitumor activity in several malignancies. This phase I study evaluated the safety, activity, and pharmacokinetics of camrelizumab in Asian patients with advanced melanoma who had failed prior first-line therapy.

Patients with advanced melanoma who had progressed after at least one prior systemic therapy were enrolled and received camrelizumab at doses of 60 mg, 200 mg, or 600 mg every two weeks. The study followed a standard 3+3 dose-escalation design in the first stage, with subsequent dose-expansion cohorts. The primary endpoints were safety and tolerability, including the incidence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and determination of the maximum tolerated dose (MTD). Secondary endpoints included antitumor activity assessed per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 and pharmacokinetic profiles.

From April 2016 to October 2017, 36 patients were enrolled (12 per dose group); 33 patients completed at least one efficacy evaluation. The cohort comprised 17 males (47.2%) and 19 females (52.8%), with a median age of 52 years (range: 29–68). Primary melanoma sites included acral (50.0%), mucosal (27.8%), cutaneous and unknown origins (19.4%), and choroid (2.8%). Half of the patients were staged as M1c/M1d per AJCC 8th edition, and 66.7% had received at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. No DLTs were observed across the three dose groups, and the MTD was not reached. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 29 patients (80.6%). The most common TRAEs were reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (63.9%), hypothyroidism (25.0%), rash (13.9%), and fever (11.1%). Grade ≥3 adverse events were infrequent, including one case of liver injury and two cases of asymptomatic elevated γ-glutamyltransferase and blood triglycerides; no TRAEs led to treatment discontinuation, and no study drug-related deaths were reported. RCCEP predominantly appeared on the face and trunk after the first treatment cycle (2–4 weeks). Thirty-three patients completed ≥2 dosing cycles, seven remained on treatment beyond 1 year, and four continued for over 2 years.

Camrelizumab demonstrated a manageable safety profile with no DLTs observed across the 60 mg, 200 mg, and 600 mg dose levels, and showed promising antitumor activity in heavily pretreated Asian patients with advanced melanoma. These findings support the continued clinical development of camrelizumab for advanced melanoma in Asian populations.
