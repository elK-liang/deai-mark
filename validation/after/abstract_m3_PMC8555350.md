Abstract

Hepatoblastoma is the most common primary liver malignancy in children, yet the molecular mechanisms driving its development and its interaction with the tumor immune microenvironment remain poorly understood. Integrative genomic and epigenomic analyses are needed to identify robust molecular signatures associated with hepatoblastoma and tumor immunity.

Hepatoblastoma-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified from two public transcriptomic datasets, GSE75271 (microarray) and GSE81928 (RNA-seq), by comparing tumor and normal liver samples. Microarray data were normalized using the robust multi-array average (RMA) method, with outlier samples removed following principal component analysis (PCA), while RNA-seq counts were standardized using log2(TPM + 1). Differentially methylated sites and corresponding genes were obtained from the GSE78732 methylation dataset. Overlapping DEGs between the two transcriptomic datasets and genes associated with differential methylation were intersected to derive a set of differentially methylated and expressed genes in hepatoblastoma. Bioinformatic analyses were then performed to explore the associations of these genes with the tumor immune microenvironment.

After normalization and removal of outlier samples, 1364 DEGs (756 upregulated and 608 downregulated) were identified from GSE75271, and 802 DEGs (528 upregulated and 274 downregulated) were identified from GSE81928. Intersection of the two DEG sets yielded 134 commonly upregulated and 191 commonly downregulated genes in hepatoblastoma. From the GSE78732 methylation dataset, 1125 hypermethylated and 2337 hypomethylated sites were detected in hepatoblastoma compared with normal liver. Integration of methylation and expression data identified 83 differentially methylated and expressed genes in hepatoblastoma, including TNFRSF19, SP5, NOTUM, NKD1, BMP4, ODAM, ROBO1, BAMBI, AXIN2, TRH, TRIB2, MSX1, HOXA3, PTK7, LAMB1, TBX3, GSTP1, RERE, COL4A1, SLC22A1, SLC10A1, HAO2, CYP1A2, GLS2, and HABP2, among others.

This study identified a set of genes that are both differentially expressed and differentially methylated in hepatoblastoma. This provides candidate genomic and epigenomic signatures for further investigation of their roles in the tumor immune microenvironment.
