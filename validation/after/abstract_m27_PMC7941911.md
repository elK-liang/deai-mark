Non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) can progress to non‑alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) through inflammatory pathways that remain incompletely characterized, and recent evidence suggests that long non‑coding RNAs (lncRNAs) may play a pivotal role in this transition. To profile dysregulated lncRNAs during NAFLD and NASH, we used db/db mice fed a normal diet (NAFLD model) or a methionine‑choline‑deficient diet (NASH model) and confirmed disease hallmarks by histology, serum ALT (2.98‑fold increase) and AST (1.94‑fold increase) levels, and F4/80 immunostaining for hepatic macrophages. We interrogated global lncRNA expression with the Mouse LncRNA Microarray V2.0. This revealed 3111 differentially expressed lncRNAs between NAFLD and control livers, 2677 between NASH and control livers, and 3337 between NASH and NAFLD livers. Venn analysis identified 63 lncRNAs consistently altered across all three groups, and series cluster analysis uncovered 381 lncRNAs that were progressively up‑regulated from normal to NAFLD and further elevated in NASH. Among these, the novel lncRNA‑Gm9795 exhibited the strongest progressive increase and its hepatic expression correlated with macrophage infiltration and the magnitude of transaminase elevations. Functional studies demonstrated that lncRNA‑Gm9795 promotes inflammatory signaling in NASH by activating the NF‑κB and JNK pathways and inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress, driving production of pro‑inflammatory cytokines. These findings establish lncRNA‑Gm9795 as a key driver of NASH‑related inflammation and suggest it as a potential therapeutic target for intercepting NAFLD‑to‑NASH progression.
