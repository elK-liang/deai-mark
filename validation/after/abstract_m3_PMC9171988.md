Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common pathological type of lung cancer, and its incidence and mortality continue to rise worldwide, making it a major public health threat. A deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD progression is essential for the development of novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Junctional adhesion molecule-like protein (JAML) is a member of the junctional adhesion molecule family that has recently attracted increasing attention for its role in cancer biology; however, its expression pattern and functional significance in LUAD remain largely undefined. In the present study, we evaluated JAML expression in LUAD clinical specimens and cell lines, examined its association with clinicopathological features, and investigated its role in tumor cell invasion and migration as well as the underlying molecular mechanism. qRT-PCR and Western blotting analyses of 15 paired LUAD and peritumor tissues revealed that JAML mRNA and protein levels were significantly elevated in tumor tissues. Consistent with these findings, immunohistochemical analysis of an additional 30 paired LUAD tissues confirmed that JAML protein expression was markedly upregulated in tumor tissues compared with matched peritumor tissues. Clinicopathological correlation analysis demonstrated that high JAML expression was significantly associated with advanced pT stage and pTNM stage, whereas no significant associations were observed with age, sex, pN stage, or pM stage. These findings suggest that JAML may serve as an oncogene in LUAD. Endogenous JAML expression was further examined in four LUAD cell lines (A549, H1299, PC9, and H1975). Expression was relatively higher in A549 and H1299 cells and relatively lower in PC9 and H1975 cells. Immunofluorescence assays indicated that JAML is localized predominantly to the cytoplasm of LUAD cells. Functional experiments demonstrated that JAML knockdown in A549 and H1299 cells significantly suppressed cell invasion and migration, whereas JAML overexpression in PC9 and H1975 cells markedly enhanced these malignant behaviors. Mechanistically, JAML promoted the invasive and migratory phenotype of LUAD cells by activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, as evidenced by the altered expression of key downstream effectors upon modulation of JAML levels. Taken together, these findings identify JAML as an upregulated oncoprotein in LUAD that promotes tumor cell invasion and migration through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. This highlights its potential as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target for LUAD.
