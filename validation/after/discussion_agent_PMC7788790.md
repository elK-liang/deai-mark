Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes are established prognostic and predictive markers in early-stage breast cancer, but their routine assessment relies on visual estimation of hematoxylin and eosin slides, a semi-quantitative method with inherent variability. In this study, we developed a multiplex immunofluorescence approach to measure stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and PD-L1 in early-stage breast cancer, motivated by the need to quantify the immune modulation induced by IRX-2, an immunotherapy previously shown to increase the mean H&E sTIL score, upregulate PD-L1 expression, and upregulate immune-related gene signatures. The multiplex sTIL score was defined, following the sTILs working group guidelines, as the sum of counts of all stromal T cells, including helper, cytotoxic, and regulatory subsets, divided by stromal area.

The agreement between the multiplex and conventional assessments supports the validity of the approach. sTILH&E and sample mean sTILmIF scores were correlated in both pre-treatment and excisional samples, with coefficients of determination of 0.59 and 0.63, respectively, both statistically significant. This level of correlation, obtained between a cell-count-based metric and a visual area-based estimate, indicates that the multiplex measurement captures the same underlying biology as the clinical H&E score while adding information that H&E cannot provide. In particular, the multiplex panel resolves the lymphocyte subsets that constitute the stromal infiltrate and permits separate quantification of CD8 density and of PD-L1 expression on immune and tumor compartments. This enables a multidimensional characterization of the immune microenvironment within a single slide.

An important observation from our analysis is the extent of intra-sample heterogeneity. sTILmIF scores varied substantially across regions of interest within a sample, with a mean coefficient of variation of 0.61 and a range from 0.27 to 1.44. This heterogeneity has direct methodological consequences: any scoring approach that samples only a portion of a slide, whether by visual estimation or by selection of representative hotspots, is exposed to sampling error of a magnitude that can obscure real treatment effects. Quantitative cell counting across multiple regions of interest, as implemented here, makes the sampling variance explicit and allows it to be reduced by increasing the number of regions analyzed. The same consideration applies to the distinction between cell counts and cell densities, since adjusting for differences in stromal and tumor compartmentalization across tumor regions provides a more reliable measure of immune cell quantity than counts alone.

The ability to measure therapy-related changes in TILs and PD-L1 quantitatively has implications beyond the IRX-2 program. Immune checkpoint inhibitors are entering the treatment of early-stage breast cancer, particularly in the triple-negative subtype, and trials increasingly seek pharmacodynamic readouts of immune activation in resected specimens. A multiplex platform that reproduces the clinical sTIL score while adding subset resolution could serve as a bridging biomarker, connecting the extensive prognostic literature based on H&E sTILs with mechanistic studies of immunotherapy response. Similarly, the PD-L1 metrics included in the panel, including the SP142-based immune cell score used clinically and quantitative fluorescence intensity measurements, allow concurrent evaluation of the ligand and its cellular context.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The multiplex sTIL score, by design, captures only stromal T cells and does not include plasma cells, which may be present in early-stage breast cancer although typically clustered in sparse regions, and it excludes other immune cells such as macrophages that may contribute to the stromal compartment. The correlation with H&E scores, while significant, was imperfect, and part of the divergence reflects genuine differences in what each method measures rather than error in either. Our analyses were performed in a modest number of samples from a single trial, and the reproducibility of the platform across laboratories, staining batches, and scanning systems remains to be established before it can be proposed for clinical use. Finally, the clinical significance of subset-resolved measures, such as the relative contributions of cytotoxic and regulatory T cells to prognosis and prediction, requires outcome-linked validation cohorts. With these caveats, our study demonstrates that multiplex immunofluorescence can reproduce and extend the conventional assessment of stromal TILs in early-stage breast cancer, quantifies the intra-tumoral heterogeneity that constrains all sampling-based metrics, and provides a tool for measuring dynamic immune changes in response to therapy.
