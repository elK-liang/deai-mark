Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for the majority of lung cancer cases and remains one of the leading causes of cancer mortality worldwide. Despite progress in targeted therapy and immunotherapy, most patients with advanced disease eventually progress. Molecular dependencies that can be exploited therapeutically therefore need to be identified. Metabolic reprogramming is now recognized as a hallmark of cancer, and tumor cells must continuously adjust nutrient uptake and osmotic balance to support proliferation and survival under the demanding conditions of the tumor microenvironment.

The solute carrier family encompasses many transporters that have emerged as metabolic vulnerabilities in cancer. Among them, SLC5A3 encodes the sodium/myo-inositol co-transporter, the principal route of cellular myo-inositol uptake. Myo-inositol serves as an organic osmolyte that protects cells from osmotic stress and is also a precursor for phosphoinositides, key regulators of signaling, membrane dynamics, and vesicle trafficking. Although the physiological roles of SLC5A3 in osmoregulation are well described in tissues such as brain and kidney, whether this transporter contributes to tumor biology has received little attention, and its expression and function in lung cancer have not been defined.

Accumulating evidence from cancer genomic datasets indicates that solute carrier genes are frequently dysregulated across tumor types. This suggests that transporter-mediated metabolic rewiring may be a general feature of malignant transformation. Determining whether SLC5A3 is similarly hijacked in NSCLC, and whether it is merely a passenger of malignant transformation or an active contributor to tumor growth, is therefore an important unanswered question with potential translational implications. Establishing the expression pattern of SLC5A3 in human lung tumors and the consequences of its perturbation in cancer cells is the necessary first step toward such an assessment.

In this study, we examined the expression of SLC5A3 in NSCLC by analyzing public genomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas together with paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues obtained from patients undergoing tumor resection, using quantitative PCR, western blotting, and immunohistochemistry. We further compared SLC5A3 expression in immortalized and primary human NSCLC cells with that in non-malignant lung epithelial cells, measured intracellular myo-inositol levels, and investigated whether SLC5A3 exerts pro-tumorigenic activity in NSCLC cells. Our findings define a previously unappreciated role for this myo-inositol co-transporter in NSCLC cell growth and suggest that targeting SLC5A3 or myo-inositol metabolism may represent a strategy to limit tumor progression.
