The development of ultra-sensitive photodetectors with polarization-resolved capabilities has attracted significant research interest due to their potential applications in imaging, communication, and sensing technologies. Black phosphorus (BP), a two-dimensional material with a direct bandgap and anisotropic crystal structure, has emerged as a promising candidate for high-performance photodetectors owing to its favorable electronic and optical properties. However, achieving low dark current while maintaining high polarization ratios remains a critical challenge in BP-based photodetector design, as conventional field-effect transistor architectures often suffer from limited sensitivity and compromised signal-to-noise ratios.

To address these limitations, we designed and fabricated a BP in-plane PN homojunction defined by ferroelectric domains. This approach utilizes the ferroelectric field to dope few-layer BP into p-type and n-type regions with opposite polarization states, and thus enables simultaneous exploitation of the photothermal electric (PTE) effect and photovoltaic (PV) effect within a single device architecture. The ferroelectric domain patterning provides a non-volatile and tunable method for creating PN junctions without introducing foreign dopants, and thus preserves the intrinsic properties of BP while achieving precise spatial control over the doping profile.

In conventional BP field-effect transistors, the PTE photocurrent is influenced by the electrical doping from metal electrodes, resulting in carrier transport driven by temperature gradients that often leads to zero net current when the doping level is uniform. Our PN junction design introduces a positive contribution to the PTE current on both sides of the junction, promoting directional carrier flow from the n-doped to p-doped regions. The Seebeck coefficient in the n-doped area exceeds that in the p-doped area for BP, further enhancing the directional PTE current. Moreover, the external electric field from the ferroelectric domains exerts remarkable tunability on the band structure along the armchair direction through the giant Stark effect, which narrows the bandgap, while the zigzag direction remains largely unaffected. This provides anisotropic control over the electronic properties.

This device architecture successfully integrates multiple mechanisms to achieve superior photodetection performance. The combination of PTE and PV effects in the PN homojunction configuration enables efficient photocarrier separation and collection, while the ferroelectric field modulation provides a powerful means to tune the Seebeck coefficient and band structure. The anisotropic response in the armchair and zigzag directions further enhances the polarization ratio, making this BP homojunction photodetector particularly suitable for polarization-sensitive applications. This work demonstrates a novel approach to engineering two-dimensional material devices through ferroelectric domain patterning, offering a pathway toward ultra-sensitive, polarization-resolved photodetectors with tunable functionality.
