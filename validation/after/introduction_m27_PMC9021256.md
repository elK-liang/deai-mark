Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is driven by the BCR‑ABL fusion oncoprotein, a constitutively active tyrosine kinase that can be effectively suppressed by theABL‑specific inhibitor imatinib (IM). Although IM dramatically improves patient outcomes, the emergence of resistance (frequently caused by point mutations within the BCR‑ABL kinase domain) remains a major clinical challenge. The T315I substitution is among the most resistant mutations, conferring a marked decrease in IM binding affinity and a 30‑fold increase in the IM half‑maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) compared with wild‑type BCR‑ABL. Consequently, there is an urgent need for strategies that can restore drug sensitivity in T315I‑mutated cells.

A hallmark of many cancer cells, including leukemic blasts, is a pronounced reliance on glycolysis even under aerobic conditions, a phenomenon known as the Warburg effect. This metabolic reprogramming supports rapid energy production, biosynthetic precursor generation, and maintenance of redox balance. Targeting glycolysis has therefore emerged as a promising therapeutic avenue, and 2‑deoxy‑D‑glucose (2‑DG), a glucose analog that inhibits hexokinase and blocks early steps of glycolysis, has been explored as a metabolic adjuvant in several tumor types. Pre‑clinical studies suggest that glycolytic inhibition can sensitize cancer cells to conventional chemotherapy, although its effect in CML, particularly in the context of IM‑resistant BCR‑ABL mutants, has not been systematically examined.

Recent evidence from our group and others indicates that BCR‑ABL mutations, especially T315I, are associated with altered cellular metabolism and changes in drug responsiveness. Moreover, metabolic stress is known to trigger autophagy, an intracellular degradation pathway that can either protect cells from nutrient deprivation or contribute to cell death depending on context. Thus, modulating energy metabolism may exert its cytotoxic effects, at least in part, through autophagy‑mediated mechanisms.

To test this hypothesis, we evaluated the single‑agent and combined cytotoxic activity of 2‑DG and IM in two closely related CML cell models: KBM5 cells expressing wild‑type BCR‑ABL and KBM5‑T315I cells harboring the resistant mutation. Viability and proliferation were assessed by CCK‑8 assay and direct cell counting, and drug interactions were quantified by calculating combination index (CI) values. Our results demonstrate that 2‑DG alone exhibits comparable potency in both cell lines (IC50 ≈ 1.3–1.6 mM) and lacks cross‑resistance with IM. Co‑treatment with 2‑DG and IM produced a dose‑dependent enhancement of growth inhibition in both KBM5 and KBM5‑T315I cells, with the majority of CI values falling below 1.0. These CI values indicate strong synergy. These findings suggest that targeting glycolysis with 2‑DG can restore IM sensitivity in T315I‑mutated CML cells and provide a mechanistic basis for further investigation of autophagy‑related pathways in this metabolic synthetic‑lethal strategy.
