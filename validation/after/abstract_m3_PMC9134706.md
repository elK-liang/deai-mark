Accumulating evidence indicates that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play critical roles in the development and progression of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC); however, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain largely uncharacterized. In this study, we analyzed two independent lncRNA + mRNA microarray profiles comprising 119 and 60 pairs of ESCC samples with matched adjacent normal tissues and identified DLEU1 as one of the most frequently overexpressed lncRNAs in ESCC. DLEU1 upregulation was observed in nearly all tumor specimens (99.2% and 98.3% in the two cohorts, respectively) and was further validated in ESCC samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and by RNAscope assays. High DLEU1 expression was associated with decreased overall survival and correlated positively with lymph node metastasis and advanced clinical stage. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed that DLEU1 expression was significantly associated with gene signatures related to cancer stem cells, metastatic prostate cancer, liver cancer with poor survival, and mitochondrial membrane permeability involved in apoptosis. This suggests a potential role for DLEU1 in mitochondrial apoptosis. Collectively, these findings identify DLEU1 as a frequently overexpressed lncRNA in ESCC that correlates with poor patient prognosis. This provides the rationale and a strong foundation for further mechanistic investigation into its role in promoting ESCC development and progression.
