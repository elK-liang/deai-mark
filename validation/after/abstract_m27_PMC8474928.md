Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is characterized by aggressive growth and rapid metastasis, yet the landscape of N6‑methyladenosine (m6A) modification and its contributors remains incompletely understood. By curating a comprehensive set of 30 m6A regulators (11 writers, 2 erasers, and 17 readers), we systematically interrogated their genetic and expression alterations in SCLC. Somatic mutation analysis of 110 primary tumors revealed that 28 samples (25.5 %) harbored at least one mutation in these regulators; readers exhibited the highest mutation frequencies, with FMR1 being the most frequently altered gene, whereas erasers were devoid of mutations. Notably, METTL3 and YTHDC2, as well as IGF2BP2 and YTHDC2, displayed co‑occurring mutations. Copy‑number variation (CNV) profiling across 53 SCLC cell lines demonstrated ubiquitous alterations: the majority of readers (11/17) showed prevalent amplification, while all erasers exhibited copy‑number loss. Integrating CNV data with transcriptomic profiles uncovered a markedly different expression pattern between normal lung tissue and SCLC, with writers and readers being broadly up‑regulated and erasers down‑regulated; IGF2BP3, a known oncogenic reader, was markedly elevated in SCLC for the first time. The coordinated CNV and expression changes suggest that genetic gains/losses drive the dysregulation of m6A regulators. Furthermore, comparison of 50 cell lines derived from primary versus metastatic sites revealed a consistent up‑regulation of writers and readers in metastatic models, implicating m6A machinery in SCLC dissemination. Together, these findings delineate pervasive genetic and transcriptional alterations of m6A regulators in SCLC. This provides a molecular foundation for future investigations into their immuno‑oncology relevance and therapeutic potential.
