Melanoma represents a heterogeneous group of malignancies arising from melanocytes, with increasing global incidence over recent decades. While cutaneous melanoma predominates in Western populations, Asian patients demonstrate a distinct epidemiological pattern characterized by a higher prevalence of acral and mucosal subtypes, which are associated with poorer prognosis and limited treatment responsiveness. Advanced melanoma has historically carried a dismal prognosis, particularly in patients who have progressed following first-line therapy. There is a critical need for novel therapeutic approaches in this population.

The advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors, particularly antibodies targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) pathway, has fundamentally transformed the management of advanced melanoma. PD-1 inhibitors such as pembrolizumab and nivolumab have demonstrated durable responses and improved overall survival in pivotal clinical trials, establishing anti-PD-1 therapy as standard of care for advanced melanoma. However, the majority of landmark studies enrolled predominantly Western patients with cutaneous melanoma, leaving a significant evidence gap regarding the efficacy and safety of these agents in Asian populations with predominantly acral and mucosal disease.

Camrelizumab (SHR-1210) is a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting PD-1 that has shown promising anti-tumor activity in various malignancies. Preclinical and early clinical studies have demonstrated its ability to disrupt PD-1 mediated immune suppression and promote T-cell activation against tumor cells. Given the unique characteristics of melanoma in Asian patients and the limited representation in prior immuno-oncology trials, evaluating camrelizumab in this specific population becomes essential for understanding its risk-benefit profile and establishing appropriate dosing.

We therefore conducted a phase I study to evaluate the safety, activity, and pharmacokinetics of camrelizumab in advanced Asian melanoma patients who had failed previous first-line treatment. This study employed a dose-escalation design with expansion cohorts to characterize the maximum tolerated dose, assess treatment-related adverse events, and preliminary efficacy across different dose levels. Herein, we report the findings from this investigation of camrelizumab in a predominantly Asian cohort with advanced melanoma, including a substantial proportion with acral and mucosal primaries.
