Abstract.  
Kidney transplant rejection (KTR) is a major cause of graft loss, yet reliable non‑invasive biomarkers for early detection and subtyping remain limited. Integrating multi‑omics data through network analysis may reveal key microRNA (miRNA)–mediated interactions specific to KTR.  
We constructed a KTR‑specific miRNA‑protein‑protein interaction network (miR‑PPIN) by combining differentially expressed miRNAs and mRNAs with miRNA‑mRNA regulatory pairs and protein–protein interactions. Topological features (degree, closeness, betweenness) were used to prioritize a CORE gene set that strengthens miRNA regulation. Network enrichment and degree distribution analyses were performed to characterize the system. Candidate miRNAs were selected based on significantly high normalized topological gain (NTGA) and normalized topological contribution (NTGC) values. Diagnostic potential was assessed by receiver‑operating‑characteristic (ROC) analysis on a training cohort and validated on an independent dataset.  
The miR‑PPIN comprised 949 miRNA‑mRNA interactions and 1,447 protein–protein links among 31 miRNAs and 642 genes, all abnormally expressed in KTR. The network genes were significantly enriched in T‑cell‑related biological processes (e.g., NK T‑cell proliferation, antigen processing/presentation, memory T‑cell activation). Node degrees followed a power‑law distribution (slope = −1.34). This indicates a hub‑and‑spoke architecture. Three miRNAs (miR‑145‑5p, miR‑155‑5p, and miR‑23b‑3p) were identified as candidate biomarkers, with miR‑145‑5p and miR‑23b‑3p downregulated and miR‑155‑5p upregulated (particularly in chronic mixed rejection). ROC analyses demonstrated that each miRNA and their combination discriminate KTR from stable grafts in both training and validation cohorts.  
The KTR‑specific miR‑PPIN reveals novel miRNA‑mediated pathways and prioritizes three miRNAs (miR‑145‑5p, miR‑155‑5p, miR‑23b‑3p) with high NTGA/NTGC values as promising non‑invasive biomarkers for KTR diagnosis and subtyping. This multi‑omics network framework provides a systems‑level resource for understanding rejection mechanisms and guiding clinical decision‑making.
